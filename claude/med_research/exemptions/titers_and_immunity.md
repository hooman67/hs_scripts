# Topic 1b — Titers / Proof of Existing Immunity (the "no new shot" door via prior evidence)

**Question:** For each vaccine required on Form I-693, does USCIS/CDC accept (a) a serologic
titer proving immunity or (b) a documented prior vaccination record IN PLACE of a new shot?
What evidence does the civil surgeon accept, and how is it recorded? Goal: identify which
requirements Hooman (and his wife) can clear with a blood test or old records and ZERO new shots.

**Researched:** 2026-07-07 by Claude (Opus 4.8), from primary sources read live this session.
**Bottom line up front:** For **MMR (measles/mumps/rubella), varicella, hepatitis A, hepatitis B,
and polio**, USCIS/CDC explicitly accept a **serologic titer showing immunity** OR an
**acceptable documented prior-vaccination record** in place of a new shot. Varicella also
accepts a reliable **history of the disease**. **Tetanus/diphtheria/pertussis (Td/Tdap),
influenza, and the age-restricted childhood vaccines (Hib, rotavirus, pneumococcal,
meningococcal) do NOT have a titer pathway** — for those, only a documented vaccination record
(or a not-age-appropriate / contraindication route) avoids a shot. This is a legitimate,
truthful door and requires **no waiver form**.

## 2. The titer / prior-record door, disease by disease

CDC's exact rule (S1, verbatim, **VERIFIED**):

> "Laboratory evidence of immunity is acceptable for **measles, mumps, rubella, hepatitis A,
> hepatitis B, polio\*, and varicella** if the applicant lacks acceptable documented history of
> vaccination for these diseases."
> (\* "Titers must include all three poliovirus types to be acceptable.")

And on records (S1, verbatim, **VERIFIED**):

> "Only those records of vaccine doses that include the dates of receipt (month, day, and year)
> are acceptable. The document must not appear to have been altered, and dates of vaccinations
> should seem reasonable." … "Self-reported vaccine doses without written documentation are not
> acceptable."

Varicella extra route (S1, verbatim, **VERIFIED**):

> "An applicant who provides a reliable written or oral history of varicella disease does not
> require laboratory confirmation or further vaccination."

### Per-vaccine clearance matrix

| **Measles (MMR)** | **YES** (S1) | YES — dated written record (S1) | — | VERIFIED |
| **Mumps (MMR)** | **YES** (S1) | YES (S1) | — | VERIFIED |
| **Rubella (MMR)** | **YES** (S1) | YES (S1) | — | VERIFIED |
| **Varicella (chickenpox)** | **YES** (S1) | YES (S1) | **Reliable written/oral history of the disease** (S1) | VERIFIED |
| **Hepatitis B** | **YES** (S1) | YES (S1) | — | VERIFIED |

**Key reading:** the titer door is a real "no new shot" path for the **7 titer-eligible
diseases** (measles, mumps, rubella, varicella, hep A, hep B, polio). For the rest, the shot is
avoided either by a **valid dated prior record**, by the vaccine **not being age-appropriate**
for an adult (Hib, rotavirus, and typically meningococcal/pneumococcal), or by a **medical
contraindication** (covered in the companion file `medical_contraindication.md`). Td/Tdap has
**no titer option** and, per S2 footnote 3, needs a booster if the last dose was >10 years ago —
so this is the one adult vaccine most likely to still call for a shot absent a contraindication.

## 3. Titer vs. prior record — which to use, and the fine print

- **Titer (serology) is an ALTERNATIVE, not a requirement.** CDC frames labs as "acceptable …
  **if the applicant lacks acceptable documented history of vaccination**" (S1). So: if Hooman
  already has a clean dated record showing the required doses, that record alone satisfies the
  requirement — no titer needed and no shot. The titer is the fallback when records are missing,
  incomplete, or unavailable. **[VERIFIED]**
- **Titer must show IMMUNITY (a positive/protective result).** A titer showing non-immunity does
  not help and would normally lead to vaccination. So the titer door only closes a requirement
  if the antibody result is protective. **[VERIFIED — direct from "evidence of immunity" language]**
- **Test quality:** CDC requires FDA-approved or CLIA-certified antibody test kits (S1). A titer
  from an accredited US clinical lab meets this. **[VERIFIED]**
- **Polio titer is stricter:** must include all three poliovirus types (S1). **[VERIFIED]**
- **Records must be dated (m/d/y), unaltered, and reasonable** (S1). Self-reported / undated
  histories are rejected. Practical implication: dig up Hooman's actual immunization records
  (childhood, school, prior employer/travel clinics, home-country records with dates).
  **[VERIFIED]**
- **Partial series:** if a prior dose exists and the next dose in a series is due, CDC says "the
  next required dose should be administered" (S1) — i.e., a partial record can still trigger a
  shot to complete a series unless a titer proves immunity or a contraindication applies.
  **[VERIFIED]**
- **Dose counts / spacing come from ACIP,** not from the CDC civil-surgeon page itself: "the
  civil surgeon should refer to the standard ACIP recommendations" for number of doses and
  spacing (S1). So "how many doses = complete" is an ACIP question (e.g., 2 doses MMR, 2 doses
  varicella) — flagged for the contraindication/requirement files to pin exactly. **[VERIFIED
  that CDC defers to ACIP; specific dose counts UNVERIFIED here]**

## 4. How the civil surgeon RECORDS immunity on Form I-693 (Part 10)

This is the mechanical answer to "how does the surgeon put it on the form."

Form I-693, **Part 10, Vaccination Record** (S3, Edition 01/20/25) is a grid. For each vaccine
row the relevant columns are (verbatim column headers, **VERIFIED**):

1. **"Vaccine History Transferred From A Written Record"** — up to four **"Date Received
   (mm/dd/yyyy)"** sub-columns. → This is where a **documented prior vaccination** is entered
   (the dated doses from old records).
2. **"Vaccine Given / Date Given by Civil Surgeon (mm/dd/yyyy)"** — only filled if a shot is
   administered at the exam. (Left blank when no new shot is given.)
3. **"Complete Series"** column, whose exact instruction on the form is (verbatim, **VERIFIED**):
   > "Mark 'X' if complete; **write date of lab test if immune** or 'VH' if varicella history"
   → **This is the immunity annotation.** For a positive titer, the surgeon **writes the date of
   the lab (titer) test** in this column. For varicella proven by disease history, the surgeon
   writes **"VH"**. For a completed series proven by record, the surgeon marks **"X"**.
4. **"Blanket Waiver(s) to be Requested from USCIS (Not Medically Appropriate)"** — sub-columns
   **"Not Age-Appropriate," "Contraindication," "Insufficient Time Interval,"** and a
   flu/COVID availability box. (This is the *contraindication* door, not the immunity door —
   see `medical_contraindication.md`.)

So, concretely, to clear a disease by immunity with **no shot**, the surgeon either:
- enters the **dated prior doses** under "Vaccine History Transferred From A Written Record" and
  marks **"X"** in Complete Series (record proves completed series); or
- **writes the titer lab-test date** in the Complete Series column (serology proves immunity); or
- for varicella, writes **"VH"** (reliable disease history).

**Results box (bottom of Part 10):** the surgeon then checks one summary box. If all required
vaccines are satisfied (by shots, records, or immunity), the correct box is **"Applicant
completed vaccination requirements or may be eligible for blanket waivers as indicated above."**
Proven immunity counts toward "completed the requirements" — it is **NOT** a "blanket waiver"
and is **NOT** the religious/moral individual-waiver box. **[VERIFIED from S2 §F + S3 results
box labels]**

USCIS adjudication side (S2, **VERIFIED**): "all age appropriate vaccine rows of the vaccination
assessment must have at least one entry before the assessment can be considered to have been
properly completed." A titer date or a transferred record IS a valid entry for that row.

## 5. COVID-19: no titer question — it is simply gone

Directly relevant to Hooman's central fear, and it removes the need to invoke immunity/titers
for COVID at all. From S2 (verbatim, **VERIFIED**):

> "On January 22, 2025, USCIS announced that as of January 20, 2025, officers would no longer
> issue any RFEs or Notices of Intent to Deny (NOIDs), or deny adjustment of status
> applications, based on an applicant's failure to submit documentation of having received the
> COVID-19 vaccination. On **March 11, 2025, CDC updated their Technical Instructions for Civil
> Surgeons to remove the COVID-19 vaccination from the list of required vaccinations** for
> immigration purposes. Therefore, applicants who are subject to the immigrant vaccination
> requirements are not required to submit documentation of having completed the COVID-19
> vaccination series for any application pending on or after January 20, 2025."

The CDC civil-surgeon page (S1) confirms: COVID-19 does **not** appear in its current required
list (15 diseases: Diphtheria, Tetanus, Pertussis, Polio, Measles, Mumps, Rubella, Rotavirus,
Hib, Hepatitis A, Hepatitis B, Meningococcal, Varicella, Pneumococcal, Influenza).

**Caveat / cross-ref:** The *form and instructions* are Edition 01/20/25 and **still print
COVID-19 rows and COVID language** (the form predates the Mar-11-2025 CDC change). That is a
stale artifact — the controlling adjudication rule (S2, current as of May 08 2026) says COVID is
not required. This belongs primarily to the requirement file (`requirement/vaccine_requirement.md`),
noted here because "no COVID shot needed" is the biggest single win and it needs **no titer**.

## 8. One-line answer to the topic question

**Yes — for measles, mumps, rubella, varicella, hepatitis A, hepatitis B, and polio, a positive
serologic titer OR a valid dated prior-vaccination record substitutes for a new shot, recorded
by the civil surgeon in Part 10 (titer → write lab-test date in "Complete Series"; record →
dated doses under "Vaccine History Transferred From A Written Record" + "X"; varicella disease
history → "VH"), with no waiver form.** Td/Tdap, influenza, and the age-restricted childhood
vaccines have no titer path and rely on records, age, or contraindication instead.
