## 0. BOTTOM LINE FOR HOOMAN (read this first)

- The religious/moral waiver is **real** and codified, but for Hooman it is **strongly DEMOTED**
  and should be treated as a reserve/last-resort door, *not* a lead. **[ASSUMED-HIGH]**
- The reason is the **first legal requirement**: he must be "**opposed to all vaccinations in any
  form**" — a categorical objection, not a COVID-specific or vaccine-specific one. Hooman has
  received "various vaccines over his life." USCIS's own rule says prior vaccination is **not an
  automatic denial**, but it explicitly shifts the burden onto him to explain *why* he took them
  (e.g., a genuine, documented change of belief since then) and to show the categorical objection
  is **applied consistently** in his life. That is a hard, adjudicator-dependent showing. **[VERIFIED — see §3.1]**
- **A COVID-specific objection does NOT qualify.** "Pick and choose" objections are denied at Step 3
  of USCIS's own checklist. Hooman's stated concern is largely COVID-vaccine-centered (the LGL link),
  which is the *opposite* of the "in any form" test. **[VERIFIED — see §3.1, §6]**
- **Moral (non-religious) objection DOES qualify** — same statute, same three-part test, same bar.
  No church membership required. So switching from "religious" to "moral" framing does **not** lower
  the bar; the sincerity + "any form" tests are identical. **[VERIFIED — see §3.2]**
- **Better doors exist and should be exhausted first** (this file only covers 1d): titers/proof of
  existing immunity (H2) and medical contraindication — his LGL/immunosuppression and his wife's
  pregnancy (H3). Those clear vaccines with **no I-601, no fee, no discretion, no sincerity fight**.
  This waiver is what you fall back to only for a vaccine that (a) is still required, (b) he has no
  immunity to, and (c) has no medical contraindication.

**One important correction to the tasking:** the correct statutory citation is **INA 212(g)(2)(C)**
(8 U.S.C. 1182(g)(2)(C)) and **8 CFR 212.7** — *not* "INA 212(g)(1)(C)" as written in the topic
brief. 212(g)(1) is the TB/physical-mental-disorder waiver; **212(g)(2)** is the vaccination waiver,
and subparagraph **(C)** is the religious-belief/moral-conviction prong. **[VERIFIED — see §2]**

## 1. Where this door sits in the funnel

The vaccination requirement (INA 212(a)(1)(A)(ii)) has, per USCIS Policy Manual Vol. 9, Part D,
Ch. 3(A), exactly **three** waiver routes, mapping to the three subparagraphs of INA 212(g)(2):

| Route | Statute | Form/fee? | Who decides | Covered in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Received the missing vaccine / documentation cured | 212(g)(2)(A) | **No form, no fee** (RFE → re-exam) | USCIS officer, auto-grant | file 1b/1c |
| Vaccine **not medically appropriate** (contraindication, age, timing, shortage) — "blanket waiver" | 212(g)(2)(B) | **No form, no fee**; civil surgeon annotates I-693 | USCIS officer, near-auto | **file 1c** (his LGL, wife's pregnancy) |
| **Religious belief / moral conviction** — "individual waiver" | **212(g)(2)(C)** | **Form I-601 + fee** | USCIS officer, **discretionary** | **THIS FILE** |

Key structural point: the first two are "**blanket waivers**" — no application form, no fee, the
civil surgeon's annotation on Form I-693 does the work. The religious/moral waiver is the **only**
one of the three that requires **filing Form I-601, paying a fee, and surviving a discretionary
adjudication**. It is therefore the most expensive, slowest, and riskiest door. **[VERIFIED — Vol.9 Pt.D Ch.3(A)]**

## 3. The three-part legal test (the heart of this door)

Verbatim from **9 USCIS-PM D.3(E)(2)** ("Requirements"). USCIS states it "in consultation with CDC,
has established the following three requirements that an applicant … has to demonstrate through
**documentary evidence**." **[VERIFIED — all quotes below read live from the Policy Manual page]**

### 3.1 Requirement 1 — "opposed to all vaccinations in any form" (the killer requirement for Hooman)

> "The applicant must be opposed to all vaccinations in any form. The applicant has to demonstrate
> that he or she opposes vaccinations in all forms; the applicant **cannot 'pick and choose'**
> between the vaccinations. **The fact that the applicant has received certain vaccinations but not
> others is not automatic grounds for the denial of a waiver.** Instead, the officer should consider
> the reasons provided for having received those vaccines. For example, the applicant's religious
> beliefs or moral convictions may have **changed substantially since the date the particular
> vaccinations were administered**, or the applicant is a child who may have already received
> certain vaccinations under the routine practices of an orphanage."

- **Historical note (USCIS footnote 16):** "The requirement that the religious or moral objection
  must apply to all vaccines has been in effect **since 1997**. The former INS created this policy in
  light of principles developed regarding conscientious objection to the military draft and
  challenges to State-mandated vaccinations for public school students." **[VERIFIED]**
- **Adjudication checklist confirms it is a hard gate:** at **Step 3** the officer reviews "whether
  the applicant opposes the vaccination requirement **in any form**"; if not → "**The waiver should
  be denied and the applicant is inadmissible**." **[VERIFIED — 9 USCIS-PM D.3(F)]**

**ADVERSARIAL ASSESSMENT (Hooman-specific) — this is the load-bearing conclusion:**
- Prior vaccines are **not an automatic bar** — the rule explicitly says so. **[VERIFIED]** But the
  burden is on Hooman to supply a **credible reason** for having taken them, and the only reason the
  Policy Manual illustrates for an adult is a **substantial change of belief since** those shots.
  **[VERIFIED that this is the illustrated path]**
- To use that path truthfully, Hooman would need a **genuine, documentable, and consistently-applied
  present-day categorical objection to *all* vaccines** that post-dates his prior vaccinations — not
  a COVID-specific safety concern. A concern rooted in "the COVID vaccine harmed me / caused my LGL"
  is a **safety/medical objection to a specific vaccine** — the archetype of the "pick and choose"
  the test rejects. **[ASSUMED-HIGH — direct application of the verified test]**
- Because "moral convictions" are defined by USCIS as things that "**categorically disregard
  self-interest**" and are "**applied consistently in the applicant's life**," an objection that
  reads as *self-interested harm avoidance* (avoiding a shot he believes injured him) is the **weakest
  possible fit** and is exactly what the sincerity prong is designed to screen out. **[ASSUMED-HIGH — see §3.3]**
- **GUARDRAIL FLAG:** manufacturing a "categorical, always-held, changed-since-then" objection that
  isn't genuinely how Hooman lives would be a **material misrepresentation** on an immigration
  benefit application → potential **permanent** inadmissibility bar under INA 212(a)(6)(C),
  categorically worse than any vaccine. This door is only usable if the belief is **actually true of
  him**. If it isn't, this door is closed — full stop. Do not engineer around it. **[VERIFIED principle — CLAUDE.md §5; INA 212(a)(6)(C)]**

**Net:** Requirement 1 is where Hooman's case is weakest. His vaccine history + COVID-specific
framing point away from the categorical "in any form" objection the statute requires. **This is why
1d is demoted.**

### 3.2 Requirement 2 — objection based on religious beliefs OR moral convictions

> "The objection must be based on religious beliefs or moral convictions. … The applicant **does not
> need to be a member of a recognized religion or attend a specific house of worship.** Note that the
> plain language of the statute refers to religious beliefs **or moral convictions**, not religious
> or moral establishments."

- **Moral (non-religious) objection fully qualifies.** **[VERIFIED]** There is **no separate or higher
  bar** for a moral vs. a religious objection — it is the same statute (212(g)(2)(C) names both), the
  same three-part test, and the same discretionary standard. Switching to a "moral" framing does
  **not** relax Requirements 1 (any-form) or 3 (sincerity). **[VERIFIED — direct from D.3(E)(2)]**
- USCIS instructs officers to handle this "with sensitivity" and balance the belief "against the
  benefit to society." **[VERIFIED]**

### 3.3 Requirement 3 — the belief must be sincere

> "The religious belief or moral conviction must be sincere. To protect only those beliefs that are
> held as a matter of conscience, the applicant must demonstrate that he or she holds the belief
> **sincerely, and in subjective good faith of an adherent.** … they must stem from religious or
> moral convictions, and **must not have been framed in terms of a particular belief so as to gain
> the legal remedy desired, such as this waiver.** … the focus of the waiver adjudication should be
> on whether that claimed belief or moral conviction is **truly held, that is, whether it is applied
> consistently in the applicant's life.** … It is necessary to distinguish between strong religious
> beliefs or moral convictions and **mere preference.** … The applicant has the **burden** of
> establishing a strong objection … as opposed to a **mere preference** against vaccinations."

- USCIS defines qualifying convictions by their power to "cause an adherent to **categorically
  disregard self-interest** in favor of religious or moral tenets." **[VERIFIED]**
- **Adversarial read for Hooman:** the sincerity prong explicitly targets beliefs "framed … so as to
  gain the legal remedy" and "mere preference." An objection assembled *for* the AOS, or that is
  really "I don't want *this* vaccine because of *my* health," is exactly what fails here. The
  "applied consistently in your life" test collides directly with a record of prior voluntary
  vaccinations. **[ASSUMED-HIGH — direct application of verified language]**

## 4. Evidence required (Policy Manual D.3(E)(3))

> "The applicant's objection … **may be established through the applicant's sworn statement.** In this
> statement, the applicant should state the **exact nature** of those religious beliefs or moral
> convictions and establish **how such beliefs would be violated or compromised** by complying with
> the vaccination requirements. **Additional corroborating evidence** supporting the background for
> the religious belief or moral conviction, if available and credible, should also be submitted …
> For example, regular participation in a congregation … affidavits from other members … or evidence
> of regular volunteer work. The officer should consider all evidence submitted." **[VERIFIED]**

Practical evidence set (assembled from the above): **[ASSUMED — reasonable synthesis]**
- Applicant's **detailed sworn statement** (nature of belief; why it forbids *all* vaccines; how AOS
  compliance violates it; when/how the belief arose; if prior vaccines exist, a truthful account of
  why and a genuine change since).
- **Corroboration**: affidavits from clergy/community/family attesting to the long-held, consistent
  practice; membership/participation records; a pattern of belief-consistent life choices; any
  contemporaneous writings predating the AOS.
- The **Form I-601** itself + fee + a copy of the I-693 showing the vaccination row(s) unmet and the
  "religious/moral" box checked by the civil surgeon (see §5).

## 5. Process & mechanics (how it actually flows)

**[VERIFIED — assembled from 8 USCIS-PM B.9 and 9 USCIS-PM D.3(A),(E),(F)]**

1. **Civil surgeon exam.** If Hooman declines a required, medically-appropriate vaccine on
   religious/moral grounds, the civil surgeon must check the I-693 box **"Applicant will request an
   individual waiver based on religious or moral convictions."** Per Vol. 8: "**this is not a blanket
   waiver, and the applicant will have to submit a waiver request on Form I-601.**" The surgeon checks
   this box **even if** a blanket waiver would otherwise apply; if both boxes are checked, the officer
   just requests the religious/moral documentation.
2. **USCIS review.** On adjudicating the I-485, the officer sees the unmet vaccine + checked box.
3. **RFE.** The officer issues an **RFE** (USCIS instructs "**only one RFE**," bundling everything,
   including the request to file the waiver) — or raises it at the I-485 interview.
4. **File Form I-601** with fee and the evidence in §4.
5. **Adjudication** against the 6-step checklist (D.3(F)): (1) is there any indication of a
   religious/moral objection? (2) did the applicant oppose the vaccines / willing to get them? →
   (3) opposes **in any form**? (4) on account of **religious/moral** grounds? (5) is the belief
   **sincere**? (6) **discretion**. Fail any of Steps 3–5 → "waiver should be denied and the applicant
   is **inadmissible** under INA 212(a)(1)(A)(ii)."
6. **No CDC review.** Unlike the medical-contraindication blanket waiver, "there is **no requirement
   that CDC review this waiver**." It's purely a USCIS legal/discretion call. **[VERIFIED]**

**Adjudicator:** a **USCIS officer** (not the civil surgeon, not CDC, not the consular physician).
The civil surgeon can only *flag* the request; the surgeon has **no authority** to grant it. **[VERIFIED]**

## 6. Discretion (D.3(E)(4))

> "As is generally the case for waivers, a waiver of the vaccination requirement requires an officer
> to consider whether the grant of the waiver is warranted **as a matter of discretion.** A favorable
> exercise of discretion is **generally warranted** if the applicant establishes that he or she
> objects to the vaccination requirement on account of religious beliefs or moral convictions." **[VERIFIED]**

- **Good news within this door:** discretion is *not* the main obstacle. The checklist (Step 6) says
  "ordinarily, the finding that the applicant holds **sincere** religious or moral objections **should
  be sufficient for a grant**." **[VERIFIED]** So if he genuinely clears Steps 3–5, discretion is
  usually a formality.
- **The obstacle is Steps 3–5**, i.e., §3 above — the "any form" + sincerity gates, which are where a
  vaccinated, COVID-focused applicant is most exposed.

## 8. Documented denial patterns / risk map

Primary-source signals of where these waivers die (all **[VERIFIED]** from D.3(E)/(F) unless noted):
- **"Pick and choose" / vaccine-specific objection** → denied at Step 3 ("in any form"). This is the
  most common failure mode and the one most relevant to Hooman.
- **Belief "framed … so as to gain the legal remedy desired"** → fails sincerity (Step 5).
- **"Mere preference" vs. strong conviction** → fails sincerity; burden on applicant.
- **Inconsistency with the applicant's actual life** (e.g., a record of prior voluntary vaccinations
  with no genuine changed belief) → undercuts "applied consistently."
- **No religious/moral basis at all** (pure medical/safety fear) → fails Step 4; that objection
  belongs in the **medical-contraindication** door (file 1c), not here.

## 9. Why 1d is DEMOTED for Hooman (plain statement, as tasked)

1. **He has taken various vaccines.** The "opposed to all vaccinations **in any form**" requirement
   (in force since 1997) puts the burden on him to explain those and show a categorical, consistently-
   held objection. Not impossible on paper, but a genuine fit is unlikely given his history. **[VERIFIED test + ASSUMED-HIGH application]**
2. **His objection is substantially COVID-specific/health-based.** That is a "pick and choose" +
   safety objection — the exact profile the test rejects (Steps 3–4). Reframing it as "moral" does not
   help, because "moral convictions" get the identical any-form + sincerity scrutiny. **[VERIFIED]**
3. **Higher-quality doors exist for his real situation.** His LGL/immunosuppression and his wife's
   pregnancy are **genuine medical contraindications** → the **212(g)(2)(B) blanket waiver**: no form,
   no fee, no discretion, no sincerity fight, documented by the civil surgeon on the I-693. And prior
   immunity → titers (212(g)(2)(A)/H2). Those are truthful, cheaper, faster, and durable. **[VERIFIED
   structure; see files 1b/1c for the vaccine-by-vaccine mapping.]**
4. **Truthfulness guardrail forbids the workaround.** The only way to "strengthen" 1d for Hooman would
   be to overstate a categorical belief he doesn't actually hold — which is misrepresentation and a
   permanent bar. So 1d is genuinely a **reserve door**, usable only if some required vaccine survives
   H2 and H3 *and* Hooman genuinely holds a categorical objection. **[VERIFIED principle]**

**Recommendation to carry into the decision map:** rank 1d **below** titers (H2) and medical
contraindication (H3); keep it only as a documented fallback, and only if a genuine categorical
objection actually applies. Do not build the case around it.
