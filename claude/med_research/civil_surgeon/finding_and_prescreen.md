## 0. Why this step even matters (the one-paragraph frame)

Per the decision map, both spouses can plausibly reach **zero new shots** through
**self-executing doors** — titers/prior-record (H2) and genuine contraindication (H3, live
vaccines only), plus age/season blanket waivers. But those doors only work **if the civil
surgeon actually records them correctly on the I-693**: writes the lab-test date in the
"Complete Series / immune" column, marks the "Contraindication" blanket-waiver reason for the
live rows Hooman's/his wife's condition genuinely contraindicates, transfers dated prior doses
from a written record, and checks the correct Results box (#1, not #3). A surgeon who is
sloppy, unfamiliar with the current form, or reflexively "just gives the shots" can force
avoidable vaccinations or trigger an RFE even when a truthful door exists. **This step is about
finding a competent, current, cooperative surgeon — not a compliant one.** VERIFIED (mechanics
from Topic 1a; documentation rules from CDC, §7).

## 1. How the USCIS "Find a Civil Surgeon" locator works  ✅ VERIFIED

**Official tool:** *Find a Civil Surgeon* — `https://www.uscis.gov/tools/find-a-civil-surgeon`
(the older link `my.uscis.gov/findadoctor` 302-redirects here). This is the **only** official
list; do not rely on third-party "immigration doctor" directories. VERIFIED (page read live via
Wayback snapshot 20260523021017, 2026-07-08).

**Search inputs the tool accepts:**
- **Address, city, state, or ZIP code** — free-text location box ("Enter your address, city,
  state, or ZIP code to find a designated civil surgeon near you"). It returns surgeons sorted
  by distance ("… miles away"). VERIFIED.
- **Languages Spoken** filter — a long dropdown (Any + ~150 languages incl. Farsi/Persian, if
  that helps for either spouse). VERIFIED.
- **Doctor Sex** filter — Any / Female / Male. (Useful for the wife's OB-adjacent comfort, her
  call.) VERIFIED.
- Results are **paginated** ("1 – 10 of 6784 total rows" nationwide in the snapshot; Previous/
  Next). VERIFIED.

**What each result row returns** (per the tool's output fields): the **doctor / civil-surgeon
name, the practice, the address, distance in miles, gender**, and contact details. VERIFIED
(field labels visible on page). The individual phone number for each listing is shown on the
result card. **[Exact per-listing field layout ASSUMED-HIGH — the results render via a live
widget that the static Wayback capture only partially preserved; confirm on the live site.]**

**Important scoping facts USCIS states on that page (VERIFIED, verbatim / near-verbatim):**
- The immigration medical exam **must** be performed by a USCIS-**designated** civil surgeon
  (except: all **military physicians** are designated for exams at military treatment

  Hooman is neither, so he needs a **designated civil surgeon from this locator.** VERIFIED.
- "Some aliens applying for a Green Card **only need the vaccination portion** of the

  VERIFIED.
- **What to bring** (VERIFIED, verbatim list): the **Form I-693** itself; **government photo
  ID** (passport / driver's license); **a record of your vaccinations/immunizations** (USCIS
  lists "DT, DTP, DTaP, Td, Tdap, OPV, IPV, MMR, Hib, Hepatitis A, Hepatitis B, Varicella,

  not cover all portions of this examination"); and **payment**.
- **Cost varies a lot** — USCIS itself says: "It may be helpful to **call a few civil surgeons

- **The sealed-envelope rule** (VERIFIED, verbatim): "After the … examination, the civil

## 3. THE PHONE PRE-SCREEN SCRIPT

**How to use it:** This is a checklist you can read almost verbatim. Keep it factual and neutral
— you are a well-prepared patient asking procedural questions, not asking for favors. Most of
these will be answered by the front-desk / medical assistant; the clinical ones (Q6–Q9) may need
the doctor or nurse. Take notes in the §4 scorecard as you go. **Everything below asks only how
they document *genuine* facts. If a question ever tempts a "we can just…" workaround answer,
that's a red flag (§5), not a win.**

---

**Opening (identify yourself, set scope):**

> "Hi — I'm calling to pre-screen for a **USCIS immigration medical exam (Form I-693)** for an
> **adjustment-of-status (I-485)** case. I found your office on the USCIS 'Find a Civil Surgeon'
> list. I have a few questions before I book, and there are two adults in my case — myself and
> **my wife, who is currently pregnant**. Do you have a couple of minutes, or is there a better
> time to call back?"

**Q1 — Designation & scope (confirm they're the real thing):**
> "Can you confirm the physician is a **current USCIS-designated civil surgeon**? And do you
> handle the **vaccination/Form I-693 portion** as well as the full exam?"
- *Want:* clear yes; they clearly do I-693 work. *(Sanity check against the locator listing.)*

**Q2 — Current form edition (must be 01/20/25):**
> "Which **edition of Form I-693** are you completing right now? I understand USCIS only accepts
> the **01/20/25 edition** for exams signed on or after July 3, 2025 — can you confirm you're on
> that edition?"
- *Want:* "01/20/25" (or "the current/latest USCIS edition"). VERIFIED that 01/20/25 is the only
  edition accepted for civil-surgeon signatures on/after 2025-07-03 (Topic 1a).
- 🚩 *Concern:* if they name an **older edition** (e.g. 03/09/23) or "aren't sure," they may be
  running stale forms — ask that they use the current one, and treat vagueness as a caution flag.

**Q3 — Do they credit PRIOR VACCINATION RECORDS (the free door):**
> "If I bring **written vaccination records with dates** for some of the required vaccines, will
> the civil surgeon **transfer those into the I-693** from the written record, so those don't
> get repeated?"
- *Want:* "Yes, bring dated written records and we'll transfer them." This is the I-693 "Vaccine
  History Transferred From A Written Record" column (Topic 1a). VERIFIED that USCIS/CDC require
  **written documentation with month/day/year** — "self-reported doses without written
  documentation are not acceptable" (CDC, §7). So the honest ask is only about *documented*
  doses.

**Q4 — Do they accept SEROLOGIC TITERS as proof of immunity (the H2 door):**
> "For the vaccines where **immunity can be shown by a blood test** — measles, mumps, rubella,
> varicella, hepatitis A, hepatitis B, and polio — will you **accept a serologic titer / lab
> report showing immunity** and record it in the 'Complete Series' column as immune, instead of
> re-vaccinating? Do you **draw the titers in-house**, or should I have them **done in advance**
> so I can bring the lab report?"
- *Want:* "Yes, positive titers count as immunity and we record the lab date; we can draw them
  here" **or** "bring a dated lab report and we'll accept it." VERIFIED that the "Complete
  Series — write date of lab test if immune" column is exactly this door (Topic 1a); CDC requires
  documented immunity, not self-report (§7).
- 🚩 *Concern:* a flat "we don't do titers, we just vaccinate" means they'll waste the H2 door —
  deprioritize them (not disqualifying if all else is strong, but you'd have to bring outside
  labs). This is a **competence/cooperation** filter, not an integrity one.

**Q5 — Do they know the blanket-waiver / contraindication columns:**
> "I understand the I-693 has **blanket-waiver columns** the civil surgeon can mark — **not
> age-appropriate**, **contraindication**, **insufficient time interval**, and **not flu
> season**. Is the physician comfortable **marking those when they genuinely apply**, so those
> vaccines don't need to be given?"
- *Want:* familiarity and a matter-of-fact yes. These are self-executing, no I-601 (Topic 1a).
- 🚩 *Concern:* confusion about what these columns are.

**Q6 — Contraindication from a documented medical condition (Hooman's LGL) — the key clinical one:**
> "I have a **hematologic condition — large granular lymphocytic (LGL) leukemia — that is
> managed by my hematologist**, and it can affect the immune system. If my **treating
> hematologist provides a letter** stating that a **live vaccine (like MMR or varicella) is
> medically contraindicated** for me, will the civil surgeon **rely on that letter and mark the
> 'contraindication' box** for those vaccines on the I-693?"
- *Want:* "Yes — if your specialist documents a genuine contraindication, we'll note it and mark
  the box." VERIFIED that USCIS treats "a condition other than pregnancy" contraindication as a
  self-executing blanket waiver and instructs officers not to RFE it (Topic 1a); CDC says the
  surgeon should **defer** the vaccine and document the contraindication (§7).
- 🚩 **Integrity boundary (important):** the honest framing is "*if* my hematologist says it's
  contraindicated." You are **not** asking the surgeon to declare a contraindication he hasn't
  judged, and the surgeon shouldn't offer to. The clinical judgment ("is this severe
  immunosuppression / altered immunocompetence?") is a **real medical determination** made by
  Hooman's hematologist and/or the civil surgeon — never something to script into existence.
  (Decision-map open item routed to the hematologist.)

**Q7 — Will they coordinate with an outside specialist letter at all:**
> "Are you generally comfortable **accepting a letter from a treating specialist** as part of
> the record, or do you prefer to make all determinations in-house?"
- *Want:* willingness to accept a specialist letter. ASSUMED helpful; some surgeons prefer to
  independently assess — that's fine and even reassuring, as long as they'll consider the letter.

**Q8 — Hepatitis B titer specifics (a flagged open item):**
> "For **hepatitis B**, will you accept a standard **anti-HBs (surface-antibody) titer** as
> evidence of immunity?"
- *Want:* yes. VERIFIED that Hep B is titer-eligible; the specific **anti-HBs** marker was
  ASSUMED in the decision map (open item 1b) — this call is a chance to confirm it with the
  surgeon.

**Q9 — Pregnancy contraindication for the wife (her live-vaccine door + comfort):**
> "My wife is **pregnant** and is a co-applicant with her own I-693. I understand **live
> vaccines like MMR and varicella are contraindicated in pregnancy**. Will the civil surgeon
> **mark the pregnancy contraindication** on her I-693 so she doesn't need those live vaccines?
> And are you **comfortable performing the immigration exam on a pregnant patient**?"
- *Want:* clear "yes, pregnancy is a standard contraindication for live vaccines and we mark it,"
  plus comfort examining a pregnant patient. VERIFIED — USCIS states that if a woman was pregnant
  at the exam and the contraindication box is marked, she is **not required to get those vaccines
  later** (Topic 1a). This is why her exam must happen **while she is still pregnant** (decision
  map §5). Note the surgeon should still record Tdap/flu per normal (those are *recommended* in
  pregnancy — not contraindicated).

**Q10 — Refusal recorded correctly (only if it ever comes to that):**
> "In the unlikely event a specific vaccine is **neither needed nor exempt** and I decide not to
> take it, how do you **document that** on the form?"
- *Want:* an accurate description — CDC's rule is that the surgeon documents "**vaccine
  requirements are not complete and the applicant refuses vaccination**" (§7). This is a **last
  resort** and leads to inadmissibility (Topic 1e); you're only checking they'd record it
  *truthfully*, not asking them to hide it. Most calls won't need this question.

**Q11 — Logistics (cost, insurance, titer turnaround, sealed copy, timeline):**
> "A few logistics: **What's the total cost** for the I-693 exam (and separately for any titers
> or the TB/blood work)? Do you **accept my insurance** for any of it? If you draw titers,
> **how long until results** so the form can be completed? Do you **give me a copy of the
> completed I-693 before you seal the envelope**? And **how soon can you schedule**, and what's
> the **turnaround** to a finished, sealed form?"
- *Want:* a clear price (USCIS itself says to shop — §1), reasonable turnaround, and — critically
  — **"yes, you get a copy before we seal it."** VERIFIED that the copy-before-sealing step is
  official (§1). Timeline matters for the wife's pregnancy window.

**Close:**
> "That's everything — thank you. I'll confirm timing with my immigration attorney and call back
> to book."

## 5. 🚩 DISQUALIFYING RED FLAGS (screen these OUT — they signal fraud, not helpfulness)

If a surgeon or their office **volunteers** any of the following, do **not** book them — and do
not steer them there. These would manufacture a misrepresentation, exposing Hooman to the
**INA 212(a)(6)(C)(i) lifetime bar** (decision map guardrail). Report egregious cases to the
**state medical board** / OPSCivilSurgeons@uscis.dhs.gov per §1.

- Offers to **mark a contraindication without any clinical basis** or without your specialist's
  documentation ("don't worry, I'll just write it's contraindicated").
- Offers to **sign the I-693 without actually examining** you / your wife, or to sign a form for
  someone they haven't seen.
- Offers to **record immunity without a real titer or dated record** ("I'll just check the
  'immune' box").
- Offers to **back-date** a vaccination, a lab test, or the form.
- Offers to **omit or hide** something true (e.g., "we just won't mention the pregnancy /
  the refusal / the missing dose").
- Suggests a **religious/moral objection** you don't actually hold, or coaches you to claim one.
- Anything framed as "how we **get around** the requirement" rather than how it's **truthfully
  documented**.

**Rule of thumb:** you want a surgeon who says *"if it's genuinely true and documented, I'll
record it correctly"* — never one who says *"I can make it say whatever you need."* The former
is who you're looking for; the latter is a trap.

## 6. Evidence packet to bring (so the good doors can actually be recorded)

Bring everything in the USCIS "what to bring" list (§1) **plus**, to activate the truthful doors:

1. **All dated prior vaccination records** (childhood/school, prior physicals, travel/employer
   clinics, **Canadian records**) — anything with month/day/year, unaltered. Feeds the
   "transfer from written record" column (H2 record door). VERIFIED that dated written docs are
   what CDC/USCIS accept (§7).
2. **Serologic titer lab reports** for MMR, varicella, Hep A, Hep B (anti-HBs), polio — **either
   done in advance** (if the surgeon prefers outside labs) **or** arranged to be drawn at the
   visit (confirm via Q4). Positive titer → recorded as immune, no shot (H2).
3. **Hooman only:** the **treating hematologist's letter** on LGL/immunosuppression and whether
   specific **live vaccines are contraindicated**, in terms the civil surgeon can transcribe
   (H3-H). This is the hinge item routed to the hematologist in the decision map — it must state
   a **genuine** clinical contraindication.
4. **Wife only:** proof of **pregnancy / OB documentation** so the surgeon can mark the
   pregnancy contraindication for live vaccines at an exam done **while pregnant** (H3-W).
5. **The blank Form I-693** (or confirm the office supplies the current 01/20/25 edition),
   **photo ID**, **insurance card**, **payment** (§1).

**Before the envelope is sealed, verify every row on your copy:**
- Dated prior doses were **transferred** into the written-record column.
- Positive **titers** are recorded as immune (lab date written) — not left blank or slated for a
  shot.
- Any **contraindication** (Hooman's live rows via the hematologist letter; wife's live rows via
  pregnancy) is **marked in the contraindication column**.
- Age/season blanket waivers (Hib, rotavirus; likely meningococcal/pneumococcal; flu if
  off-season) are marked "not age-appropriate" / "not flu season" where applicable.
- The **Results box #1** ("completed … or may be eligible for blanket waivers as indicated
  above") is checked — **not** box #3 ("does not meet immunization requirements"), unless a true
  refusal was the deliberate outcome. (Box #2 = the I-601 religious/moral path, a reserve.)
- Then **get your copy** and let them **seal** the original. Never open the sealed envelope.
