## Interpretation

- An IGRA detects an immune response consistent with infection by *M. tuberculosis*; it does not
  distinguish latent infection from active TB disease. It is not proof that Hooman currently has
  contagious disease. **VERIFIED-HIGH** (CDC).
- TB is airborne, spread from a person with active pulmonary/laryngeal TB through shared air. It
  is not classified as an STI. If this represents true infection, it may have been acquired years
  ago and remained latent; the source and date cannot be reconstructed from an IGRA.
- No symptoms are reassuring but do not alone rule out active disease, particularly when immunity
  may be altered. Cancer/leukemia and immunosuppressive treatment can increase progression risk.
- A false positive is possible, especially in a truly low-risk person, and the reporting lab
  itself recommends repeat testing for low-risk patients. This is not merely a threshold-edge
  positive: both antigen-minus-Nil values exceed 0.35 and the controls performed appropriately.
  Birth/residence outside the United States adds some pretest probability. It is therefore not
  supportable to label the result "likely false positive." A repeat IGRA or different test such
  as T-SPOT may help a TB/ID specialist resolve clinical uncertainty, but it does not erase the
  documented positive for I-693 purposes. BCG vaccination does not cause a positive IGRA.

## I-693 pathway

- Current CDC Civil Surgeon Technical Instructions require a chest radiograph for every applicant
  with a positive IGRA. For applicants age 10 or older, the required baseline study is a standard
  digital PA chest X-ray. MRI, ultrasound, repeat IGRA, TST, sputum testing, or CT are not listed
  substitutes. CT also generally carries substantially more radiation.
- Normal chest X-ray + no TB symptoms + no known HIV infection → Class B2 latent TB infection.
  The civil surgeon reports it to the health department/eMedical and may complete I-693. Treatment
  is recommended for health but is not required for status-adjustment clearance.
- Chest X-ray suggestive of infectious TB, TB symptoms, or known HIV → required health-department
  referral and three sputum specimens for smear/culture; I-693 is not signed until that evaluation
  is complete.
- Repeating the IGRA for clinical confirmation does not avoid the immigration X-ray: CDC says an
  applicant with a positive IGRA or documented history of one must have the radiograph, and all
  IGRA dates/results are documented.

## Radiation calibration

- RadiologyInfo (ACR/RSNA) estimates an adult chest X-ray at about **0.1 mSv**, comparable to
  roughly **10 days of natural background radiation**. A chest CT is approximately 6.1 mSv in the
  same comparison table. Individual doses vary. The diagnostic X-ray is not radiation therapy.
- There is no accepted radiation-free I-693 substitute under current CDC instructions. If Hooman
  declines the radiograph, the practical option is to pause I-693 and discuss the risk with the
  civil surgeon, radiologist, hematologist, and/or TB specialist; the surgeon cannot complete TB
  clearance on the positive IGRA alone.
- Sensible dose minimization: use the CDC-required digital PA study, avoid CT or extra views unless
  clinically justified, provide any prior chest imaging for comparison, and ask the facility to
  follow ALARA technique.
