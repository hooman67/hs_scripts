## 2026-10-07 financing and leasing update

- Cash remains the economic default for Tustin when the offered loan APR exceeds roughly 2.4%–2.8%,
  the current approximate after-tax safe-cash yield range. Financing above that threshold buys
  liquidity but does not minimize cost.
- The best live third-party 2026 gas SX Prestige lease benchmark found for 95121 is $840.88/month,
  zero due at signing, 36 months/10,000 miles, 0.00231 money factor, and $31,750.85 residual.
- Because Hooman would probably buy the vehicle, that lease is rejected: minimum nominal cost
  through the taxable buyout is approximately $64.4k–$65.2k plus purchase-option/DMV charges.
- SGOV is low risk but not principal-guaranteed. A qualifying FDIC-insured CD has stronger principal
  protection; VGIT's duration risk makes it unsuitable as the risk-free side of this comparison.

## Decision

**The used-car thesis remains correct for this exact trim, but the best reliability/value target
is probably a 2024 Kia CPO rather than automatically a 2022 or 2023.** The cheapest current
in-boundary leftover-new example is approximately $54.0k–$54.2k OTD, nearly $9.0k above Hooman's
absolute ceiling. A current 2024 Kia CPO candidate is approximately $43.2k–$43.4k OTD with 26,567
miles and meaningful Kia warranty coverage. A current 2022 non-CPO candidate is approximately
$34.6k–$34.8k OTD with 53,667 miles, but most of its original basic warranty is consumed and it
requires substantially more diligence.

The practical choice is:

- **Reliability-first under the $45k hard ceiling:** investigate the 2024 Kia CPO in Irvine first.
- **Price-first under the $40k target:** investigate the 2022 AutoNation Hayward vehicle first,
  conditional on full history, service/recall evidence, cosmetic review, and an independent PPI.
- **New:** do not pursue unless the budget rises to roughly $52k–$54k OTD or an unusually large
  additional discount appears in a written buyer's order.

Neither used listing is purchase-ready: no seller was contacted and full history, recall status,
physical availability, inspection, and written OTD remain open.

## Why the conclusion differs by used vehicle

The live exact-trim evidence shows that depreciation is available; it is unevenly distributed:

- The 2022 Hayward asking basis is about 35% below the model's original $47,635 MSRP plus
  destination, before comparing tax and fees.
- The 2024 Irvine CPO asking basis is about $9,800 below the current local leftover-new 2025.
- The 2025 Dublin used vehicle is only about $9,500 below the local leftover-new 2025 despite
  47,091 miles and no advertised Kia CPO coverage. It is the kind of lightly used vehicle for which
  the Twitter-thread criticism is valid.

Warranty changes the comparison. Ordinary subsequent owners should not assume Kia's original
10-year/100,000-mile powertrain coverage transfers. Kia CPO advertises remaining
10-year/100,000-mile limited powertrain coverage from original in-service/zero miles, a
1-year/12,000-mile Platinum warranty, 165-point inspection, and assistance benefits. CPO reduces
risk but does not replace full history review, recall verification, and an independent inspection.

## Cash, finance, or lease

**Cash is the clean baseline, but financing can win if the written APR is low enough and does not
forfeit rebates.** Compare total paid and keep the cash-rebate and promotional-APR offers separate.
The current leftover-new listing disclosures say incentives may not combine with special finance
or lease programs. Kia's public offers page did not expose a current Carnival loyalty benefit for
ZIP 95121; owning the 2024 Niro PHEV therefore has **no value in this comparison unless a dealer
identifies the program number, eligibility, expiration, and stacking rules in writing**.

Given low annual mileage and a stated intention to keep the vehicle, **leasing is not the default
recommendation**. It buys short-term warranty/flexibility but usually charges for the early
depreciation and leaves no owned vehicle unless Hooman later pays the residual. Consider it only if
the priority changes to a three-year exit/flexibility option and a complete written lease worksheet
beats both purchase alternatives on total cash outlay—not monthly payment.

## Timing

The model-year turnover has already occurred: 2027 is current, 2026 is leftover, and 2025 is deeply
leftover. Waiting solely for “the next year” is therefore unlikely to solve the new-car budget gap;
the best discovered 2026 example is still roughly $6.8k–$7.0k above the $45k OTD ceiling. December
may create negotiating leverage, but it may also remove the best used candidate. Current stated
2026 cash incentives expire 2026-11-02, so they must be rechecked rather than assumed through
December.

## Twitter-thread claims

- “Used market is insane” is directionally understandable but too broad. Cox's mid-September 2026
  index remained high in absolute terms at 206.2, while falling 1% month over month and 0.4% year
  over year; wholesale supply had increased. The market was cooling, not uniformly becoming more
  expensive.
- “Buy new and drive it until it drops” is sensible when the new-used spread is only a few thousand
  dollars. Here the verified spread is about $10.8k for a 2024 CPO and about $19.4k for the 2022
  candidate, so the premise does not apply cleanly.
- The BMW and Jeep resale anecdotes are not comparable transaction evidence for a Carnival SX
  Prestige.
- A desperate private seller can be cheaper, but that conflicts with Hooman's low-risk vendor and
  hidden-problem requirements.
- Leasing can fit a planned three-year exit; it conflicts with the stated long-hold preference
  unless flexibility becomes more valuable than ownership.

## Uncertainty and reversal conditions

Confidence is **high** that new/current leftover-new does not fit the current $45k OTD ceiling,
**medium-high** that the 2024 CPO is the best current risk-adjusted path, and **medium** that either
specific used listing is actually purchasable on the stated terms.

The conclusion would change if:

1. a genuine new SX Prestige buyer's order reaches $45k OTD or less without inapplicable rebates or
   mandatory products;
2. the 2024 CPO's full history, condition, CPO paperwork, recall status, or written OTD is materially
   worse than advertised;
3. the 2022 Hayward vehicle fails history/PPI/cosmetic review or its OTD rises enough to erase its
   discount; or
4. Hooman decides flexibility over three years matters more than long-term ownership.

The cheapest next validation is to obtain, with Hooman's approval, itemized 95121 OTD worksheets,
full history reports, original stickers, recall proof, and CPO paperwork for the Hayward and Irvine
vehicles before arranging either inspection or travel.
