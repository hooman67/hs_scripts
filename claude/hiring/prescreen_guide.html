Hooman's Pre-Screen Questions

Part 1 — Technical (10 min)

A · Diffusion / flow fundamentals — warm-up

"Walk me through what a diffusion model actually learns — forward vs. reverse process, and what the network predicts (ε / x₀ / v)." → then: "DDPM vs. flow-matching / rectified flow — what changes, and why do we care for few-step sampling?"

Explains the objective crisply, knows the prediction-target trade-offs, connects to sampling. Hand-waves "it denoises," can't say what the loss is on, only knows the API.
B · Post-training & the gate — ASK EVERYONE

"SFT vs. preference optimization (DPO) vs. RL (RLHF/GRPO) vs. distillation for a T2I/T2V model — when do you reach for each, and what do you actually need to run each one?"

Follow-up: "Where does the reward / preference signal come from for an image or video model, and what goes wrong with it?" (reward hacking, aesthetic vs. alignment, eval noise)

Has personally run at least one; can talk data/compute cost, failure modes, eval. Method is secondary — depth of hands-on is the check. Only names techniques; can't describe building the signal or a run they did.
C · Conditioning & guidance — architecture depth

"How does text conditioning enter a diffusion transformer — cross-attention vs. adaLN? What is classifier-free guidance, and what breaks at high CFG?" (oversaturation, artifacts, diversity loss)

Concrete, has tuned this. Textbook-only.
D · Sampling acceleration / deployment — great for a "shipped it" claim

"You have a 50-step model; product needs 1–4 steps at interactive latency. Options and trade-offs?" (distillation, consistency/LCM, guidance distillation, quantization, caching)

Knows quality/latency trade-offs from experience. Only "use fewer steps."
E · Debugging — best signal for real practitioners

"Your T2I model gives blurry / mode-collapsed / prompt-ignoring outputs. How do you diagnose, in order?" (data → conditioning → loss/schedule → CFG → eval harness)

Systematic, isolates variables, has war stories. Guesses randomly.

Optional systems depth (only if they claim training-infra strength): numerically stable softmax / mixed-precision in attention; why RoPE encodes relative position; sharding a big DiT run.

Part 2 — Experience (10 min) — the E2E-ownership probe

The "one project" drill (spend most of the 10 min here):

  1. "Pick the project you consider most your own. Take me from the initial problem to what shipped." Let them talk, then drill:
  2. Whose idea was it? Who decided the architecture / data strategy / eval?
  3. "Draw the full pipeline — data → training → post-training → eval → deployment — and mark which boxes you personally owned end-to-end, which you collaborated on, which you handed off." → This one question separates a lead from a strong contributor.
  4. "What broke, and how did you personally unblock it?" (technical + cross-team)
  5. "What was the measurable outcome — and how do you know your part caused it?"
  6. "Who else was on it, and what did they do?" (cross-check the ownership story)
Owns decisions under ambiguity, drove data/train/post-train/deploy, can attribute impact, generous about naming teammates while still clearly the driver. "We" with no "I"; owned one component (e.g. just the distillation, just a paper) but frames it as leading the whole thing; can't explain decisions outside their slice; vague on outcome.

Scale/scope quickies: biggest model (params) & GPU count they've driven; largest dataset; latency/throughput or adoption numbers they're proud of.

Part 3 — Culture fit (10–20 min)

Screen for: collaborator · listens · responsible · takes initiative. Strong culture fit can outweigh a slightly-off skillset; a strong-skills candidate who can't listen / doesn't own failure / is dismissive is a NO unless you believe it's coachable. Score some of this from how they behave in the screen itself, not just their answers.

Collaborator

"Tell me about a technical disagreement with a peer or a partner team. How did it resolve?"

Sought to understand the other view, changed their mind at least once, kept the relationship. "They were wrong"; steamrolled; only wins.

Listens (score live + one question)

Live signal: do they answer the question you actually asked? Build on your follow-ups? Ask clarifying questions before answering a vague prompt?

"A time real feedback changed your technical approach — what was it and what did you do?"

Concrete example, integrated it. Can't recall one; treats feedback as an obstacle.

Responsible / owns outcomes

"Something you shipped broke, or a project slipped. What did you do?"

Owns it, root-causes, fixes the system, no blame. Blames team/manager/data; passive.

Takes initiative (go-getter)

"Something you started that nobody asked you to do" — bonus if it grew the team/org investment, or you convinced leadership to fund it.

Self-started 0→1, pitched up, competition/challenge wins, intern→lead conversions. Only executes assigned work.

Motivation (2 min)

"Why Firefly / generative media, and what do you want to own next?"

Wants to lead a gen project E2E; excited about the modality. Generic "big models"; or only wants pure research with no shipping appetite.
