## Part 1 — Technical — 2 / 4

He talks fluently and the GRPO/reward answers are genuinely good — but **scoring the proof, not the

**backwards**, cross-attention **Q/KV backwards** and doubled down, adaLN missed, distillation

**Q: Walk me through what a diffusion model learns; forward vs. reverse process.**
A: Correct and fluent — samples from a noisy distribution and denoises step-by-step back to the
data distribution; described latent diffusion (encode image/video → latent, denoise in latent,
decode). 🟢

**Q: In the forward pass, what does the model predict?**
A: Fumbled first ("latent distribution transformations"), then on the second prod gave the right
answer — the **noise** at that timestep. Needed prompting but landed it. 🟡

**Q: Do we always predict the noise, or are there other targets?**
A: Strong — "predict signal, predict noise, predict momentum / **v-prediction**, and the latest
papers go back to directly predicting **x/pixels**." Knows ε / x₀ / v and current trends. 🟢

**Q: DDPM vs. rectified flow?**
A: Roughly correct — DDPM predicts the signal (noted his 2023 paper was DDPM-based); rectified flow
is **ODE-based and predicts velocity**. A bit vague on the details but the core distinction is
right. 🟢

**Q: Difference between SFT, DPO, GRPO? (his stated area)**
A: **Excellent — the strongest part of the interview.**
- SFT: teach the model new knowledge from ground-truth data. ✓
- DPO: for T2I, same prompt → a preferred vs. negative output pair; push the model toward the
  positive. ✓
- GRPO: genuinely hands-on — explained *when* to use it (limited data but spare compute; baseline
  ~1–2/10 success = good GRPO starting point, 0/10 = do SFT first), the rollout-of-N mechanism,
  reward model scoring, group mean/std normalization, push above group average. 🟢🟢

**Q: Example reward model / what reward?**
A: Concrete — **HPS v3** as an aesthetic reward (use its score directly); VLMs for prompt-following
reward. Clearly from real practice. 🟢

**Q: How does text conditioning enter a diffusion transformer?**
A: CLIP text encoder → embeddings → **cross-attention** in the DiT blocks across the denoising
steps. ✓ core mechanism right. 🟢

**Q: Are the CLIP embeddings keys, queries, or values?**
A: **Got this backwards** — said text = query and image latents = keys/values, and doubled down
when probed. Standard T2I cross-attention is the reverse (image latents are queries; text is K/V). 🔴

**Q: How would adaLN / DiT conditioning work?**
A: Missed it — re-described QKV cross-attention rather than adaLN's scale/shift modulation of
LayerNorm. 🔴

**Q: What is CFG? How do you enable it in training? What happens if it's too high? Relationship to
prompt adherence?**
A: **Weakest technical area — a real conceptual gap.** He framed CFG almost entirely as *training-
time random masking of conditions for robustness* (that's conditioning dropout — only half the
story) and **missed the inference-time guidance extrapolation** entirely. He then interpreted "CFG
too high" as "mask ratio too high," concluding the model ignores the prompt / hallucinates, and
said **prompt adherence goes *down* as CFG increases** — backwards (higher CFG increases adherence,
at the cost of diversity/oversaturation). He admitted "I haven't done this test." 🔴

**Q: 50-step → 5-step, what mechanisms and trade-offs?**
A: Punted honestly — "this is a distillation question; I'm lacking the knowledge because we start
from already-distilled models." No attempt at consistency/LCM/guidance distillation. Zero, but
candid. 🔴 (knowledge) / 🟢 (honesty)

**Q: Debug a blurry / prompt-ignoring T2I model (isolate one at a time).**
A: Reasonable systematic instinct once redirected. Blurry → too few denoise steps, VAE decoder.
Mode collapse → adversarial. Prompt-ignoring → text encoder (✓), token length/truncation (✓ — I
confirmed both correct), dataset ambiguity (✓). Missed the simplest first move — **reduce CFG** —
consistent with his CFG blind spot; I gave him that one.

**Technical takeaway:** Real hands-on depth on post-training (GRPO/reward models) and fundamentals,
but **on proof he got core conditioning/guidance wrong** (CFG backwards, Q/KV backwards, adaLN
missed) and had no distillation knowledge. Fluent ≠ correct. For a Staff gen scientist that's below
bar overall. **2/4.**
