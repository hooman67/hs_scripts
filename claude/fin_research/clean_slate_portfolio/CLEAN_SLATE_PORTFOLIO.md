# Clean-Slate Portfolio — Evidence-Only Build

**Prepared 2026-07-10 (Opus 4.8) as a SIDE EXERCISE.** RESEARCH, NOT FINANCIAL ADVICE.

**The rules of this exercise:** I ignored ALL of Hooman's stated beliefs, preferences, and prior
work (USD-collapse thesis, commodity supercycle, gold/uranium/China/AI views, the 3-bucket framework,
individual stock-picking, his target allocations, the whole `fin_research` project). I built purely
from (a) the weight of academic + practitioner **evidence** (`research/evidence_base.md`) and (b) his
**objective situation facts** (`research/situation_constraints.md`) — because ignoring those wouldn't be
"unbiased," it'd just be generic. Every price is a live pull 2026-07-10.

**Objective facts used (nothing else):** Canadian citizen, US tax-resident (TN, green card pending);
~$556k investable; ~$732k Adobe RSUs vesting ~4yr (concentrated employer stock); separate $250k CAD
house reserve; house purchase in 3–5yr (US or Canada, undecided); 20yr horizon after; low-maintenance
preference; not a confident forecaster.

---

## THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

An evidence-only portfolio for this person is **boring, cheap, and shaped almost entirely by two facts
that have nothing to do with markets: the 3–5 year house goal (which forces a large safe bucket) and the
$732k Adobe concentration (which forces the growth bucket to deliberately AVOID US tech).** It is ~3–5
index funds, not 45 stocks. It holds NO thematic bets (no gold sleeve, no commodities, no uranium, no
single stocks) because the evidence says those don't reliably improve risk-adjusted returns and this
investor has no demonstrated forecasting edge. The single highest-value action isn't a purchase at all —
it's **selling Adobe on vest.**

---

## STEP 1 — THE SPLIT IS DICTATED BY THE HOUSE GOAL, NOT BY MARKETS

A known, large cash need in 3–5 years is the dominant constraint. Planning evidence (sequence-of-returns
risk for a near-term liability) says money you'll spend in <5yr should not be at meaningful equity risk.
So the $556k splits into two buckets by *when the money is needed*, before any market view enters:

| Bucket | Purpose | Size | Instruments |
|---|---|--:|---|
| **SAFE — house + near-term** | can't fall before the purchase | **~$300k (≈55%)** | T-bills/HISA + short TIPS + GIC ladder, split CAD/USD |
| **GROWTH — 20yr wealth** | long-horizon compounding | **~$256k (≈45%)** | 3-fund global index + small diversifier |

> Note: this ~55/45 is close to what Hooman independently arrived at — but the *reasoning* is pure
> goal-based planning (sequence risk on a near-term liability), not a market call. The evidence route
> lands in a similar zip code on the cash weight, which is a mild validation of that instinct. Where it
> **diverges sharply** is what's *inside* each bucket (next).

If the house is <3yr away, push SAFE toward 65%; if closer to 5yr, 45% is defensible. $250k CAD reserve
is already the core of "safe" — so the safe bucket here can be a bit smaller if that reserve counts.

---

## STEP 2 — THE SAFE BUCKET (~$300k)

Pure principal protection, no reaching for yield (that's the evidence — near-term money doesn't take
duration or credit risk). Currency split by house-location probability (undecided → ~50/50):

| Holding | ~$ | Why |
|---|--:|---|
| **US T-bills / SGOV** (USD) | ~$110k | ~3.85% yield, ~0 duration, principal stable |
| **CAD HISA / GIC ladder** (CAD) | ~$110k | currency-match if he buys in Canada; matches the CAD reserve |
| **Short TIPS — VTIP** (USD) | ~$50k | inflation protection for a multi-year wait without duration risk |
| **Cash buffer** | ~$30k | closing costs / flexibility |

No long bonds, no corporate credit, no "bond funds" with duration — the money has a near-term job.

## STEP 3 — THE GROWTH BUCKET (~$256k) — WHERE EVIDENCE OVERRIDES INSTINCT MOST

This is a **3-fund global index core + one diversifier**, cap-weighted, ~0.05% blended fee. The evidence
is overwhelming and least-contested here: **84% of US / 95% of Canadian active managers trail the index
over 10yr; ~57% of individual stocks underperform T-bills over their life; the behavior gap costs ~1%/yr.**
For someone with no forecasting edge, the index IS the answer.

### The critical modification: build AROUND the Adobe concentration
A plain S&P 500 is ~32% Mag-7 and heavily US-tech. Layering that on top of $732k Adobe would **double down
on the exact risk that most needs diversifying** (his human capital + $732k equity are already one US-tech
bet). So the growth bucket deliberately **underweights US / US-tech** relative to a naive cap-weight:

| Holding | Ticker | ~$ | ~% of growth | Live px | Role |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| **Intl developed + EM (all ex-US)** | **VXUS** | ~$115k | 45% | $84.90 | the anti-Adobe core; ex-US = zero Adobe/Mag-7 overlap; FTC-claimable in taxable |
| **US total market** | **VTI** | ~$77k | 30% | $371.45 | still own the US engine, just *underweighted* vs its ~64% global cap weight |
| **Global small-cap value** | **AVUV/AVDV** | ~$26k | 10% | $123.22 | the one evidence-backed tilt (value+size+profitability), and it's the *opposite* of mega-cap tech |
| **Intermediate govt bonds** | **IEF** | ~$26k | 10% | $93.71 | 3.88% yield; regime-dependent but the best ex-ante diversifier since 2008; the growth bucket's shock absorber |
| **Managed futures / trend** | **DBMF** | ~$13k | 5% | $30.76 | best "crisis alpha" evidence (+27% in 2022 when stocks+bonds both fell); the only diversifier that earned its place |

**Deliberately EXCLUDED (and why — this is the contrast with the belief-driven build):**
- **No gold sleeve.** Evidence: ~0% long-run real return, unreliable crisis hedge. (Belief-build had ~9%.)
- **No broad commodities / energy / uranium / copper.** Long-run return drag; inflation hedge is real but
  narrow and doesn't belong in a no-view portfolio. (Belief-build had ~28% commodity megafactor.)
- **No individual stocks, no thematic ETFs.** No edge → no concentration. (Belief-build had ~45 names.)
- **No crypto.** No evidentiary basis for a required allocation; pure speculation in this frame.
- **Net US weight** across VTI (30% of growth) ≈ 13.5% of growth-bucket US + the ex-US in VXUS → US is
  **~35% of the growth bucket vs ~64% cap-weight** — the intentional Adobe offset.

## STEP 4 — THE HIGHEST-VALUE ACTION ISN'T IN THIS PORTFOLIO

**Sell Adobe on vest, mechanically.** Evidence (JPMorgan "Agony & Ecstasy": 44% of Russell 3000 stocks
suffered permanent −70% losses, 59% in tech; Bessembinder: most stocks underperform T-bills; the
employer-stock double-risk from the Enron/Meulbroek literature) says a ~57%-of-net-worth single-stock
position that is also your paycheck is the portfolio's dominant risk. RSU cost basis ≈ vest price, so
selling immediately is ~tax-neutral. Proceeds flow into the plan above (mostly VXUS, to offset the tech
tilt). **No allocation decision matters as much as this one.**

## HOW THIS DIFFERS FROM THE BELIEF-DRIVEN BUILD (the interesting part)

| Dimension | Belief-driven (our main project) | Clean-slate (evidence-only) |
|---|---|---|
| Commodity/real-asset megafactor | ~28% | **0%** |
| Gold | ~9% | **0%** |
| Individual stocks / themes | many | **0** |
| Global equity index core | ~25% | **~39%** |
| US tech posture | several AI/tech names | **deliberately underweight US** |
| Bonds | minimal (USD-decay view) | **~15% (TIPS + IEF)** |
| # holdings | ~20+ | **~9** |
| Cash for house | ~25–30% | **~54%** (goal-driven) |
| Single biggest lever | sector/theme selection | **sell Adobe** |

**What they AGREE on (worth noting):** a large safe bucket for the house (~50%+), VXUS held separately
for the Canadian FTC, intermediate govt bonds as the recession hedge, a small-value tilt, and that the
Adobe concentration is the #1 risk. The evidence independently validated several of Hooman's structural
choices — it just strips out every *thematic* bet as unsupported-by-evidence for a no-edge investor.

**The honest tension the exercise surfaces:** the clean-slate build is almost certainly **higher
risk-adjusted expected return with far less effort** (that's what the evidence predicts). The belief-driven
build is a bet that Hooman's specific macro views (USD decay, commodity supercycle) are *more right than
the market's consensus* — which the literature says is a low-probability wager for any individual. The
value of the belief-driven version is behavioral: he'll actually hold through drawdowns things he
believes in, whereas he might panic-sell a "boring" portfolio. That's not nothing — the behavior gap is
real — but it's the honest case for the themes, not an expected-return case.
