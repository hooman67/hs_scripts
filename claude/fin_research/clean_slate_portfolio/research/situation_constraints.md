## §1 — The 3–5 Year House Goal → Required Conservatism

**The standard rule.** Money needed within a few years belongs in cash and short-term fixed
income, not equities; the shorter the horizon, the more it shifts to cash equivalents.
- **Fidelity** ("Investment strategies for short-term goals," updated Dec 3, 2025): <1 yr → money
  market ("liquidity and safety over return"); 1–3 yrs → CDs/CD ladders, high-quality short-term
  bond funds (IG only), **no stock allocation**; 3+ yrs → only "a relatively small portion" in
  stocks, rest conservative. **VERIFIED.**
- **Schwab** bucket strategy (Aug 21, 2025): Bucket 1 (0–5 yrs) = cash + CD ladder + money market.
  A 3–5 yr house fund is a **Bucket-1 liability**. **VERIFIED.**
- **SEC / Investor.gov**: cash equivalents safest/lowest-return, appropriate for short-term;
  equities for long-term ("wait out slow cycles"). **VERIFIED.**
- **Morningstar**: at ~3 years, "emphasize cash and short-term bonds." **VERIFIED (excerpt); model %s UNVERIFIED (page 403).**

**Sequence-of-returns risk for a fixed-date liability.** You cannot wait out a drawdown when you
must spend on a set date — you become a forced seller at the bottom. Loss-frequency data (why
equities are wrong here):
- **Schwab** (Jul 18, 2025): S&P 500 positive in only **75.6%** of 1,177 rolling 12-mo periods
  1926–2024 → **~1 in 4 losing years**. **VERIFIED.**
- **DQYDJ** (data to Jul 2026): worst rolling *total* returns — 1-yr **−62.3%**, 3-yr **−40.2%**,
  5-yr **−17.3%**. **VERIFIED.**
- **Marotta** (1928–2023): **~20.4%** of rolling 5-yr periods negative; worst −71.1% cumulative.
  **VERIFIED (secondary).**
- Sequence-risk *term* itself (Investopedia/Kitces) could not be fetched — mechanism corroborated
  by the above; formal citation **UNVERIFIED**.

**Quantifying the de-risk.** No institution publishes one canonical %, but the direction is clear:
- Buying in **<3 yrs / already-funded amount you can't afford to shrink** → effectively **100% safe**
  (HYSA / money market / GIC / T-bill / short-CD ladder). Fidelity's 1–3 yr tier has no equities.
- Buying in **3–5 yrs** → safe core still dominates; a modest short-term high-quality bond sleeve,
  and at most a *small* equity slice **only if over-funded** and the timeline/price is flexible.
- **Funded vs not-yet-funded:** if the down payment is in hand → capital-preservation / liability-
  match (GIC or T-bill/CD ladder maturing at target date). If still accumulating and flexible →
  a modest early growth sleeve on a **glide path** that ends near-100% cash in the final 1–2 yrs.
- Direction **VERIFIED**; specific %s and glide-path start **ASSUMED/UNVERIFIED**.

**Instruments.** *CAD:* GICs (CDIC-insured, laddered to closing date = textbook match), HISA ETFs
(CASH.TO/CSAV/PSA, daily liquid, not fund-level CDIC-insured), ultra-short bond ETFs (ZST/CBIL),

## §2 — Currency (CAD vs USD)

**Match the safe bucket to the spending currency.** Goals-based/ALM consensus: assets funding a
specific goal should sit in the currency you'll actually spend (Naylor; UBS WM, 2025-07-25; Cramer
ALM framing). **VERIFIED.** When the liability currency is *genuinely uncertain* (his case): don't
force a single-currency bet — **diversify across candidate currencies and keep flexibility to
re-weight** (Naylor: "focus on global diversification… flexibility to reallocate later"). **VERIFIED.**
→ Practical synthesis (**ASSUMED**): split the safe bucket CAD/USD by the probability of buying in
each country; shift toward the winner as the decision clarifies.

**Hedging foreign EQUITY for a Canadian — leave it UNHEDGED (near-unanimous).**
- Ben Felix / Dan Bortolotti (Rational Reminder Ep. 379): currencies have no expected return, so
  hedging shouldn't change expected return; they run "totally unhedged equity portfolio." **VERIFIED.**
- Dimensional (2020-10-08) & Morningstar: equity vol dominates currency vol → hedging equities adds
  little; "case for hedging is far stronger in fixed income than equity." **VERIFIED.**
- Justin Bender / CPM (2020-08-31): USD ≈ 65% of a Canadian's foreign-currency exposure; hedging
  "sells 'safer' currencies" that cushion crashes — in 2007–09 unhedged US equity fell ~42% in CAD
  vs ~60% hedged. **VERIFIED.**
- Hedging cost/tracking error: forward contracts reset ~monthly (imprecise), historically large
  cap-gains distributions (tax drag). **VERIFIED (qualitative); hard bps UNVERIFIED.**

**Bonds are the opposite — foreign fixed income SHOULD be hedged** to home/spending currency
(Bender: "bond ETFs should probably be 100% hedged"; Vanguard; Dimensional). **VERIFIED.**

**USD human capital → which bucket gets which currency (the key insight — they diverge):**
- **Safe/house bucket → follows the liability** (currency of expected spend; USD is the natural
  default given US residence + Bay Area option, but split for the CAD chance).
- **Growth bucket → follows diversification away from human capital.** His salary + RSUs already
  make the balance sheet heavily USD; the long-term portfolio should lean toward **non-USD / global**
  (relatively more CAD + international), NOT more USD. Naylor supports only a "slight bias toward base
  currency." → Principle **VERIFIED**; the currency-diversification step and tilt %s **ASSUMED/UNVERIFIED**.

**Implementation — Norbert's Gambit** for cheap CAD↔USD conversion: buy **DLR.TO**, journal to
**DLR.U.TO**, sell for USD (~4–5 business days); ~0.2% + commissions vs ~1.5–2% bank FX. Worth it
above ~$3–5k; CAD→USD is the cleaner direction. **VERIFIED (mechanics).**

  **VERIFIED.**
- Bessembinder (JFE 2018): **~57%** of US stocks since 1926 underperformed 1-mo T-bills over their
  lifetime; the best **4%** of firms account for the entire market's net gain. **VERIFIED (SSRN page

- Morgan Stanley: single stocks avg 37% vol vs 15% index; ~40% of stocks that fell 50% "never
  recovered fully." **VERIFIED.**
- Concentration thresholds: Fidelity ≥5%, Schwab >10% (>20% if restricted), Plancorp 10–20% flag /

## §4 — Account Structure & Cross-Border Tax (Canadian citizen, US tax resident)

**Framing:** he files a US 1040 as a resident (IRC §7701(b)(1)(A) via substantial presence). The
Canadian-citizenship point (relevant for OFAC/sanctions) does NOT exempt him from US resident
income-tax and information-reporting obligations. **VERIFIED.**

**RRSP.**
- US recognizes RRSP tax deferral under **Treaty Article XVIII(7)**; the election is **automatic since
  Rev. Proc. 2014-55** — **Form 8891 obsolete after Dec 31, 2014**. No annual US tax on internal
  growth; distributions taxed under §72. **VERIFIED (primary text read).**
- The **15% US dividend-withholding exemption** for the RRSP as a pension trust flows from **Article
  XXI(2)** (Exempt Organizations), not XVIII — worth noting since the common shorthand cites XVIII(7).
  Effect: **US-listed US securities held directly in an RRSP receive US dividends at 0% withholding.**
  **VERIFIED (treaty text).**
- RRSP is **still reportable**: FBAR (FinCEN 114) + Form 8938 still apply. **VERIFIED.**
- "Eligible individual" caveat: automatic treatment assumes he never previously reported undistributed
  RRSP earnings as US income; if he did, needs Commissioner consent. **VERIFIED.**

**Foreign-withholding-tax layers (Bender/PWL framework).** Recoverability by account:
- RRSP: US withholding on US-listed US-equity funds **exempt (0%)**.
- Taxable: withheld but **recoverable via foreign tax credit** (≈ neutral).
- TFSA/RESP: withheld and **NOT recoverable** → pure ~15% drag on US dividends.
→ Lowest drag = US-listed US-equity ETF in RRSP; worst = US-dividend exposure in a TFSA/RESP.
**ASSUMED** (PWL white-paper PDFs all 403'd; framework widely cited; per-structure numbers UNVERIFIED).

**Classic asset-location rule:** US-dividend equities in the RRSP (0% withholding); if in taxable,
withholding is FTC-recoverable. **VERIFIED (concept).**

**TFSA — problematic for a US person.**
- Not tax-free to the IRS → interest/dividends/gains **taxable annually on the 1040**; no treaty
  shelter. **VERIFIED.**
- Likely a **foreign grantor trust → Forms 3520/3520-A** ($10k+ penalties); practice is split, genuine
  gray area. **VERIFIED.** Rev. Proc. 2020-17 relief **likely does NOT cover a TFSA** (general-purpose,
  contributions not limited to earned income, no medical/education/retirement condition) — my read of
  the primary text; **ASSUMED/UNVERIFIED, CPA call.** RESP is a stronger 2020-17 candidate.
- PFIC trap if it holds Canadian funds → Form 8621 per fund. **VERIFIED.**
- Consensus: TFSA is tax-inefficient + compliance-heavy for a US person; many advisors say avoid/unwind.

**Asset-location priority (conventional framework — VERIFIED):** bonds/interest → tax-sheltered
(RRSP/401k); US-dividend equity → RRSP or 401k; international equity → taxable (FTC recoverable);
TFSA (if kept) → most tax-efficient, non-PFIC, low-withholding assets.
> ⚠️ **Major caveat:** this ordering is written for a **Canadian resident**. He is a **US resident**
> with a US 401k in the mix and possible US-state tax that may not honor the treaty deferral. Do NOT
> apply the rules of thumb mechanically — re-derive for his facts. **ASSUMED/UNVERIFIED → CPA.**

## §5 — The Green-Card-Pending Wrinkle (PFIC & US-person default)

**PFIC rules.** A foreign corporation is a PFIC if ≥75% passive income OR ≥50% passive assets —
foreign (incl. Canadian) mutual funds/ETFs "almost always qualify." Default **§1291** regime is
punitive: excess distributions & gains spread across the holding period, taxed at the **highest**
ordinary rate per year **+ compounding interest**; QEF/mark-to-market alternatives are complex and
need fund-provided statements Canadian funds rarely give; **Form 8621 per fund, annually**. **VERIFIED
(IRS Form 8621 instructions rev. 12/2025; IRC §1291; Forbes Nov 2025; Taxes for Expats May 2026).**
- **US-domiciled funds are NOT PFICs** (domestic entity) even when holding foreign stocks → the clean
  workaround. Individual operating-company stocks are also not PFICs. **VERIFIED.**

**Residency, not the green card, is the trigger.** Substantial presence already makes him a US
resident alien taxed on worldwide income; PFIC/8621 attach to "US persons" incl. resident aliens
(IRS SPT page, rev. Mar 14 2026). **So PFIC rules ALREADY apply.** The pending green card mainly
**removes doubt and makes the exposure permanent** (green-card holders stay US persons even if they
leave). **VERIFIED.**

**TFSA / RESP as foreign-trust + PFIC problems.** TFSA: taxable to IRS, likely 3520/3520-A, PFIC if
holding funds. RESP: foreign trust, usually 3520-exempt under Rev. Proc. 2020-17 if within limits,
but still PFIC/8621 on any Canadian funds inside. **VERIFIED (TFSA) / VERIFIED-via-snippet (RESP).**

**RRSP is PFIC-EXEMPT.** Treaty-protected pension — Form 8621 instructions carve out PFIC stock held
through a treaty foreign pension fund (Reg. §1.1298-1(c)(4)). **So Canadian funds inside an RRSP are
fine.** **VERIFIED.**

**The safe default given uncertainty:**
1. Taxable + US accounts → **US-domiciled ETFs / individual stocks only** (no PFIC).
2. Canadian-domiciled funds → **only inside an RRSP**.
3. TFSA → **avoid new contributions**; review existing for 3520/8621.
4. RESP → likely 3520-exempt, but no Canadian funds inside (use US-domiciled funds / stocks).
5. No renunciation or drastic action — just **structure cleanly now** to sidestep landmines.
**VERIFIED (synthesis of §5 sources).**
