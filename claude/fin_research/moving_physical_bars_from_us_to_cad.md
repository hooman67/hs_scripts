# Moving Physical Gold Bars from the U.S. to Canada

**Last verified:** 2026-09-10  
**Scenario:** Personally carrying a 99.999%-pure, 10-troy-ounce investment gold bar worth approximately US$40,000-$50,000.

## Bottom Line

It is generally legal to personally carry an investment-grade gold bar from the United States into Canada. There is no general quantity or value limit, but the bar should be affirmatively declared to the Canada Border Services Agency as a high-value good.

A gold bar is ordinarily not currency or a monetary instrument for either Canada's CAD$10,000 cross-border currency-reporting rule or the U.S. FinCEN Form 105 rule. A qualifying 99.999%-pure bar also exceeds Canada's 99.5% statutory purity threshold for gold “precious metal.” Such precious metal is prescribed as non-taxable on importation, and the Canadian customs tariff rate for gold bullion is free.

## Canadian Border Treatment

| Issue | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Declaration | Declare the bar to CBSA and state its current value in Canadian dollars, weight, purity, serial number and purpose. |
| CAD$10,000 currency report | Generally not applicable to a gold bar. The threshold applies to currency and defined bearer monetary instruments, not bullion. |
| Customs duty | Gold bullion is generally duty-free under Chapter 71 of the Canadian Customs Tariff. |
| GST/HST | A gold bar refined to at least 99.5% qualifies as “precious metal.” The Non-Taxable Imported Goods (GST/HST) Regulations prescribe precious metals imported under any circumstances as non-taxable. |
| Documentation | Carry the purchase invoice, assay certificate, serial number, photographs and proof of payment/source of funds. |

Even though the CAD$10,000 currency-reporting form should not apply, the bar remains a good that must be reported to CBSA. Do not conceal it or describe it as cash.

## Visiting Canada

When entering temporarily, tell the officer:

> I am carrying one 10-troy-ounce, 99.999%-pure personal investment gold bar worth approximately CAD$___ . It is not for sale and I intend to take it back to the United States when I leave Canada.

Practical steps:

1. Use a regular customs declaration channel and affirmatively disclose the bar.
2. Ask whether CBSA wants to document the temporary importation on Form E29B.
3. Retain all stamped temporary-import documentation.
4. If CBSA issues an E29B or seals the package, present the bar and documentation for acquittal before exporting it from Canada.
5. Do not sell, gift or leave the bar in Canada without first addressing permanent-import and tax consequences.

CBSA may require security for temporarily imported baggage. Because qualifying investment bullion is duty-free and non-taxable, the potential duty-and-tax security should ordinarily be nil, but the port officer controls the documentation process.

## Moving Permanently to Canada

List the bar on Form BSF186 as “goods accompanying,” including:

- description and refiner;
- 10-troy-ounce weight;
- 99.999% purity;
- serial number;
- purchase date; and
- fair market value in Canadian dollars on the import date.

If it will arrive later, include it on the original “goods to follow” list.

Returning former residents generally must have owned, possessed and used personal effects abroad for at least six months to obtain the former-resident exemption. The six-month requirement is waived after residing abroad for at least five years. First-time settlers must have owned, possessed and used the goods abroad before arriving, but the published guidance does not impose the same six-month period.

Those relocation rules should still be followed, but qualifying precious metal has an independent non-taxable-import rule and a free customs-duty rate. Keep the invoice and evidence of the bar's value when Canadian tax residency begins because a later sale can create income-tax and cost-basis questions.

## U.S. Departure and Return

### FinCEN Form 105

FinCEN Form 105 applies when more than US$10,000 of currency or defined monetary instruments is physically transported across the U.S. border. Its definitions cover legal-tender currency, traveller's checks, certain bearer negotiable instruments and bearer securities. They do not include ordinary gold bullion bars. Therefore, a personal investment bar ordinarily does not require Form 105 merely because its value exceeds US$10,000.

### Electronic Export Information

U.S. Census guidance generally exempts exports from the United States to Canada from Electronic Export Information/AES filing unless an export licence is required. Ordinary personal investment bullion generally does not require such a licence, but unusual ownership, sanctions or commercial circumstances should be checked separately.

### Returning to the United States

Before departure, consider taking the bar, invoice and serial-number documentation to a U.S. Customs and Border Protection office and asking whether it can be registered on CBP Form 4457. This is not the currency-reporting form; it is evidence that the item was already owned in the United States before travel. It can reduce questions when bringing the same bar back.

## Travel and Security Checklist

- Use a recognizable investment-grade refiner and retain the sealed assay package.
- Photograph the bar and serial number before travel.
- Carry the invoice, assay certificate and proof of payment separately from the bar.
- Put the bar in carry-on baggage, not checked luggage.
- Review the airline's valuables and liability exclusions; ordinary baggage coverage is usually inadequate.
- Arrive early because dense metal may prompt secondary screening.
- Do not split, disguise or conceal the bar to avoid reporting.
- State clearly that it is personal investment bullion and whether it is entering temporarily or permanently.
- Obtain and retain any CBSA or CBP documentation issued.
- Consider specialty transit insurance for a US$40,000-$50,000 item.

## Important Distinctions

- **Declare the good:** yes.
- **Canadian CAD$10,000 currency form:** ordinarily no for a bullion bar.
- **U.S. FinCEN Form 105:** ordinarily no for a bullion bar.
- **Canadian customs duty:** generally free.
- **Canadian GST/HST:** generally non-taxable for a bar meeting the statutory precious-metal purity threshold.
- **Capital-gains tax:** transporting the same personally owned bar is not itself a sale, but later disposition and a change in tax residency can affect reporting and basis.

## Caution

This is general customs and tax information, not individualized legal advice. Rules, forms and officer practices can change. For a US$40,000-$50,000 bar, contact the specific Canadian port of entry before travelling and ask whether it wants advance E29B documentation. For a permanent move, coordinate the BSF186 treatment and tax-residency date with a U.S.-Canada cross-border tax professional.
