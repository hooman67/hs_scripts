## 4. Cross-cutting themes (the high-value synthesis)

### Theme A — The "AI-deflation panic" cluster (INTU, CTSH, TCS)

A whole IT-services / software complex sold off **~47–65% in ~6 months** on one thesis: GenAI collapses the
labor-arbitrage / SaaS model. This panic manufactured the **cheapest names in the run**.
- **CTSH −~48%** (EV/EBITDA 4.8, P/E 8.9), **TCS −~51%** (P/E 15 vs a 27x decade median), **INTU −~62%**
  (fwd P/E ~10 vs a 57–70x history). The sector fell in lockstep off Accenture's June guidance cut.
- **Is the market right?** The deep dives converge on: **the fear is real but not yet confirmed by the
  companies' own results.** *Bear evidence (real):* IT CEOs now say "deflation"/"degrowth" out loud;
  net headcount is decoupling from revenue; TCS cut ~12,000 and posted its **first-ever revenue decline**;
  INTU's TurboTax lost the price-sensitive filer. *Bull evidence (also real):* GenAI *bookings* are large and
  growing (Accenture $5.9B, IBM >$12.5B book); CTSH revenue +5.8% and bookings +21%; INTU raised guidance and
  GBS grew +15%; "AI needs integrators to deploy it." The market has priced a severe outcome the P&Ls haven't
  yet delivered.
- **Conclusion (per the deep dives): CTSH > TCS on risk/reward.** CTSH is cheaper on every cash multiple
  (P/E 9 vs 15, EV/EBITDA 4.9 vs 9.8, **P/B 1.3 vs 6.8**, so a real balance-sheet margin of safety), and its
  revenue has **already re-inflected to +6.4% cc** while TCS's just went negative. TCS is the higher-*quality*
  franchise (48% vs 15% ROE) but you pay ~7x book with no cushion. **INTU is the most asymmetric of the three**
  (best quality + biggest de-rate) but also the most contested. **Net take: CTSH and INTU carry the theme;
  TCS is a WATCH pending a growth inflection.**

### Theme B — The commodity / insurance peak-cycle cluster (EOG, NEM, K.TO, SREN, CNOOC)

The critical distinction: **cheap on genuinely-normalized earnings vs cheap on peak earnings.**
- **EOG — cheap on NORMALIZED earnings (the one true bargain in the cluster).** WTI at ~$68 is *mid-cycle*
  (~30% below the 2022 peak); EOG's earnings have *already* compressed 36% to match, so the low multiple is on

- **NEM & K.TO — cheap on PEAK earnings.** Gold hit an all-time high (~$5,600 Jan-2026, ~$4,100–4,400 now vs a
  $2,000 reserve price); realized prices drove a ~40% YoY earnings jump on ~flat volume. Their ~8–10x forward
  P/Es are **peak multiples** — on normalized $3,000–3,500 gold they are only *fairly* valued (~14–15x). Their
  durable value is the **net-cash balance sheets**, not the multiple.
- **SREN — cheap on PEAK earnings.** Reinsurance rates peaked Jan-2023 and are now softening at double digits
  (−12% Jan-2026); the FY2025 79.5% combined ratio / ~20% ROE is a cyclical peak. 1.8x book is fair, not cheap.
- **Within-cluster rank (best→worst value on *normalized* earnings): EOG > (CNOOC, if it were legal) > NEM ≈
  K.TO > SREN.** Rule: for commodity/insurance names the scorecard's raw cheapness score **overstates value**
  — always normalize to mid-cycle, and prefer to buy these *after* a price/rate drawdown, not after a run.

## 6. Method, confidence & limitations

- **Ranking basis.** Sorted on each deep dive's **weighted scorecard-table total** (CLAUDE.md §3 weights:
  Cheapness 30 / Quality 30 / Trap-risk-inverse 20 / Moat 15 / Catalyst 5). Where a file's headline text and
  its scorecard table disagreed, I used the **table** (e.g. DGE.L: headline "~6.0–6.6," table math = 6.55 →
  ranked 6.55; CNOOC: scored 77.5/100 = 7.75/10 on merits but gated out; ITC/CTSH/NTES headline
  "WATCH→BUY" reconciled to their table totals). **Qualitative gates override rank:** CNOOC (sanctions) is
  pulled out entirely; ITC (tobacco/ESG) is flagged conditional in-place.
- **Single-source snapshot.** Every fundamental rests on **one yfinance `.info` pull, 2026-07-02** —
  a real fetch, but **not** a point-in-time database and mostly **not** cross-checked against a second live
  quote. US filers (CTSH, NEM, EOG, INTU, TROW) were **EDGAR-tied-out**; most non-US names (Buzzi, Diageo,
  Intesa, Swiss Re, the China/India names) have **no EDGAR backstop** and rest on company IR / HKEXnews
  filings (primary, but management-produced) — a filings tie-out remains a follow-up for those.
- **The currency-mix ADR artifact.** yfinance silently mixes a USD market cap with local-currency
  (RMB/INR) balance-sheet items for foreign-filer ADRs, producing garbage ratios. **NetEase's screen
  figures (EV/EBITDA 6.4, P/B 17.1, FCF-yield 41%) were WRONG** — rebuilt in one currency they are
  ~10.5x / ~3.5x / ~8.8%. TCS's EV/EBITDA and P/B, and the Chinese lifers' P/E, carry similar risk — treat
  ADR EV/EBITDA / P/B / FCF-yield as suspect until rebuilt in a single currency.
- **UNVERIFIED valuation anchors (flagged in the files, not relied on for the verdict).** ITC's segment
  EBIT split (drives the SOTP) and global-tobacco comps; Buzzi's country EBIT split + normalized-margin
  (single-source, no second wire); Kinross's P/NAV / reserves (40-F not pulled); NetEase's 10-yr P/E band
  and Youdao/Cloud-Music splits; Diageo's historical peak-multiple band; CNOOC's exact NS-CMIC currency to
  2026-07-02; the China lifers' peer P/EV (computed, FX/timing noise) and payout-policy floors; several
  analyst price targets (aggregator-sourced).
- **Damodaran annual lag.** Sector-cheapness verdicts (which drove the BOTH-bucket tagging) are Damodaran
  **as-of 2026-01-05** — annual simple-average snapshots, ~6 months stale vs the daily-moving company
  metrics. Combined for *bucketing*, not netted.
- **Two score scales are NOT comparable across the financials boundary.** The non-financials merged rank
  and the financials screen used different percentile pools; the finalists' *screen* scores aren't directly
  comparable across that line. The Stage-5 ranking here uses the **deep-dive scorecards** (same 0–10 rubric
  for all 16), which *are* comparable.

  TCS) are high-variance "cheap-because-of-a-live-thesis" ideas; size as bets on bounded risk, not as safe
  compounders. (2) **More earnings prints** — INTU FY27 guide (Aug-2026), CTSH/TCS revenue-inflection prints,
  ITC cigarette volumes through H1 FY2027, Diageo US-Spirits depletions. (3) **EDGAR/filings tie-outs** for
  the non-US names not yet cross-checked. (4) **Normalized-earnings models** for every commodity/insurance
  name (EOG, NEM, K.TO, SREN, Buzzi) to separate cheap-on-trough from cheap-on-peak. (5) **Re-confirm CNOOC's
