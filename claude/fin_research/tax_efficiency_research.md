# Tax-Efficiency Research: Bonus Depreciation and Bitcoin Mining

**As of:** 2026-09-21  
**Taxpayer profile used:** Married filing jointly; approximately $400,000 of 2026 household taxable income; California resident; income currently comes almost entirely from Hooman's Adobe W-2; spouse does not currently work; U.S. immigration/work-authorization status must be confirmed before either spouse operates a side business.  
**Scope:** Federal and California tax mechanics, Bitcoin-mining economics, leverage, and better-fitting alternatives. This is research, not tax, legal, immigration, or investment advice.

## Bottom line

The thread combines one real tax provision with several misleading leaps.

1. **The core tax fact is real.** Federal law now generally permits permanent 100% bonus depreciation under IRC Section 168(k) for qualifying depreciable property acquired after January 19, 2025. New or certain used tangible business equipment with a MACRS recovery period of 20 years or less can qualify when it is actually placed in service.
2. **Bitcoin miners can likely qualify, but ownership alone is insufficient.** The equipment must belong to a bona fide profit-seeking trade or business, be depreciable, and be ready and available for business use. A contract, deposit, or machine not yet operating by year-end is not enough.
3. **The deduction does not automatically offset Adobe wages.** The mining loss must survive basis, at-risk, hobby-loss, excess-business-loss and—most importantly—passive-activity rules. A hosted, largely outsourced mining investment presents a serious material-participation problem.
4. **The quoted $37,000 benefit is wrong for this household.** At approximately $400,000 of 2026 married-filing-jointly taxable income, the federal marginal bracket is generally 24%, not 37%. A clean $100,000 deduction would therefore save approximately $24,000 federally, before considering mining income. California does not conform to federal 100% bonus depreciation.
5. **The promoted mining figures appear to be gross revenue, not profit.** Using live September 2026 hashprice and efficient current equipment, the thread's $6,200/$8,300/$10,300 monthly figures closely match revenue before electricity, hosting, pool fees, downtime, repairs and difficulty growth.
6. **Depreciation is predominantly timing, not free money.** It reduces the equipment's basis to zero. Later sale proceeds can be ordinary-income depreciation recapture, and future mining profits no longer have equipment depreciation sheltering them.
7. **Borrowing against a home or Bitcoin makes this substantially worse.** It adds fixed financing cost and liquidation or foreclosure risk to an already volatile, rapidly depreciating operating asset.

**Recommendation for Hooman:** Do not deploy $100,000 and do not borrow for this strategy. A $100,000 position is approximately 14.5% of the roughly $689,000 investment portfolio and more than seven times the portfolio's 2% crypto target. The tax benefit does not justify that concentration. If genuine operating interest remains after CPA and immigration review, restrict any experiment to one cash-funded machine—roughly $5,000-$10,000 and inside the existing speculation allocation—not as a tax-bill elimination strategy.

## How Section 168(k) actually works

IRS Notice 2026-11 and Publication 946 state that qualifying property acquired after January 19, 2025 generally receives a 100% additional first-year depreciation deduction. Qualifying property includes tangible MACRS property with a recovery period of 20 years or less and can include certain used property.

For mining equipment, the practical requirements include:

- the taxpayer owns the equipment rather than merely purchasing a revenue-sharing contract;
- the equipment is used predominantly in a genuine trade or business;
- it has a determinable useful life and is depreciable under MACRS;
- it is acquired after January 19, 2025 under the statutory acquisition rules;
- it is installed, connected and **placed in service**—ready and available for mining—during the deduction year;
- related-party and tax-exempt-use exclusions do not apply; and
- the taxpayer reports the acquisition and depreciation consistently, normally using Form 4562.

The deduction reduces tax basis. If $100,000 is fully deducted, the federal basis generally becomes zero. If the miners are later sold for $25,000, Section 1245 can generally recapture up to $25,000 as ordinary income. If the equipment becomes commercially obsolete and has no resale value, there may be no sale proceeds, but there is also no remaining depreciable basis to deduct.

## The decisive issue: can the loss offset W-2 income?

### Bona fide business versus investment or hobby

The activity must be conducted with an actual profit motive and in a businesslike manner. Relevant evidence includes a written business plan, separate bank and wallet records, books and records, vendor due diligence, operating decisions, attempts to improve profitability, insurance, contracts, invoices, serial numbers and consistent reporting.

Buying machines mainly to manufacture a year-end loss is dangerous. Tax savings can be a consideration, but the activity should make economic sense before tax under realistic assumptions.

### Material participation

Trade or business losses are passive if the taxpayer does not materially participate. Passive losses generally offset passive income, not Adobe wages, and unused losses carry forward.

Common material-participation tests include:

- more than 500 hours of participation during the year;
- substantially all participation in the activity;
- more than 100 hours and at least as much participation as any other individual; or
- other tests for significant-participation or prior-year activities.

Spousal participation counts, even if the spouse does not own the activity. However, investor work—reviewing reports, analyzing finances and monitoring nonmanagerially—generally does not count unless the taxpayer is directly involved in day-to-day management or operations.

This creates a structural conflict for hosted mining:

- Outsourcing power, installation, repair, cooling, uptime and pool operations makes the investment convenient.
- The same outsourcing makes it harder to show that Hooman or his spouse materially participates.
- Artificially recording hundreds of hours of research or monitoring will not convert investor activity into operating work.

Before relying on a W-2 offset, a qualified CPA or tax attorney should provide a written position on which specific material-participation test will be met and what activities count.

### Other loss limitations

- **At-risk rules:** Deductions generally cannot exceed the amount economically at risk. Nonrecourse or protected financing can reduce the allowed loss.
- **Excess business loss:** For 2025, the joint-return threshold was $626,000 and it is inflation-adjusted thereafter. A standalone $100,000 loss is unlikely to hit this limit, but larger marketed-loss strategies can.
- **Basis limits:** Entity structure matters. Partnership or S-corporation losses require sufficient tax basis and may face additional limitations.
- **Hobby-loss rules:** An activity not engaged in for profit cannot freely generate deductions against wages.

## Personalized federal and California tax math

### Federal marginal rate

The IRS 2026 married-filing-jointly brackets are:

- 24% from $211,400 through $403,550;
- 32% from $403,550 through $512,450;
- 35% from $512,450 through $768,700; and
- 37% only above $768,700.

At approximately $400,000 of **taxable income**, Hooman is near the top of the 24% bracket. A $100,000 deduction taking taxable income from $400,000 to $300,000 therefore produces approximately:

| Item | Approximate amount |
|---|---:|
| Federal taxable-income reduction | $100,000 |
| Federal marginal rate across deduction | 24% |
| Approximate federal tax reduction | **$24,000** |
| Promoted claim | $37,000 |
| Overstatement | **Approximately $13,000** |

If $400,000 refers to gross wages rather than taxable income, the actual marginal benefit could be lower after deductions and bracket crossing. The bonus deduction does not reverse Social Security or Medicare tax already withheld from Adobe wages.

### California treatment

California explicitly does not conform to the OBBBA's permanent 100% federal bonus depreciation under Section 168(k). A federal-to-California adjustment is required, and the asset retains a different California basis and depreciation schedule.

California may still permit regular state depreciation and a much smaller Section 179 deduction. The 2025 individual limit was $25,000, subject to a $200,000 investment phaseout and business-income rules; the exact 2026 treatment should be verified before purchase. The strategy therefore does **not** create an immediate $100,000 California deduction.

### First-year income reduces the advertised loss

The promotional calculation counts the $100,000 deduction but ignores the taxable mining income generated during the same year.

Illustration using the current efficient-equipment model below, BTC at $63,000 and $0.07/kWh:

| Year-one item | Approximate amount |
|---|---:|
| Equipment cash purchase | -$100,000 |
| Operating cash margin after modeled power, pool fee and downtime | +$38,900 |
| Taxable result after 100% equipment depreciation | **-$61,100** |
| Federal tax reduction at 24% | **Approximately $14,700** |
| Net year-one cash outflow after federal tax benefit | **Approximately $46,400** |

The taxpayer still owns depreciated mining equipment, but its resale value is uncertain and may decline rapidly. The tax benefit is not $37,000 and is not free capital.

In later years, there is no remaining federal equipment depreciation. Operating profit is ordinary business income and may be subject to self-employment tax. Because Adobe wages likely already exceed the Social Security wage base, the interaction will differ from a lower-wage taxpayer, but Medicare and Additional Medicare Tax still require modeling.

## Taxation of mined Bitcoin

IRS Notice 2014-21 provides:

- Bitcoin received from successful mining is ordinary gross income at its U.S.-dollar fair market value when received.
- If mining constitutes a trade or business and is not performed as an employee, net earnings are generally self-employment income.
- The amount included in income becomes the tax basis of the Bitcoin.
- A later sale or exchange creates a separate capital gain or loss based on the difference between sale proceeds and that basis.

This means mining does not create untaxed Bitcoin. It creates ordinary income first and possible capital gain or loss later.

### Risks omitted by the promotion

- **Difficulty growth:** More global hashrate reduces Bitcoin earned per machine even if Bitcoin's price is unchanged.
- **Halving:** The next scheduled block-subsidy halving is expected in 2028, within the useful life assumed for equipment purchased now.
- **Hardware obsolescence:** Newer ASICs can compress both operating margins and resale value quickly.
- **Hosting counterparty risk:** Bankruptcy, curtailment, theft, liens, delayed installation and opaque electricity markups can impair ownership and deductions.
- **Uptime and repair:** Hydro systems and industrial electrical infrastructure are not passive consumer appliances.
- **Price basis risk:** Revenue varies with Bitcoin and transaction fees while much of the power and financing cost is fixed.
- **Tax timing:** First-year depreciation front-loads deductions; it does not improve physical mining efficiency.

## Prepaying power

IRS Publication 538 says an advance expense is generally deductible only in the year to which it applies unless it qualifies for the 12-month rule. The right or benefit must not extend beyond the earlier of:

- 12 months after the benefit begins; or
- the end of the tax year after the payment year.

That rule is an exception from capitalization, not a universal promise of immediate deductibility. Important questions include:

- Is the taxpayer on the cash or accrual method?
- Is the payment nonrefundable and actually for future service, or merely a deposit?
- When does the power/hosting benefit begin and end?
- Does the arrangement materially distort income?
- Is the charge truly electricity, or partly equipment, hosting or financing?

Do not prepay merely to enlarge a deduction. Require the CPA to review the exact hosting contract before payment.

## Borrowing against the home or Bitcoin

### Home-equity borrowing

Loan proceeds are generally not taxable income. But a HELOC used for mining is not automatically deductible home-mortgage debt. IRS Publication 936 states that home-equity interest is not deductible as qualified-residence interest unless proceeds buy, build or substantially improve the securing home. Interest traceably used in a business may instead be reported under business-interest rules, subject to the applicable limitations.

Economically, a $100,000 loan at 7% adds $7,000 of annual fixed cost:

- At $47,000 Bitcoin and $0.09/kWh, the modeled operating margin is only about $1,300 before financing; the loan converts it into an approximately $5,700 annual cash loss.
- A HELOC is commonly variable-rate rather than truly fixed.
- The collateral is the family home, while the purchased asset is rapidly depreciating and Bitcoin-sensitive.

**Verdict: Do not use home-equity leverage for mining.**

### Borrowing against mined Bitcoin

A properly structured loan against Bitcoin is generally not a sale at origination, but it introduces:

- margin-call and forced-liquidation risk;
- counterparty, custody and rehypothecation risk;
- taxable gain or loss if collateral is liquidated;
- floating interest expense; and
- stacked exposure because both revenue and collateral depend on Bitcoin.

**Verdict: Do not layer Bitcoin-backed borrowing onto mining.**

## Immigration constraint

Hooman has previously indicated that he is living in the United States on a visa. Material participation is a tax concept, but actually operating a side business can also constitute work for immigration purposes. Passive ownership and active day-to-day operation are not necessarily treated the same.

Before Hooman or his spouse performs the operating activities needed to make the loss nonpassive, an immigration attorney must confirm that the relevant visa and employment authorization permit those activities. A tax strategy is unusable if the participation needed for the deduction violates immigration conditions.

## Better tax-efficiency hierarchy for this household

The appropriate principle is **maximize after-tax wealth, not deductions**. A deduction created by spending or losing $1 is generally worth only $0.24 federally at the current marginal bracket.

### Priority 1: exhaust low-risk compensation benefits

Verify annually:

- maximum traditional/pre-tax Adobe 401(k) contributions;
- HSA contributions if enrolled in an eligible high-deductible health plan;
- whether Adobe offers a nonqualified deferred-compensation plan to Hooman's employee level, and its distribution/creditor-risk terms;
- employee-stock and bonus withholding versus estimated-tax needs; and
- spouse IRA/backdoor-Roth eligibility—the latter improves long-term tax efficiency but does not create a current deduction.

These generally dominate buying an unwanted business asset solely for depreciation.

### Priority 2: make the taxable portfolio tax-aware

- Use low-turnover ETFs for core equity exposure.
- Harvest genuine losses while respecting wash-sale rules across both spouses and all accounts.
- Locate ordinary-income assets in tax-deferred accounts where practical.
- Consider California municipal bonds for taxable fixed-income exposure after comparing after-tax yields.
- Donate appreciated securities through a donor-advised fund only when there is real charitable intent; this can avoid capital gains and produce an itemized deduction.

### Priority 3: investigate a real spouse-operated business only if wanted independently

Because Hooman's spouse currently has no other employment, a genuinely operated business may create planning opportunities. The business should be selected for economic merit and personal fit, not for its depreciation schedule. Once profitable, it can potentially support deductible retirement contributions.

### Priority 4: real estate only if the operating thesis is genuine

A nonworking spouse can sometimes qualify as a real-estate professional by performing more than 750 hours and more than half of personal-service time in real-property businesses, with material participation. Certain short-term rentals can also fall outside the rental-activity definition and become nonpassive when the taxpayer materially participates.

Cost segregation and bonus depreciation can then create federal losses, but:

- the activity must be real and heavily documented;
- California does not follow federal bonus depreciation;
- depreciation recapture and exit taxes matter;
- local short-term-rental rules and operating risk matter; and
- visa/work authorization must permit the activity.

This is a legitimate area for a separate CPA/attorney feasibility study, not an automatic recommendation.

### Avoid marketed loss generators

Avoid any pitch whose primary return is a tax deduction, particularly syndicated conservation easements, micro-captive insurance arrangements, opaque equipment-leasing programs, abusive charitable structures or promoters who combine tax savings with optimistic gross revenue.

## Recommended decision

### Do not implement the advertised $100,000 mining strategy

Reasons, in order:

1. It exceeds the portfolio's intended risk limits.
2. The advertised revenue omits the largest operating cost.
3. The household's likely federal benefit is closer to 24% than 37%.
4. California does not provide the advertised bonus deduction.
5. Hosted mining creates material-participation uncertainty.
6. Active participation may conflict with visa/work authorization.
7. Borrowing adds unacceptable downside asymmetry.

### If Hooman still wants a learning experiment

Limit it to one machine or approximately $5,000-$10,000, paid in cash and counted inside the existing speculation allocation. Proceed only after all of the following are satisfied:

- a CPA confirms in writing the entity, equipment class life, Section 168(k) treatment, placed-in-service evidence, passive/nonpassive treatment, at-risk amount, California adjustment, self-employment tax and Bitcoin accounting;
- immigration counsel confirms that the planned activities are authorized;
- the purchase contract identifies legal title and individual equipment serial numbers;
- the machine pays mining proceeds directly to a wallet controlled by Hooman;
- the host discloses the all-in power rate, pool fee, curtailment, repair pricing, insurance, termination rights and bankruptcy treatment;
- the investment remains viable under at least $47,000 Bitcoin, 20% higher network difficulty, 90% uptime and the full loan-free operating cost; and
- there is no requirement to prepay an uneconomic power contract to create the deduction.

On the live numbers, even the efficient example earns only about $1,600 per machine annually at $47,000 Bitcoin and $0.07/kWh before repairs, setup, taxes and difficulty growth. A 20% adverse difficulty move can erase that margin. The pilot is therefore unlikely to pass a conservative pre-tax underwriting test.
