## User Thesis and Constraints

China may earn substantial export revenue from clean-energy technology needed to address electricity shortages created by AI data-center growth and high energy prices.

The investable thesis should:

- Exclude electric vehicles and general battery manufacturers.
- Focus on grid equipment, transformers, switchgear, power electronics, inverters, generation equipment, and related infrastructure.
- Use securities available through the user's existing U.S. or Canadian brokerage accounts.
- Avoid meaningful delisting risk; political, tariff, and sanctions volatility are tolerable.
- Be evaluated on its merits before assigning it a portfolio sleeve.

## Does the Thesis Check Out?

The thesis is directionally credible over a three-to-five-year horizon, but the best opportunity is not generic Chinese solar manufacturing.

- The IEA estimated data centers used about 415 TWh in 2024 and could consume roughly 945 TWh by 2030.
- Renewables may meet about half of incremental data-center electricity demand, with natural gas, grids, storage, and nuclear also important.
- China controls more than 80% of manufacturing capacity across major solar supply-chain stages.
- The IEA projects roughly 4,600 GW of renewable additions during 2025-2030, with solar representing nearly 80%.
- Chinese transformer exports have benefited from worldwide equipment shortages, including demand associated with data centers.
- Chinese wind-equipment exports have grown, but trade investigations, service quality, and local-content barriers raise risk.

High oil prices are only an indirect support. Oil is not an important source of grid electricity. AI power shortages more directly benefit grid equipment, transformers, inverters, natural gas, nuclear power, and selected renewable generation.

| Nuclear exports | Hard to access | Often state-controlled and difficult to isolate through liquid securities |
| Hydrogen equipment | Too early | Weak current economics and uncertain adoption path |

## Recommendation

1. **Do not buy immediately.** The portfolio already has about 9.8% in the broader AI-power and electrification chain.
2. **Treat the China-specific export thesis as a potential satellite, not a missing core allocation.** Direct exposure is currently effectively zero, but the portfolio already captures much of the global demand outcome through GEV, ETN, nuclear, utilities, and industrial metals.
3. **Keep 2809/9809 and its major grid-equipment holdings on a watchlist.** It is the best packaged thematic fit but is not yet operationally clean for this U.S.-tax-resident investor.
4. **Before any purchase, confirm brokerage access, PFIC treatment, RRSP treatment, California taxation, bid/ask spreads, and fund liquidity.**
5. **If those issues are resolved, cap an exploratory position at 0.5% of the portfolio.** Fund it from existing China or electrification exposure rather than creating additional aggregate thematic risk.
6. **Prefer grid equipment and power electronics over solar-module manufacturers.** Export growth alone is not enough when industry margins and cash flows are poor.
