## 1. Business & thesis

**What EOG is.** EOG Resources is a large-cap US independent oil & gas explorer & producer ("E&P"),
market cap ~$69B (VERIFIED, yfinance 2026-07-02). It is a **premium, low-cost multi-basin shale
operator** — core acreage in the Delaware Basin (Permian), Eagle Ford, Powder River Basin, and, since
Aug 2025, the **Utica shale** via the $4.484B all-cash Encino acquisition (VERIFIED, EOG Q4-2025
release via secondary aggregation, 2026-02-24). FY2025 revenue $22.6B, net income $4.98B, operating
cash flow $10.04B (VERIFIED, SEC XBRL FY2025 10-K). It runs a **manufacturing-style, high-return
"premium/double-premium drilling" model**: only sanction wells that clear a hard return hurdle (~30%+
ATROR at conservative price decks), which is the source of its structurally-higher returns vs peers.

**The value thesis (bottom-up "quality on relative sale").** EOG is the **best-quality large US E&P**
— highest ROE in its peer set (18.2% vs DVN 15%, COP 11%, FANG ~0%), the cleanest balance sheet
(lowest net debt, §3), a low corporate breakeven (~sub-$50 WTI, §2), and a long, deep, self-funded
drilling inventory. It trades at EV/EBITDA 5.8× and P/E 12.8× — cheap on an absolute basis and
roughly in line with lower-quality peers, so you are getting the quality leader **without paying a
big quality premium on trailing numbers.** Piotroski 7/7 (VERIFIED, screen). The Stage-1a sector work
did **not** flag Energy E&P as cheap-vs-own-history — this is a bottom-up "great company at a
reasonable price" catch, not a cheap-sector play.

**Cheap-cyclical vs value — the honest framing.** E&P is a **price-taker in a commodity cycle**; there
is no durable pricing power. So "cheap on trailing P/E 12.8×" is only a *real* bargain if trailing
earnings are near mid-cycle, not near a peak. EOG's genuine, durable edge is **relative** (lowest cost,
lowest debt, best inventory) — it will out-earn peers across the cycle and survive troughs that impair
weaker operators. That relative moat is real (§3). The absolute *multiple*, though, must be stress-
tested against where oil is in its cycle (§2). This is a quality-cyclical, not a secular compounder.

### 2c. Peak-cycle test — the four sub-tests

1. **Where is oil in the cycle?** WTI ~**$67–68/bbl** mid-2026 (UNVERIFIED — oilprice.com /
   liveoilprices.com aggregation, ~2026-07-02; the EIA authoritative page returned stale data this
   session, flag). That is **well below** the 2022 spike (WTI averaged ~$95) and modestly below the
   2023–24 ~$77 average. Henry Hub gas forecast ~$3.90/MMBtu 2026 (UNVERIFIED, EIA STEO via
   aggregation). **Verdict: oil is NOT at a cyclical peak today** — it has already normalized down ~30%
   from the 2022 peak, which is exactly why 2025 earnings fell. So EOG's trailing numbers are struck on
   a *mid-cycle-ish, arguably soft* oil price, NOT a peak. This substantially *weakens* the classic
   "low multiple on peak oil = trap" concern.

2. **Corporate breakeven.** EOG cites a **~sub-$50 WTI breakeven** to fund the 2026 capital plan +
   regular dividend (UNVERIFIED, EOG guidance via aggregation, 2026-02-24; a second source says
   "$45–50"). With WTI at ~$68, EOG has a **~$18–23/bbl cushion** over breakeven — it generates FCF
   comfortably at current strip, and could sustain the base dividend even in a meaningful oil
   drawdown. This is the low-cost-operator thesis in one number.

3. **FCF at strip.** FY2025 FCF **$4.7B**; 2026 guided FCF **~$4.5B at current strip pricing** on a
   **flat $6.5B capital budget** (UNVERIFIED, EOG guidance via aggregation, 2026-02-24). On a $69B
   market cap that's a **~6.5% FCF yield at ~$68 WTI** — a *mid-cycle*, not peak, FCF yield. EOG
   returned **100% of 2025 FCF** to holders ($2.2B dividends +8%, $2.5B buybacks). (UNVERIFIED,
   secondary.)

4. **Reserve life / runway.** Proved reserves **~5.5 BBoe** at YE2025 (+16% YoY), **254% reserve
   replacement** of 2025 production ex-price-revisions (UNVERIFIED, EOG Q4-2025 via aggregation).
   Deep multi-year premium inventory across four basins + the new Utica leg → **no near-term inventory
   exhaustion**, unlike some Permian-only pure-plays. (Runway = years, ASSUMED — verify exact
   drilling-location count/years against 10-K text at conviction stage.)

**Peak-cycle CALL: LOW-to-MODERATE peak risk — NOT a peak-cycle trap.** The bear's classic trap
("low P/E on peak oil earnings") does **not** cleanly apply here, because oil has *already fallen* ~30%
from its 2022 peak and EOG's earnings have *already compressed* 36% to reflect it. You are buying a
low multiple on **post-normalization** earnings struck at ~$68 WTI, with a sub-$50 breakeven cushion.
The residual risk is **downside from here** (a recession/OPEC+ oversupply push to $50–55 WTI would cut
FCF further and the multiple would prove less cheap), not that you're mistaking a peak for a trough.
**Both sides:** BEAR — E&P earnings are inherently non-recurring; at $68 WTI 2026 FCF yield is only
~6.5%, hardly a fat trough bargain, and the stock could de-rate with oil. BULL — this is the lowest-
cost, lowest-debt operator with the deepest inventory; at ~mid-cycle prices it out-FCFs peers and the
downside is cushioned by the breakeven; buying it *after* the earnings reset (not at the 2022 peak) is
the disciplined entry.

## 3. Quality (the core of the thesis)

- **ROE / returns (VERIFIED + company).** ROE 18.2% (yfinance 2026-07-02) — highest in the peer set.
  Company-reported **ROCE 19% for 2025** (UNVERIFIED, EOG release via aggregation). Returns have
  compressed with oil (see §2b) but remain best-in-class among large E&Ps.
- **Fortress balance sheet (VERIFIED).** Total debt $8.31B, cash $3.85B → **net debt ~$4.5B**, the
  lowest in the peer group both absolutely and relative to EBITDA (net debt/EBITDA ~0.36×). D/E ~27%,
  lowest of the four. EOG is famous for running a large gross cash balance and low leverage; the Encino
  deal was funded with $3.47B new senior notes but the balance sheet remains conservatively geared.
  Net debt actually *rose* only modestly despite a $4.5B acquisition — a testament to FCF funding.
- **FCF generation (VERIFIED OCF / company FCF).** OCF $10.04B FY2025; FCF ~$4.7B; ~$4.5B guided 2026.
  Consistent multi-year FCF even as commodity prices normalized (OCF was $11–12B in the peak years).
- **Capital returns (UNVERIFIED, secondary).** Regular dividend $4.08/sh (3.17% yield), raised **+8%**;
  long history of **regular + special/variable dividends** and buybacks; returned **100% of 2025 FCF**
  ($2.2B div + $2.5B repurchase). Payout ratio ~39% of earnings (VERIFIED yfinance) — the base
  dividend is well-covered and low-breakeven-protected.
- **Well productivity / inventory (UNVERIFIED, company).** "Premium/double-premium" drilling discipline;
  50% faster completions and ~6% lower drilling cost since 2020; 585 net wells planned 2026; ~5.5 BBoe
  proved, 254% replacement, four-basin + Utica optionality. Deep runway.

**Quality verdict: genuinely top-tier for the sector** — the balance sheet + cost + inventory trifecta
is EOG's real, durable *relative* moat. The one honest caveat is that ROE/earnings are *cyclically*
elevated relative to a $50 WTI world, so "18% ROE" is not a through-cycle constant.

## 4. Bear case / risks

1. **Oil & gas price (DOMINANT, ~80% of the thesis).** EOG is a price-taker. A push to $50–55 WTI
   (recession, OPEC+ oversupply, demand shock) would cut FCF materially and the "cheap 5.8× EV/EBITDA"
   would re-rate to look ordinary. The falling earnings trend (§2b) is the live evidence that price
   moves dominate everything else. This is the risk that matters.
2. **Buying after a partial de-rate, not a trough.** At ~$68 WTI the 2026 FCF yield is ~6.5% — decent,
   not a screaming trough bargain. If you believe oil round-trips lower, EOG is not "cheap enough yet."
3. **Inventory / location runway (MODERATE, mitigated).** Shale wells decline fast (~high first-year
   decline), so EOG must continually replace inventory. Its 254% replacement + Utica add mitigate this
   near-term, but long-run core-acreage quality erosion is the structural E&P risk; Encino/Utica quality
   is less proven than its Permian/Eagle Ford core (integration + type-curve risk). (ASSUMED.)
4. **Energy-transition / long-run demand (LOW-MODERATE, long-dated).** Secular EV/renewables adoption
   caps long-run oil demand growth; a slow-motion terminal-value risk, not a 1–3yr thesis-killer.
   Gas has a nearer-term *tailwind* (LNG export + data-center/AI power demand) that partly offsets.
5. **Capital allocation (LOW).** EOG's discipline is a strength, but the $4.5B Encino deal is a reminder
   that management can spend the fortress balance sheet on M&A; watch that future deals clear the same
   return hurdle rather than chase production growth. Track record here is good.

### VERDICT: **WATCH → lean BUY-worth-more-work.**

At **6.9/10**, EOG lands in the upper "WATCH" band and is worth more work before a buy. It is
unambiguously the **highest-quality large US E&P** — fortress balance sheet, lowest cost, deepest
inventory — available at a reasonable (not fire-sale) valuation. Critically, it is **not a classic
peak-cycle trap**: oil has already fallen ~30% from its 2022 peak and EOG's earnings have already
compressed 36% to match, so you're buying a low multiple on *post-normalization* earnings with a
sub-$50 breakeven cushion, not a low multiple on peak profits. The gate to a BUY is a **view on oil
from ~$68**: if you're neutral-to-constructive on WTI holding $65–75, EOG is an attractive quality-
cyclical with ~6.5% FCF yield fully returned to holders and ~20% consensus upside. The reason it's not
a slam-dunk BUY is precisely that the whole thesis rests on the one variable EOG can't control —
oil/gas price — and at $68 it's fairly (not cheaply) priced for that; a drawdown to $50–55 WTI would
make the current multiple look ordinary. **Top risk: commodity price (oil), dominant and un-hedgeable
at the thesis level.**
