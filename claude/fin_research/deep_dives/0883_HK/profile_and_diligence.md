# PART A — THE BUSINESS PRIMER (for a newcomer)

## A1. What CNOOC actually does — in plain language

**CNOOC Ltd is a pure "upstream" oil & gas company.** In oil-industry terms:
- **Upstream = exploration & production ("E&P")** — finding oil/gas in the ground (or under the
  seabed) and pumping it out. This is the commodity-price-sensitive part of the industry.
- **Midstream** = pipelines/transport; **Downstream** = refining crude into fuel + selling it at gas
  stations. **CNOOC does almost none of the down/midstream** — it sells the raw crude and gas it
  produces. That matters: it is a *cleaner bet on the oil price* than an "integrated" major (Exxon,
  Shell, PetroChina) that also owns refineries and gas stations. No refining margin to smooth
  earnings, but also no low-return refining/retail drag.

**"Offshore-focused"** — most of its production comes from wells under the sea, not on land. Its home
base is **China's offshore basins**: the **Bohai Sea** (its biggest producing region, in China's
north), the **South China Sea** (east and west), and the **East China Sea**. It runs essentially all
of China's offshore oil & gas. [VERIFIED — company profile + FY2024 annual report.]

**Overseas book** — it is not only China. CNOOC owns stakes in some of the best oil assets on earth:
- **Guyana — the Stabroek block: CNOOC holds a 25% interest** (ExxonMobil operates 45%, Hess 30%).
  This is one of the largest oil discoveries of the last two decades and is ramping hard — a key
  growth engine. Yellowtail development came onstream Aug 2025. [VERIFIED, deep_dive.md + FY2024 deck.]
- **Brazil pre-salt** — stakes in the giant Búzios and Mero fields (Búzios7 and Mero4 units came
  onstream H1 2025). [VERIFIED.]
- Plus assets in the **US Gulf of Mexico, Nigeria, Iraq, the UK, Canada (oil sands, from the 2013
  Nexen deal), and Australia.** [VERIFIED, FY2024 AR.]

**Jargon — "boe" and "mmboe":** oil and gas are measured together in **barrels of oil equivalent
(boe)** — gas is converted into the amount of oil that holds the same energy. **mmboe = million
barrels of oil equivalent.** "mmboe/d" = per day.

**Production scale (VERIFIED, CNOOC FY2025 annual results, released 2026-03-26):**
- **FY2025 net production: 777.3 mmboe (+7.0% YoY)** — roughly **~2.13 million boe per day.**
- That is a big producer — about half the daily output of ExxonMobil (~4.7 mmboe/d) but comparable to
  ConocoPhillips, and *growing faster than either.* [Exxon/COP figures VERIFIED, peer research 2026-07-03.]
- **Proved reserves: 7,773 mmboe (record high)** — proved reserves are the oil/gas the company is
  reasonably certain it can still pump out. Divided by annual production that's a **~10-year reserve
  life** (it has held ≥10 years for 7+ straight years). [VERIFIED.]

**What SOE control means for you as a minority shareholder (the governance risk):**
1. **Capital allocation can be state-directed, not return-maximizing.** Documented examples:
   - The **2013 Nexen acquisition (~US$15.1 bn)** — China's largest-ever foreign takeover, widely read
     as a state-championed strategic/trophy purchase at a cyclical top; the Long Lake oil-sands assets
     chronically underperformed. [VERIFIED operationally.]
   - The **"Seven-Year Action Plan" (2019–2025)** — Beijing directed the big-three national oil
     companies to *raise* domestic exploration spending for **energy security**, i.e. drill for
     national-strategic rather than purely commercial reasons. [VERIFIED, IEA + Enerdata.]
2. **Massive related-party transactions with the parent group** (VERIFIED, FY2024 annual report):
   - **~59% of CNOOC's revenue is oil & gas SOLD TO the parent group** (RMB 247.4 bn in 2024). The
     majority of the top line is a related-party sale — so the parent effectively sets a big chunk of
     realized pricing.
   - **RMB 96.7 bn of services BOUGHT FROM the group** in 2024 (drilling/oilfield services via COSL,
     engineering/FPSO via COOEC). The filing discloses these by category, not by named affiliate, so a
     clean COSL/COOEC split isn't in CNOOC's own reports (a disclosure gap). [VERIFIED.]
   - These are governed by a Comprehensive Framework Agreement with annual caps that **independent
     shareholders approved at an EGM** and independent directors/auditor sign off on — so the HKEX
     minority-protection machinery does run. But the *scale* means you are structurally exposed to
     parent pricing. [VERIFIED.]
3. **The offsetting good news — the parent LIKES dividends.** Because CNOOC Group collects ~62% of
   every dividend, its interests are *aligned with yours on distributions.* CNOOC has a long, reliable,
   rising dividend record and a formal payout floor (§A3, §B5). Buybacks are authorized big but used
   tiny (HK$727m in 2024, nil in 2023 — trivial vs the dividend). Returns come almost entirely through
   dividends. [VERIFIED.] Dilution history is limited: the 2022 A-share IPO diluted existing H-holders
   ~6%; no other equity raises 2020–2025. [VERIFIED.]

**Governance takeaway:** a genuinely well-run *operator* (see the cost/reserve record) wrapped in a
*state-controlled ownership box*. You get a low-cost, growing producer and a reliable dividend; you do
NOT get a say, and you accept that Beijing's energy-security agenda and parent-group related-party
pricing sit above your interests. This is the core reason it trades at a discount to Western majors.

## A3. How it makes money & the moat — ultra-low cost is the real edge

CNOOC makes money the simplest way in the industry: **it pumps a barrel for far less than it sells it
for.** The moat is **cost leadership** — and unlike most "moat" claims, this one is quantified in the
filings.

**Jargon — "all-in cost per boe":** CNOOC's own metric for the total cost to get one barrel out and
sold — operating cost + **DD&A** (depreciation, depletion & amortization: spreading the cost of the
oilfield over the barrels produced) + dismantlement + SG&A (overhead) + other taxes. It is broader than
the "lifting cost" that Western majors quote (lifting cost = just the day-to-day pumping cost), so
CNOOC's number looks higher than theirs but is measuring MORE. Don't compare the two labels naively.

**CNOOC all-in cost per boe (VERIFIED, company results decks):**

| FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | **FY2025** |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| $30.39 | $28.83 | $28.52 | **$27.90** |

- The cost is **falling while production grows** — the signature of a disciplined operator, not a
  spend-at-any-cost SOE.
- Against a **FY2025 realized crude price of $66.47/bbl** [VERIFIED], that's roughly a **$40/boe cash
  margin** and a wide profitability buffer — CNOOC stays profitable far below today's oil price.
- Company claims its per-barrel *operating* cost is among the lowest vs ConocoPhillips/BP/Exxon/
  Shell/Chevron/Equinor/Occidental. [VERIFIED as a company claim, FY2023 deck.] (For a genuine
  cross-company view see §B6 — cost labels aren't apples-to-apples, but CNOOC is clearly near the
  low-cost end.)

**Reserve base & reserve life (VERIFIED):** proved reserves at a record 7,773 mmboe (FY2025);
~10-year reserve life held for 7+ straight years; the reserve-replacement ratio (RRR = how much new
reserve it books per barrel produced) was **167% in 2024, 180% in 2023** — i.e. it replaces *more*
than it produces, so the resource base is growing, not depleting. (FY2025 RRR not disclosed in the
results release; would be in the full annual report.) [VERIFIED for 2023–24; FY2025 RRR NOT FOUND.]

**Production-growth strategy:** CNOOC is unusual — a *cheap* oil name that is also a *growth* oil name.
Net production went 623.8 (2022) → 678.0 (2023) → 726.8 (2024) → **777.3 mmboe (2025, +7.0%)**. Guidance:
**780–800 mmboe for 2026** [VERIFIED, FY2025 release]. Growth engines: Guyana (Stabroek/Yellowtail),
Brazil pre-salt (Búzios/Mero), Bohai, and the "Deep Sea No.1" gas project. ~7–8%/yr volume growth.
[VERIFIED directionally.]

## B7. What to watch / key uncertainties

Ranked by how much they should worry a would-be holder:

1. **Oil price (the #1 swing factor).** Earnings move directly with Brent. FY2025 profit fell -11.5%
   on ~$66 realized crude; the FY2020 precedent shows a severe low-oil year forces a dividend cut. The
   $27.90 all-in cost + net-cash balance sheet give a wide buffer, and oil is currently **mid/low-cycle,
   not a peak** (2025 avg Brent $69.14 vs the 2022 peak of $100.93 [VERIFIED, EIA]) — so downside is
   more oil-price than multiple. But this is a leveraged bet on oil staying above ~$50–60. **Watch:
   Brent, OPEC+ supply decisions, and CNOOC's realized-price prints each quarter.**

2. **China SOE capital-allocation risk (the #2, structural one).** Beijing can direct capex, pricing,
   and strategic acquisitions against minority interests (Nexen, mandated domestic drilling, ~59% of
   revenue sold to the parent group). The offsetting force is that the parent collects ~62% of
   dividends and wants them paid. **Watch: any new state-directed spending mandate, changes to the
   ≥45% payout floor after 2027, and related-party-transaction cap renewals.**

3. **Guyana / Brazil ramp (the growth catalyst — and its execution risk).** The +7–8%/yr growth thesis
   leans heavily on Stabroek (Guyana) and pre-salt (Brazil) coming onstream on schedule. Delays,
   cost overruns, or the Guyana-Venezuela border tension would dent the growth story. **Watch:
   Stabroek phase startups and CNOOC's production guidance each March.**

4. **Geopolitical / sanctions-adjacent (noted, not re-litigated).** CNOOC is on the OFAC **NS-CMIC**
   list — this bars **US persons**, and was the basis of the 2021 NYSE/TSX delistings. **Per
   `canadian_investability.md`, this does NOT bind a Canadian who is not a US green-card holder,
   transacts from outside the US, and uses a non-US broker.** The residual risks *for a Canadian* are:
   (a) escalation from NS-CMIC to a full SDN block (would freeze the position) in a US-China/Taiwan
   rupture, (b) HK-market-access curbs, and (c) your **broker's own compliance policy** — many brokers
   block CMIC securities firm-wide as policy regardless of law, so confirm your (non-US) broker will
   actually execute 0883.HK before relying on the thesis. Re-check CNOOC's OFAC/SDN status at time of
   trade. [See `canadian_investability.md` for the full clearance — not repeated here.]

5. **FX / capital controls (lower-order).** Shares priced in HKD (USD-pegged), earnings in RMB,
   dividends declared in HKD. RMB depreciation, tighter cross-border capital controls, or
   dividend-repatriation friction are live China/EM risks. [ASSUMED.]
