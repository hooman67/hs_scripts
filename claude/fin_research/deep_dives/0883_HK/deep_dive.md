## 1. Business & thesis

**What it is.** CNOOC Ltd is China's dominant **offshore** oil & gas explorer/producer — the listed
arm of state-owned CNOOC Group. It is a **pure upstream E&P** (no big refining/retail drag, unlike
Sinopec/PetroChina), operating the bulk of China's offshore fields (Bohai, South China Sea, East
China Sea) plus a growing international book: **25% of ExxonMobil's Guyana Stabroek block**, stakes
in Brazil's pre-salt (Búzios, Mero), plus assets in Nigeria, Iraq, Canada, US GoM, UK, Australia.
Listed on HKEX Feb 2001 [VERIFIED, Wikipedia]. FY2024 net production **726.8 mmboe** (~2.0 mmboe/day),
proved reserves **7,271 mmboe** [VERIFIED, FY2024 results announcement, 2025-03-27,
`cnoocltd.com/.../P020250327582739648081.pdf`].

**The value thesis (bull case).** A low-cost, low-debt, high-return upstream producer growing volumes
~7–8%/yr, trading at ~3× EV/EBITDA and ~7× earnings with a 6.3% yield and a 45% payout floor. On
fundamentals alone it dominates every peer in the screen. The cheapness is not a quality problem — it
is a **China discount + geopolitical/sanctions overhang** discount, not a broken-business discount.

**Why is it so cheap? Three stacked discounts (this is the crux):**
1. **Commodity cycle** — partial only. Oil is ~$69–71 Brent (2025 avg $69.14 [VERIFIED, EIA]), i.e.
   **mid-to-low cycle, NOT a peak** (§2 peak-cycle test). So earnings are not inflated peak-cycle
   earnings — this discount is *not* the main driver and cuts in CNOOC's favor.
2. **China / SOE discount** — real and large. State ownership, minority-shareholder subordination to
   Beijing's energy-security agenda, RMB/HKD and capital-control risk, and broad foreign de-rating of
   Chinese equities. PetroChina (0.86× P/B) and Sinopec (0.53× P/B) trade even cheaper on book —
   consistent with a sector-wide China-SOE discount, not a CNOOC-specific flaw.
3. **US sanctions / investability overhang** — the single biggest structural buyer removal. CNOOC was
   forcibly delisted from NYSE (Mar 2021) and US persons are banned from owning it (§4a). Losing the
   entire US institutional bid is a permanent structural de-rating force, not a cycle.

**Net:** it is cheap primarily because it is Chinese, state-owned, and **US-sanctioned** — not because
it is low-quality. That is exactly why the investability question (§5) dominates the valuation question.

## 4. Bear case / risks — this name is RISK-HEAVY

### (a) 🚨 US SANCTIONS — US PERSONS ARE PROHIBITED FROM INVESTING (the hard blocker)
**This is the single most important finding in this report and it likely overrides everything below.**

- CNOOC is on OFAC's **Non-SDN Chinese Military-Industrial Complex Companies List (NS-CMIC List)**,
  designated under **Executive Order 13959** (as amended by EO 14032). CNOOC carries the eCFR
  identifier **`[CMIC-EO13959]`** [VERIFIED — eCFR Title 31 Part 586 / Federal Register; also
  Wikipedia "Executive Order 13959": CNOOC named among the companies added 2020-12-03].
- **The prohibition [VERIFIED — OFAC FAQ topic 5671, ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/topic/5671]:** US persons
  are prohibited from the **"purchase or sale ... of any publicly traded securities"** of an NS-CMIC
  company. The ban **explicitly extends to** derivatives (futures/options/swaps), warrants, **ADRs,
  GDRs, ETFs, index funds, and mutual funds** that hold the security. A one-time **365-day divestment
  window** was allowed after listing (CNOOC's expired long ago — divestment deadline was
  **2021-11-11** per EO 13959; NYSE ceased trading **2021-03-09** [VERIFIED, Wikipedia / NYSE-ICE
  delisting press release]). After that window, **US persons may not buy at all**, and even selling
  requires OFAC authorization.
- **NYSE forcibly delisted CNOOC** in early 2021 in compliance with EO 13959 [VERIFIED — ICE/NYSE
  press release "NYSE to Commence Delisting Proceedings Against CNOOC Limited"].
- **Still in force?** The EO 13959 / NS-CMIC regime **remains in effect** — it survived multiple
  administrations and the DoD moved Chinese-military-company designations to an annual **Section
  1260H** process from June 2021 [VERIFIED regime persistence, Wikipedia]. I found **no** 2024–2026
  source indicating the CNOOC investment ban has been lifted. **[ASSUMED still in force as of
  2026-07-02; UNVERIFIED that it hasn't quietly changed — this is the ONE thing to reconfirm on
  ofac.treasury.gov before acting.]** CNOOC's precise presence on the *latest* DoD 1260H list was
  not independently confirmed here [UNVERIFIED] — but 1260H is a *labeling* list; the **NS-CMIC
  listing is the one that actually bars investment, and that is VERIFIED.**

**Bottom line on (a): For a US person, buying 0883.HK shares (or an ADR/ETF/fund holding it) is a
prohibited transaction under US sanctions law. This is a legal/compliance blocker, not a risk to be
priced. Hooman is a US person employed by a US company; this almost certainly makes CNOOC
UN-INVESTABLE for him regardless of how cheap it is.** Do not route around it via HK brokers/ETFs —
the prohibition follows the US person, and derivatives/funds are explicitly covered.

### (b) China SOE governance / minority-shareholder risk — HIGH
State (CNOOC Group) controls the company and can subordinate minority holders to Beijing's
energy-security/price/capex agenda (e.g. directed spending, price interventions, related-party
dealings). Minorities are passengers. This is a permanent structural discount, not a cycle. [ASSUMED,
standard SOE analysis — no specific abuse cited here.]

### (c) Oil price — MODERATE (and currently mid/low, not peak)
Earnings swing with Brent. 9M 2025 profit fell 12.6% on ~$68 oil [VERIFIED]. A sustained drop toward
the $40s (2020-style) would pressure earnings and, at the margin, the dividend — though the ~$27–29
all-in cost gives a wide breakeven buffer and the balance sheet is net-cash. Since oil is **not** at a
peak, the cyclical downside is more moderate than it would be at $100 Brent.

### (d) HKD / capital-controls / FX — MODERATE
Shares priced in HKD (USD-pegged), earnings in RMB, dividends declared in HKD. RMB depreciation,
tightening of cross-border capital controls, or dividend-repatriation friction are live EM/China
risks. [ASSUMED.]

### (e) Geopolitical (US-China / Taiwan) — HIGH and rising
A Taiwan crisis or further US-China escalation could bring additional sanctions (e.g. escalation from
NS-CMIC to a full SDN block, secondary sanctions, HK-market access curbs). Offshore SCS assets sit in
contested waters. The 2026 Mideast disruption backdrop [UNVERIFIED, secondary] adds oil-supply and
tail-risk noise. This is precisely the kind of name most exposed to a US-China rupture.
