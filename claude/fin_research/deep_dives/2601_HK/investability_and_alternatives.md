## Jargon, in one place (for a newcomer)

- **Ordinary shares ("ords"):** the normal shares that trade on the company's home exchange (e.g. CPIC's
  H-shares on Hong Kong, priced in HKD). To buy them you need a broker with access to *that* exchange.
- **ADR (American Depositary Receipt):** a US-traded certificate that *represents* a set number of the
  foreign ordinary shares, held in custody by a US bank. Lets you buy a foreign company in USD through a
  US broker. **ADR ratio** = how many ords one ADR = (e.g. PUK 1 ADR : 2 ords; AIA 1 ADR : 4 ords).
- **GDR (Global Depositary Receipt):** same idea as an ADR but usually listed in London/Luxembourg,
  often USD-denominated. CPIC has a London GDR (LSE ticker "CPIC").
- **NYSE-listed ADR (a "full" or "sponsored, exchange-listed" ADR):** an ADR admitted to a real
  exchange (NYSE/Nasdaq). Trades like any US stock — full liquidity, $0 commission at most brokers. **PUK
  is this.** The cleanest way for a North American to own a foreign company.
- **OTC / pink-sheet ADR:** an ADR (or foreign ordinary) that trades **over-the-counter** (dealer
  network, "OTC Pink" tier) instead of on a real exchange. It's still real and buyable, BUT: **thin
  volume, wider bid-ask spreads, prices can lag the home-market close by a time zone, extra fees, and
  often restricted in registered (RRSP/TFSA) accounts.** AIA's AAGIY and Ping An's PNGAY are this.
- **Embedded Value (EV) / VNB / P/EV:** the right valuation metrics for a life insurer (present value of
  future profits from the in-force book / from this year's new sales / price-to-EV). Explained in
  `profile_and_diligence.md` §A1. For a lifeco, **P/EV is the analogue of price-to-book.**

## 3.3 Is CPIC worth the access hassle + China risk?

**On the numbers, CPIC is the better *value*** (0.72x book, ~0.5x EV, ~17% ROE, VNB +40%, ~4.8% covered
yield — the deep dive's 7.2/10 BUY stands). **But it fails the practical test on Hooman's brokers:**
- On **Questrade it is simply un-buyable** (no HKEX, no US ADR).
- On **Schwab** it needs a *separate* Global Account and the HK ordinary — a real, ongoing hassle.
- The clean route (IBKR) means opening yet another broker.
- And you're still carrying the **China SOE + falling-rate negative-spread + RMB** risk stack.

**Pragmatic bottom line:** if Hooman is willing to open **Interactive Brokers (or a Schwab Global
Account)** and hold direct China risk, **CPIC remains the cheapest-quality expression** and is worth it

frictions (access + China) that make CPIC hard to own. **MFC.TO/SLF.TO** are the softer, home-market
variant if diversification and comfort outrank cheapness/purity.
