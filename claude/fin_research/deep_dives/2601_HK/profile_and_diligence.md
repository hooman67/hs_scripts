# PART A — BUSINESS PRIMER (for a newcomer)

## A1. What CPIC does — in plain language

**China Pacific Insurance (Group) Co., Ltd. ("CPIC", 中国太保)** is one of China's **big-3/4 insurers**.
It is a Shanghai-headquartered holding company that owns three businesses:

1. **CPIC Life** (~56% of the group) — sells **life & health insurance** (savings policies, protection
   policies, annuities, critical-illness) mostly through **tied agents** (~78% of new business is
   agency-sold). This is the **higher-margin, embedded-value-generating engine** — the reason a life
   insurer is valued differently from a bank or an industrial.
2. **CPIC P&C** (~44%) — **property & casualty**: mostly **auto/motor** insurance plus non-auto (home,
   liability, agriculture). This is short-tail, priced-annually insurance, valued on its **combined
   ratio** (below).
3. **Asset management + pension + health** (CPIC AMC etc.) — manages the group's ~RMB 2.9tn investment
   book and third-party money.

**Market position (VERIFIED, CPIC FY2025 results via IR, 2026-03-26):** by primary premium income,
**both CPIC Life and CPIC P&C rank #3 in China** in their respective segments. Group total assets were
**RMB 2.73tn (FY2024) → RMB 2.92tn (1H2025)** (VERIFIED, CPIC filings). It is a genuinely top-tier,
national-scale franchise.

### How a life insurer actually makes money (the two profit engines)

A newcomer's instinct is "premiums minus claims." That is only half of it. A life insurer earns from
**two** distinct sources:

- **(1) Underwriting margin** — it collects premiums and pays out claims/benefits; the difference
  (after costs) is underwriting profit. For P&C this is measured by the **combined ratio**:

  > **Combined ratio = (claims + expenses) ÷ premiums earned.** **Below 100% = underwriting profit**
  > (you kept more than you paid out); above 100% = underwriting loss. CPIC P&C ran **98.6% (FY2024)**
  > and **96.3% (1H2025)** — i.e. it makes an underwriting *profit*, which is the mark of a good P&C
  > book (many insurers run >100% and rely purely on investment income). (VERIFIED, CPIC filings.)

- **(2) Investment spread on "the float."** This is the big one for a life insurer. When you pay a life
  premium, the insurer holds that cash for *years or decades* before it pays out. That pool of
  policyholder money it gets to invest in the meantime is called the **float**. The insurer invests the
  float (mostly bonds, some equities) and keeps the **spread** — the gap between what it *earns* on the
  float and what it *promised* to credit policyholders (the guaranteed/participating rate). A life
  insurer is, in large part, **a leveraged bond-and-equity portfolio funded by cheap, sticky
  policyholder money.** CPIC's total investment yield was **5.6% (FY2024)** vs 2.6% (FY2023) — the swing
  is what drove the earnings recovery (see Part B). (VERIFIED, CPIC filings.)

### Why life insurers are valued on Embedded Value (EV) and VNB — NOT just P/E

This is the single most important concept for a newcomer to this name. A life policy sold today
generates profit *over the next 20–40 years*. Standard accounting (and P/E) captures a single year, so
it badly misrepresents a life insurer. The industry solves this with two forward-looking measures:

- **Embedded Value (EV)** — the insurer's estimate of its **true economic worth today**: (net asset
  value) **+** (the present value of all future profits already locked into the in-force book of
  policies). Think of it as "liquidation net worth **plus** the discounted profit stream from every
  policy already sold." It is the **balance-sheet analogue** for a life insurer, and the right
  denominator for valuation. **CPIC group EV = RMB 562.1bn (FY2024) → 588.9bn (1H2025) → 613.4bn
  (FY2025, +9.1% YoY).** (VERIFIED, CPIC filings.)
  - **Price-to-EV (P/EV)** is the life-insurer equivalent of P/B. Trading **below 1.0x EV** means the
    market is paying *less than* the present value of profits already contracted — before valuing a
    single future sale. **CPIC A-share P/EV ≈ 0.48–0.51x** (DERIVED: price ÷ filed EV). Cheap.

- **Value of New Business (VNB, a.k.a. NBV)** — the present value of future profit from **this year's
  new sales only.** It is the **income-statement analogue** — the "organic growth engine." If VNB is
  *growing*, the franchise is getting *more* valuable each year; if VNB is collapsing, the franchise is
  dying regardless of what reported profit says. **CPIC Life VNB grew +20.9% (FY2024) → +32.3% (1H2025)
  → +40.1% (FY2025, to RMB 18.6bn)** — accelerating. (VERIFIED, CPIC filings + IR.) This *accelerating*
  VNB is the strongest single piece of evidence that CPIC is a cyclical-trough recovery, not a broken
  business.

> **Why P/E is misleading here:** reported net profit swings violently with markets because, under
> IFRS 9, mark-to-market gains/losses on the equity portfolio flow straight through the P&L. CPIC's
> profit went RMB 27.3bn (2023) → 45.0bn (2024, +64.9%) → 53.5bn (2025, +19%) largely on **investment
> swings, not operating change.** A single-year P/E of ~4x therefore over- or under-states the
> business depending on where markets were. **Anchor on P/EV, VNB growth, and P/B — not P/E.**

## A2. Ownership, management & governance

- **Ownership: Shanghai-SOE-backed.** CPIC is a **state-owned enterprise (SOE)** controlled by
  **central-Shanghai-SASAC-linked state entities** (Shanghai municipal state investment vehicles are
  the anchor shareholders). It is not privately controlled. (VERIFIED status; exact stake % not pulled
  here — a gap.)
- **Three listings, one company** (total ~**9.62bn shares**, VERIFIED from the FY2025 dividend base):
  - **A-share — 601601.SS** (Shanghai, listed 2007), priced in RMB. The **cleanest valuation read** (no
    FX noise) and the largest share class.
  - **H-share — 2601.HK** (Hong Kong, listed 2009), priced in HKD. **HSCEI index constituent** →
    passive/index demand. This is the line a Canadian would most likely buy. HKD is USD-pegged, so FX
    risk vs USD is low; but the *underlying business is RMB*, so there is RMB→HKD translation risk.
  - **London GDR — "CPIC"** (LSE, via the Shanghai-London Stock Connect), priced in USD. A third access
    route. (GDR size/liquidity UNVERIFIED — likely thin.)
  - The A-share typically trades at a premium to the H-share (the usual A/H gap); the H-share is the
    cheaper way in and the more liquid for a foreign investor.
- **Governance implications for minorities (real, standard-for-cohort discount).** As a state-controlled
  insurer, the state's policy goals — financial-system stability, holding domestic bonds/equities to
  support markets, "national-service" capital allocation — **can outrank minority-shareholder return
  maximization.** This is the core reason Chinese SOE insurers carry a persistent discount to book/EV.
  It is a real limit on how hard you should expect management to work the balance sheet for you (e.g.
  buybacks are rare; payout policy is conservative). Not disqualifying, but priced-in for a reason.
- **Dividend policy & track record.** DPS (RMB): 1.00 → 1.02 → 1.02 → 1.08 (FY2021–24) → **1.15
  (FY2025)**; the FY2025 payout was **~RMB 11.06bn** (VERIFIED via CPIC FY2025 results). The dividend is
  **uninterrupted and growing**, payout ~20–36% of net profit, and comfortably covered. **H-share yield
  ~4.9%** (A-share ~3.8%). (Values VERIFIED; a *stated* payout-policy floor is UNVERIFIED — CPIC is
  understood to target ~30% but this is not asserted from a primary source.)

## A3. The cheap-quality case (why so cheap given the quality)

**The quality on offer (VERIFIED, CPIC filings):**
- **Normalized ROE ~16–18%** — FY2024 16.6%, **FY2025 18.0%** (weighted-avg, up 1.4pts). High for any
  insurer, anywhere.
- **EV compounding** ~6–9%/yr through a weak macro; **VNB accelerating** to +40%.
- **Fortress solvency** (C-ROSS II): Group **core 206% / comprehensive 273%** (FY2025) — **2–4x the
  regulatory minimums** (50% core / 100% comprehensive). Solvency is not a concern.
- **P&C underwriting profit** (combined ratio <100%), and a **conservative ~75% fixed-income book**.

**The price you pay for it:**
- **~0.72x P/B** (below book, on a ~17% ROE franchise), **~0.48–0.51x P/EV** (paying half of embedded
  value), **~4–5x P/E** (distorted, see A1), **~4.9% dividend yield** (H-share). (P/B, P/E, ROE, yield
  VERIFIED yfinance 2026-07-02; P/EV DERIVED.)

**Why it's this cheap — three overlapping reasons, all real:**
1. **The "China discount"** — SOE governance, geopolitical risk, RMB/capital-controls concern, and
   foreign-investor aversion to Chinese equities generally. The whole China-lifeco sector de-rated
   2022–2024.
2. **Rate fears** (the *structural* one — see Part B7) — falling Chinese interest rates threaten the
   investment spread on the guaranteed-return back-book. This is the legitimate part of the discount.
3. **Earnings optics** — the volatile, market-driven P/E and a lagging "-6% earnings growth" screen
   reading (a stale/trailing artifact; the trough was FY2023 and reversed hard) make the name *look*
   worse on a naive screen than it is.

**The case in one line:** a #3-in-China, ~17% ROE, EV-compounding, fortress-solvency insurer trading at
**half its embedded value and below book with a covered ~5% yield** — cheap-quality — where the discount
is *part* justified (rate/spread structural risk) and *part* an opportunity (China/SOE optics on an
intact, scaled franchise).

## B7. What to watch

**(1) THE STRUCTURAL RISK — falling Chinese interest rates → negative-spread risk (the Japan-1990s
analogy).** This is the dominant, load-bearing worry for the whole cohort and for CPIC specifically:
- China's 10-yr government bond yield fell **below 2% for the first time on record** (Dec 2024) and sits
  **~1.7%** (2026). Legacy life books carry **guaranteed crediting rates of ~3.0–4.025%.** If the insurer
  can't earn *above* the guaranteed rate on reinvested float, it earns a **negative spread** and slowly
  bleeds — exactly what sank **seven Japanese life insurers 1997–2001** after Japan's rates collapsed.
  Swiss Re and Moody's (negative China-life sector outlook) explicitly draw the Japan-1990s parallel and
  note China has *fewer* mitigants (limited overseas investment, shrinking/aging population).
- **This is precisely why CPIC (and peers) cut their long-run investment-return assumption** — which
  mechanically lowered reported EV/VNB. It is also why the sector trades below book/EV. **Mitigants:**
  the regulator has cut the **max new-policy guaranteed rate 3.0% → 2.5% (Sep 2024) → 2.0% (Sep 2025)**
  to protect *new* liabilities; CPIC is mix-shifting toward protection/participating products; VNB
  *margin* is rising. But the **back-book guarantees remain** — this is a slow, multi-year grind, not an
  acute event. **VERIFIED (direction/rates); magnitude ASSUMED.** *This is the #1 thing to watch — track
  the 10-yr CGB yield and CPIC's net investment yield vs its guaranteed-rate stack.*

**(2) Investment-portfolio exposure to China equities & property (drives the earnings volatility).**
~**14.5% equity allocation** on a ~RMB 2.9tn book means group profit swings hard with the CSI 300 — the
entire 2023-trough → 2024-surge round-trip was investment-income-led. This keeps single-year P/E and ROE
unreliable and the stock's earnings genuinely volatile (cuts both ways — a market drop reverses it).
**China property-developer exposure** appears *modest* (closest disclosure: "real estate" ≈ 11–13% of a
*non-public financing sub-book*, ~RMB 33bn at 1H2025, described AAA-rated) — but **direct developer
exposure is not separately disclosed (a transparency gap; UNVERIFIED).** *Watch quarterly investment
yield and any property-impairment lines.*

**Also monitor (lower-order):**
- **Regulatory changes** — further guaranteed-rate cuts (helps new business, signals rate stress),
  solvency-regime tweaks, and any state "national-service" capital directives (e.g. mandated equity
  buying to support markets, or the ~RMB 200bn sector special-bond recapitalization plan that named
  peers).
- **Agent-channel productivity** — CPIC Life is ~78% agency-sold; agent-force reform and per-agent
  productivity are the operating lever behind VNB. Watch agent headcount vs VNB/agent.
- **SOE governance / minority alignment** — payout-policy changes, any capital allocation that favors
  state goals over minority return.
