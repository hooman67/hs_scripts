## 1. Business & thesis

**Business (VERIFIED, Kinross FY2025 release + Northern Miner):** Kinross is a mid-to-large
gold producer, **~2.0 Moz gold-equivalent attributable production in 2025**. Core operating
mines span the **Americas + West Africa**:
- **Tasiast** (Mauritania) — largest / lowest-cost mine, West Africa.
- **Paracatu** (Brazil) — large open pit.
- **Fort Knox** + **Manh Choh** (Alaska), **Round Mountain** + **Bald Mountain** (Nevada) — US.
- **La Coipa** (Chile).
- **Great Bear** (Ontario, Canada) — flagship development project (see §3/§4).

Jurisdiction note: Kinross **exited Russia in 2022** (sold Kupol/Udinsk) — a de-risking event.
Current base is **majority Americas + Mauritania**; no top-tier-A-only jurisdiction concentration,
but Mauritania (Tasiast) is a meaningful single-asset West-Africa exposure. *Confidence:
asset list VERIFIED; per-mine production split UNVERIFIED (couldn't pin a clean primary table).*

**The value thesis (bull framing):** A profitable, **net-cash**, high-ROE (35%) gold major
trading at ~10× earnings and ~6–6.5× EBITDA with 7/7 Piotroski and a fully-funded Tier-1
growth project (Great Bear) in a stable jurisdiction (Canada). At a gold price that is at
record highs, it gushes FCF (~$2.5B attributable FCF in 2025), and it is returning that cash
(buybacks + growing dividend).

**Cheap-cyclical vs genuine value (the central question):** This is **mostly cheap-cyclical,
not genuine deep value.** The low P/E and EV/EBITDA are a direct product of **gold at all-time
highs**. See §2 peak-cycle test — the earnings base is elevated, so a low multiple on it is
the market correctly refusing to capitalize peak earnings at a growth multiple. It is a
*quality* cyclical caught at a good moment, not a mispriced compounder.

## 2. Valuation & COMMODITY PEAK-CYCLE test

### 2a. Own history — earnings are unmistakably at a cyclical high
VERIFIED (yfinance annual financials, KGC, 2026-07-02; cross-checks FY2025 release):

| FY | Revenue | Op income | Net income | EBITDA | FCF | Total debt | Cash | Equity |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 2022 | $3.46B | $0.09B | **−$0.61B** | $0.96B | $0.29B | $2.64B | $0.42B | $5.82B |
| 2023 | $4.24B | $0.76B | $0.42B | $1.76B | $0.51B | $2.23B | $0.35B | $6.08B |
| 2024 | $5.15B | $1.50B | $0.95B | $2.68B | $1.37B | $1.44B | $0.61B | $6.86B |
| 2025 | $7.05B | $3.23B | **$2.39B** | $4.38B | $2.57B | $0.74B | $1.74B | $8.58B |

The pattern is textbook peak-cycle: revenue **+104%** and net income from a **−$605M loss (2022)
to +$2.39B (2025)** in three years, driven overwhelmingly by the **realized gold price**, not
by volume (production is ~flat at ~2 Moz). Kinross's **2025 realized price was $3,423/oz**;
**Q4 2025 realized $4,144/oz** (VERIFIED, FY2025 release). EBITDA more than **4.5×'d** while
production barely moved. **This is the definition of earnings on a commodity peak.**

### 2b. Gold-price context — at all-time highs
- Gold **broke $4,000/oz for the first time in October 2025**, up **>50% on the year** — the
  largest annual gain since 1979 (VERIFIED, Wikipedia "Gold as an investment", citing CME).
- **Mid-2026 spot ~$4,400/oz** (goldprice.org, June-2026 article reference; *confidence
  ASSUMED — could not read a live tick, but consistent with Q4-2025 $4,144 realized and rising*).
- So Kinross's earnings base is on gold at/near record levels. **Any low multiple here must be
  discounted as a peak-cycle multiple.**

### 2c. AISC & margin — the cost side is inflating too
VERIFIED (Kinross FY2025 release):
- FY2025 **AISC $1,571/oz**, production cost of sales $1,135/oz, realized $3,423/oz →
  **~$1,850/oz cash margin over AISC** (a record margin driven by price).
- **2026 guidance: AISC $1,730/oz** (up ~10% YoY), cost of sales $1,360/oz, capex $1.5B,
  production ~2.0 Moz ±5%. **Rising AISC is the tell** — cost inflation is eating some of the
  price gain, and if gold reverts, margins compress fast from both sides.
- **Three-year outlook: production flat at ~2.0 Moz ±5% for 2027 and 2028** (VERIFIED). So there
  is **no volume growth** in the base plan until Great Bear — the earnings lever is 100% price.

## 3. Quality

**Strong on current-cycle quality (VERIFIED):**
- **ROE 35.5%** (highest of the majors) — but note it is peak-margin-driven; on equity of $8.58B
  and NI $2.39B it's real, yet not durable through-cycle.
- **Deleveraging thesis CONFIRMED:** total debt **$2.64B (2022) → $0.74B (2025)**, cash up to
  $1.74B → **net cash ~$1.0B** (FY2025 release). Repaid $0.71–0.99B of debt every year 2022–25.
  Balance sheet is now a genuine strength.
- **FCF:** attributable FCF ~$2.5B in 2025 (~8.5% of KGC market cap); OCF $3.76B.
- **Capital return:** 2025 returned $752M ($152M dividends + $600M buybacks); **2026 dividend
  +14% to $0.04/qtr** (VERIFIED). Yield is low (~0.6%) — this is a **buyback/deleverage** story,
  not an income story, consistent with the project's total-return tilt.
- **Piotroski 7/7** (screen) — consistent with rising margins, rising OCF, falling leverage.
- **Jurisdiction:** exited Russia 2022 (de-risking, VERIFIED). Base now Americas + Mauritania;
  **Great Bear adds Tier-1 Canada** over time. Reasonable, not pristine (Tasiast/Mauritania,
  Paracatu/Brazil, La Coipa/Chile carry EM/political exposure).

**Quality caveats:** ROE/margins are cyclically inflated; **no organic volume growth** to 2028;
the 3.2× P/B says the market already credits the quality. Great Bear is the only real growth, and
it is capex + execution risk (§4).

## 4. Bear case / risks

1. **Gold-price sensitivity (the dominant risk).** Earnings are ~100% a function of price with
   flat volume and *rising* AISC. A revert from ~$4,000–4,400 toward $2,500 would collapse the
   $1,850/oz margin toward ~$800–900/oz — cutting EBITDA/FCF by more than half and re-rating the
   "10× P/E" into the 20s–30s. 2022 (net **loss** at ~$1,800 gold) is the recent proof point.
2. **Peak-cycle multiple trap.** Buying a commodity producer at a trough P/E on peak earnings is
   the classic value trap; the screen's cheapness signal is least reliable exactly here.
3. **Jurisdiction / geopolitical.** Tasiast (Mauritania) is a large single-country West-Africa
   asset; Paracatu (Brazil), La Coipa (Chile) add EM/political/royalty risk. Lower quality than
   Agnico's Canada/Australia-weighted base.
4. **Cost inflation.** 2026 AISC guided **+10% to $1,730/oz** — labor, energy, strip ratios rising;
   erodes margin even if gold holds.
5. **Great Bear execution.** ~**$1.4B** capex (Northern Miner), **PEA Sept 2024** (early-stage —
   not yet feasibility-locked), Ontario **fast-tracked Feb 2026** ("One Project One Process").
   Development miners routinely see capex overruns, permitting slips, and grade/recovery
   surprises. First-pour date and annual-production figures **UNVERIFIED this session** — flag.
6. **Reserve replacement.** Flat production to 2028 + ~2 Moz/yr depletion means Kinross must keep
   replacing reserves (Great Bear is the answer, but it's not producing yet). Reserve statement
   not verified this session (in the 40-F).

**Bull rebuttal (argue both sides):** Gold's drivers (central-bank buying, de-dollarization,
geopolitical risk, fiscal deficits) look structural, not a spike; if $4,000+ gold *persists*,
Kinross compounds FCF, finishes deleveraging, buys back stock, and delivers Great Bear into a
high-price environment — in which case 10× is genuinely cheap and the analyst ~55% upside
(consensus PT $37.94 vs $24.53, Moderate Buy, 11 buy / 3 hold / 0 sell, MarketBeat 2026-07-02)
is achievable. The debate is entirely **"is $4,000 gold the new normal or a cycle top?"**

## 5. Catalysts & Verdict

**Catalysts:** (1) gold price holding/rising; (2) continued buybacks + net-cash → net-cash-plus;
(3) Great Bear feasibility/permit milestones and first pour; (4) further dividend increases;
(5) potential re-rating toward Agnico's multiple if jurisdiction mix improves via Great Bear.

### Scorecard (weights: Cheapness 30 / Quality 30 / Trap 20 / Moat 15 / Catalyst 5)

| Dimension | Score 0–10 | Rationale |
|---|:--:|---|
| **Cheapness (30%)** | 5 | ~10× P/E, ~6–6.5× EV/EBITDA look cheap, BUT on **peak-gold earnings** — a low multiple on a cyclical high. Net-cash helps; P/B 3.2 rich. Discount heavily. |
| **Quality (30%)** | 7 | 35% ROE, net cash, deleveraged, 7/7 Piotroski, strong FCF, real capital return — but margins/ROE are cyclically inflated and volume is flat. |
| **Trap-risk / inverse (20%)** | 4 | HIGH peak-cycle trap risk: earnings ~100% price-driven, rising AISC, 2022 loss proves downside. Mitigant: net cash cushions a downturn. |
| **Moat & leadership (15%)** | 5 | Miners have no product moat; edge is asset quality + cost + balance sheet. Kinross is mid-cost ($1,571 AISC) — decent, below Agnico. Good management (Russia exit, deleveraging). |
| **Catalyst (5%)** | 7 | Great Bear growth, buybacks, dividend growth, gold momentum, ~55% analyst upside. |

**Weighted total = 5(0.30) + 7(0.30) + 4(0.20) + 5(0.15) + 7(0.05)**
= 1.50 + 2.10 + 0.80 + 0.75 + 0.35 = **6.4 / 10**

### VERDICT: **WATCH** (not BUY-worth-more-work; not a hard PASS)

A genuinely high-quality, well-managed, deleveraged gold major — but bought at ~10× **peak-gold**
earnings it is a cyclical bet on the gold price staying at record highs, not a mispriced
compounder. The cheapness signal that put it #3 in the screen is the least trustworthy kind
(low multiple × peak commodity earnings). **Own it only as a deliberate gold-price / macro-hedge
sleeve, sized as a cyclical, not as a "cheap quality compounder."**

### K.TO vs NEM (both in this finalist set)
**NEM is the better single pick** if you want *one* gold major, for a risk-adjusted holder:
- NEM is **larger and more diversified** (bigger asset base, copper by-product, less
  single-jurisdiction West-Africa concentration than Kinross's Tasiast weight).
- NEM's **lower P/B (2.96 vs 3.22)** and only slightly higher P/E (12.5 vs 10.4) mean you pay
  **less premium on peak book** — i.e. NEM's cheapness is a touch more defensible.
- Kinross wins on **ROE (35.5% vs 25.8%)**, **net cash / balance sheet**, and **screen cheapness**,
  and it has a cleaner Great-Bear growth story in Tier-1 Canada.
- **Take:** Kinross is the higher-torque, higher-quality-on-paper name; NEM is the lower-risk,
  more-diversified default. Neither escapes the peak-cycle problem — **if the portfolio holds
  gold at all, one major is enough; NEM is the safer core, Kinross the higher-beta alternative.**

### Top risk (one line)
**Gold-price reversion:** earnings/FCF are ~100% price-driven with rising AISC and flat volume —
a move from ~$4,000+ back toward mid-cycle ~$2,500 would more than halve FCF and turn today's
"cheap 10×" into an expensive 20–30× (2022's net loss is the proof).
