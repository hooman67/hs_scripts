## 1. Business & Thesis

**What ITC is.** ITC Ltd is an Indian conglomerate and **the dominant cigarette maker in India**
— "the largest cigarette manufacturer and seller in the country" (Screener.in company page,
2026-07-02, VERIFIED) and the **third-largest tobacco company in the world** (Wikipedia "ITC
Limited," 2026-07-02, ASSUMED — secondary source). Post the hotels demerger it operates roughly
five segments: **Cigarettes** (the cash cow), **FMCG-Others** (foods/Aashirvaad, Sunfeast,
Bingo, Yippee, personal care — #2 FMCG player in India per Wikipedia), **Agri Business**,
**Paperboards & Packaging**, and residual **IT/other**. The **Hotels** business was demerged
into a separately-listed entity in Jan 2025 (see below).

**The economic engine.** Cigarettes are ~40% of consolidated revenue but the large majority of
segment EBIT — India's oligopolistic, heavily-taxed cigarette market gives ITC extraordinary
pricing power and near-monopolistic share, throwing off cash that funds (a) a ~5% dividend, and
(b) the build-out of FMCG-Others.

> **Segment split — confidence.** ITC's exact FY2025/FY2026 segment revenue and EBIT split were
> **NOT retrievable** from reachable aggregators (they show consolidated totals only), and I did
> not reach ITC's segment filing directly. The widely-cited historical shape — **cigarettes ~40%
> of revenue but ~75–80% of segment profit; FMCG-Others large in revenue but low-single-digit to
> ~10% EBIT margin** — is **ASSUMED / UNVERIFIED** here and should be confirmed against ITC's
> FY2026 Annual Report segment note before it drives a SOTP. Flagged as a gap.

**The value thesis (bull).** ITC is a rare combination: a **high-ROIC (~39% ROCE), net-cash,
36%-operating-margin** staple, trading at **P/E ~17.5 / EV/EBITDA ~12.6** — a large discount to
Indian staples peers (HUL, Nestlé India at 30–50x EV/EBITDA; see §2) and only a modest premium
to global tobacco. You are paid a **~5% dividend** to wait, with the cigarette cash cow
subsidizing an FMCG business that is scaling toward better margins. The stock is **near its
52-week low (~32% off the high)** after a cigarette-tax-driven de-rating in early 2026 — i.e. a
quality compounder on a cyclical/regulatory sale. Beta is ~0 (defensive).

**Is the multiple reasonable for a high-ROIC staple?** Yes — arguably cheap on quality-adjusted
terms. A business earning ~29% ROE / ~39% ROCE with net cash, a wide moat, and a 5% yield would
command 25–35x in most staples markets; ITC at ~17.5x reflects (1) the perennial
tobacco-tax/regulation overhang, (2) the ESG "un-ownable" discount, and (3) a conglomerate
discount. The debate is whether those discounts are *permanent* (structural cap on the multiple)
or *cyclical* (re-rate as FMCG scales and tax shocks pass). **Both sides argued in §4/§5.**

**Does the hotels demerger change the story?** Modestly, positively. Hotels was a
capital-intensive, low-ROIC drag on the consolidated return profile and a favorite of the
"unlock value" bulls. Demerging it (completed 1 Jan 2025; ITC Hotels Ltd listed 29 Jan 2025 —
VERIFIED, see below) (a) **improves ITC's blended ROCE/asset turns**, (b) removes one leg of the
conglomerate discount, and (c) gives ITC shareholders a direct, separately-valued stake in
hotels. ITC retained ~40% of ITC Hotels, so it still consolidates some hotel exposure. Net: a
cleaner, higher-return "cigarettes + FMCG + paper/agri" ITC — a structural positive for the
multiple, though not a game-changer given hotels was a small profit contributor.

### 2b. Sum-of-the-parts & conglomerate discount

A rough SOTP framing (**directional only — segment EBIT split is ASSUMED/UNVERIFIED, so treat
as illustrative, not a target**):
- **Cigarettes** — highest-quality, ~75–80% of segment EBIT (ASSUMED). Deserves a premium
  tobacco multiple given India's growth vs Western decline; but capped by tax risk.
- **FMCG-Others** — a scaling #2 Indian FMCG franchise. If valued anywhere near HUL/Nestlé
  multiples (30–50x EV/EBITDA), even a thin-margin FMCG business is worth a large chunk of ITC's
  EV. This is the crux of the SOTP bull case: **the market values consolidated ITC at ~12.6x
  EV/EBITDA, but the FMCG leg alone would fetch a multiple of that if standalone.**
- **Hotels (demerged)** — now a separate line; ITC retains ~40% of ITC Hotels Ltd (~₹36,900 cr
  market cap for ITC Hotels, Finology 2026-07-02, VERIFIED → ITC's ~40% ≈ ~₹15,000 cr of value).
- **Paper/Agri** — steady, cyclical, modest multiple.

**Is there a conglomerate discount? Yes, plausibly.** ITC's blended EV/EBITDA (~12.6x) is well
below what a parts-summed valuation of its cigarette + FMCG legs would suggest, and no reachable
brokerage note *quantified* the discount in 2025–26 (SOTP articles were network-blocked;
UNVERIFIED). The hotels demerger was itself a partial de-conglomeration move that closes some of
the gap. **Bear counter:** a "discount" that has persisted for years despite repeated
unlock-value calls may not be a discount at all — it may be the correct price for
tax/ESG/capital-allocation risk (see §4).

## 3. Quality

Very high on the quality axis (the dominant Stage-1 filter):

- **Returns.** ROE **29.3%** (10-yr median 28%), ROCE **~38.9%** (Screener 2026-07-02, VERIFIED).
  Screen ROIC proxy ~45% (ASSUMED). These are elite, durable returns — hallmark of a wide-moat
  franchise.
- **Margins.** Operating margin **~36.4%**, net margin ~26% (yfinance 2026-07-02, VERIFIED) —
  extraordinary for a business that is largely consumer staples, driven by cigarette pricing power.
- **Balance sheet.** **Net cash ~₹221 bn** (cash ₹245 bn vs debt ₹24 bn; yfinance 2026-07-02,
  VERIFIED). Essentially debt-free — a fortress balance sheet.
- **Cash flow.** Operating cash flow ₹184.6 bn, free cash flow ₹163 bn (FY2026), with low capex
  (~₹22 bn) (yfinance/Screener, VERIFIED). Highly cash-generative; FCF comfortably funds the
  dividend.
- **Dividend track record.** Long-standing high payout; ~5% current yield (VERIFIED). Consistent
  large distributions signal capital discipline and confidence.
- **Cigarette pricing power + volume.** India's oligopoly + heavy taxation lets ITC pass through
  cost/tax as price. Historically volumes stay resilient because ITC can shift mix and take
  price; the **May 2026 ~17% price hike** (see §4) is the current test of that pricing power.
- **FMCG scaling economics.** FMCG consumer spend on ITC brands rose ~8.8% to ~₹37,000 cr in
  FY26, reaching ~28 cr homes (Groww, VERIFIED as reported). Margins are still thin vs HUL/Nestlé
  but improving with scale — the multi-year quality lever.

**Quality read:** genuinely one of the highest-quality names in the whole candidate set on
returns, margins, balance sheet, and cash generation. The only quality asterisk is FMCG margins
still being sub-scale (§4) and profit growth being modest recently (3-yr compounded profit growth
~3%, Screener VERIFIED — a flag).

## 4. Bear Case / Risks

**(1) Tobacco taxation / regulation — THE perennial overhang, and it just bit (2026).**
This is the single most important risk and it is *live right now*:
- India moved to **"GST 2.0"** at the 56th GST Council (3 Sept 2025), creating a **40% "sin/
  demerit" slab** for tobacco (PIB PRID 2163555, 3 Sept 2025, VERIFIED). Cigarettes initially
  **stayed at the old 28% + compensation cess** pending discharge of cess-loan obligations
  (PIB, 3 Sept 2025, VERIFIED).
- The transition was then executed via **additional excise duty effective 1 Feb 2026** — the
  **first excise increase in ~7 years** (Livemint, 2 Jan 2026, VERIFIED as reported; per-stick
  rupee figures rest on a single source, flagged). Specific new excise (per 1,000 sticks) ranged
  ~₹2,050 (short) to ~₹8,500 (longest) (Livemint 2 Jan 2026, single-source).
- **Market reaction was a clear net-negative:** stock fell ~5.5–8% on the Feb 1 notification;
  **six brokerages downgraded**; **Systematix cut revenue/EPS ~24%** and modeled cigarette
  **volumes down 8–10% in H1**; consumers face a **~17% cigarette price hike from May 2026**
  (MSN-rehosted / Livemint, ~Jan–Jun 2026, VERIFIED as reported). Nomura was less bearish on
  volumes. This is exactly the tax-shock de-rating that recurs in ITC's history — and is why the
  stock sits near a 52-week low.
- **Structural point:** India's tobacco tax incidence (~53%) is still below WHO's 75% target
  (Livemint 2 Jan 2026), so **further tax headroom exists** — the overhang never fully clears.

**(2) ESG exclusion — FLAG FOR HOOMAN (explicit).** ITC is a tobacco company. Many institutional
mandates, ESG funds, and personal exclusion lists **cannot or will not own tobacco** at all,
regardless of valuation. This is (a) a real, permanent structural cap on ITC's investor base and
multiple (part of *why* it looks cheap), and (b) **a values/mandate decision only Hooman can
make**. If tobacco is excludable for Hooman, ITC is a PASS on principle irrespective of its
score. **This must be an explicit yes/no before any action.** Argued both ways: the ESG discount
is also the source of the cheapness for those able to own it.

**(3) FMCG margins still thin.** The FMCG-Others growth story is real but margins remain
well below HUL/Nestlé; the SOTP bull case leans on FMCG re-rating that has been "coming" for
years. Recent consolidated profit growth is soft (3-yr compounded ~3%, Screener VERIFIED).

**(4) BAT ~23% stake overhang (potential further selldown).** BAT held ~28.5% of ITC, sold
~3.5% in **March 2024** (28.5%→25.5%, ~₹166.9 bn proceeds; BAT RNS 12–13 Mar 2024, VERIFIED),
then a further **~2.3% in May 2025** (→**~23.1%**; BAT RNS 27 May 2025, VERIFIED directly). BAT
is deleveraging and calls ITC "a core strategic component" with **no stated target for further
ITC-Limited selldown** (BAT RNS 27 May 2025, VERIFIED) — but the pattern of opportunistic block
sales is a recurring technical overhang on the shares. (Separately, BAT announced a sale of
7–15.3% of *ITC Hotels* in Dec 2025 — a different entity, does not touch ITC Ltd; BAT RNS 4 Dec
2025, VERIFIED.)

**(5) Currency.** INR-denominated. For a USD-based investor, INR depreciation vs USD erodes
returns; not hedged in this analysis (ASSUMED risk, not quantified).

**(6) Capital allocation / conglomerate.** ~₹20,000 cr capex and "new business creation" flagged
by the chairman (MSN ~Mar 2026, VERIFIED as reported) — value-accretive if disciplined, but
conglomerates carry reinvestment-quality risk. The hotels demerger is a mitigant.

## 5. Catalysts & Verdict

**Catalysts (potential re-rating triggers):**
- Cigarette **volume resilience** post the May 2026 ~17% price hike — if volumes hold, the
  Systematix-style bear estimates prove too harsh and the stock re-rates off the tax scare
  (near-term, most important).
- **FMCG margin expansion** continuing as scale builds (multi-year).
- **No further GST/excise shock** in the near term (the Feb 2026 hike may have front-loaded the
  pain; tax certainty is itself a catalyst).
- Further **de-conglomeration / value unlock** after the hotels demerger.
- ~5% dividend provides carry while waiting.

### Verdict: **WATCH → BUY-worth-more-work (conditional on the ESG decision)**

Score **7.4/10** places ITC firmly in the actionable range. **But the verdict is explicitly
gated on two things:**
1. **The tobacco/ESG decision is Hooman's to make.** If tobacco is excludable for Hooman on
   mandate/values grounds, ITC is a **PASS** regardless of the 7.4 score — this must be answered
   first.
2. If tobacco is investable, ITC is **BUY-worth-more-work**: a wide-moat, net-cash, ~29%-ROE
   staple yielding ~5%, near a 52-week low after a tax scare that may be over-discounted. The
   "more work" is: (a) confirm the **segment EBIT split** against ITC's FY2026 Annual Report
   (the SOTP hinges on it — currently ASSUMED/UNVERIFIED), (b) verify **global-tobacco multiples**
   for the cig-leg comparison (currently ASSUMED), and (c) track **cigarette volumes** through
   H1 FY2027 to see if the tax-hike volume hit is as bad as Systematix fears.

**Tax/ESG consideration (explicit, per instructions):** ITC's cheapness is inseparable from its
tobacco taxation and ESG discounts. The Feb 2026 excise hike (first in ~7 years) is a real
earnings cut and India retains tax headroom (~53% vs WHO 75%), so the overhang is structural, not
one-off. Simultaneously, tobacco exclusion permanently narrows ITC's investor base — which is
both *why* it is cheap and a reason many investors simply cannot own it.

**Top risk:** the **Indian cigarette tax/GST/excise regime** — a single-line, out-of-ITC's-control
policy decision can cut earnings and re-rate the stock (as it did in Feb 2026), and India still
has room to tax cigarettes harder.
