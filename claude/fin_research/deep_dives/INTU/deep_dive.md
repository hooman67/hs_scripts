## 1. Business & Thesis

**What it is.** Intuit is the dominant US small-business + consumer-finance software franchise:

- **Global Business Solutions (GBS) ≈ 55–60% of revenue** — QuickBooks (SMB accounting, the category
  standard in the US), QuickBooks Live/assisted bookkeeping, payments, payroll, and **Mailchimp**
  (email/CRM, the weak asset). Recurring, subscription, high-retention. Q3-FY26 GBS $3.3B, **+15% YoY**.
- **Consumer ≈ 28% of revenue** — **TurboTax** + TurboTax Live (assisted). Highly seasonal: the bulk
  of Consumer revenue lands in the **Feb–Apr tax quarter = fiscal Q3**. Q3-FY26 Consumer $5.3B (+8%),
  of which TurboTax $4.4B (**+7%**).
- **Credit Karma ≈ 8–10%** — consumer credit/fintech marketplace (lead-gen for cards/loans). Cyclical,
  ad/credit-market-sensitive. Q3-FY26 $631M, **+15%**.
- **ProTax** — software for professional accountants. ~$0.3B/qtr, low single-digit growth.

*(Segment figures: Intuit Q3 FY2026 release via StockTitan / stockanalysis.com, reported 2026-05-20 — **VERIFIED** on aggregate revenue $8.558B/+10%; segment splits **ASSUMED** from single-source republish.)*

**The classic thesis (GARP / quality-at-a-price):** wide-moat category leader (QuickBooks + TurboTax
are the default nouns for their tasks in the US), ~81% gross margins, ~38–44% ROIC ex-goodwill,
asset-light (capex <1% of revenue), strong FCF, growing double digits with an "AI-powered expert
platform" (Intuit Assist) upsell story. Historically the market paid **55–70x earnings** for that.

**The thesis TODAY (post-crash):** the compounder is intact operationally (still +10–18% revenue,
raised FY26 guidance) but the market has **repriced it from a premium-SaaS multiple to a GARP/utility
multiple** on two fears — (a) TurboTax is losing the price-sensitive DIY tax filer, and (b) AI agents
structurally disrupt SaaS. The bet is that these fears are **overshoot vs. the durable franchise**.

## 2. Valuation — IS IT ACTUALLY CHEAP?

### 2a. Vs. its own history — YES, dramatically de-rated (the strongest bull point)

|---|---|---|---|
| FY2021 | 70.1 | 50.1 | 15.0 |
| FY2022 | 62.7 | 40.0 | 10.1 |
| FY2023 | 60.8 | 37.1 | 10.0 |
| FY2024 | 62.1 | 41.6 | 11.1 |
| FY2025 | 57.4 | 38.6 | 11.6 |
| **NOW (2026-07-02)** | **~16.2** | **~11.4** | **~3.5** |

*(FY2021-25: stockanalysis.com/stocks/INTU/financials/ratios — **VERIFIED**.)*

This is the crux. INTU spent 2021–2025 at **57–70x P/E and 37–50x EV/EBITDA**. It now trades at
**~16x P/E and ~11x EV/EBITDA — roughly a 70–75% multiple compression**, near the lowest valuation
in its modern history. On its own history, INTU has **never** been this cheap as a public
double-digit grower. This is the answer to "is it just less-expensive-than-usual": **no — it is
outright cheap on an absolute AND relative-to-history basis, for the first time in over a decade.**

### 2b. Vs. peers — now CHEAPER than lower-growth peers (unusual)

| Company | P/E | Fwd P/E | Div yld | Rev growth (TTM) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **INTU** | **~16.2** | **~10.0** | 1.8% | **~10–17%** |
| ADP | 22.0 | 19.6 | 2.84% | +6.9% |
| Paychex (PAYX) | 21.3 | 17.5 | 4.57% | +16.9% |

*(ADP, PAYX: stockanalysis.com, 2026-07-02 — **VERIFIED**.)*

INTU now trades at a **discount to ADP and Paychex** — HR/payroll processors that grow slower (ADP
+6.9%) and lack INTU's ~81% gross margin. Historically INTU carried a **large premium** to these
names. A faster-growing, higher-margin, wider-moat franchise trading below its slower peers is the
textbook signature of either a fallen angel or a value trap. On a **forward** P/E of ~10x, INTU is
priced like a no-growth cash cow, not a 13–14% grower.

### 2c. Growth-adjusted (PEG / EV-EBIT vs growth)

- Trailing **PEG ≈ 0.68–0.69** (yfinance), i.e. P/E 16 against ~13–15% consensus EPS growth → **well
  below 1.0**, cheap on a growth-adjusted basis. **VERIFIED** (yfinance trailingPegRatio 0.69).
- Forward P/E ~10 on FY26 EPS guide of $23.80–23.85 (+~13%) → PEG well under 1. If INTU merely holds
  a 10x forward multiple and grows EPS mid-teens, the return is the earnings growth + any re-rating.

## 4. Bear Case / Risks (this is why the stock is down 62%)

**1. TurboTax pricing shock — the actual trigger (HIGH severity).** On the Q3-FY26 print
(2026-05-20, after close), INTU disclosed that despite a headline beat-and-raise, TurboTax "did not
have the overall tax season we expected." The 2026-season detail: **TurboTax Online units −~2%**,
paying units only +~2% (all growth from **ARPU +~11%**, i.e. mix-shift up, not volume), and **e-file
share −~1pt**. Management conceded it "lost on price" among the most price-sensitive DIY filers.
Stock fell **~20% the next day (2026-05-21)**. Simultaneously announced a **~17% workforce cut**
(~$300M+ restructuring). *(Reuters/Investing.com/CNBC headlines 2026-05-20; law-firm case pages;
StockTitan release — **VERIFIED via subagent**.)* → The premium moat narrative on TurboTax cracked:
if the only growth is price/mix while units shrink, that's a maturing/contested franchise, not a
compounder.

**2. Securities-fraud investigations (MEDIUM — overhang, not yet a fundamental).** ≥6 firms (BFA
Law, Schall, Bragar Eagel, KSF, Bronstein, Pomerantz) opened investigations alleging INTU
misrepresented TurboTax's price positioning/competitiveness before the 2026 season (KSF class period
**Dec 2, 2025 – May 20, 2026**). As of late-June-2026 these are **investigations/solicitations — no
filed complaint located**. *(Law-firm pages 2026 — **VERIFIED via subagent**.)* Litigation risk +
credibility damage; not yet a P&L event.

**3. "SaaSpocalypse" / GenAI disruption (HIGH, but debated).** The dominant macro overhang. Fear:
AI agents make tax prep and SMB bookkeeping cheap/free, collapsing TurboTax and QuickBooks moats.
Catalysts included Anthropic's "Claude Cowork" finance plugins (2026-03-05, INTU −11% that day) and
named AI-native accounting startup **Basis** (bookkeeping) + LLM tax help. Software forward multiples
sector-wide compressed from ~39x to ~21x; ADBE −42%, WDAY −39%, ZS −43% YTD. **Goldman's Sell thesis
explicitly models AI gutting TurboTax revenue by 2030.** *(Built In 2026-03-05; Benzinga 2026-06-14;
Goldman via subagent — **VERIFIED**.)* → This is the reason a 16x P/E might be a *value trap*: if AI
structurally impairs both flagships, the DCF's growth assumption is wrong and "cheap" gets cheaper.

**4. Valuation margin of safety is smaller than the DCF implies (MEDIUM).** The $271 vs ~$560 bear-DCF
gap only exists if FCF grows. The market's ~9% FCF yield + forward P/E of 10 says investors are
pricing flat-to-declining FCF. If growth slows to low-single-digits, "fair value" converges toward
the current price, and the "72% de-rating = bargain" argument weakens.

**5. Credit Karma cyclicality (LOW-MEDIUM).** ~8–10% of revenue, tied to consumer credit/lending
cycles and card-issuer ad budgets — can swing hard in a downturn. Currently +15%, so not a live drag.

**6. Mailchimp weakness (LOW).** Repeatedly flagged as the underperforming asset (sub-double-digit
growth since acquisition) — a persistent drag inside an otherwise strong GBS segment.

**7. IRS Direct File — RESOLVED IN INTU'S FAVOR (was a top brief risk, now a POSITIVE).** The brief
listed IRS Direct File / free-filing as a headline threat. **The Trump administration KILLED Direct
File**: the IRS notified 25 partner states in Nov-2025 that "Direct File will not be available in
Filing Season 2026" with "no launch date set for the future." *(USA Today / Federal News Network /
WSJ, Nov-2025 — **VERIFIED via subagent**.)* → The government-free-filing overhang that hurt the 2024–25
narrative is **removed**. Notably, the stock STILL fell ~62% — proving the pain is pricing/AI, not
Direct File. This is a genuine bull data point the brief's premise had backwards.

## 5. Catalysts & Verdict

### Catalysts (bull)
- **Q4 FY26 / FY27 guidance (Aug-2026):** if GBS stays +15%+ and TurboTax stabilizes, the "franchise
  broken" thesis is refuted → re-rating from 10x forward.
- **Multiple recovery:** at 10x forward P/E vs a 57–70x history, even a partial re-rate to ~15–20x on
  mid-teens EPS growth is a large return; consensus PT ~$498 implies ~80% upside if realized.
- **AI monetization proof:** TurboTax Live / QuickBooks Live continuing to grow fast would flip the
  AI narrative from threat to tailwind (Intuit Assist upsell + Anthropic/OpenAI distribution).
- **Aggressive buybacks** into the drawdown (already $3.34B in 9 mo) compound per-share value if the
  franchise holds.

### Catalysts (bear)
- 2027 tax season shows further TurboTax unit erosion / continued low-end share loss.
- A filed securities class action + adverse disclosures.
- Any QuickBooks growth deceleration = the AI-disruption thesis validated on the crown jewel.

### VERDICT: **BUY — worth more work** (high-conviction, high-variance)

INTU scores **7.65/10** — the highest-quality franchise in the run, now genuinely cheap for the first
time in a decade, but riding a live disruption narrative that makes it a **barbell, not a sleeper**.
The setup is a classic fallen-angel: a moaty compounder de-rated 70% on a real-but-possibly-overshot
fear. If the AI/pricing fears are cyclical/overshoot, the risk/reward (DCF ~$560–750 vs $271, PEG
~0.7) is exceptional. If AI structurally impairs TurboTax + QuickBooks, "cheap" is a trap and the
DCF's growth assumption is wrong. **Recommend promoting to the finalist deep-comparison as a HIGH-
CONVICTION-BUT-CONTESTED name — this is the single most asymmetric idea in the run, contingent on a
judgment call about AI's threat to the two flagships.** The "more work" needed: (a) scrub the FY27
guide when it drops (Aug-2026), (b) form an independent view on AI-native bookkeeping/tax threat
(the whole bear case), (c) monitor whether a class action is filed and its class period.
