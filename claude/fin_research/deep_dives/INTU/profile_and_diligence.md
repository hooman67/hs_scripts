# PART A — BUSINESS PRIMER (for a newcomer)

## A1. What Intuit does — plain language

Intuit sells **financial software subscriptions** to two big audiences: **small businesses** and
**consumers/tax filers**. Four product franchises (the pre-FY26 four-segment view, with **VERIFIED FY2025
revenue** from the 10-K, CIK 0000896878, filed 2025-09-03):

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **QuickBooks** *(Global Business Solutions / GBS)* | The default US **small-business accounting** software — bookkeeping, invoicing, plus bolt-ons: **payments** (card processing), **payroll**, **capital** (lending), and **Mailchimp** (email marketing). This is the growth + margin engine. | **$11,077M** | **58.8%** | Recurring subscription; the moat |
| **TurboTax** *(Consumer)* | #1 **do-it-yourself US tax-prep** software; plus **TurboTax Live** (a human expert reviews/does your return). | **$4,870M** | **25.9%** | Highly **seasonal** (Feb–Apr) |

**The one number that matters most — the "Online Ecosystem."** Inside GBS, the piece investors watch is the

whole company.

one quarter — vs. only **13.7% in Q1** and **18.4% in Q2**. *(Derived from EDGAR cumulative op-income $534M / $1,389M
/ $5,409M and revenue $3,885M / $8,536M / $17,094M — **VERIFIED (my calc)**.)* Never annualize a single Intuit quarter.

## A2. Management & strategy

- **CEO: Sasan Goodarzi** — became CEO **January 2019**; also **Chairman** now. A long-tenured insider (joined ~2004;
  previously ran the Small Business group and TurboTax) — an internal-promotion continuity CEO, not a turnaround hire.
  *(CEO-since-2019 & chairman: confirmed in the Q3-FY26 press release byline "Sasan Goodarzi, chairman…" — **VERIFIED**;
  tenure detail cross-referenced in `deep_dive.md`.)*
- **CFO: Sandeep Aujla.** *(Q3-FY26 release — VERIFIED.)*
- **Strategy — "AI-driven expert platform."** Goodarzi's central bet: embed generative AI (**Intuit Assist**) across
  every product to (a) automate the grunt-work and (b) **upsell users into higher-priced "assisted" tiers**
  (TurboTax **Live**, QuickBooks **Live** bookkeeping) where a human + AI does the work. His own framing in the
  Q3-FY26 release: *"disrupting the assisted tax segment, expanding our money portfolio, and serving mid-market
  businesses growing north of 30 percent."* *(Q3-FY26 press release — **VERIFIED**.)* The whole bull/bear debate is
  whether AI is a **channel** (upsell, as management claims) or a **threat** (commoditizes tax/bookkeeping) — that is
  the separate theme agent's job.
- **Founder / culture heritage.** Co-founded **1983** by **Scott Cook** (ex-P&G) and **Tom Proulx** (Stanford
  programmer) around **Quicken**. *(Wikipedia — VERIFIED.)* Cook remains on the board (chair of the executive
  committee). Intuit's operating culture traces to the late **Bill Campbell ("the Coach," Intuit CEO 1994–98, chairman
  to 2016)** — the famed Silicon Valley leadership mentor. This is a founder-influenced, long-horizon culture, which
  colors the aggressive capital-return + "Big Bets" posture below.
- **Capital allocation (see A3/B1) is a strategy signal:** management is **leaning into the 66% drawdown** — new **$8B
  buyback authorization** + a **15% dividend hike** announced with the very print that cratered the stock. Either
  conviction, or a bet that could destroy value if the franchise is impaired.

## A3. Business model & moat

- **SaaS economics.** ~**81% gross margin** (yfinance grossMargins ≈0.81 — VERIFIED-ish; note Intuit's income
  statement does not print a "gross profit" line, so this is a derived figure). **Capex <1% of revenue** ($84M on
  $18,831M in FY2025 — EDGAR VERIFIED) → asset-light, cash-generative. FY2025 **operating cash flow $6.21B**;
  9-month FY26 OCF already **$7.51B** (EDGAR VERIFIED).
- **Switching costs (the real moat).** For a small business, QuickBooks holds the books, payroll, payments, and tax
  data — ripping it out mid-life is painful and risky. Critically, **accountants are trained on and recommend
  QuickBooks**, creating a professional-network lock-in that compounds the SMB stickiness. TurboTax has weaker lock-in
  (you can switch tax software year to year) but a dominant **brand** and prior-year data carryover.
- **Pricing power — strong in QuickBooks, newly *contested* in TurboTax.** The FY2026 tax season exposed the flank:
  guidance says **TurboTax Online total units −~2%**, paying units only **+2%**, all growth from **ARPU +~11%** (mix-up
  into assisted/faster-refund tiers), and **e-file share −~1 point**. *(Q3-FY26 release — VERIFIED, see B1.)* Translation:
  Intuit is **losing the price-sensitive low-end DIY filer** and holding revenue only by charging the up-market filer more.
- **Tax-prep quasi-duopoly + a removed threat.** US consumer DIY tax is effectively **TurboTax vs. H&R Block**, with the
  **IRS Direct File** free-filing program **killed for the 2026 season** (Trump administration, ~Nov-2025) — a genuine
  *positive* that removes a top-cited overhang. *(Verified in `deep_dive.md`; theme agent tracking.)*
- **Rule-of-40 (a SaaS quality test = revenue growth % + profit margin % ≥ 40).** Revenue growth ~13–14% + FCF margin
  ~30% ≈ **43–47% → passes comfortably.** *(Derived — VERIFIED-ish.)*

## B2. Valuation now vs. history — the de-rating confirmed

INTU trades at ~**16× trailing GAAP EPS** today. To put a hard EDGAR number on "trailing": TTM diluted GAAP EPS through
Q3-FY26 = FY2025 $13.67 − 9-mo-FY25 $12.33 + 9-mo-FY26 $15.05 = **$16.39**, so **P/E = $271 / $16.39 ≈ 16.5×** (or ~16.8×
at $275). *(Components all EDGAR-VERIFIED; my calc.)*

**Versus its own 5-year history — dramatically cheaper (this is the core "value" fact):**

| FY2022 | 62.7 | 40.0 | 10.1 |
| FY2023 | 60.8 | 37.1 | 10.0 |
| FY2024 | 62.1 | 41.6 | 11.1 |
| FY2025 | 57.4 | 38.6 | 11.6 |
| **NOW (2026-07)** | **~16.5** | **~11.4** | **~3.5** |

*(FY21–25: stockanalysis.com ratios — VERIFIED in `deep_dive.md`; NOW: my calc / yfinance — VERIFIED.)*

- **~70–75% multiple compression** vs. its 2021–25 norm of **57–70× P/E and 37–50× EV/EBITDA**. INTU has **never** been
  this cheap as a public double-digit grower.
- **Forward P/E ≈ 11.4×** on the FY26 **non-GAAP** guide ($23.80–23.85) — or **~10×** as the existing deep dive frames it
  on slightly higher forward estimates. Either way, priced like a low-growth cash cow, not a mid-teens grower.
- **FCF yield ≈ 8–9%** on market cap (FCF ~$6–7B / ~$74B cap) — high for a compounder; the market is pricing flat/declining FCF.
- **PEG ≈ 0.7** (trailing) — cheap on a growth-adjusted basis.

**Bottom line:** the de-rating is **real and verified** (not a data artifact — shares 273.5M, no split; see `deep_dive.md`
§0). The debate is entirely about **durability of FCF**, not about whether it's statistically cheap. It is.

## B4. What to watch (diligence checklist)

1. **TurboTax pricing/volume (the #1 fundamental watch-item).** The FY26 season showed **units −2%, ARPU +11%,
   e-file share −1pt** — growth is now price/mix, not volume. Watch the **FY2027 tax season** (reported in fiscal
   Q3, ~May-2027): does low-end unit erosion continue or stabilize? If units keep falling, "TurboTax is a maturing/
   contested franchise" hardens. *(Q3-FY26 release — VERIFIED.)*
2. **QuickBooks / Online Ecosystem growth (the crown jewel).** Online Ecosystem +19% (ex-Mailchimp +22%) and mid-market
   "growing north of 30%" is the growth engine. **Any deceleration here** = the AI-disruption thesis validated on the
   most important asset. Watch Online Ecosystem YoY and QuickBooks Online Accounting each quarter. *(VERIFIED.)*
3. **IRS Direct File — resolved in INTU's favor.** Killed for the 2026 season (~Nov-2025). Watch for any 2027 revival
   under a future administration; for now it is a *removed* threat, a bull data point. *(Verified in `deep_dive.md`.)*
4. **Credit Karma cyclicality.** ~12% of FY25 revenue, tied to consumer-credit/lending cycles and card-issuer ad
   budgets; currently strong (+15–19%) but the first thing to crack in a consumer-credit downturn. *(EDGAR/release — VERIFIED.)*
5. **The AI question (defer to theme agent).** Does generative AI commoditize DIY tax + SMB bookkeeping (bear), or is it
   an upsell channel into TurboTax Live / QuickBooks Live (Intuit's claim)? This is the swing factor for the whole thesis

6. **Execution on the 17% headcount cut** + whether the aggressive **$8B buyback** compounds per-share value (bull) or
