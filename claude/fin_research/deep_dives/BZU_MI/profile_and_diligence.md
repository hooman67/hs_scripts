## PART A — BUSINESS PRIMER (for a newcomer)

### A1. What Buzzi does (plain language)

Buzzi is a **heavy building-materials maker**: it digs up limestone, bakes it into **cement**,
and sells cement plus two related products — **ready-mix concrete** (cement + sand + gravel +
water, mixed and delivered wet in those rotating-drum trucks) and **aggregates** (crushed
stone, sand and gravel — the bulk filler in concrete and roadbeds). Cement is the core and the
profit driver; concrete and aggregates are the "downstream" businesses that consume the cement.

- **Scale:** operations in **~13 countries**, roughly **10,000 employees**, HQ in Casale
  Monferrato, Italy. Listed on the **FTSE MIB** (Italy's blue-chip index) as BZU.MI.
  [buzzi.com/our-operations, as-of 2026-07 — VERIFIED]
- **History / name:** family firm founded 1907; long known as **Buzzi Unicem**; **renamed
  "Buzzi S.p.A." in 2023**. Its US business is still widely called **Buzzi Unicem USA**
  (rooted in the historical Lone Star Industries / RC Lonestar acquisitions). [VERIFIED — company history]

**Where the PROFIT comes from — the single most important fact for a newcomer.**
Buzzi's revenue is spread across Europe and the Americas, but **profit is heavily US-weighted**.

### A2. Ownership & management (the family control question)

- **The Buzzi family controls the company.** They hold **~53.0% of the economic capital** via a
  holding chain (Fimedi → Presa). [FY2025 corporate-governance report — VERIFIED]
- **The voting wedge — critical for a minority holder.** Buzzi has a **"loyalty double-voting"
  mechanism**: shares held continuously for a qualifying period earn a **second vote**. That
  mechanism **vested on 2026-06-01 and lifts the family to ~69.18% of VOTING rights** while they
  still own only ~53% of the economics. [FY2025 CG report / voting communications 2026-06 — VERIFIED]
  - *Plain language:* the family puts up ~half the money but controls ~69% of the votes. There is
    **no separate super-voting share class** — the wedge comes purely from the loyalty-vote scheme.
- **Management is family-run.** CEO **Pietro Buzzi** also chairs both upstream holding companies
  (Fimedi and Presa) — a **related-party overlap** flagged in the deep dive. [VERIFIED]
- **Capital-allocation culture:** conservative. Low dividend payout (~14%), through-cycle
  deleveraging, cash hoarding over aggressive distribution. Historically a *plus* for durability.
- **Hidden asset the family sits on:** the 33.33% **Mexico JV (Corporación Moctezuma)** is carried
  at **~€266m book** but the **listed-market fair value of the stake is ~€1,192m** — value well
  above what the balance sheet shows. [FY2025 AR Notes 14/24 — VERIFIED]

**Governance implication for a minority holder (mixed).** *Positive:* an owner-operator family
with skin in the game and a multi-generation horizon has produced the conservative, net-cash balance
sheet that makes Buzzi unusual. *Negative:* the **69%-votes-vs-53%-economics wedge** means minorities
can be outvoted on any matter (M&A, related-party deals, buy-in of the rest of the company at a price
the family sets). This is a **minority-protection red flag** and part of why the stock may carry a
persistent discount (a "governance discount").

### A3. Business model & moat (why cement is special, and why Buzzi is unusual)

**Cement is a local / regional business with a freight moat.** Cement is cheap per tonne but
**heavy** (very low value-to-weight). Trucking it more than ~a few hundred km costs more than the
cement is worth, so each plant effectively serves a **regional market**. That gives incumbents
**regional pricing power** — you can't economically import cement from a low-cost region far away,
so local producers set local prices. (This is the classic "moat" in cement: geography + the cost
and permitting difficulty of building a new plant/quarry nearby.)

**The offsetting realities:**
- **Cyclical.** Demand tracks **construction** (housing, infrastructure, commercial building), which
  swings with interest rates, government spending and the economy. Volumes fall in downturns.
- **Energy-intensive.** Making clinker (the reactive core of cement) requires heating limestone to
  ~1,450°C, burning a lot of fuel (gas, coal, petcoke). **Fuel is a big, volatile cost.**
- **Carbon-cost-intensive in the EU.** ~60% of cement's CO₂ comes from the *chemistry* of limestone
  (unavoidable "process" emissions), not just fuel. In Europe this now carries a rising cash cost
  (see B7 on **EU ETS**).

**Why Buzzi's balance sheet is the standout.** Cement peers are typically **debt-laden** — the
industry consolidated via leveraged M&A (Holcim, Heidelberg, CRH, Cemex all carry $4–20B of net
debt). Buzzi is the opposite: it runs **NET CASH** — more cash than total debt.

- FY2025 **net financial position = +€1,130.9m net cash** (up from +€755.2m a year earlier); this
  compares with **~€1.3B of net *debt* back in 2022** — Buzzi deleveraged straight through the cycle.
  [Buzzi FY2025 results 2026-03-31 — VERIFIED]
- (Jargon: **net financial position / NFP** = total cash − total financial debt. A **positive** NFP
  means net cash; a negative one means net debt.)
- Consequence: Buzzi's **enterprise value (EV) is *below* its market cap** (EV = market cap + net
  debt − cash; with net cash it subtracts). That's why the EV/EBITDA multiple (~6×) looks even
  cheaper than the P/E — the market is effectively getting the operations for less than the sticker.

**The cyclical question (is the margin at a peak?).** This is the crux. Two competing readings:
- **Bear:** the **27.3% margin still embeds the 2022–24 pricing windfall** (vs 22.3% in 2022). If
  prices soften while energy stays cheap and volumes stay weak, margin could mean-revert toward the
  **mid-20s%**, which would raise the *normalized* P/E meaningfully above the headline ~9× — i.e. the
  stock is cheaper on *this year's* elevated margin than on a mid-cycle one.
- **Bull (the deep dive's nuance):** this is **NOT a classic top-of-cycle setup by VOLUME**. Buzzi's
  biggest region (US, ~47% of profit) is **past its volume peak and troughing into 2026** with prices
  holding near records; Europe (~24%) is **depressed/bottoming**, i.e. *below* mid-cycle. So the risk
  is **margin normalization, not a volume cliff off a peak.** [US: USGS MCS 2025 + FRED TTLCONS + BLS
  PPI cement — VERIFIED in deep dive; ACA 2026-trough forecast — UNVERIFIED magnitude.]

**Synthesis:** margins are **cyclically elevated (not peak)**; volumes are near/below mid-cycle. The
honest framing is *"a cheap cyclical whose price is normal-to-low and whose margin is somewhat high."*
Build a **normalized-margin (mid-20s%) earnings estimate** before treating the ~9× P/E at face value.

### B7. What to watch (the seven that move the thesis)

1. **US construction cycle (the profit engine, ~47% of EBITDA).** This *is* the thesis. Watch US
   cement consumption (~110 Mt, down from ~114 Mt in 2022), construction spending (FRED **TTLCONS**,
   peaked ~$2.26T Jan-2025, −2.9% YoY by early 2026), and the **IIJA infrastructure funding
   reauthorization** (an open cliff — US makers lobbied Congress June-2026). Bull case = 2026 volume
   trough → 2027 recovery re-rates the profit engine. [USGS MCS 2025 / FRED / World Cement — VERIFIED;
   ACA trough magnitude — UNVERIFIED.]
2. **EU ETS carbon-cost phase-out (structural, decade-long EU-margin drag).** *Plain language:* the
   **EU Emissions Trading System** makes companies pay per tonne of CO₂ they emit. Cement historically
   got most of its allowances **free**; those free allowances now **phase out on a fixed schedule to
   ZERO by 2034** (−2.5% in 2026, −48.5% by 2030, **−100% by 2034**). Carbon price ≈ **€79.6/t (2026-07-02)**.
   Cement emits ~0.6–0.8 t CO₂/t, so at full phase-out this is a **~€48–64/t cement cost** — potentially
   comparable to product value on the EU legs. **CBAM** (an import carbon tariff, definitive from
   2026-01-01) protects the EU *domestic* market but **not exports**. Bites **only ~24% of EBITDA
   (Italy+Germany)** — real but diluted by the ~63% non-EU (US/Mexico/Brazil) profit mix.
   [EU Parliament EPRS / ICAP / tradingeconomics carbon — VERIFIED.]
3. **Energy costs.** Fuel (gas/coal/petcoke) is a big, volatile input. EU gas/power well below the
   2022 peak = a **margin tailwind into 2026** — but it also means the 2023-era pricing windfall may
   erode (a soft-price risk). [Directional — UNVERIFIED on 2026 price points.]
4. **Russia exposure.** SLK Cement (100% via Dyckerhoff) is **still fully consolidated** — **NOT sold**
   — with **€496.8m net assets** and €76.7m FY2025 EBITDA, despite "ceasing operational involvement"
   in May-2022. Sanctions / impairment / forced-sale risk; excluded from the decarbonization roadmap.
   [FY2025 AR Note 5 — VERIFIED.] Watch for any sale (would remove an overhang) or write-down.
5. **Milan-listing liquidity / index weight.** Thinner trading and a home-bias discount vs the US/German
   majors — a real reason the cheapness may *persist* (a "value-trap-lite" risk), not just an opportunity.
6. **Family governance.** The **69.18% votes vs 53% economics** loyalty-vote wedge (vested 2026-06-01)
   plus the CEO chairing both holdcos means minorities can be outvoted (M&A, related-party, a buy-in of
   minorities at a family-set price). Watch related-party dealings and any change to the voting structure.
7. **FX.** ~63% of EBITDA is **non-EUR** (USD, MXN, BRL, RUB). A strong euro compresses reported results
   (FY2025 US sales were −7% *reported* vs only −2.9% at constant FX). [Buzzi FY2025 — VERIFIED.]
