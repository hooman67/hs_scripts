## 3. The family-control question (69% votes / 53% economics)

Hooman asked specifically how big a deal this is. Breaking it into the pieces that matter:

### 3a. The mechanics (VERIFIED)
- Family holds **53.037% of the economics** but **69.182% of the votes**, via a **loyalty double-vote**
  (voto maggiorato) that vested 1 Jun 2026 — legal, common in Italy/France, and *earned* by long
  holding, not a special share class handed out at IPO. Any minority holder can *also* register for the
  double vote after 24 months — but the family's head start plus its 53% base means it will always
  dominate.

### 3b. Minority-shareholder risks (the bear points)
1. **Minorities can be outvoted on everything** — board election (9 of 10 seats already come from the
   family list), mergers, related-party approvals, changes to the bylaws. The 69% is above the 2/3
   supermajority thresholds that matter under Italian law.
2. **Takeover premium is forgone.** With 69% of votes locked, **no unsolicited bid can succeed** — you
   will almost certainly never receive a control premium for your shares. The optionality that CRH/Eagle
   holders enjoy (a strategic could bid) is off the table.
3. **Related-party overlap.** CEO Pietro Buzzi chairs both holdcos (Fimedi/Presa); the chain is
   Fimedi→Presa→Buzzi. Any dealings between Buzzi and family entities need scrutiny (though none are
   flagged as abusive in the filings this session).
4. **The wedge can *widen* the discount, not just cap it** — a Milan-listed, family-controlled name is
   exactly the kind of stock that can *stay* cheap (value-trap-lite), because the marginal buyer
   (index funds, takeover arbs, activists) is structurally absent.

### 3c. The family's ACTUAL track record with minorities (the green flags)
This is the crux, and it's **favorable**:
- **Capital discipline → fortress balance sheet.** The family ran the company from ~€1.3B net *debt*
  (2022) to **+€1.13B net cash** (FY2025), deleveraging *through* the cycle. That conservatism protects
  *all* shareholders in a downturn. [VERIFIED, `deep_dive.md`]
- **No empire-building / value-destructive M&A.** Bolt-ons (Brazil buy-in) funded from cash, not
  dilutive equity or reckless leverage. Contrast with the industry's history of leveraged mega-mergers.
- **Returns to shareholders, growing:** DPS €0.60 (2024) → **€0.70 (2025, +17%)**; low ~14% payout
  leaves room to grow; **plus a €200m buyback-for-cancellation** launched Feb-2026 (~7% treasury being
  cancelled). Buybacks-for-cancellation are *pro-minority* — they shrink the share count and raise every
  remaining holder's per-share claim, rather than the family quietly accumulating. [VERIFIED]
- **No history of squeeze-outs, dilutive rights issues at depressed prices, or minority-abusive related-
  party transfers** surfaced in the filings.
- **Alignment:** the family has ~53% of the economics — it eats its own cooking; value destruction hits
  them hardest. A multi-generation owner-operator with a net-cash balance sheet is the *good* version of
  concentrated control.

### 3d. Vs CRH/Eagle governance
CRH and Eagle have **dispersed ownership, no controlling family, one-share-one-vote, full board
accountability to the market, and live takeover optionality.** On a pure governance/minority-rights
scorecard they are cleaner. That cleanliness is part of what their premium multiple pays for.

### 3e. Net verdict on family control: **DISCOUNT-JUSTIFIER, not a dealbreaker.**
The 69/53 wedge is a **real, permanent minority-protection risk** (no takeover premium, can be outvoted)
and a **legitimate reason for a persistent valuation discount** — so it is *not* an "overblown worry."
But it is **not a dealbreaker**, because the family's *actual conduct* toward minorities has been
exemplary for the industry: conservative leverage, growing dividend, buybacks-for-cancellation, no
empire-building, high alignment. **The same control that caps your upside optionality is what built the
fortress balance sheet that is the entire quality case.** Price it in (it's part of *why* the stock is
cheap), don't let it veto the position. Watch: any related-party transaction, any equity raise below
book, any move toward taking minorities out at a family-set price, or any change to the voting structure.
