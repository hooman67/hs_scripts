## 1. Business & thesis

**Business.** Diageo is the world's largest premium-spirits company by value: **Johnnie Walker**
(Scotch), **Guinness** (stout — the standout grower), **Don Julio** and **Casamigos** (tequila),
**Smirnoff**, **Captain Morgan**, **Tanqueray**, **Baileys**, **Crown Royal**, plus a ~34% stake
in Moët Hennessy is NOT held (that's LVMH — Diageo's premium sits in-house). FY2025 net sales
**$20,245m** across ~180 markets (FY25 prelim, 5 Aug 2025 —
https://www.diageo.com/en/news-and-media/press-releases/2025/2025-preliminary-results-year-ended-30-june-2025).
North America is the largest region and profit engine; the "Rest of World"/emerging markets
(India via United Spirits, Africa, LatAm) are the growth engine. **VERIFIED.**

**Value thesis (bull).** A best-in-class consumer-staples compounder — 60% gross margin, ~28-31%
operating margin, deep brand moat, pricing power, a 250-year Scotch aging moat — has **de-rated
−62.8% from its Jan-2022 peak** (4,098p → 1,524p; yfinance, VERIFIED) to **12.6x forward
earnings and 9.2x EV/EBITDA** — multiples this business has not traded at in over a decade.
If the current spirits weakness is a **post-COVID inventory/demand air-pocket** rather than a
permanent demand impairment, patient buyers are getting a wide-moat compounder at a
once-in-a-cycle price.

### THE CRUX: cyclical opportunity or structural threat?

This is the whole call. My read, from the evidence in §4: **predominantly cyclical, with a
genuine — but currently over-hyped — structural overlay.** Argued both sides:

- **Cyclical (opportunity) — the stronger-supported view.** IWSR states plainly that *"much of
  the recent decline is cyclical, not structural"* (IWSR, 26 Jun 2025 —
  https://www.theiwsr.com/insight/press-release/gen-z-not-the-generation-of-moderation-survey-reveals/).
  The 2023-24 collapse was driven by (a) **post-COVID channel over-stocking then destocking**
  (US/LatAm distributors had over-ordered in 2021-22; Diageo's Nov-2023 profit warning was an
  explicit LatAm channel-inventory unwind — https://www.just-drinks.com/news/latin-america-to-hit-h1-profits-diageo-warns/)
  and (b) **cost-of-living downtrading** by an inflation-pressured consumer. Both self-correct.
  By FY25 Diageo's US inventory was "appropriate" and depletions/shipments had realigned. **VERIFIED.**
- **Structural (trap) — real but smaller than the narrative.** Global total-beverage-alcohol
  volume has now fallen **three straight years (2023 −1%, 2024 −1%, 2025 −2%)** and — the worrying
  new signal — in 2025 **value fell faster (−4%) than volume (−2%)**, i.e. premiumization (the
  historic offset) *stalled* (IWSR, 2 Apr 2026 —
  https://www.thespiritsbusiness.com/2026/04/iwsr-spirits-worst-performing-category-in-2025/).
  Health-attitude shift is durable: **53% of Americans now say moderate drinking is bad for
  health** (Gallup, 13 Aug 2025 — https://news.gallup.com/poll/693362/five-facts-drinking.aspx).
  See §4 for GLP-1. **VERIFIED.**
- **The tell that it's *not purely* cyclical:** IWSR pushed its "return to growth" forecast from
  *2025* (as of Jun 2024) out to **2031** (as of Jun 2026 —
  https://www.theiwsr.com/insight/press-release/global-beverage-alcohol-to-drop-further-before-recovering/).
  The weakness has outlasted a clean destocking story. This is why the stock is a WATCH, not a BUY.

## 3. Quality

- **Returns.** ROE (FY25) ~**19.7%** (yf) / 21.2% (computed NI $2,354m ÷ equity $11,090m).
  ROIC proxy **~12.8%** on before-exceptional operating profit (NOPAT ~$4.45bn ÷ invested capital
  $34.8bn, 22% tax), or ~9.7% on the impairment-hit reported number. Solidly above cost of capital.
  **VERIFIED** (computed from yf statement values that match FY25 prelim). Note ROIC has drifted
  down as debt-funded M&A (Casamigos $1bn, Aviation/Don Julio, Don Papa) inflated invested capital.
- **Margins.** Gross **60.0%**; operating margin **28.2% before exceptionals** in FY25 (reported
  21.4% after a ~$1.4bn impairment/restructuring charge). Margin trend: FY22 28.7% → FY23 27.0%
  → FY24 29.6% → FY25 21.4% reported (28.2% clean). The reported dip is a one-off write-down, not
  operating collapse. **VERIFIED** (FY25 prelim).
- **Moat & pricing power.** Textbook wide moat: #1 global spirits share, irreplaceable brands
  (Johnnie Walker, Guinness, Don Julio), and a **structural Scotch-aging moat** (12/18-yr stock
  laid down years ago = a barrier no new entrant can leap). Historically strong pricing power —
  though the FY26 data shows pricing power is *currently* capped: H1 FY26 price/mix was **−1.9%**
  as the company had to lean on promotion (H1 FY26, 25 Feb 2026 —
  https://www.diageo.com/en/news-and-media/press-releases/2026/2026-interim-results-half-year-ended-31-december-2025). **VERIFIED.**
- **Free cash flow.** FY25 FCF **$2,748m** (up $139m yoy) — genuinely cash-generative; FY26 guide
  reiterated at **~$3bn** (Q1/H1 FY26). This is the balance-sheet backstop. **VERIFIED.**
- **Dividend track record — the streak just broke.** Diageo was a ~25-year progressive raiser
  (post-1997 GrandMet/Guinness merger). **FY2025: dividend HELD FLAT at 103.48c** (unchanged vs
  FY24 — ending the increase streak). **H1 FY2026: CUT — interim dividend 20c, a >50% reduction**,
  with a new policy of a **30–50% payout ratio and a 50c/yr floor** (CEO Lewis called it a
  "difficult decision to reduce the dividend to a more appropriate level"; H1 FY26, 25 Feb 2026).
  **VERIFIED.** This is a quality *demerit* — but arguably a sensible reset that funds deleveraging.
- **Debt load — the real quality caveat.** Net debt **$21.9bn (30 Jun 2025)**, **3.4x net
  debt/adj EBITDA** (company guidance range 3.3–3.5x). Management has committed to bring leverage
  to a **2.5–3.0x** target "no later than fiscal 28," aided by the pending East African Breweries/
  Kenya disposal to Asahi (~$2.3bn, ~−0.25x leverage). **VERIFIED** (FY25 prelim). 3.4x is high for
  a staple and is *why* the dividend was cut — it constrains flexibility if EBITDA keeps sliding.
- **Organic growth trend — deteriorating, the key negative.** FY25 organic net sales **+1.7%**
  (volume +0.9%, price/mix +0.8%). But FY26 turned negative: Q1 FY26 **0.0%** (flat), **H1 FY26
  −2.8%** (volume −0.9%, price/mix −1.9%), dragged by **US Spirits −9.3%** and Chinese white
  spirits. Tequila −23.1% in H1. The medium-term "+5–7% organic" framework has been **dropped**
  from guidance; FY26 guide is now organic net sales **"flat to slightly down."** **VERIFIED.**

## 4. Bear case / risks

### 4a. THE STRUCTURAL-DECLINE THESIS (GLP-1 + Gen-Z) — addressed head-on

**The bear claim:** GLP-1 weight-loss drugs (Ozempic/Wegovy/Zepbound) suppress the urge to drink,
and Gen-Z is structurally drinking less — so spirits demand is in permanent secular decline and
Diageo's ~20x-of-old multiple was justified only in a now-dead growth world.

**What the data actually shows (both sides):**
- **GLP-1 effect is real at the individual level but small in aggregate.** Two RCTs now support
  reduced drinking: a JAMA Psychiatry semaglutide AUD trial (n=48; cut alcohol consumed and
  cravings, but NOT drinking-days; Hendershot et al., 12 Feb 2025 —
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39937469/) and the larger Danish SEMALCO trial (n=108; heavy
  drinking days −13.7pp vs placebo; The Lancet, 2 May 2026 —
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42070571/). BUT a competing 2025 meta-analysis (n=263,616) found
  **no significant pooled reduction** in consumption (Drug & Alcohol Dependence, 1 Oct 2025 —
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40845737/) — the evidence genuinely conflicts.
- **Aggregate math is modest.** Morgan Stanley models only a **~2–3% US alcohol-consumption drop
  by 2035** even under GLP-1 uptake (~24M users, ~7% of population; MS 5 Sep 2023 —
  https://www.morganstanley.com/ideas/obesity-drugs-food-industry; updated 20 Apr 2024). BCG puts
  the GLP-1 hit to overall alcohol demand at **"less than 3%"** and "not the thing driving" the
  downturn (Bloomberg via Seattle Times, 13 Jun 2026). Bernstein: GLP-1 explains ~20% of recent US
  weakness but is *"a narrative in search of a story"* (~6-7% UK GLP-1 adoption, no real change in
  beer volumes; The Drinks Business, 4 Feb 2026 — https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2026/02/tale-of-the-tape/).
  Diageo's own management: with ~15M US users, *"we have not yet seen a material impact… distinct
  from broader moderation"* (CAGNY, 20 Feb 2025). **VERIFIED.**
- **Gen-Z: moderation, not abandonment.** US drinking hit a **record-low 54% in 2025** (from 62%
  in 2023; 18-34s 59%→50%; Gallup, 13 Aug 2025). BUT IWSR directly rebuts the abandonment story:
  legal-age Gen Z who drank in the past 6 months **rose 66%→73% (Apr 2023→Mar 2025)** — *"don't
  blame Gen Z"* (IWSR, 26 Jun 2025 — https://www.theiwsr.com/dont-blame-gen-z-as-challenges-persist-younger-legal-drinking-age-consumers-are-re-engaging-with-alcohol/).
  Actual abstention is near-flat across generations (~18-19%). The shift is *less/better*, not zero.
- **Offsets:** emerging-market growth is intact — India TBA +4-6%, forecast to overtake the US by
  servings by 2032 (+38% servings by 2035; IWSR, 5 Mar 2026); global legal-drinking-age population
  +9% to 2035. Diageo FY25 group +1.7% masked Brazil +18%, Colombia/Venezuela +21.5%. **VERIFIED.**

**My structural verdict:** the structural drag is **real but ~2-3% cumulative over a decade** —
a headwind, not a cliff. Wine's decline is genuinely structural; premium *spirits*' is not (yet).
The GLP-1 pillar of the bear case is the **weakest-evidenced** — it's a valuation *sentiment*
driver more than a demonstrated volume driver. **This is the trap test, and Diageo mostly passes:
the −63% de-rate has priced in a structural-decline scenario that the data does not yet support.**

### 4b. Other risks (all VERIFIED unless noted)
- **US destocking cleared, but demand is now the problem.** H1 FY26 North America organic **−6.8%**,
  US Spirits **−9.3%**, tequila **−23.1%** — this is *demand*, not inventory (shipments now ~1pp
  *behind* depletions). The trajectory is still worsening. **Top near-term risk.**
- **China white spirits** cut group sales ~2.5% in Q1 FY26 — a fresh drag (Q1 FY26, 6 Nov 2025 —
  https://www.diageo.com/en/news-and-media/press-releases/2025/diageo-issues-fiscal-26-q1-trading-statement).
- **High leverage (3.4x)** in a falling-EBITDA environment forced the dividend cut and limits
  buyback firepower until deleveraging progresses.
- **US tariffs.** ~**$200m unmitigated annualised** operating-profit hit (10% UK / 15% EU import
  tariffs; Tequila assumed USMCA-exempt in base case); ~half mitigable before pricing (FY25 prelim,
  restated Q1 FY26). Manageable but a drag; risk if Tequila exemption is lost.
- **Management turnover.** CEO Debra Crew departed abruptly **16 Jul 2025**; CFO Nik Jhangiani was
  interim CEO; **Sir Dave Lewis (ex-Tesco "Drastic Dave") appointed permanent CEO, effective
  1 Jan 2026** (announced 10 Nov 2025). A credible turnaround operator, but it's a *reset* year —
  execution unproven at Diageo, and turnaround CEOs often "kitchen-sink" (impairments, dividend cut,
  guide-down already done). **VERIFIED.**
- **Profit warnings history.** Nov-2023 LatAm warning (shares −11%), Nov-2025 FY26 guidance cut.
  Credibility with the market is damaged — needs a clean beat to re-rate.

## 5. Catalysts & verdict

### Catalysts to watch
- **A visible US Spirits inflection** (return toward flat/positive depletions) — the single most
  important re-rating trigger. Not yet present (H1 FY26 still −9.3%).
- **"Accelerate" cost program** — target *raised* to **~$625m over 3 years** (from ~$500m); ~50% of
  savings expected in FY26 — supports margin/FCF even on soft sales (Q1/H1 FY26). **VERIFIED.**
- **Deleveraging** below 3.0x (East African Breweries/Asahi ~$2.3bn disposal; FCF ~$3bn) → removes
  the dividend/buyback overhang.
- **New CEO strategy reset** under Dave Lewis (a portfolio/cost credibility catalyst if delivered).
- **FCF ~$3bn maintained** → ~9% FCF yield support at the current cap.

### VERDICT: **WATCH** (lean-positive; upgrade to BUY-worth-more-work on a US inflection)

A genuinely high-quality, wide-moat compounder at its cheapest valuation in a decade, where the
scary structural thesis (GLP-1 / Gen-Z) is — on the actual data — a **modest ~2-3%/decade headwind
being priced as a cliff**. That asymmetry is attractive. But it is **not yet a BUY**: the operating
trajectory is still *deteriorating* (H1 FY26 US Spirits −9.3%, guidance flat-to-down), the dividend
was just cut, leverage is a high 3.4x, and a brand-new turnaround CEO has an unproven hand. This is
a classic "great business, wrong moment" — the falling-knife phase of a cyclical trough. **WATCH,
with a hard trigger: promote to BUY-worth-more-work when US Spirits depletions turn back toward
flat/positive OR leverage clears 3.0x with the dividend stabilized.** Sizing note vs Pernod:
Diageo is the higher-quality horse; Pernod (10.8x, 7.6% yield, but 8.6% ROE) is the cheaper, more
value-trap-prone one — Diageo is the better quality-at-a-price expression of the same thesis.
