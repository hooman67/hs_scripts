## Executive conclusion

Lululemon remains a **financially strong but operationally impaired turnaround**. Its brand, balance sheet, margins and historical returns on capital are real strengths. Its valuation is low enough that the stock could rebound substantially if the new management team stabilizes product demand. Those attributes make it an interesting **watchlist turnaround**, but they do not make it a current core holding for this portfolio.

The decisive evidence is the mismatch between the investment and Hooman's stated mandate:

1. **USER-CONFIRMED:** the purchase was a rebound trade, not a long-term Lululemon thesis.
2. **USER-CONFIRMED:** Hooman would not hold it for three years through a 30–40% drawdown.
3. **VERIFIED:** current operations can plausibly produce that drawdown: Q2 comparable sales fell 9%, Americas comps fell 12%, first-half operating income fell 24%, and management guided Q3 revenue down 10–11%.
4. **VERIFIED:** the apparent Q2 margin strength was distorted by a one-time $134.5 million tariff refund. Reported Q2 gross margin rose 200 basis points, but the refund contributed 560 basis points; excluding it, gross margin was approximately 54.9%, about 360 basis points below the prior year. Reported operating margin was 18.8%; excluding the refund contribution it was approximately 13.2%, about 750 basis points below the prior year.
5. **VERIFIED:** the company is only weeks into a major leadership reset. Heidi O'Neill became CEO on September 8, new product and operations leaders began October 26, two senior leaders departed November 6, and searches remained open for brand, communications and technology chiefs.

The company may go higher. That possibility is the turnaround thesis itself. It does not justify moving the position into a sleeve whose purpose it does not serve.

## What is attractive

### Brand and economics

- **VERIFIED:** Q2 reported gross margin remained high at 60.5%, although the one-time refund materially inflated it.
- **VERIFIED:** the company had $1.390 billion of cash at August 2, 2026 and no borrowings under its committed revolver other than $6.3 million in letters of credit/guarantees. The large liability often shown by data vendors as “debt” is primarily store lease liabilities, not conventional funded debt.
- **VERIFIED:** the company repurchased 2.7 million shares for $330 million in Q2 and $712 million in the first half, reducing diluted weighted shares by about 5% year over year.
- **VERIFIED:** international reported revenue grew 4% in Q2, although constant-currency comparable sales still fell 6%. Store expansion therefore supplied part of the reported growth.

These are the reasons the shares can rebound. Lululemon has a recognized premium brand, substantial cash generation, no conventional balance-sheet debt problem, and a depressed valuation.

### Management quality and catalyst

- **VERIFIED:** Heidi O'Neill spent 27 years at Nike and most recently led consumer, product and brand. Her background is relevant to Lululemon's product and brand problems.
- **VERIFIED:** the company created product and operating leadership roles, hired Maggie Gauger as President and Chief Product Officer, and Joseph Godsey as COO.
- **ASSUMED:** the new team could improve product cadence and restore demand. This is credible but unproven; the team had not produced a reported quarter as of this review.
- **VERIFIED:** founder Chip Wilson obtained a board appointment under a cooperation agreement, adding pressure for product-led change.

## What is broken

### Demand and earnings trend

- **VERIFIED:** Q2 revenue fell 4% reported and 5% constant currency; total comparable sales fell 9% reported and 10% constant currency.
- **VERIFIED:** Americas revenue fell 8% and Americas comparable sales fell 12%.
- **VERIFIED:** Canada revenue fell 11% reported and 9% constant currency.
- **VERIFIED:** China Mainland revenue rose 4% reported but fell 2% constant currency; comparable sales fell 8% constant currency. “International growth” is therefore not yet a clean same-store recovery.
- **VERIFIED:** first-half operating income fell from $962.4 million to $730.6 million and net income fell from $685.5 million to $524.3 million, both approximately 24%.
- **VERIFIED:** management guided FY2026 revenue down 5–7% and Q3 revenue down 10–11%.

### Earnings quality

- **VERIFIED:** Q2 EPS of $2.92 included $0.86 from the one-time tariff refund and related interest. Core Q2 EPS was therefore approximately $2.06, about 34% below the prior-year $3.10.
- **VERIFIED:** FY2026 EPS guidance of $9.48–$9.73 also includes the same $0.86 one-time benefit. Subtracting it gives approximately $8.62–$8.87 of ongoing guidance earnings.
- **ASSUMED:** the ongoing earnings range is the more relevant denominator for valuation because the refund is not a recurring operating benefit.

### Turnaround execution risk

The new CEO has the right resume, but the organization is still being rebuilt. Product, brand and technology leadership searches, multiple new roles, recent departures and founder involvement create potential upside and substantial execution uncertainty. There is no reported evidence yet that the new structure has repaired demand.

- At that price, LULU trades at approximately **12.1–12.5 times** management's FY2026 EPS guidance after removing the $0.86 one-time benefit. That is cheap for the historical franchise, but the denominator is falling.

The following are **ASSUMED scenarios**, not price forecasts:

## Recommendation and alternatives

### Primary recommendation — sell all 1,000 CDRs

**ASSUMED investment judgment, high confidence on fit:** sell the full `LULU.TO` position now that it is above the recorded basis. Use a limit order because the CDR can have a wider spread than the U.S. common.

Why full rather than half:

1. Hooman explicitly said that if the position remains speculation, he will sell it.
2. The original rebound objective has been achieved with an approximate 6.6% gross CAD gain.
3. Holding half solely because the shares might rise would be a new turnaround bet, not completion of the old trade.
4. The position conflicts with Hooman's stated unwillingness to tolerate the plausible downside.
5. The RRSP does not create a current capital-gains tax reason to delay an intra-account sale.

### Second-best alternative — sell half

Selling 500 and retaining 500 is defensible only if Hooman deliberately authorizes a small turnaround position and accepts that the retained half could fall 30–40%. It reduces regret if the turnaround succeeds, but it is inconsistent with the stated decision rule and risk tolerance. It is therefore not the recommendation.

### What to do after sale

Keep LULU on the quality-turnaround watchlist. Exiting now does not assert that the stock cannot rise; it preserves the portfolio mandate while allowing re-entry after evidence improves.

## What would change the conclusion

Reconsider Lululemon for the quality sleeve after evidence of all three:

1. two consecutive quarters of improving Americas comparable sales;
2. core gross and operating margins stabilizing after removing one-time refunds; and
3. the new CEO/product team producing a credible product pipeline without another major leadership reset.

A change in Hooman's mandate—specifically willingness to accept a 30–40% drawdown for a multi-year turnaround—would also change the suitability conclusion.

## Evidence limitations

- Q2 2026 was the latest reported quarter as of November 11, 2026. The new management team had not yet reported a full quarter under its structure.
- The live CDR price and basis are USER-CONFIRMED/broker-derived. No official intraday CDR ratio was obtained, so valuation scenarios use percentage exposure to the U.S. underlying rather than precise CDR target prices.
- Long-term product relevance and brand recovery cannot be verified from filings; they are the core uncertainty.
- No DCF was needed for the sleeve decision. The scenario ranges deliberately expose the sensitivity to earnings and multiple assumptions.
