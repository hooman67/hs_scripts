# Calian Group (TSX: CGY) — raw evidence

Evidence cutoff and retrieval date: 2026-10-20. Currency: issuer financial statements and TSX quote are CAD. Dollar figures below are C$ millions unless stated otherwise.

## Primary source 1 — Q3 FY2026 combined release, statements and MD&A

- Publisher: Calian Group Ltd.
- Published: 2026-08-12; period ended 2026-06-30
- URL: https://investors.calian.com/app/uploads/sites/2/2026/02/Q3-FY26-Combined-PR-FS-and-MDA.pdf
- Supporting evidence:
  - Q3 revenue 230.397 versus 192.216, up 20%; acquisitive growth 4% from Advanced Medical Solutions and InField Scientific; organic growth 16%. Nine-month revenue 667.098 versus 570.930, up 17%; acquisitive growth 6%; organic growth 11%. The 16% and 11% figures apply to different periods and are consistent.
  - Q3 gross profit 78.475 (34.1% margin); adjusted EBITDA 25.570 (11.1% margin); net profit 5.941; adjusted net profit 12.891; issuer-defined operating free cash flow 17.532. Nine-month adjusted EBITDA 76.256 (11.4%); net profit 17.753; adjusted net profit 39.751; issuer-defined operating free cash flow 54.774.
  - Q3 Defence & Space revenue 149.815, 65.0% of total; Essential Industries 80.582, 35.0%. Nine-month Defence & Space was 444.794, 66.7%. The label includes defence, commercial space, technology, health, and training activities; it is not equivalent to Canadian defence revenue.
  - Realisable backlog was 1.448 billion through FY2030 after management excluded 180.162 it estimated as unrealizable. Firm remaining performance obligations in the statements were lower at 1.248 billion (699.858 due within 24 months and 548.480 thereafter). Fee-for-service contracts allow effort adjustments, so realized revenue can differ.
  - Management says about C$1 billion of backlog relates to defence. Quarter signings included 62 of IT/cyber work across commercial and defence customers; 29 space; 16 GNSS; 10 defence learning/training; 8 healthcare with Canadian defence agencies; and 7 nuclear. The C$296 million, 15-year British Army agreement announced 2026-08-11 followed quarter-end and should not automatically be treated as included in June backlog.
  - Cash 46.335 including 4.076 restricted; credit-facility debt 141.250; reported net debt 94.915; net-debt/TTM-adjusted-EBITDA 0.9x; lease liabilities 43.295; earnout liabilities 5.157.
  - Nine-month IFRS cash from operations 31.629 versus adjusted EBITDA 76.256; capex 8.408; lease principal 4.971. Conventional after-capex cash was 23.221 before leases and 18.250 after leases. The issuer's 54.774 operating-free-cash-flow measure adds back 21.124 of working-capital outflow and 2.021 of acquisition/integration costs and does not deduct capex. This is a material metric-definition gap.
  - Common shares outstanding rose to 11,512,163 from 11,350,168 during the first nine months (129,483 compensation shares and 32,512 employee-plan shares), a 1.43% gross increase. No shares were repurchased in the period.
- Limitations: adjusted EBITDA, adjusted net profit, and operating free cash flow are non-IFRS. Backlog depends on management's realizability judgment and contains fee-for-service arrangements.

## Primary source 2 — FY2025 annual report

- Publisher: Calian Group Ltd.
- Published: 2025-11; year ended 2025-09-30
- URL: https://investors.calian.com/app/uploads/sites/2/2025/02/Calian-AR-2025-FINAL.pdf
- Supporting evidence:
  - Government customers represented 52% of FY2025 revenue and commercial customers 48%; Canada represented 56% and outside Canada 44%. Calian is therefore diversified beyond Canadian defence spending.
  - Goodwill and acquired intangibles remain substantial. At Q3 FY2026 they totalled 323.458, about 43% of assets, reflecting an acquisitive model and integration/impairment exposure.
  - FY2025 operating cash was 45.434 and capex 10.598. Using nine-month comparable figures, TTM operating cash through Q3 FY2026 was 45.434 - 39.028 + 31.629 = 38.035; TTM capex was 10.598 - 7.310 + 8.408 = 11.696; conventional pre-lease TTM free cash flow was about 26.339.
- Limitations: the segment presentation changed in FY2026; historical comparisons need mapping.

## Primary source 3 — 2025 circular and AI disclosures

- Publishers: Calian Group Ltd.
- Circular published: 2025-12-15; meeting held 2026-02-12
- AI page published 2025-06-12, modified 2025-12-11
- URLs:
  - https://investors.calian.com/app/uploads/sites/2/2026/01/Calian_CIR_2025-WEB.pdf
  - https://www.calian.com/ai-innovation/
- Supporting evidence:
  - Mawer Investment Management owned about 14% and QV Investors about 13%; there was no insider controlling shareholder. Eight of nine directors, including the chair, were independent.
  - Calian lists delivered AI/ML projects: Canadian Space Agency anomaly detection estimated to reduce operator fault-detection/isolation/recovery time by 65%; a NATO training-data engine; MaestroEDE AI assistant; a nuclear shift-log proof of concept using LLM/multi-agent methods; Bin-Sense machine-learning noise reduction; and AI-compute platform work with technology partners.
  - Neither the AI page nor financial filings disclose AI-specific revenue, margin, contract value, or backlog. The capability is real, while its financial materiality is unquantified.
- Limitations: project examples and estimated efficiency do not establish commercial scale or attributable revenue.

## Thesis fit and bear evidence

- Calian is the strongest of the three for demonstrated AI delivery and has meaningful defence backlog, but only about two-thirds of current revenue sits in the broad Defence & Space segment. It is not a pure Canadian defence beneficiary.
- Moat evidence: trusted relationships with DND/CAF and allied customers, long contracts, specialized space/cyber/training/health credentials, and diversified mission-critical services.
- Bear evidence: low conventional cash conversion, working-capital add-backs in the preferred non-GAAP metric, acquisition goodwill/intangibles, modest dilution, mixed end markets, and acquisition/integration execution risk.
