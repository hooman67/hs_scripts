# PetroVietnam Ca Mau Fertilizer (HOSE: DCM) Buy Review

**As of:** 2026-09-07. Vietnam's market was closed when this review was finalized; the reference
price is the 2026-09-07 close of **VND 31,800**.

**Issuer confirmed:** **PetroVietnam Ca Mau Fertilizer Corporation (PVCFC)**, listed on the Ho Chi
Minh Stock Exchange under ticker **DCM**. It is not PetroVietnam Chemical and Services Corporation
(PVChem, HNX: PVC).

## Bottom line

**Verdict: WATCH / DO NOT OPEN DIRECTLY through Hooman's ordinary U.S. or Canadian accounts.**

DCM is a real, profitable and unusually well-capitalized nitrogen-fertilizer producer—not an obvious
value trap. Its Ca Mau urea complex is a nationally important low-cost asset; it owns 660,000 tonnes
of annual NPK capacity across Ca Mau and the acquired Korea-Vietnam Fertilizer plant; 2025 profit
rose 37%; and reviewed first-half 2026 profit rose another 55%. At year-end 2025, cash and term
deposits exceeded financial debt by roughly **VND 5.3 trillion**. The stock also trades at about
**6.5-7.5 times forecast 2026 earnings**.

The apparent bargain is nevertheless less compelling than it first looks:

- record first-half earnings benefited from a favorable urea-price/gas-cost spread and VAT recovery,
  while Chinese exports and restored Russian capacity were already pressuring second-half urea prices;
- 2025 operating cash flow was **negative VND 776 billion**, despite VND 1.96 trillion of accounting
  profit, because receivables and inventories absorbed cash;
- the trailing VND 2,000 dividend implies a 6.3% yield, but the board's 2026 plan provides only
  **VND 1,000**, a forward planned yield of about **3.1%**;
- the state parent owns 75.56%, the gas supplier is a related state group, an old gas-pricing settlement
  remains unresolved, and large 2027-2028 projects could turn net cash into debt;
- most U.S. and Canadian retail brokers do not offer the Ho Chi Minh Stock Exchange. Direct ownership
  normally requires a Vietnam securities-trading code, local custody/brokerage, a VND investment
  account and additional tax/reporting administration. DCM reports no securities traded abroad, and
  no sponsored ADR or usable U.S. OTC line was found.

For Hooman, DCM is not a missing strategic sleeve. It substantially duplicates the nitrogen-producer
economics already supplied by **CF Industries (CF)**, while **PDBA** provides different exposure to
agricultural commodities. Opening Vietnam custody for a tiny single-name position is not worthwhile.

### Conditional plan if direct Vietnam access already exists

- Limit DCM to **0.25% of the total portfolio**, about **$1,655** at the current approximately
  $662,049 portfolio value. At VND 31,800 and roughly VND 26,000/USD, a practical 100-share-lot order
  would be about **1,300 shares**, or roughly $1,590 before fees.
- Fund it by reducing **CF from 1.00% to 0.75%**. Do not add it on top of the Agriculture allocation,
  and do not take funding from the global-index core or house reserve.
- Treat it as part of the **1% Speculation cap** unless and until access, governance, cash conversion
  and the post-2032 gas-supply economics become sufficiently predictable.
- Prefer **0.125% near VND 28,000-31,000** and the remaining 0.125% only below roughly VND 26,000
  with the thesis intact, or after second-half results demonstrate both margin resilience and positive
  operating cash conversion. At VND 31,800, wait rather than chase just above the first band.

The simpler recommendation is to keep **CF** as the liquid U.S.-listed nitrogen producer and watch
DCM. A Vietnam country ETF is not a faithful substitute for DCM.

## Business and competitive position

PVCFC was created by PetroVietnam to operate the Ca Mau fertilizer complex and began commercial
production in 2012. The state parent, now named Vietnam National Industry-Energy Group, remains the
controlling shareholder. The operating portfolio has expanded beyond one urea plant:

| Asset/activity | Capacity or 2025 output | Role |
|---|---:|---|
| Ca Mau urea plant | Approximately 800,000 t/year design; 966,730 t converted urea produced in 2025 | Core earnings and export engine |
| Ca Mau NPK plant | 300,000 t/year | Higher-value compound fertilizer |
| Korea-Vietnam Fertilizer (KVF) | 360,000 t/year NPK; acquired April 2024 | Southern distribution and product breadth |
| Fertilizer trading | 310,160 t sold in 2025 | Completes product range but adds working-capital and lower-margin exposure |
| Chemicals/industrial gases | Early stage | Potential diversification from urea |

In 2025, the group produced 966,730 tonnes of converted urea and 347,230 tonnes of NPK. It sold
804,710 tonnes of urea, 331,320 tonnes of NPK and 310,160 tonnes of traded fertilizer. Total product
sales reached 1.43 million tonnes, up 8%, and NPK sales rose 32%. Export revenue was approximately
**VND 4.89 trillion, 29% of consolidated net revenue**, versus 21% in 2024; products reached 22
countries.

This is a stronger franchise than a small undifferentiated fertilizer importer. The Ca Mau brand,
distribution network, plant reliability and access to domestic offshore gas are real advantages.
However, commodity urea still sets the marginal economics. Branding can support distribution and
some premium, but it does not remove exposure to international nitrogen supply, crop economics and
natural-gas input costs.

### Revenue mix

Company-hosted analyst data indicate approximate 2025 revenue contributions of VND 8.65T from urea,
VND 3.88T from NPK, VND 4.01T from fertilizer trading and VND 0.99T from chemicals. These category
figures do not reconcile perfectly to audited net revenue because of presentation and elimination
differences, but they establish the direction: urea remains the largest profit driver, while NPK and
trading broaden volume without yet eliminating nitrogen-cycle dependence.

Geographic diversification is improving but creates new exposure to ocean freight, export policy,
trade remedies and foreign-currency settlements. Vietnam itself produces roughly 2.3-2.4 million
tonnes of urea against domestic demand near 2.05-2.1 million tonnes in normal conditions. Exports are
therefore necessary, not optional incremental upside.

## Fertilizer cycle and feedstock economics

### Why first-half 2026 was exceptional

Reviewed first-half 2026 consolidated revenue rose **25.3% to VND 12.31T**, while cost of goods rose
only 16.8%. Profit before tax increased 53.6% to VND 2.06T and after-tax profit increased 54.7% to
**VND 1.85T**—already well above the board's conservative VND 1.18T full-year plan.

The second quarter illustrates the operating leverage. A company-hosted BVSC report estimated:

- average urea selling price rose about 41% year over year;
- urea revenue rose about 60%, with export volume more than doubling;
- gross margin expanded roughly 9.3 percentage points to 29.8%; and
- VAT recovery added to the improvement.

This is exactly why nitrogen producers can generate extraordinary cash earnings in a tight market.
It is also why a low peak-cycle P/E is not sufficient evidence of permanent undervaluation.

### The second-half normalization risk

BVSC expected global urea prices to normalize toward approximately **$380-$440 per tonne** in the
second half, about 25% below the first-half average, as China resumed exports, Russian plants returned
and energy input prices softened. The report still forecast 2026 after-tax profit of VND 2.51T,
roughly 31% above 2025, but that implies a sharp deceleration after the exceptional first half.

The central cycle variables are:

1. Chinese export quotas and timing;
2. Russian and Middle Eastern operating rates and logistics;
3. European marginal production economics, especially gas;
4. grain prices, farm affordability and weather-driven demand;
5. Vietnam's import/export and VAT policy; and
6. DCM's own gas price relative to realized urea prices.

Hooman's broader commodity-supercycle thesis supports owning some agricultural and energy exposure,
but it does not make every fertilizer equity a one-way trade. Fertilizer equities add operational,
capital-allocation and country risks to the commodity cycle.

### Gas contract: valuable visibility, not permanent certainty

DCM's principal feedstock contract covers approximately 495 million standard cubic metres of gas per
year from the PM3 CAA / 46-Cai Nuoc area. Its 20-year operating term runs from **2012-04-24 through
2032-04-23**, with a monthly market-linked pricing formula. The seller was transferred from the state
parent to PV GAS effective 2025-11-18.

The contract reduces near-term volume uncertainty, but investors should not treat gas as permanently
subsidized or fixed-price. The price moves under a formula, offshore supply eventually declines, and
the post-2032 renewal economics are a central terminal-value risk. The financial statements also
continue to disclose an unresolved final settlement concerning historical 2015-2018 gas pricing.
That old issue is not currently large enough to threaten solvency, but it is a reminder that a state
controlled buyer and state-linked supplier do not eliminate contractual uncertainty.

## Financial quality

### Earnings and margins

| VND trillion unless stated | 2024 | 2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Consolidated net revenue | 13.44 | 16.63 | 12.31 |
| Profit before tax | 1.52 | 2.21 | 2.06 |
| Profit after tax | 1.43 | 1.96 | 1.85 |
| Gross margin | 18.7% | 24.3% | — |
| Audited EPS after fund allocation | VND 2,359 | VND 3,203 | — |

2025 ROE was 18.7% and ROA 11.8%. Those are good returns for a capital-intensive producer, and H1
2026 demonstrates strong upside when the urea/gas spread widens. They should not be extrapolated as a
steady compounder margin.

### Balance sheet

At 2025 year-end, DCM reported approximately:

- VND 3.59T of cash and equivalents;
- VND 4.08T of term deposits/debt investments;
- VND 2.23T of short-term borrowings/current debt;
- roughly VND 0.12T of long-term debt and leases;
- VND 17.64T of total assets; and
- current and quick ratios of about 2.43x and 1.62x.

This implies approximately **VND 5.3T of net cash-like liquidity**, a substantial cushion against a
fertilizer downturn. Later market data show somewhat higher total debt, but the company remained net
cash. The balance sheet is one of the strongest parts of the thesis.

### Cash flow is the main financial warning

2025 profit did not turn into cash. Operating cash flow was approximately **negative VND 776B** after
receivables absorbed VND 1.14T and inventory absorbed VND 1.90T. Capital expenditure was about
VND 695B, making simple free cash flow approximately **negative VND 1.47T**. Cash balances were
supported by maturing financial investments and additional borrowings rather than internally generated
free cash flow.

Some working-capital build is understandable during fast expansion and commodity-price changes, but
the next results must show inventory and receivables converting back to cash. A buy case based on the
headline P/E and dividend while ignoring cash conversion is incomplete.

### Capital expenditure and leverage trajectory

Management's 2026 plan calls for roughly VND 720B of investment disbursement. The longer-term strategy
is much larger: planning materials contemplate about **VND 6.12T of investment and VND 3.48T of new
borrowing in 2027**, followed by roughly VND 2.47T of investment and VND 1.69T of borrowing in 2028.
Projects include food-grade CO2, industrial gases, port/logistics infrastructure, plant upgrades and
other fertilizer/chemical capacity.

These projects could diversify revenue and support a VND 35T 2030 revenue ambition. They also mean
today's net cash is not necessarily distributable excess capital. Poor project returns or cost overruns
could turn a clean cyclical producer into a leveraged state-directed conglomerate.

## Dividend: attractive historically, not assured

DCM paid **VND 2,000 per share** from 2024 profit in 2025 and again distributed VND 2,000 in July
2026 from 2025 profit. At VND 31,800, the trailing cash yield is about **6.3%**. Yet the official 2026
business plan sets a dividend equal to 10% of par value—**VND 1,000 per share**, or only **3.1%** at
the current price.

The state parent has an incentive to receive dividends, and DCM has historically distributed meaningful
cash. But payout follows cyclical profit, investment needs and government-approved plans. This should
be analyzed as a variable total-return stock, not an income security with a dependable 6% yield.

For a U.S. tax resident, Vietnamese cash dividends generally face local withholding, commonly cited at
5% for individuals, and remain reportable as worldwide income in the United States. A foreign tax credit
may be available subject to the investor's circumstances. Account type, treaty eligibility and Canadian
account registration can change the result; a cross-border tax adviser should confirm implementation.

## Ownership, governance and related parties

Vietnam National Industry-Energy Group/PetroVietnam owned **400,023,057 shares, or 75.56%**, at
2025 year-end. Foreign investors owned about 34.2 million shares, 6.46%. No other shareholder controls
the company.

The controlling state ownership provides strategic support and gas-system access, but minority owners
cannot determine capital allocation, executive appointments, related-party contracts or dividends. It
also limits effective public float to 24.44% before considering insiders and long-term institutions.

Related-party purchases were approximately **VND 4.63T in 2025**, including VND 3.16T from the state
parent, VND 1.03T from PV GAS, VND 293B from PV Power and VND 139B from PetroVietnam Fertilizer and
Chemicals. The large gas arrangement received shareholder and independent-audit-committee review, and
interested shareholders were excluded from the relevant vote. Those protections are positive, but they
do not make the transactions arm's length in the same sense as unrelated competitive procurement.

Other governance considerations include Vietnamese disclosure/accessibility, state policy priorities,
minority-remedy limitations and VND currency risk. None makes DCM uninvestable; together they justify a
discount and a small maximum position.

### Why the shares fell

From a 52-week high of VND 52,900 on 2026-03-09 to VND 31,800, DCM declined about **40%**. The
52-week low was VND 28,500 on 2026-07-23. The decline reflects:

1. normalization expectations after the first-half urea-price surge;
2. renewed Chinese exports and recovering global supply;
3. management's conservative 2026 plan, including a planned profit decline and dividend halving;
4. de-rating after a rapid commodity-driven share-price advance;
5. rising inventory/receivables and weak 2025 operating cash conversion; and
6. normal Vietnam/frontier-market liquidity and policy risk.

The stock is near its 50-day average around VND 31,500 but below its 100- and 200-day averages around
VND 34,700 and VND 36,000. That is not proof of a bottom. Technical context supports staging rather
than overriding the fundamental cycle.

## Catalysts and failure conditions

### Catalysts

- second-half urea prices and spreads hold above conservative assumptions;
- China exports less than feared or weather supports global grain/fertilizer demand;
- inventories and receivables unwind, restoring positive operating cash flow;
- KVF and Ca Mau NPK utilization/margins continue improving;
- VAT treatment and export economics remain supportive;
- industrial-gas/chemical projects start on time with disclosed returns;
- management preserves net cash and renews gas supply beyond 2032 on economic terms; and
- dividend guidance rises with sustainable cash earnings rather than balance-sheet depletion.

### Thesis failure / sell rules

- two reporting periods of weak cash conversion without a clear seasonal explanation;
- net debt rises materially before new projects demonstrate acceptable returns;
- a structurally adverse gas-price reset or no credible post-2032 supply plan;
- recurring related-party settlements or capex that transfer value away from minorities;
- export restrictions, sanctions or foreign-ownership/access changes impairing liquidity;
- normalized urea spreads imply EPS below roughly VND 2,500 without offsetting NPK/chemical growth; or
- the position exceeds 0.25% through appreciation while it still carries frontier/speculation status.

## Can Hooman actually buy DCM?

### Exchange access and custody

DCM trades in VND on HOSE. Its annual report states that the company has **no securities traded
abroad**. No sponsored ADR, U.S. exchange listing or credible liquid OTC instrument was identified.

A foreign investor can legally own DCM, and foreign ownership was only about 6.4% versus an official
49% ceiling in the company-hosted BVSC data, so foreign-room headroom is ample. The operational problem
is brokerage access. A direct foreign investor generally needs:

1. a securities-trading code issued through the Vietnam Securities Depository and Clearing Corporation;
2. an account with a Vietnam-licensed securities company and custody bank;
3. a VND indirect-investment capital/cash account and compliant FX funding;
4. local know-your-customer, passport, tax and account documentation; and
5. acceptance of local trading rules, normally 100-share board lots and T+2 settlement.

The ordinary international-market menus published by major U.S. and Canadian retail brokers do not
generally include Vietnam/HOSE. Hooman should therefore assume his existing U.S. and Canadian brokerage
accounts **cannot place DCM orders** until the broker explicitly confirms otherwise. A specialist
Vietnam broker/custodian may solve access, but setup, wires, FX spreads, custody fees and tax reporting
are disproportionate for a roughly $1,600 position.

### Sanctions and ownership restrictions

No DCM/PVCFC match was identified in the U.S. Treasury sanctions search during this review. Because
the company is state-controlled and sanctions lists can change, the executing broker must screen the
issuer, parent and custodian at trade time. This review is not a sanctions legal opinion.

### Tax and reporting for a U.S. resident

Likely considerations include:

- Vietnamese withholding on dividends, generally cited as 5% for individuals;
- Vietnamese securities-transfer tax, generally 0.1% of gross sale proceeds for individuals;
- U.S. tax on worldwide dividends and capital gains, potentially with a foreign tax credit;
- FBAR and Form 8938 reporting if foreign-account thresholds are met; and
- additional Canadian-account consequences depending on whether an account remains legally available
  and correctly registered after U.S. residency.

Direct DCM common stock is ordinarily an operating-company share rather than a passive foreign
investment company. By contrast, a Vietnam-domiciled fund could create PFIC complexity for a U.S.
taxpayer. Hooman should not buy through a Canadian or Vietnamese wrapper merely to obtain CAD/VND
diversification without U.S.-Canada cross-border tax confirmation.

Currency diversification is also not free. DCM's reported return arrives in VND, while fertilizer
exports and inputs retain substantial USD-linked economics. A weaker VND can reduce USD returns even
when the local share price performs.

## Alternatives and portfolio overlap

| Instrument | What it provides | Fit versus DCM |
|---|---|---|
| **CF** | U.S.-listed, liquid, low-cost natural-gas nitrogen producer | **Best practical substitute; already owned/planned** |
| **NTR** | Potash, nitrogen and retail diversification | Broader fertilizer exposure, but less pure nitrogen and overlaps Canada/resources |
| **MOO** | Global agribusiness ETF, about 0.56% expense ratio | Diversified but not low-cost or a clean fertilizer/urea exposure |
| **VNM** | U.S.-listed Vietnam equity ETF, about 0.66% expense ratio | Country proxy; DCM is absent or too small to reproduce the thesis |
| **PDBA** | Agricultural commodity futures | Complements rather than substitutes; no producer execution/dividend risk |
| **DPM** | Vietnamese nitrogen peer | Same access, state-governance and cycle problems; not a practical solution |

DCM and CF respond to the same fundamental spread: nitrogen fertilizer prices minus natural-gas and
operating costs. Geography, gas contracts, export markets and governance differ, but owning both as
full positions is not genuine sleeve diversification. DCM should replace a small portion of CF, not
increase total Agriculture exposure.

PDBA is different. It owns agricultural-futures exposure and is driven more directly by crop prices,
weather and futures-curve effects. A fertilizer producer can sometimes benefit from high crop prices,
but its profit depends on input costs, capacity, policy and management. Keeping PDBA plus one fertilizer
producer is more diversified than owning several nitrogen equities.

## Precise recommendation

1. **Do not open a Vietnam brokerage/custody arrangement solely to buy DCM.** Keep CF as the practical
   fertilizer-company implementation and PDBA as agricultural-commodity exposure.
2. Add DCM to the watchlist with a **VND 28,000-31,000 first-entry band** and a **VND 26,000 second
   band**, conditional on positive cash conversion and no deterioration in gas/capex governance.
3. If an existing broker confirms direct HOSE access and the cross-border tax burden is acceptable,
   cap DCM at **0.25%**. Start with 0.125%; fund the entire eventual position by trimming CF by the
   same amount.
4. Do not use a Vietnam ETF as a synthetic DCM position. It adds country/PFIC or expense complexity
   without delivering the same fertilizer economics.
5. Reassess after full-year 2026 results for operating cash flow, inventory, receivables, the actual
   dividend proposal, realized urea/gas spread, 2027 capex/debt approval and post-2032 gas planning.
