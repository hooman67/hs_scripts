# Nike (NKE) Buy Review

**As of:** 2026-09-07. The U.S. market is closed for Labor Day, so the price used is the
2026-09-04 close of **$38.40**.  
**Decision status:** Accepted as a watchlist plan by Hooman on 2026-09-07; no purchase authorized yet.

## Bottom line

**Classification: good company, damaged franchise economics, credible turnaround, speculative
staged buy—not a proven bargain and not yet a core holding.**

Nike is not a classic balance-sheet value trap. It remains the world's largest athletic-footwear
and apparel seller, retains an exceptional global brand and athlete network, produced an 18.7%
company-defined ROIC in a trough year, and ended fiscal 2026 with about $9.0 billion of cash and
short-term investments against $7.9 billion of funded debt. The current price is the lowest
sales multiple in at least a decade and is attractive against conservatively normalized earnings
and free cash flow.

But the market is not offering a healthy Nike at 18 times earnings. Fiscal 2026 EPS included a
one-time tariff recovery; excluding it, EPS was only **$1.58**, making the stock about **24 times
trough underlying EPS**. Direct-to-consumer revenue, digital traffic, Greater China, Converse,
Sportswear and Jordan Streetwear remain weak. Sportswear and Jordan Streetwear together are about
half of revenue and management expects both to remain negative in fiscal 2027, with improvement
only in the second half. Meanwhile, On, HOKA and adidas are still growing faster.

### Recommendation for Hooman

Hooman accepted the following conditional plan but chose to **watch for now**:

- **Use a staged entry, not a full purchase.** A **0.25% starter** near $36-$39 is reasonable if
  Hooman wants exposure before the evidence is complete. At the roughly $662,000 portfolio size,
  that is about **$1,655 or 43 shares** at $38.40.
- **Add no more than another 0.25%** after either (a) operational confirmation or (b) a price below
  roughly $34 with the thesis intact. The maximum while LULU remains owned should be **0.5% of the
  portfolio, about $3,310 or 86 shares** at the current price.
- **Fund it only from the unused speculation allocation.** NKE plus LULU should remain within the
  existing 1% speculation cap. Do not fund NKE by shrinking the global-index core, house reserve,
  or another approved strategic sleeve.
- **Do not call it a quality compounder yet.** Promote it out of Speculation only after revenue,
  full-price selling and adjusted margins demonstrate a durable inflection.
- A standard one-contract cash-secured put would control 100 shares, about **$3,840** at the current
  price, which is already larger than the proposed 0.5% NKE ceiling and likely larger than the
  speculation room remaining after LULU. Shares are the cleaner implementation unless a lower strike
  keeps assignment inside the cap.

If Hooman prefers not to own two fallen consumer-brand turnarounds, **waiting is the better default**.
Skipping NKE has little portfolio cost because broad U.S./global index exposure already owns it.

## Why the stock is down so much

NKE closed at $177.51 on 2021-11-05 and at $38.40 on 2026-09-04, a decline of about **78%**.
The decline is not one problem; it is four layers occurring together.

### 1. The 2021 valuation was unsustainably high

Nike traded at roughly 38 times fiscal-2021 EPS at the May 2021 fiscal year-end and rose still
further afterward. That price assumed durable double-digit growth, digital success and premium
margins. Higher discount rates and slower growth would have compressed that multiple even if Nike
had executed well. Part of the drawdown is therefore a rational reversal of excess valuation.

### 2. Nike's channel and product execution deteriorated

Nike pushed heavily toward its own digital/direct channels, reduced emphasis on wholesale partners,
and leaned too hard on a small group of classic lifestyle franchises. Current management does not
label the prior strategy a mistake, but its corrective actions make the diagnosis clear:

- reinvest in wholesale distribution and repair partner relationships;
- reposition Nike Digital as a premium, full-price channel rather than a promotional outlet;
- liquidate old inventory and reduce the supply of selected classic footwear;
- restore sport-specific product teams and innovation under the “Sport Offense” model.

In fiscal 2026, management removed more than **$2 billion** of classic-franchise supply. Wholesale
revenue grew 6% reported and 4% currency-neutral, but Nike Direct fell 6%, Nike Digital fell 12%,
and stores fell 4%. This is primarily an **execution problem**, and execution problems can be fixed,
but the repair depresses sales and margins before new product scales.

### 3. The income statement has genuinely deteriorated

| Fiscal year | Revenue | Net income | Diluted EPS | EBIT margin | FCF |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2024 | $51.36B | $5.70B | $3.73 | 12.7% | $6.62B |
| 2025 | $46.31B | $3.22B | $2.16 | 8.2% | $3.27B |
| 2026 reported | $46.40B | $3.11B | $2.10 | 8.3% | $2.18B |
| 2026 excluding tariff recovery | $46.40B | — | **$1.58** | about **6.2%** | — |

Fiscal-2026 reported gross margin was 42.9%, but the $986 million IEEPA tariff recovery added about
210 basis points for the year. Underlying gross margin was approximately **40.8%**, below the 42.7%
reported in fiscal 2025. Thus the apparent stabilization in GAAP margin overstates the operating
improvement.

Revenue compounded only about **3.7% annually from fiscal 2016 through fiscal 2026**, and only about
**0.8% annually from fiscal 2021 through fiscal 2026**. Nike is therefore not currently a growth
company in the financial sense; a growth-first investor is underwriting a return to growth, not buying
growth already visible in reported results.

### 4. Competition and China are more than a one-quarter cycle

- **On:** Q2 2026 sales rose 13.5% reported and 21.6% currency-neutral; DTC rose 26%, and gross margin
  reached 65.4% while fully absorbing higher U.S. tariffs.
- **HOKA/Deckers:** HOKA Q1 fiscal-2027 sales rose 7.7%, with DTC up 17.3%.
- **adidas:** Q2 2026 currency-neutral revenue grew 14%; running grew around 30%; gross margin rose
  to 52.5%.
- **lululemon:** Q2 2026 revenue fell 4%, including an 8% decline in the Americas. It is not currently
  an example of a challenger taking share everywhere, but it is still a direct competitor in premium
  athletic apparel and another troubled single-name consumer holding already in Hooman's portfolio.
- **Greater China:** Nike revenue fell from $7.55 billion in fiscal 2024 to $6.59 billion in fiscal
  2025 and $5.85 billion in fiscal 2026. Q4 fiscal-2026 revenue fell another 17%. Local relevance,
  local brands, promotions and traffic are structural challenges even if the consumer cycle improves.

## Cyclical, execution-related, or structural?

| Type | Materiality | Evidence and interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Cyclical | **Meaningful but not dominant** | Consumers are pressured, traffic weakened during Q4, FX moved reported results, and lifestyle footwear is promotional across the industry. These should eventually ease. |
| Execution | **Dominant** | DTC/wholesale imbalance, over-distributed classics, weak digital traffic, slow product pipeline, inventory cleanup and inconsistent local execution are company-specific. |
| Structural | **Real** | On/HOKA/adidas have stronger current momentum; China is more local and competitive; global sourcing faces persistent tariffs; consumer attention is more fragmented. Nike may recover without regaining its old market share or valuation multiple. |

The base case assumes that most of the execution damage is repairable, while some share and margin
loss is permanent. A valuation that requires a return to the 2021 multiple is not credible.

## Brand health and product innovation

### What remains strong

- Nike remains the world's largest athletic-footwear and apparel seller and owns globally recognized
  trademarks, athlete relationships and distribution reach that would be extremely expensive to copy.
- Performance is responding before lifestyle. Management reported five consecutive quarters of
  double-digit Nike Running growth, roughly $1 billion added to Running over that period, and five
  points of “statement footwear” running share gained across North America and Western Europe in fiscal
  2026. These are company-reported metrics but are meaningful early proof points.
- Global football, training, tennis, golf and ACG showed pockets of growth. The second half of fiscal
  2027 includes more than a dozen new Sportswear footwear styles.
- Wholesale relationships are healing: fiscal-2026 wholesale revenue grew 4% currency-neutral, and
  Nike refreshed more than 15,000 wholesale spaces.

### What is still broken

- Sportswear and Jordan Streetwear—about half of company revenue—are expected to remain negative in
  fiscal 2027 before possible second-half improvement.
- Fiscal-2026 footwear revenue was flat reported and down 2% currency-neutral; units fell 1%, and mix
  reduced average selling price.
- Nike Digital revenue fell to $8.6 billion from $9.6 billion as traffic declined. A deliberately
  smaller but full-price digital business can be healthier, but revenue decline is not proof of that
  outcome.
- Inventory was flat at $7.50 billion while reported revenue was flat and currency-neutral revenue
  fell. The balance-sheet inventory reserve improved to $213 million from $233 million, but EMEA still
  had elevated inventory and promotions; management is explicitly tightening future buys.
- Converse revenue fell 31% and EBIT fell 93% in fiscal 2026. This is a separate turnaround inside the
  larger turnaround.

**Conclusion:** brand equity is impaired, not destroyed. Running demonstrates that Nike can still win
when product, sport marketing and distribution align. The investment question is whether that playbook
can scale into the much larger lifestyle businesses before competitors entrench further.

## Elliott Hill's turnaround

Hill became CEO in October 2024 after a long Nike career that included leading consumer and marketplace
operations. The strategy is directionally correct:

1. restore sport-specific teams and accountability;
2. accelerate differentiated performance product;
3. reduce over-supplied classics and discounting;
4. rebuild wholesale while making Digital premium again;
5. localize product and execution, especially in China;
6. simplify supply chain and technology costs.

Evidence of progress exists in running, North American wholesale, lower off-price digital selling in
EMEA, better full-price realization in China and sequential margin improvement. But headline revenue,
Digital, China, Sportswear and Jordan do not yet confirm a full turnaround. Nike also changed CFOs in
August 2026, appointing David Denton, which may improve operating discipline but adds transition risk.

The two most important near-term checkpoints are:

- **the next quarterly result**, for whether fiscal-2027 revenue remains within the low-to-mid-single-
  digit decline range and gross margin expands excluding unusual items; and
- **Investor Day on November 16-17, 2026**, when management plans to describe the next phase after
  “Win Now.”

## Balance sheet, cash flow, buybacks and dividend

### Balance sheet

At 2026-05-31 Nike had:

- $7.56 billion cash plus $1.46 billion short-term investments;
- $2.00 billion current debt plus $5.94 billion long-term debt;
- $24.60 billion current assets versus $12.55 billion current liabilities;
- approximately $1.09 billion of net cash before operating-lease liabilities.

This is a strong balance sheet. A $2 billion debt maturity in fiscal 2027 is manageable against liquidity.
Solvency is not the investment risk; brand relevance and normalized profitability are.

### Cash flow and capital returns

Fiscal-2026 operating cash flow was $2.87 billion and capital expenditures were $684 million, leaving
about **$2.18 billion of simple FCF**. Dividends consumed $2.41 billion, so the dividend was approximately
110% of FCF in the trough year. The current annualized dividend is about $1.64 per share, a **4.27% yield**
at $38.40. It equals roughly 78% of reported fiscal-2026 EPS and 104% of management's $1.58 EPS excluding
the tariff recovery.

Nike can bridge a temporary coverage shortfall from its balance sheet, but the dividend is not free money:
it depends on the turnaround. Buybacks are not an active near-term floor. Nike paused repurchases in Q1
fiscal 2026, spent only $146 million on repurchases for the year versus $2.99 billion in fiscal 2025, and
repurchased no shares in Q4. Approximately $5.9 billion remains authorized.

For Hooman's growth-first objective, the 4%+ yield is a useful return component and a valuation signal,
not a reason to ignore operating deterioration.

## AI disruption and benefit

Nike is **less directly exposed to AI substitution than Adobe or many software businesses**. Generative
AI cannot replace physical shoes, athlete credibility, retail placement or decades of accumulated brand
association. That makes Nike a useful source of business-model diversification from Hooman's very large
Adobe exposure.

AI is not a complete shield, however:

- cheaper design, content creation, personalization and digital merchandising can help smaller brands
  compete for attention;
- better demand forecasting and rapid product iteration can make already-agile competitors faster;
- algorithm-driven shopping weakens the advantage of owning a destination website if consumers discover
  products elsewhere;
- Nike's own technology and demand-planning execution has to improve for AI to create value.

Nike says it is investing in advanced tools to improve speed, precision and reliability across product
creation, manufacturing, planning and product movement. That is a plausible cost and working-capital
benefit, but Nike has not disclosed AI-attributable revenue, savings or returns. **AI resilience is a
supporting reason to prefer Nike over a directly disrupted business, not an independent buy thesis.**

## Valuation

### Current versus trough and normalized economics

At $38.40 and approximately 1.483 billion shares, equity value is roughly **$56.9 billion**.

| Measure | Current/trough basis | Normalized basis | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| P/E | 18.3x reported FY26 EPS | 12.0-13.6x on $2.82-$3.20 normalized EPS | Reported EPS contains a one-time tariff benefit. |
| Adjusted P/E | 24.3x on $1.58 FY26 EPS excluding tariff recovery | — | Current operations are not optically cheap. |
| Price/sales | 1.23x FY26 revenue | — | Lowest fiscal-year-end vicinity in at least a decade. |
| Price/FCF | 26.1x FY26 FCF | about 12.4x eight-year median FCF | The thesis depends on cash-flow normalization. |
| Dividend yield | 4.27% | — | Attractive, but FY26 FCF did not fully cover dividends. |

The normalized EPS range applies a **9%-10% net margin** to current fiscal-2026 revenue and divides by
current shares. That is below Nike's 2019-2024 non-pandemic margin peaks and does not assume the old
valuation multiple. The eight-year fiscal-2019-to-2026 median FCF is approximately $4.61 billion.

### Own-history context

Using the closing price nearest each May fiscal year-end and that year's diluted EPS, Nike's fiscal-year-
end P/E had a median of roughly **31.7x from 2016 through 2024**. The comparable price/sales median was
about **3.23x**. Current multiples are far lower.

That historical premium should not be treated as fair value. It included stronger growth, lower rates,
greater confidence in Nike's moat and, in some years, temporarily depressed earnings. A sensible base
case uses a 16x-18x terminal P/E, not 30x.

These are underwriting ranges, not price targets. They use the $38.40 starting price and include estimated

The wide range is the point. NKE offers meaningful upside if normalization occurs, but a permanent brand

## Entry framework

| Price band | Action | Valuation logic |
|---|---|---|
| Above $50 | **Do not initiate without confirmed recovery** | Roughly 16-18x normalized EPS; margin of safety largely gone. |
| $36-$40 | **0.25% starter only** | Current band; attractive on normalized economics, not on trough earnings. |
| $30-$35 | **Add toward 0.5% if thesis remains intact** | Roughly 9-12x normalized EPS and about a 4.7%-5.5% dividend yield. |
| Below $30 | **Re-underwrite; do not buy mechanically** | Price likely signals new information. Buy only if failure conditions have not occurred. |

An evidence-based add can occur above $40 if Nike proves revenue stabilization and adjusted margin recovery.
Price confirmation is less important than business confirmation.

## Catalysts

1. Fiscal-2027 gross-margin expansion excluding tariff refunds and other unusual items.
2. Continued double-digit Running growth broadening into training, basketball, football and ACG.
3. Sportswear and Jordan Streetwear improving in the second half of fiscal 2027.
4. Wholesale growth accompanied by retailer sell-through, rather than inventory merely shifting from Nike
   to partners.
5. Lower promotion and better full-price realization in Digital, EMEA and China.
6. Investor Day on **2026-11-16 and 2026-11-17**, with credible long-term revenue, margin and capital-allocation
   targets.
7. New CFO David Denton demonstrating cost, working-capital and capital-allocation discipline.

## Failure conditions

Stop averaging down and reassess if any combination of the following occurs:

1. Fiscal-2027 currency-neutral revenue declines materially worse than the low-to-mid-single-digit path
   discussed for the first half, without a deliberate inventory-cleanup explanation.
2. Sportswear and Jordan Streetwear remain negative after the promised second-half fiscal-2027 improvement
   window, or performance growth fails to broaden beyond Running.
3. Greater China continues double-digit declines through fiscal 2027 with no improvement in full-price
   realization or local product relevance.
4. Gross margin excluding tariff/refund effects fails to improve from roughly 40.8%, or EBIT margin cannot
   credibly recover toward double digits by fiscal 2028.
5. Inventory grows faster than sales for multiple quarters, inventory reserves rise, or wholesale sell-in
   outpaces consumer sell-through.
6. FCF remains below dividends for a second year and management funds the payout with debt or forgoes
   necessary reinvestment.
7. Management resumes large buybacks before operating cash flow covers dividends, capex and turnaround needs.
8. The November Investor Day relies on brand language without quantified milestones and accountable timelines.

## Portfolio fit

- **Positive:** NKE diversifies economic exposure away from Adobe and software; direct AI substitution risk is
  low; its dividend adds cash return; the balance sheet limits ruin risk.
- **Negative:** It is still a cyclical equity and cannot protect a 3-5-year house horizon. NKE and LULU share
  consumer-discretionary, apparel, brand-fashion, traffic and execution factors. Two cheap charts are not two
  independent diversifiers.
- **Broad-index overlap:** The strategic global/U.S. index core already owns Nike. A direct position is only
  justified if Hooman wants concentrated turnaround alpha and accepts idiosyncratic loss.
- **Sizing:** Keep NKE at 0.25%-0.5%; keep NKE plus LULU at or below the 1% speculation cap. If conviction in
  NKE rises, funding should come from the speculation allocation or a deliberate rotation from LULU—not from
  creating a new sleeve.
