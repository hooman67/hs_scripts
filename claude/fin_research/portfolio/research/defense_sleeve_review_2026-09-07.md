**As of:** 2026-09-07 (markets last closed 2026-09-04)

**Investor context:** Growth first; U.S. tax resident and Canadian citizen; 3–5-year house horizon;

## Executive conclusion

**A dedicated defense allocation is justified, but 2% is slightly larger than necessary and ITA/PPA
are not the best implementations.** The demand thesis is unusually durable: NATO Europe and Canada
were estimated to raise real defense spending 15.9% in 2025, NATO allies committed to 3.5% of GDP for
core defense plus as much as 1.5% for defense/security infrastructure by 2035, and the largest U.S.
primes have multi-year backlogs. Defense also diversifies Hooman's Adobe/technology and commodity
risks better than another conventional growth sleeve.

The caveats are important. Defense remains equity, not a house-reserve hedge. Procurement can be
delayed or cancelled, fixed-price programs can destroy margins, and the favorable spending story is
widely recognized. Current ETF portfolio P/Es remain approximately 28–34x even after meaningful
pullbacks.

## Should the portfolio own defense at all?

### The case for a dedicated tilt

1. **The spending regime has changed.** NATO's 2025 estimates put U.S. defense spending at $980B and
   NATO Europe/Canada at $608B, up from $935B and $516B in 2024. In constant 2021 dollars, Europe and
   Canada rose 15.94% in 2025 after 18.59% in 2024. Their defense share of GDP rose from 1.99% to 2.27%.
2. **The 2035 commitment creates a long runway.** At The Hague, allies committed to at least 3.5% of
   GDP for core defense and up to 1.5% for infrastructure, networks, resilience and industrial capacity.
   The target is political rather than contractual, but even partial implementation implies years of
   procurement growth.
3. **Backlogs make the story more than a slogan.** At 2026 Q2, RTX reported $289B total backlog,
   including $119B defense; Lockheed Martin reported $230.4B; Northrop Grumman $104.7B; and General
   Dynamics $136.5B. These figures include different mixes and are not directly comparable, but they
   demonstrate substantial funded/unfunded demand visibility.
4. **Replenishment and modernization overlap.** Missile and munition replacement, air and missile
   defense, naval capacity, autonomous systems, sensors, secure communications, cyber, space and AI
   are receiving funding simultaneously. That favors a basket over trying to select one platform.
5. **The factor exposure is genuinely different.** Using adjusted daily returns from SHLD's inception
   through 2026-09-04, SHLD's correlation was about 0.48 with VTI and 0.13 with Adobe. ITA/PPA/XAR
   correlations with VTI were 0.65/0.71/0.70. Defense is still equity risk, but it is not another
   software or commodity bet.

### Why not simply rely on VTI and VXUS?

Vanguard's 2026-07-31 full holdings show that VTI had approximately:

- **1.38%** in a strict basket of U.S. defense primes/technology names such as RTX, PLTR, LMT, GD,
  NOC, LHX, LDOS, BWXT and HII; and
- **2.37%** in the broader ITA/XAR aerospace-and-defense universe after adding commercially exposed
  GE Aerospace, Boeing, Howmet, TransDigm and suppliers.

VXUS held approximately **0.74%** in the major non-U.S. names represented by SHLD—BAE Systems,
Rheinmetall, Hanwha Aerospace, Thales, Leonardo, Saab, Elbit, Kongsberg and others. Including mixed
commercial/defense aerospace companies would raise the VXUS figure modestly.

The live portfolio currently owns only **$5,316 of VTI and $3,448 of VXUS**. Their embedded defense/
aerospace exposure is therefore only approximately **$150**, or about **0.02% of the total portfolio**.
Even after the 20% global-equity target is fully funded as 11% VTI plus 9% VXUS, strict defense
look-through would be only about **0.22%**; the broad aerospace-and-defense definition would be roughly
**0.35–0.40%**.

The accepted nuclear sleeve also matters. A 0.5% direct BWXT target plus BWXT's approximately 5.4%
weight inside the 1.5% NLR target gives approximately **0.58% portfolio exposure to BWXT**. BWXT has
major U.S. naval nuclear/defense operations, so the portfolio is not truly defense-free even without a
dedicated fund.

**Conclusion:** broad indexes alone underweight the thesis, but the combination of broad-index
look-through and BWXT argues for a **1.5% dedicated sleeve rather than 2%**. At target, total strict
defense exposure would land around 2.3% after including SHLD, BWXT/NLR and the broad core.

## What can go wrong?

- **Policy is not a contract.** The NATO 2035 target can be delayed by elections, deficits or a change
  in the security environment. Continuing resolutions can postpone U.S. awards even when long-run
  appropriations remain favorable.
- **Backlog does not equal profit.** Backlog includes unfunded amounts and can be reduced. Fixed-price
  development programs, inflation, supply-chain problems, labor shortages and technical failures can
  produce charges despite strong orders.
- **The buyer has unusual power.** Governments control specifications, export approvals, pricing,
  reimbursement and termination rights. Political pressure can cap margins when profits appear high.
- **Commercial aerospace is cyclical.** ITA, PPA and XAR hold large commercial-aircraft and supplier
  exposures. Airline cycles, Boeing execution and engine/supply-chain problems can overwhelm defense
  spending for portions of those funds.
- **Technology baskets add valuation risk.** SHLD's largest holding is PLTR at 10.54%; drone, space and
  small defense-technology holdings can be highly volatile or unprofitable.
- **This is not a crash hedge.** Three-year annualized volatility was approximately 19–24% across the
  reviewed funds. They can fall with equities and should not be funded from the protected house reserve.
- **Ethical/regulatory risk is not zero.** Weapons exposure may conflict with personal restrictions;
  sanctions, export controls and controversy screens can change the investable universe.

they can create **PFIC reporting and punitive U.S. tax treatment** for a U.S. tax resident. Do not use a
foreign-domiciled ETF in a U.S. taxable account without cross-border tax advice. U.S.-domiciled SHLD,
XAR, PPA and ITA avoid that specific structure problem.

Leveraged defense products are rejected.

### Monitoring and exit rules

- **HOLD/ADD:** major NATO budgets keep growing faster than inflation; prime backlog/book-to-bill stays
  healthy; margins and cash conversion do not deteriorate materially.
- **PAUSE:** SHLD exceeds 30x earnings without corresponding estimate growth, PLTR exceeds 15%, or the
  top five exceed 50% of fund assets.
- **TRIM:** the dedicated sleeve exceeds 2% of the portfolio from appreciation; return it to 1.5%.
- **SELL/THESIS BREAK:** core NATO spending commitments reverse, major members cut real procurement for
  two consecutive budget cycles, or widespread cancellations/charges show that backlog is not converting
  into economic profit.
- A ceasefire or peace agreement alone is **not** automatically a sell signal: replenishment and the
  long-term NATO capability gap can persist. It becomes a sell signal only if budgets/orders follow down.

## Dividends and tax/account implementation

This is **not a dividend sleeve**. Current indicated/trailing yields are only about 0.3–0.7%; the thesis
depends on earnings and valuation, not income.

All four preferred candidates are U.S.-domiciled equity ETFs:

- no Schedule K-1;
- ordinary Form 1099-DIV reporting in a U.S. taxable account;
- distributions may qualify for reduced U.S. rates to the extent the underlying dividends and holding
  periods qualify;
- taxable is a reasonable location because yields and turnover-related distributions are modest;
- SHLD's foreign holdings can suffer source-country withholding inside the fund, and its current foreign
  share is likely too low for it to pass through a foreign-tax credit to shareholders;
- direct ownership in an RRSP can preserve U.S.-treaty tax deferral for an eligible U.S. resident under
  Revenue Procedure 2014-55, but FBAR/Form 8938 and Canadian-broker restrictions still need separate
  compliance. The low yield gives no strong reason to consume scarce RRSP capacity for this sleeve.

Prefer the U.S. taxable account if USD is already available. Do not convert CAD solely to create this
position unless the all-in FX spread is immaterial. This is tax-structure guidance, not individualized
cross-border tax advice.
