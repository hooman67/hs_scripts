# The Trade Desk (TTD) Buy Review

**As of:** 2026-09-07. U.S. markets were closed on 2026-09-07, so market data uses the
2026-09-04 close of **$14.43**.  
**Mandate:** Determine whether TTD is a good company, why it fell, whether it is genuinely cheap,
and whether it belongs in Hooman's portfolio.

## Bottom line

**Verdict: WATCH; do not buy before the next earnings report. TTD is now statistically cheap, but
the operating reset is too fresh to distinguish a temporary execution failure from a structural loss
of share.**

The Trade Desk still owns a valuable franchise: an independent, scaled demand-side advertising
platform; more than 95% reported customer retention; direct integrations with more than 430 exchanges,
publishers and supply-side platforms; more than 370 data partners; a net-cash balance sheet; and real
cash generation. Its neutrality—TTD does not own media—is an authentic advantage when agencies and
advertisers want transparent access to the open internet rather than buying from a platform that also
sells its own inventory.

The stock is not merely “cheap compared with a bubble.” At $14.43 it is approximately **2.3x trailing
revenue, 1.9x enterprise value/revenue, 17x trailing GAAP earnings, 8x headline free cash flow and
roughly 17x free cash flow after treating stock compensation as an economic cost**. That is below
several slower or lower-quality independent ad-tech peers and dramatically below TTD's own peak
valuation.

But the cheapness is attached to a real break in the operating trend:

- revenue growth slowed from **26% in 2024** to **18% in 2025**, **12% in Q1 2026**, and **3% in Q2**;
- Q3 2026 guidance of at least $650 million implies approximately **12% year-over-year contraction**;
- Q2 existing-client gross spend declined even though reported customer retention remained above 95%;
- Q2 adjusted EBITDA fell 11%, and Q3 guidance implies about a **50% decline** from Q3 2025;
- on 2026-09-03 the company approved a **15% workforce reduction**, only four weeks after Q2 results.

That last disclosure makes buying immediately an unnecessary event bet. The next report should reveal
whether the retrenchment is a deliberate margin reset around a still-healthy platform or a response to
lost wallet share and weaker demand.

### Recommendation for Hooman

- **No order now at $14.43.** Wait for Q3 results and the first post-restructuring outlook.
- If Hooman wants to pre-commit to a contrarian entry, authorize only a **0.25% starter at $12.50-$13.25**
  after confirming there is no new guidance cut. At a $662,000 portfolio value, 0.25% is about **$1,655**,
  or approximately 125 shares at $13.25.
- A better-quality entry is **0.25% at up to $18** after evidence that existing-client spend has turned
  positive, forward revenue is no longer contracting, and adjusted EBITDA margin is at least 28%-30%.
- Increase only after a second confirming quarter; **hard maximum 0.5%** until TTD again produces
  double-digit growth without take-rate/accounting assistance and reduces stock compensation below
  12% of revenue.
- Fund only from the **1% Speculation** sleeve. Do not fund it from the index core, protected house
  capital, or by adding more growth exposure. TTD, LULU and any NKE purchase must collectively remain
  under the cap. If NKE reaches its accepted 0.5% ceiling while LULU remains near 0.48%, there is no room
  for TTD.
- TTD pays no dividend, so the RRSP withholding advantage is irrelevant. If eventually purchased, a
  USD taxable account is the cleanest default, subject to Hooman's cross-border tax professional.

## What caused the collapse?

TTD closed at $141.53 on 2024-12-04 and $14.43 on 2026-09-04, a decline of approximately **90%**.
The fall combines an extreme starting valuation with several genuine operating disappointments.

### 1. The starting valuation was a bubble

Around the December 2024 peak, approximately 502 million diluted shares valued TTD near $71 billion.
Against 2024 revenue of $2.445 billion, that was roughly **29x sales**. The market was capitalizing TTD
as if 20%-plus growth and premium margins would persist for many years. Even flawless execution would
have left substantial multiple-compression risk.

### 2. The first self-inflicted miss broke the premium narrative

TTD guided Q4 2024 revenue to at least $756 million but delivered $741 million. The shortfall was only
about 2%, yet management said it was disappointed to miss its own expectations and had reorganized in
December. The stock fell 33% on 2025-02-13 because a company priced for perfection had demonstrated
that its forecasting and execution were not perfect.

### 3. The forward growth trajectory kept weakening

| Period | Revenue | YoY growth | Adjusted EBITDA | Margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FY 2024 | $2.445B | 26% | not used here | — |
| FY 2025 | $2.896B | 18% | $1.196B | 41% |
| Q1 2026 | $689M | 12% | $206M | 30% |
| Q2 2026 | $715M | 3% | $241M | 34% |
| Q3 2026 guidance | at least $650M | about **-12%** | about $160M | about 25% |

The stock fell 39% on 2025-08-08 after Q2 results, a lower forward growth profile and a CFO transition.
It fell another 22% on 2026-08-07 after Q2 2026 results and the contractionary Q3 guide. Those are not
normal reactions to a healthy 20%-growth compounder; they show repeated repricing of the business model.

### 4. The September restructuring confirms an operating reset

On 2026-09-03, TTD approved eliminating approximately 15% of its workforce, with $39-$51 million of
cash restructuring charges expected in Q3. Cost reduction can eventually restore margins, but a large
cut immediately after weak guidance is also evidence that prior staffing and growth expectations were
too high. Investors should not assume all savings drop to profit: client service, platform operations
and product velocity matter in a competitive market.

## Business quality and moat

### What remains attractive

- **Independent buy-side position:** TTD buys across the open internet without owning media. That
  reduces the conflict inherent when Google or Amazon recommends buying inventory it also sells.
- **Scale and connectivity:** more than 430 inventory integrations and 370 data vendors create a broad
  marketplace and substantial technical integration work for a challenger to reproduce.
- **Agency workflow and switching costs:** campaigns, measurement, data, training and agency processes
  become embedded. Reported customer retention has exceeded 95% for more than a decade.
- **Omnichannel reach:** CTV, video, audio, mobile, display and digital-out-of-home let a buyer manage
  frequency and measurement across channels.
- **Net cash and profitability:** unlike many ad-tech companies, TTD is GAAP profitable and can fund
  innovation through a downturn without raising capital.

### What is weaker than the retention statistic suggests

TTD's retention figure is not a disclosed net-revenue-retention measure. A client can remain active
while spending materially less. Q2 2026 is the proof: the company retained more than 95% of customers,
yet **gross spend from existing clients decreased**. New clients and pricing/mix offset that decline.

Concentration also exists above the contractual-client level. In 2025, two advertising holding
companies represented **30% of gross billings** in aggregate. They are not generally single contractual
clients—TTD contracts with their agencies separately—but budget consolidation or agency leverage can
still affect pricing and wallet share.

### Take rate: do not over-read the apparent improvement

| Year | Gross spend | Revenue | Revenue / gross spend |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2024 | $12.04B | $2.445B | 20.3% |
| 2025 | $13.40B | $2.896B | 21.6% |

This ratio is only an **implied monetization ratio**, not a clean contractual take rate. Revenue includes
platform fees plus value-added services and data, and the company says it can vary with service mix,
pricing, discounts and whether supplier-provided components are recorded as a revenue reduction or as
platform-operations expense. Q2 2026 revenue was helped by higher value-added-service pricing and by
some supplier costs moving from revenue offsets into operating expense. That can lift reported revenue
while pressuring margins; it is not equivalent to stronger underlying ad spend.

## Competitive reality

### Google / DV360

Google has the strongest combination of advertiser demand, identity, measurement, YouTube inventory,
Chrome/Android distribution and cloud/AI capacity. Its vertical integration creates conflicts that
support TTD's neutral pitch, while the U.S. ad-tech antitrust case could reduce some exclusionary
advantages. But regulatory relief is slow and uncertain; it does not transfer Google's data to TTD.

### Amazon DSP

Amazon can join shopping intent and transaction data to Prime Video, Twitch and third-party inventory.
That is particularly formidable in retail media and lower-funnel measurement. TTD can remain the
cross-retailer neutral layer, but it cannot duplicate Amazon's first-party purchase graph.

### Meta

Meta is not primarily an open-internet DSP, yet it competes directly for advertiser budgets. Its vast
engagement data, owned inventory and automated AI campaign products can deliver performance without an
agency needing an independent DSP. A budget shifted to Meta is a budget unavailable to TTD.

### Microsoft and AppLovin

Microsoft has identity, browser, search, cloud and advertising assets, though it has shown less
consistent focus than Google or Amazon. AppLovin is strongest in mobile-app performance advertising
and combines buying, mediation and exchange economics. It is growing much faster, but its vertical
integration and mobile concentration make it a different—and currently far more richly valued—risk.

### Smaller independent peers

DoubleVerify is measurement rather than a direct DSP substitute; Magnite and PubMatic are mainly
supply-side. They compete for pieces of the same ad-tech economics and illustrate how quickly pricing,
intermediation and customer concentration can change, but none matches TTD's scaled independent
buy-side position.

## AI: a real tool, not a guaranteed moat

### The credible upside

- More inference can improve bid selection, pacing, frequency management, identity resolution,
  relevance and measurement—areas where tiny efficiency gains matter across billions of auctions.
- TTD sees cross-publisher demand and outcome signals that a single publisher does not see. Kokai can
  use that breadth to allocate budgets across channels rather than optimize only one garden.
- Agentic campaign tooling and first-party data integrations can reduce agency labor and increase the
  number of campaigns economically served, especially in the mid-market.
- AI-generated content may expand advertising inventory and creative testing, increasing decisioning
  complexity and the value of an effective buying layer.

### The risks

- **Models commoditize:** cloud providers and competitors can buy similar model capability. The moat is
  proprietary feedback data and distribution, not the word “AI.”
- **Hyperscaler data advantage:** Google, Amazon and Meta possess deterministic search, purchase and
  engagement data that TTD cannot independently recreate.
- **Customer disintermediation:** agencies and large brands can use automated platform tools, clean
  rooms or direct publisher deals, reducing the value captured by an intermediary.
- **Cost pressure:** TTD says it will keep investing in hosting infrastructure for AI and machine
  learning. Platform-operations expense rose 25% in H1 2026, much faster than revenue.
- **Measurement risk:** generative answers and agentic commerce can reduce open-web clicks and change
  where consumers discover products, potentially shifting inventory toward closed AI/search platforms.

**Conclusion:** TTD is an AI user and potential productivity beneficiary, not an AI hedge. Buying it
would add to Hooman's application/software and digital-advertising exposure, not diversify it.

## Financial quality

### GAAP versus adjusted profit

| Metric | FY 2025 | H1 2026 | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Revenue | $2.896B | $1.404B | Growth has sharply decelerated. |
| GAAP net income | $443M | $104M | Profitable, but H1 fell 26% YoY. |
| GAAP net margin | 15% | 7% | Material compression. |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $1.196B | $447M | Excludes large stock compensation. |
| Adjusted EBITDA margin | 41% | 32% | Still strong, but falling. |
| Stock compensation | $491M | $219M | 17% and 16% of revenue, respectively. |

Adjusted EBITDA is useful for operating comparison but overstates distributable economics because
stock compensation is recurring and material. H1 stock compensation was **2.1 times GAAP net income**.
The 2021 CEO Performance Option made historical SBC especially lumpy; its accounting expense was
expected to be fully recognized by Q1 2026, but ordinary employee equity awards remain substantial.

### Free-cash-flow quality

TTM through Q2 2026 calculations from the filings:

- operating cash flow: approximately **$1.082B**;
- property/equipment plus capitalized software purchases: approximately **$234M**;
- headline free cash flow: approximately **$848M**, or a 12.5% equity FCF yield;
- TTM stock compensation: approximately **$452M**;
- FCF after treating SBC as an economic cost: approximately **$396M**, or a 5.8% equity yield.

Neither extreme is perfect. Subtracting every dollar of SBC assumes equity is equivalent to cash,
while ignoring it assumes dilution is free. The practical test is shares outstanding and cash spent
to prevent dilution. TTD spent $1.388 billion repurchasing shares in 2025 and $241 million in H1 2026;
diluted average shares fell 1.7% in 2025 and 5.2% year over year in Q2 2026. Buybacks have therefore
more than offset current issuance, but at a very large cash cost. The program is explicitly described
as helping offset employee-stock dilution, not as a commitment to return surplus capital.

### Balance sheet and capital allocation

At 2026-06-30 TTD had approximately **$1.12B cash plus $362M short-term investments**, no conventional
funded debt, and about $434M of lease liabilities. Net cash after leases was about $1.05B. Accounts
receivable and payable are large because TTD bills gross media spend while recognizing revenue net;
their scale should not be mistaken for funded leverage.

The remaining repurchase authorization was $269 million at Q2 end. Repurchases at prices far below
the prior grants can create value if the franchise recovers; they cannot compensate for a permanently
shrinking revenue base.

## Governance

- Class B shares receive ten votes each and automatically convert to Class A no later than
  2035-12-22.
- As of the 2026 proxy record date, founder/CEO Jeff Green held approximately **49.7% of total voting
  power** and 97.6% of Class B shares. Outside shareholders cannot meaningfully change control.
- The 2021 CEO Performance Option has a $68.29 exercise price and eight price hurdles from $90 to
  $340. Two tranches vested before the collapse; 14.4 million option shares shown at 2025 year-end
  were unexercisable and deeply out of the money at $14.43.
- Founder control supports long-horizon investment and TTD's independence, but it also weakens the
  board's ability to correct strategy or capital allocation if Green is wrong.

## Catalysts

- Q3 or Q4 forward guidance shows revenue growth returning to at least low single digits.
- Existing-client gross spend turns positive and growth no longer depends primarily on new logos or
  value-added-service pricing/accounting mix.
- The 15% workforce reduction restores adjusted EBITDA margins without hurting retention or product
  delivery.
- CTV and retail-media partnerships translate into measurable gross-spend acceleration.
- Kokai adoption improves campaign outcomes and value-added-service utilization without further
  platform-cost inflation.
- Google ad-tech remedies improve neutral access to inventory or reduce self-preferencing.
- Repurchases at current prices produce durable per-share growth rather than merely offsetting SBC.

## Failure conditions

Do not buy—or sell a starter—if any two occur:

- existing-client spend remains negative for two consecutive quarters;
- reported revenue remains negative year over year beyond Q3 2026;
- customer retention falls below 95% or a major agency holding company materially reduces spend;
- adjusted EBITDA margin remains below 28% after restructuring benefits begin;
- stock compensation remains above 15% of revenue while diluted shares stop falling;
- cash/investments fall below $1 billion because buybacks mask weak economics;
- OpenPath, UID2 or CTV access loses material partners, or walled gardens further restrict inventory;
- management issues another guide-down or announces another major reorganization before stabilization.

## Portfolio fit

TTD is not a solution to Hooman's central portfolio problem. Approximately $732,000 of Adobe equity
already creates extreme employer, U.S.-software, digital-media and AI exposure. Planned or existing
GOOGL and AMZN add the two competitors with the strongest search, commerce, cloud and advertising
data. TTD would add a volatile, cyclical ad-tech recovery bet that can fall at the same time as Adobe
and mega-cap growth.

There is one favorable fit point: TTD is an independent open-internet platform, so it is not identical
to a publisher or creative-software company. But that distinction is too narrow to justify a strategic
sleeve. Broad-index holdings already own TTD at market weight.
