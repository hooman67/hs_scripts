### Main NLR risks

- **It remains speculative.** SMR, OKLO, X-energy, and NNE alone are 12.22%; several miners are pre-production.
- **Uranium-price and power-demand factors differ.** A weak uranium spot market can hurt miners even while nuclear generators benefit from strong electricity demand.
- **International policy risk matters.** China, Kazakhstan, and other jurisdictions introduce state ownership, sanctions, currency, and market-access risks.
- **Concentration is meaningful.** The top ten are 60.8%; this is diversification within a narrow theme, not broad-equity diversification.
- **Index composition can migrate.** NLR's current portfolio is much more fuel/miner and advanced-reactor oriented than older descriptions of it as a utility-heavy nuclear fund.

## Company-by-company decisions

### CCJ — TRIM to 0.75%; retain as a direct anchor

Hooman's favorable thesis is substantively sound:

- Cameco is one of the world's largest uranium suppliers, with tier-one Canadian assets, fuel services, a 49% interest in Westinghouse, and a disciplined long-term contracting strategy.
- Q2 2026 guidance retained **19.5–21.5M lb** attributable uranium production and **29–32M lb** sales/deliveries.
- 2026 consolidated revenue guidance is **C$3.32–C$3.57B**.
- At 2026-06-30, Cameco had **C$1.1B cash, C$1.0B debt, and a C$1.0B undrawn revolver**.
- First-half adjusted EBITDA was **C$899M**, despite Westinghouse comparisons against a one-time Czech-project contribution in the prior year.

The problem is valuation and sizing, not business quality. At $100.74, the approximate US$43.8B equity value is more than 30 times annualized first-half adjusted EBITDA before adjusting for seasonality, and current earnings remain far below what the share price assumes. Operational disruptions, Kazakhstan/Inkai exposure, long-term contract timing, uranium-price volatility, and Westinghouse execution remain real.

**Action:** keep the company, but trim from 150 to approximately **49 shares**. A 1.0% NLR position adds another 0.08% portfolio look-through to CCJ, so effective CCJ exposure remains approximately **0.83%**.

### CEG — ADD toward 0.4%, but stage the purchase

CEG is the best direct match for operating nuclear generation monetizing AI/data-center power demand:

- Q2 adjusted operating EPS was $2.55 and 2026 guidance increased to **$11.50–$12.50**.
- It signed approximately **920 MW** of long-term nuclear PPAs; the contracts average **18.5 years** and take expected baseload clean generation under long-term agreements to about **30% by 2032**.
- Its nuclear production tax credit provides downside support when eligible realized nuclear revenue falls below the inflation-adjusted threshold.
- Credit ratings remained **BBB+/Baa1**.

At $298.96, CEG trades at about **24.9x** the midpoint of 2026 adjusted EPS—not cheap. The Calpine acquisition raised long-term debt to approximately **$19.6B** and adds gas-generation, integration, and regulatory risk. Nuclear outages, power prices, PPA/FERC decisions, and execution matter.

**Action:** increase from 8 to approximately **9 shares**. The single additional share can be bought around a valuation of 23x midpoint guidance (about $276) or after the next earnings confirmation rather than chased at $299.

### BWXT — HOLD quality; trim only to fit the sleeve

BWXT has the strongest moat in the current group:

- It supplies naval nuclear propulsion and specialized nuclear components/services where qualification barriers and customer relationships are unusually durable.
- Q2 revenue rose 18% to **$901.6M**; 2026 revenue guidance is approximately **$3.8B**.
- Remaining performance obligations were **$8.40B**, with about 55% expected to be recognized through 2027.
- 2026 adjusted EPS guidance rose to **$4.70–$4.80** and FCF guidance to **$345–$360M**.

Quality is already priced in. At $157.59, BWXT is approximately **33x** midpoint adjusted EPS and **41x** midpoint FCF. It also had about **$2.02B long-term debt versus $608M cash** after acquisitions. Government-budget timing, contract execution, nuclear-project delays, and integration are key risks.

**Action:** trim from 29 to approximately **15 shares**, retaining a 0.35% direct anchor. A 1.0% NLR position adds another 0.05% look-through.

## Tax and account structure

- NLR is a **U.S.-domiciled 1940 Act equity ETF**. It does not use a commodity-pool/K-1 structure and is not a foreign PFIC for a U.S. taxpayer.
- A U.S. taxable account is the simplest operational location for a tactical holding: normal Form 1099 reporting, tax-loss harvesting, and no foreign-account administration.
- An existing RRSP can hold NLR, and U.S. federal tax deferral may apply under the treaty/Revenue Procedure 2014-55. State treatment can differ from federal treatment, so confirm state and cross-border treatment before choosing the RRSP.
- Do not use the Canadian Sprott Physical Uranium Trust in a U.S. taxable account without specific PFIC advice. Sprott itself identifies its physical trusts as PFICs.
- The Canadian-versus-U.S. brokerage location does not itself create CAD exposure. NLR trades in USD, but its underlying company revenues and assets are global.

## Decision rules after implementation

- **Rebalance, do not chase:** review quarterly and rebalance when uranium/nuclear breaches 2.0% or 3.0%, not on daily price movement.
- **CCJ thesis break:** sustained mine execution failures, inability to fulfill contracted deliveries economically, deterioration in contracting discipline, or Westinghouse balance-sheet/cash-flow impairment.

- **BWXT thesis break:** material government-program cancellation, persistent contract execution losses, or deterioration in FCF conversion and leverage.
- **NLR review trigger:** methodology change, assets below $500M, persistent wide spreads, or a composition shift above 70% miners/fuel or above 20% pre-revenue reactor developers.
- **Valuation discipline:** a good nuclear thesis does not justify unlimited multiples. Add through scheduled tranches, not after vertical price moves.
