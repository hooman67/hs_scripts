## Executive conclusion

1. **Keep a small 2.5% dedicated sleeve, but define it honestly as `financials/insurance allocators`, not as a recession hedge.** The sleeve is justified as a modest value, capital-allocation and sector-diversification tilt because only 20% of the target portfolio is broad global equity and the rest deliberately omits much of the financial sector. It is not required for a complete portfolio, should not exceed 2.5%, and must be funded only from cash above the protected house reserve.
2. **Cigna is not a financial holding.** CI is a healthcare-benefits, PBM and specialty-care company. Reclassify its current 0.68% position to Healthcare. If retained, cap it at 1.0% and make it replace 1% of the broad-healthcare core rather than sit outside the 5% Healthcare budget.
3. **Use the 2.5% financial sleeve as 1.5% BRK.B + 1.0% FFH.** The current four Fairfax shares already fill approximately 1.0%. Buy Berkshire in two tranches toward roughly 20 B shares. This structure fits Hooman's growth-first preference better than a dividend-focused bank fund: both companies compound retained capital, operate insurance businesses, and own diversified operating/investment assets.
4. **Do not add a financial ETF on top of BRK.B and FFH.** At the target VTI/VXUS/VWO/KWEB weights, broad funds already contribute approximately 3.84% portfolio look-through to financials. The 2.5% direct sleeve takes total economic financial exposure to approximately 6.34%. A third financial vehicle adds complexity and overlap without solving a material gap.
5. **VFH is the best ETF fallback.** If Hooman decides two individual allocators violate his diversification preference, replace the 1.5% BRK.B target with 1.5% VFH; do not add VFH on top. VFH costs 0.09%, owns 426 stocks and covers banks, payments, capital markets, insurers and asset managers. XLF is slightly cheaper and vastly more liquid but much more concentrated. KIE is the better insurance-only ETF than IAK, but insurance is already supplied by BRK.B and FFH. KBE/KBWB are tactical bank bets, not necessary strategic holdings.
6. **Timing favors staged buying, not waiting indefinitely and not filling everything immediately.** Broad financial ETFs and bank funds are near 52-week highs, credit spreads are tight, and financial conditions are loose. Berkshire and Fairfax are more reasonably valued. Buy half the BRK.B target now, reserve the second half for approximately $490 or lower—or for the next results confirming book-value and operating-earnings progress. Hold FFH; do not chase more.

## Should this portfolio have a financial sleeve at all?

### The valid thesis

- **Diversification away from Hooman's dominant risks.** Adobe equity and employment already create extraordinary U.S.-technology exposure. The liquid portfolio also has deliberate commodity, energy, gold, uranium and Canadian concentration. Insurance allocators have different profit drivers: underwriting discipline, float, investment income and capital allocation.
- **A real valuation counterweight.** VFH's official July 2026 portfolio P/E was 16.2x versus VTI's 24.7x. KIE's official forward P/E was 11.27x. BRK.B is about 1.45x June book value and Fairfax about 1.28x June book value. These are less duration-sensitive valuations than the portfolio's high-growth technology exposure.
- **Capital returns can compound without requiring a high dividend.** Berkshire pays no dividend and resumed repurchases in 2026. Fairfax repurchased 4.2% of effective shares in the first half. CI pays a 2.21% indicated dividend but its healthcare economics, not its payout, determine whether it deserves a position.
- **Higher nominal yields help insurance investment income.** Berkshire held about $359.2B of cash, equivalents and Treasury bills at June 30. Fairfax held $71.7B of insurance investments, including $8.2B cash/short term and $37.6B government/high-quality corporate bonds. This is a meaningful earnings channel in a positive-rate regime.
- **AI is more likely to be a productivity tool than an existential threat.** Claims automation, underwriting, fraud detection, service and coding can lower costs. That is upside optionality, not a sufficient reason to buy: competitors can copy tools, savings can be passed to customers, biased models create regulatory/litigation exposure, and cyber/model risk rises.

### What the sleeve does not do

- **It is not a recession hedge.** Broad financials, banks and insurers can be hit by credit losses, falling asset prices, lower deal activity, deposit competition and catastrophe losses at the same time that equities decline. In the 2007–09 window, VFH's maximum drawdown was approximately 78% versus 55% for SPY; in the February–April 2020 shock, VFH fell approximately 44% versus 34% for SPY. Berkshire was more resilient but still suffered material equity drawdowns.
- **It does not protect the 3–5-year house purchase.** The entire sleeve is risk capital. Cash/T-bills/GICs remain the house protection.
- **It is not needed merely to “own every sector.”** VTI, VXUS and VWO already do that. The only defensible reason for a separate sleeve is a deliberate modest tilt toward value-priced financial/insurance compounders.
- **Banks are not automatically inflation hedges.** A steeper curve can improve net interest margins, but deposit betas, credit losses, securities marks and refinancing stress can overwhelm that benefit. P&C insurers can earn more on float, but inflation also raises claims severity.

### Current cycle

The macro backdrop is supportive but not cheap or low-risk:

- Effective fed funds was **3.63%** on 2026-09-03; the 2-year Treasury was **4.34%** and the 10-year **4.77%**, a modestly positive curve.
- The Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index was **-0.558** on 2026-08-28, indicating looser-than-average financial conditions.
- U.S. high-yield spreads were only **2.65 percentage points** on 2026-09-03, leaving little recession premium.
- Commercial-bank delinquency rates remained low in Q2 2026: approximately **1.42% for all loans** and **1.27% for business loans**.

That combination supports current earnings but argues against chasing banks after a strong run. It also means today's low credit losses should not be capitalized as permanent.

**Verdict on having the sleeve: conditionally sound at 2.5%; do not increase it.** If the broad global-equity core is eventually increased well beyond 20%, the separate sleeve can shrink or disappear.

### BRK.B — BUY in two tranches toward 1.5%

Berkshire is the best new direct holding for this specific portfolio, but not because it is a pure financial stock or a dividend play.

- June 30 shareholders' equity was **$747.9B**. With 1.431693M Class-A-equivalent shares outstanding, book value was approximately **$348.25 per B-equivalent share**. At the $506.03 September 4 close, the stock was about **1.45x book**.
- Berkshire held approximately **$359.2B** of consolidated cash, equivalents and Treasury bills at June 30, providing liquidity and optionality in a downturn.
- Insurance float was **$177.5B**, and underwriting generated a negative cost of float in the first half.
- First-half after-tax operating components before investment gains totaled approximately **$24.3B**. Simple annualization implies roughly a 4.5% operating-earnings yield at the current market value; this is a rough normalization, not guidance.
- Operating breadth is real: insurance, BNSF railroad, regulated energy/utilities, manufacturing/service/retail and a large public-equity portfolio. The five largest public-equity holdings were 66% of that portfolio, so this is diversified but not index-like.
- Berkshire repurchased **$4.8B** of stock in the first half of 2026, mostly in Q2, under a policy requiring management to believe price is below conservatively determined intrinsic value.

The negatives are size, succession risk, slower growth, catastrophe exposure, BHE wildfire/regulatory liabilities, acquisition discipline and equity-portfolio concentration. Berkshire's investment income already fell as short rates declined. It can outperform through resilience and capital allocation, but a return to its historical 20% compounding rate is not a reasonable base case at today's scale.

**Action:** target **1.5% / about $9,931 / 20 B shares**. Buy approximately 10 shares in the first tranche. Buy the remaining 10 shares at **$490 or lower**—about 1.41x June book—or after the next quarterly report confirms operating earnings and book-value growth. Pause new purchases above approximately **$555**—about 1.59x June book—unless book value or normalized earnings have increased enough to preserve the valuation.

**Invalidation:** persistent underwriting deterioration unrelated to catastrophes, major capital-allocation errors under the post-Buffett operating structure, unmanaged BHE liabilities, or valuation above 1.6x current book without commensurate earnings growth.

## Tax and account implementation

- **All ETF candidates here are U.S.-domiciled and normally report on Form 1099, not Schedule K-1.** They avoid the PFIC issue that can arise when a U.S. tax resident holds a Canadian-domiciled ETF in a taxable account.
- **BRK.B is the cleanest taxable-account holding** because Berkshire pays no dividend; U.S. tax is generally deferred until sale. The trade-off is no current income.
- **VFH/XLF/KIE/IAK/KBE/KBWB distribute dividends.** In a U.S. taxable account, qualified-dividend treatment depends on the fund's reported character and the investor's holding period; do not assume the full distribution is qualified before the year-end tax statement.
- **FFH is a Canadian operating company, not a Canadian ETF.** Its Canadian-source dividend is generally subject to treaty withholding in a U.S. taxable account, with a potential U.S. foreign-tax credit. Its roughly 0.9% yield makes this a minor economic issue, but account/broker reporting must be verified.
- **A qualifying RRSP generally receives U.S.-Canada treaty retirement-plan treatment and automatic U.S. tax deferral under Revenue Procedure 2014-55, subject to eligibility and reporting.** Confirm current broker withholding and cross-border treatment before relying on it.
- The brokerage country, settlement currency and ticker currency do not remove the issuer's economic currency exposure. Use USD for BRK.B/VFH because that is the native, liquid listing; use native TSX FFH in CAD rather than thin OTC FRFHF if the position is held at a broker with efficient TSX execution.
