# Gold-Miners Tactical Review — 2026-09-18

## Decision

Gold miners are **not** presently “the cheapest they have ever been,” especially relative to gold.
They are highly profitable at current bullion prices and headline earnings multiples look reasonable,
but the group has already rerated sharply. A small tactical allocation is defensible; a large immediate
purchase or mechanically raising total gold exposure to 20% is not.

The preferred action is **rotation before addition**:

1. Preserve physical gold, MNT and other long-term bullion holdings.
2. Keep the existing AEM position; do not chase it near current prices.
3. Trim expensive, oversized WPM toward its accepted target and redirect approximately **0.5% of the
   portfolio** into a diversified senior-miner ETF when the entry is more favorable.
4. Permit a second 0.5% ETF tranche only after a deeper correction or renewed operating confirmation.
5. Do not use GDXJ as the core miner vehicle.

This is a research conclusion, not a recorded trade or a change to the live allocation.

The current GDX/gold ratio remains below the extreme peaks reached in earlier commodity cycles, so miners
are not at an all-time relative extreme. But the opposite claim—that they have never been cheaper—is false.

## Why the Multiples Still Look Low

Current operating margins are exceptional because realized gold prices are far above all-in sustaining
costs. That produces apparently inexpensive price/earnings ratios and record free cash flow:

| Company | Forward P/E | Approx. FCF yield | Q2 realized gold | Q2 AISC | Balance sheet |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| AEM | 16.1x | 4.2% | $4,483/oz | $1,459/oz | $3.27B net cash |
| NEM | 12.1x | 6.8% | $4,414/oz | $1,621/oz | $3.4B net cash |
| KGC | 9.2x | 9.0% | $4,483/oz | $1,821/oz | $1.9B net cash |
| WPM | 30.4x | materially lower | Streamer | Not comparable | Asset-light but richly valued |

Those multiples are not directly comparable with stable industrial compounders. If gold falls to
$3,500/oz while costs remain unchanged, per-ounce margins would fall by roughly one-third for AEM, NEM
and KGC. At $3,000, margins would fall by about one-half. The market is discounting commodity cyclicality,
cost inflation, reserve replacement, jurisdiction, project execution and capital-allocation risk.

The miners are therefore **cash-generative at today's gold price, but not plainly cheap on normalized
earnings**.

## Company Assessment

### AEM — Keep, Do Not Chase

Agnico Eagle remains the highest-quality individual choice: strong jurisdictions, low relative costs,
excellent balance sheet and disciplined execution. Q2 free cash flow was $1.34B and net cash reached
$3.27B. However, the stock is no longer obviously cheap at roughly 16x forward earnings and a roughly
4.2% FCF yield. The company repurchased stock at an average $178.86 in Q2, below the approximately $200
market price used in this review.

**Action:** keep the existing 0.58%; add only after a materially better price or another quarter of
operational confirmation.

### NEM — Best Large-Cap Value/Quality Compromise

Newmont offers the strongest valuation among the two largest diversified producers: roughly 12x forward
earnings, approximately 6.8% FCF yield, net cash and a large remaining repurchase authorization. Its cost
base and operating complexity are worse than AEM's.

**Action:** the best individual alternative if deliberately choosing one stock, but an ETF is still a
better fit for this portfolio's diversification preference.

### KGC — Cheapest, but Higher Risk

Kinross is the cheapest of the reviewed majors and generates substantial cash. It also has the highest
reported AISC, greater project and jurisdiction risk, and more sensitivity to a lower gold price.

**Action:** speculation-sized only; not the core recommendation.

### WPM — Quality, but Too Expensive for the Current Weight

Wheaton's streaming model avoids much direct mine-operating risk, but the shares trade near 30x forward
earnings and roughly 7x book. The live 3.41% position is already above the accepted 1.92% target and is a
less compelling source of tactical torque than reasonably valued producers.

**Action:** fund any miner purchase primarily by trimming WPM rather than by increasing aggregate
precious-metals risk from cash.

RING's official data showed a 16.90 P/E, 3.53 price/book ratio and 40.42% three-year standard deviation.
GDX had approximately $28.5B in assets, compared with approximately $2.5B for RING. The annual fee

liquidity is worth more than the small fee saving.

**Preferred vehicle: GDX.** Use RING only if the expected holding period becomes clearly multi-year and
options/liquidity are unimportant.

## Position Size and Entry Plan

Do not treat the user's willingness to permit 20% total gold exposure as a target. Miners are leveraged
cyclical equities, not substitutes for physical gold's long-term role.

Recommended tactical miner structure:

| Component | Target/limit | Action |
|---|---:|---|
| Existing AEM | Approximately 0.6% | Keep |
| Existing EMPYF | Approximately 0.3% | Keep only as the speculative component; do not add |
| GDX | Initial 0.5%; maximum 1.0% | Add in stages, preferably funded from WPM |

- 0.5% is approximately **$3,413**.
- 1.0% is approximately **$6,826**.

1. No urgency at approximately $96.
2. Buy the first 0.5% near the 200-day average, approximately **$90–$91**, if the gold thesis and company

   costs remain controlled and free cash flow remains strong.
4. Do not average down if the decline is caused by persistent cost inflation, reserve impairment or a

If Hooman wants immediate exposure despite the valuation evidence, cap the immediate starter at 0.25%
and fund it by trimming WPM.

## Account Placement

For this tactical sleeve, prefer the **U.S. taxable brokerage using existing USD**, because:

- losses can be harvested if the tactical thesis fails;
- the capital is not trapped behind RRSP withdrawal consequences;
- GDX's dividend yield is low, so RRSP treaty treatment of U.S. dividends is not a major advantage; and
- a one-to-three-year holding can qualify for long-term U.S. capital-gains treatment if held over one year.

Use the RRSP instead only if the capital is unquestionably retirement-only and USD is already present.
Avoid a Canadian-listed miner ETF in a U.S. taxable account without confirming PFIC treatment.

## What Would Change the Recommendation?

Become more constructive if several of these occur together:

- GDX approaches or falls below its 200-day average without a breakdown in gold;
- the GDX/gold ratio returns toward its five- or ten-year median;
- gold remains firm while AISC guidance remains controlled;
- miners continue using free cash flow for buybacks, debt reduction and disciplined dividends rather
  than expensive acquisitions; or
- NEM/AEM valuations compress without operating deterioration.

Become less constructive if gold falls below the industry's high-margin regime, AISC inflation accelerates,
large acquisitions return, or the miners materially underperform gold despite strong bullion prices.

## Bottom Line

Gold miners are **good businesses at an excellent gold price**, not a historically unprecedented bargain.
The portfolio already owns enough precious-metals equity that the clean opportunity is to improve its
composition: reduce richly valued WPM, retain AEM, and add a small staged GDX position only at a better
entry. Physical gold remains the strategic holding; miners remain a capped tactical satellite.
