U.S. Treasuries now make sense for Hooman as a **small tactical recession/disinflation hedge** and a
potential source of capital gains if yields fall. They do **not** replace the portfolio's inflation
hedges, and the long-duration TLT ETF is not the right default vehicle under Hooman's stated 10%

The preferred vehicle is **VGIT**, the Vanguard Intermediate-Term Treasury ETF:

- 0.03% expense ratio;
- approximately 4.9 years of duration;
- 4.88% 30-day SEC yield as of 2026-09-29; and
- enough duration to benefit from a recession without TLT's roughly 15-year duration risk.

## User Constraints

- Primary objectives: recession protection and capital gains when yields fall.
- Inflation protection is desirable, but not the only objective.
- The funding source will be decided later.
- Maximum acceptable temporary principal loss: approximately 10%; 20% is unacceptable.
- The money may be needed before a long Treasury reaches maturity.
- Evaluate the full yield curve rather than treating a 6% ten-year yield as the only trigger.
- Current tax residency: United States; Canadian citizen; possible future move to Canada.

### What “temporary loss” means here

It means the mark-to-market decline between purchase and recovery. Treasury credit may be effectively
risk-free in nominal U.S.-dollar terms if held to maturity, but a Treasury or Treasury ETF can still
fall materially while yields rise. If Hooman must sell before recovery or maturity, the temporary loss
becomes a realized permanent loss.

No intermediate- or long-duration instrument can guarantee a sub-10% drawdown if it must remain
liquid for early sale. Position size can cap the effect on the total portfolio, but it cannot eliminate
the instrument's own price risk.

A 6% nominal ten-year yield is not automatically a buy. Its composition matters:

- **Attractive:** 6% nominal with a real yield around or above 3% and breakeven inflation below about
  2.75%. This pays unusually high real compensation.
- **Less attractive:** 6% nominal caused mainly by breakeven inflation rising above about 3%. That is
  a warning for nominal duration; TIPS or existing real assets are better inflation protection.

## Fit With the Macro Thesis

### Why Treasuries now make sense

The portfolio remains structurally long inflation and real assets through gold, energy, agriculture,
metals and resource equities. Those positions can work during the first inflationary phase but tend to
suffer when high prices cause demand destruction and recession. Nominal Treasuries are one of the few
assets in the portfolio that can appreciate materially in that second phase.

This changes the prior decision only conditionally. The July decision used VPU instead of IEF because
Hooman distrusted government duration. VPU is a useful defensive/electrification equity, but it is not
a Treasury substitute: VPU fell about 36% in the 2020 crash window while intermediate Treasuries rose.
If the goal is specifically recession convexity, regulated utilities cannot do the entire job.

### Why Treasuries are not the inflation hedge

- Nominal Treasury principal and coupons do not rise with inflation. Unexpected inflation can push
  yields higher and prices lower.
- TIPS adjust principal with CPI, but a TIPS ETF can still lose money when real yields rise.
- Short-duration TIPS such as VTIP are the cleanest liquid inflation instrument under a 10% loss
  tolerance, but their recession upside is modest.
- Hooman already owns a large inflation-sensitive allocation. The new bond sleeve should primarily
  hedge the opposite outcome rather than make every sleeve serve both regimes poorly.

## Direct Treasuries Versus ETFs

### Direct Treasury

Advantages:

- no fund expense ratio;
- known coupon, maturity date and par repayment if held to maturity;
- can match a known spending date with a bill/note ladder; and
- interest is federally taxable but exempt from state and local income tax.

Disadvantages for this objective:

- selling before maturity exposes Hooman to the same rate-driven market loss;
- duration declines as the bond approaches maturity, so the recession hedge weakens over time;
- a single issue is less convenient than a diversified, continuously maintained maturity band; and
- TreasuryDirect cannot execute a sale. A security held there must be transferred to a bank, broker
  or dealer, and newly purchased marketable securities have a 45-day transfer/sale hold.

Hooman can buy marketable Treasuries through a bank, broker or dealer. For a tactical position that
may be sold, use the U.S. brokerage rather than TreasuryDirect. Confirm the live bid/ask and any
broker pricing before execution.

### Treasury ETF

Advantages:

- liquid intraday;
- automatically replaces bonds as they leave the target maturity range;
- maintains approximately constant duration, which is useful for a continuing recession hedge; and
- easy to scale, rebalance and sell.

Disadvantages:

- never matures and therefore never promises to return the investor's purchase price on a date;
- maintains rate risk indefinitely because it keeps rolling;
- charges an annual expense ratio; and
- sale before recovery realizes any market loss.

The automatic roll is a benefit for Hooman's tactical macro objective, not a free source of return.
It keeps the intended exposure constant. It is a disadvantage only if the real objective is to lock
money for a known date and receive par at maturity.

## Tax Structure

For a U.S. taxable investor:

- direct Treasury interest is ordinary federal taxable income and exempt from state and local income
  tax;
- distributions from a Treasury-only ETF are generally ordinary federal income, while the portion
  attributable to qualifying U.S. government obligations can be state-tax exempt;
- California requires at least 50% of a fund's assets to consist of qualifying U.S./California
  obligations for the attributed dividend exemption; pure Treasury ETFs normally clear this test,
  but use the issuer's annual tax supplement when filing;
- selling either a bond or an ETF before maturity/recovery can create a taxable capital gain or loss;
  and
- direct TIPS can create annual taxable inflation accretion even before principal is received at
  maturity. A TIPS ETF handles the portfolio accounting and distributes income, but it does not make
  the income tax-free.

Therefore a pure Treasury ETF is **roughly as tax-efficient as direct Treasuries for ongoing interest**,
but not identical. Direct holdings avoid the expense ratio and offer maturity certainty; ETFs offer
liquidity and operational simplicity. The ETF is preferable for this tactical, potentially early-sale
use case.

The simplest location while Hooman is a U.S. tax resident is generally the **U.S. taxable brokerage**:
the funds remain accessible for a house or tactical exit, and qualifying Treasury income receives the
state-tax treatment. RRSP use is a poor fit for money that may be needed, and California treatment of
Canadian registered accounts is a cross-border-tax issue that should be confirmed with a qualified
CPA before using the RRSP for this sleeve.

4. **Indicative staging:** 1% when 10-year real yield is at least 2.75% and breakeven inflation is no

5. **If 6% is inflation-driven:** do not automatically add nominal duration when the 10-year
   breakeven is above 3%. Reassess VTIP or leave the portfolio's existing real-asset hedges to cover
   that regime.
6. **Exit as a tactical hedge:** reassess after a 100-150 basis-point fall in intermediate yields, a
   roughly 10%-15% total return, or when recession is fully reflected in prices. Do not keep chasing
   capital gains after the rate-cut cycle is mature.

| Is it an inflation hedge? | **Nominal Treasuries: no.** Short TIPS: yes, but with less recession upside. |
| Should Hooman use TLT? | **No as the core.** Its duration violates the stated risk tolerance. |
| Best ETF | **VGIT**; low cost, middle-of-curve exposure and acceptable compromise. |
