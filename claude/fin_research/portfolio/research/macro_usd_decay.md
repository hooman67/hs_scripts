Context: DXY peaked ~114 in late 2022, sold off hard in H1-2025 (to ~97), and has since *recovered* to ~101. All-time high 164.72 (Feb 1985). So on a 12-month view the dollar is **modestly stronger**, not weaker — but it is well below its 2022 highs.
- **USD/CAD (his relevant pair):** **1.4213 on 2026-07-07. CAD weakened −3.98% vs USD over 12 months, −1.83% on the month.** [VERIFIED — TradingEconomics, 2026-07-07]. Drivers cited: lower oil, USMCA/trade uncertainty. **This directly contradicts the thesis for a Canadian:** the USD gained on the loonie.
- **Gold:** **$4,131/oz on 2026-07-07, +25.1% over 12 months; all-time high $5,608 in Jan 2026** (i.e. gold has since pulled back ~26% from that spike). [VERIFIED — TradingEconomics, 2026-07-07]. Gold is where "USD is losing value" is unambiguously true.

**Reconciliation:** USD ↑ vs CAD, ↑ (12mo) on DXY, but ↓ ~20% vs gold. The dollar is losing value **against hard assets and to inflation**, while holding or gaining **against other paper currencies**. That distinction is the whole ballgame (see §4).

Against ~$5T federal receipts that is **roughly 20–24% of revenue** [ASSUMED — my calc from cited components; net-interest-to-public figure is somewhat lower]. ### Fed balance sheet — actually shrinking

This is a factual weak point in the thesis *right now* (though it could reverse in the next recession).

### Foreign demand for Treasuries — rising in aggregate, China exiting

### De-dollarization — real but glacial
- **USD share of allocated FX reserves (IMF COFER): 56.8% (2025), down from 59.5% (2020) and 69.7% (2000).** [VERIFIED — IMF COFER via Wikipedia Reserve Currency]. EUR 20.3%, RMB just 1.95% (and *falling* from 2.3% in 2020 — the yuan is not gaining).

- **Where de-dollarization is visible:** energy increasingly priced in non-USD (esp. Russian oil), and **gold's share of EM central-bank reserves doubled from ~4% a decade ago to ~9% now.** [VERIFIED — J.P. Morgan Research].
- **Central-bank gold buying: >1,000 tonnes/yr for three straight years — 1,050.8t (2023), 1,044.6t (2024)**, with 2022 a record (~1,080t) [2023/2024 VERIFIED — World Gold Council; 2022 record figure ASSUMED]. This is the clearest "smart money hedging the dollar" signal and it directly supports the *softened* thesis.

2. **No viable alternative.** EUR (no unified fiscal/bond market, fragmentation risk), CNY (capital controls, not convertible, share *falling* to 1.95%). The dollar wins by default. [VERIFIED — COFER + JPM]
3. **USD strengthened over the last 12 months** (DXY +3.5%, vs CAD +4%). The "collapse" isn't in the recent tape. [VERIFIED — TradingEconomics 2026-07-07]
4. **Risk-off flight-to-dollar reflex.** In every crisis since WWII, the world buys dollars and Treasuries. A US recession would likely *strengthen* the USD short-term, punishing a naked "short USD" bet. [ASSUMED — strong historical regularity]

## 4. The Critical Nuance He's Missing (read this twice)

**"USD loses value" is three different claims needing three different hedges:**

| Frame | What it means | Trailing-12mo reality | Correct hedge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) vs other currencies (FX)** | USD/CAD, DXY fall | USD **ROSE** ~4% vs CAD; DXY +3.5% | Hold non-USD currency / foreign assets — but **CAD is itself weak** |
| **(b) vs goods (inflation)** | Purchasing power erodes | ~2–3%/yr, not a crisis | TIPS, short-duration, real yield |
| **(c) vs hard assets (gold)** | Gold/commodities rise | Gold **+25%** — thesis TRUE here | Gold, commodity producers, real assets |

His conviction is really about **(c)**, and possibly **(b)** — the debasement of paper against hard assets. He is likely **wrong about (a)**, which is the frame that actually determines his wealth as a Canadian.

**The Canadian-specific traps:**
- **His real currency risk is USD/CAD, and the CAD is a commodity currency.** If the USD debases via inflation, that usually coincides with *high commodity prices* → a *stronger* CAD, which already hedges him. But if the world goes risk-off, both USD/CAD dynamics and commodities can whipsaw. Over the past year the CAD *lost* to the USD despite the "debasement" narrative — because oil was soft. He must not assume "USD down = I win as a Canadian."
- **Canadian energy + gold producers do NOT cleanly hedge USD debasement — they add correlated commodity beta.** They are levered bets on oil/gold prices *and* on operational execution, priced in CAD, often reported in USD. That's three exposures stacked, not a clean currency hedge. In a deflationary shock they fall hard even if the "USD debasement" story is intact long-term.
- **A global equity index already hedges USD substantially.** An MSCI World / VXC-type fund holds companies with foreign earnings and foreign-currency assets; its USD exposure is partial and diversified. He may already be more hedged than he thinks, and stacking gold + energy + short-USD on top could be an *over*-concentrated single bet on one macro outcome.
- **Gold is not free.** It yields nothing, is volatile (just fell 26% from its Jan-2026 spike), and can trade sideways for a decade. It hedges (c) well but is a poor core holding at 20-yr horizon vs productive assets.

## 5. Is "70% over 10 years" Plausible? The Math

"USD loses 70%" means purchasing power → 30% of today's. **Against what?**

- **vs goods (inflation):** requires 0.30^(1/10) ≈ **~11.3% compounded annual inflation for a decade.** That is Argentina/Turkey territory. For the issuer of the world reserve currency with an independent-ish Fed, that is a **low-probability tail** [my calc, ASSUMED]. Base-case US inflation is ~2–3%; even a "financial-repression" regime is ~3–5%. Over 10 yrs at 4%, USD loses ~33% of purchasing power — meaningful, but less than half his number.
- **vs CAD (FX):** USD/CAD would need to roughly halve (1.42 → ~0.43). **No major-currency pair has moved that far in a decade** absent hyperinflation. Effectively **implausible.**
- **vs gold:** gold would need to ~3.3x (to ~$13,600/oz). Given gold went ~$1,300→$4,100 in the last several years, a further 3.3x is **aggressive but not crazy** if debasement fears intensify. This is the *only* frame where 70% is even discussable — and it's a bet on gold, not a forecast of "the dollar."

**Evidence-based expectation instead:**
- USD real purchasing-power loss (vs goods): **~25–40% over 10 yrs** (i.e. 3–4.5%/yr inflation) is a defensible, even slightly hawkish-on-inflation base case.
- USD reserve share: continues eroding **~0.5–1%/yr** → high-40s%/low-50s% by 2036. Decline, not collapse.
- vs CAD: essentially a coin-flip around commodity cycles; no reliable structural depreciation.
- vs gold/real assets: continued gradual loss likely, magnitude highly path-dependent.

**So: his direction is right, his magnitude is ~2–3x too aggressive, and he's applying a gold-frame number to a currency-frame claim.**

## 6. Best Liquid Instruments for a Canadian to Hedge USD Debasement

Ranked for his actual situation (Canadian, CAD-spending for the house, USD-earning at Adobe, 3–5yr + 20yr horizons). Not advice — a menu to research.

1. **Broad global/ex-North-America equity (CAD-denominated or CAD-hedged optional):** the cleanest, highest-expected-return real-asset hedge. Foreign earnings + real productive assets debase far slower than cash. Likely his core. [reasoning, ASSUMED]
2. **TIPS / real-return bonds (US TIPS or Canadian RRBs):** the *direct* hedge for frame (b) inflation — principal indexed to CPI. Underused by retail, exactly the instrument for "inflation eats my cash." Canadian Real Return Bonds avoid USD/CAD noise for his house goal.
3. **Gold (modest sleeve, ~5–10%):** legitimate frame-(c) hedge, validated by central-bank buying, but no yield and volatile — a satellite, not a core. Physical/ETF (e.g., CAD-listed gold ETF) over miners for a *clean* hedge.
4. **Commodity producers / Canadian energy:** only as *return-seeking* commodity exposure, NOT as a currency hedge. Correlated beta, operational risk. Size accordingly.
5. **Short-duration / T-bills for the 3–5yr house money:** debasement over 5 yrs is modest; *sequence risk* (a 30% equity drawdown right before he buys) is the bigger danger to the house goal. Real assets are for the 20-yr money; the house down-payment wants stability + real-return bonds/HISA.
6. **Currency itself (holding CAD vs USD):** the *worst* standalone hedge — CAD is a volatile commodity currency and just lost 4% to the USD. Diversify across currencies via #1 rather than betting the pair.

**Key structural point:** the house goal (3–5yr, CAD) and the debasement hedge (20yr, real assets) are **different problems**. Don't hedge a 5-year CAD liability with volatile 20-year debasement bets.
