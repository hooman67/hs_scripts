## Where I deviate from an equal / obvious split — and why

1. **Sleeve 1 — VTI 11 / VXUS 9, not the suggested 12/8.** The Adobe $732k position already delivers
   overwhelming US-large-cap-tech exposure; adding it again through the core is doubling down on the
   one risk he's most concentrated in. Tilting the core ~5 pts toward international is the cheapest
   diversification available. AVUV, when added, comes out of VTI (stays in the US budget but moves to
   small-value — the opposite factor from Adobe).
2. **Sleeve 2 — LLY held light (2.5%) and flagged do-not-add.** At $1,196 it's *above* Hooman's own
   <$1,080 add trigger and is the priciest name in the book (P/B 34). Equal-thirds (2.67% each) would
   imply buying LLY at these levels; instead GOOGL (cheapest, best conviction) gets the overweight and
   LLY is a hold-ceiling pending a pullback.
3. **Sleeve 3 — KWEB 3 / BABA 2, not 50/50.** At a hard *cap* on a structurally risky market, the
   diversified basket should dominate; BABA is the single-name conviction tilt, not half the sleeve.
4. **Sleeve 4 — CI 2 / KBWB 1, not CI 3.** CI is the conviction value anchor but is managed-care, not
   banks — so it leaves the "financials" sleeve with no bank/capital-markets exposure. KBWB (banks)
   adds the missing breadth; deliberately chose it over IAK (insurance) to avoid doubling CI's
   insurance-adjacent risk.
