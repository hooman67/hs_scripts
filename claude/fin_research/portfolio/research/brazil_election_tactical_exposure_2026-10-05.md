## What Is Actually Happening

### Election catalyst

- **VERIFIED:** Flávio Bolsonaro won 47.03% of the first-round vote and Lula won 45.16%. Because neither
  exceeded 50%, they proceed to an October 25 runoff.
- **VERIFIED:** Bolsonaro's Liberal Party became the largest bloc in both chambers. It elected 19
  senators and will hold 28 of 81 Senate seats; it elected 121 of 513 lower-house members. This improves
  the implementability of a Bolsonaro
  agenda, although it does not guarantee passage.
- **ASSUMED:** Flávio is now the favorite. The first-round margin and congressional result support that
  interpretation, and multiple Brazil analysts describe a high probability of victory, but three weeks
  of campaigning and an unusually high 21.08% abstention rate leave real outcome risk.

### What markets are buying

The bullish election thesis is not simply "right-wing candidate wins." It is a prospective decline in
Brazil's fiscal and political risk premium through:

- a smaller government and fewer ministries;
- lower taxes and a more business-friendly regulatory stance;
- better relations with the United States and other major trading partners;
- improved odds of spending restraint and debt stabilization; and
- eventual lower interest rates, a stronger Brazilian real and higher domestic equity multiples.

Flávio's stated platform includes eliminating at least ten ministries, reducing taxes, and investing
approximately $174 billion in transport infrastructure over four years. Those goals are not internally
fiscal-conservative unless spending cuts and reforms credibly fund them.

The material bearish countercase is therefore **implementation disappointment**. Independent analysts
quoted by Americas Quarterly specifically describe the promised rapid fiscal adjustment as relying on
agendas that are not very feasible. Amnesty for Jair Bolsonaro and January 8 defendants also creates
institutional-conflict risk that could displace the economic agenda.

## Economic Outlook

Brazil is **not currently a high-growth secular macro story**. The October 2 Banco Central do Brasil Focus
survey median forecasts were:

| Measure | 2026 | 2027 | 2028 | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Real GDP growth | 1.85% | 1.40% | 1.81% | Modest trend growth |
| IPCA inflation | 5.01% | 4.30% | 3.80% | Improving, but still sticky |
| Year-end Selic | 13.50% | 12.00% | 10.50% | Very restrictive; creates rate-cut upside only if inflation/fiscal risk improve |
| Net public debt / GDP | 70.00% | 73.75% | 76.46% | Deteriorating rather than stabilizing |
| Nominal fiscal balance / GDP | -8.88% | — | — | Large current fiscal problem |

The positive side is a forecast 2026 trade surplus of approximately $78.3 billion and approximately
$80 billion of direct investment. Brazil has globally important agriculture, mining and energy assets,
and high real rates can support the currency if fiscal credibility improves.

**Conclusion:** the economy supports a conditional rerating trade, not a 2% strategic country bet today.
The upside is created by a low starting valuation, high rates and a potentially lower risk premium—not by
strong underlying growth.

## Recommended Trade Framework

### Size

- **Starter:** 0.5% of portfolio, approximately **$3,358**.
- **Maximum:** 1.0%, approximately **$6,716**.
- **Reject 2% for now:** the macro and fiscal evidence is not strong enough, and Brazil ETFs compound
  existing commodity/cyclical exposure.

At full target funding, a 0.5% dedicated position would lift direct Brazil exposure from approximately
0.24% to 0.74%. A 1.0% position would lift it to approximately 1.24%, before adjusting whichever sleeve
ultimately funds the trade.

### Entry

1. **Do not buy the October 5 gap.** EWZ's approximately $42.98 reference price was about 12.5% above
   Friday's $38.19 close; FLBR and EWZS moved similarly or more.
2. **Preferred first entry:** consider 0.5% EWZ around approximately **$39.50–$40.50**, a roughly 6%–8%
   pullback from the initial post-election quote, only if Flávio remains favored and the fiscal thesis
   has not weakened.
3. **Alternative confirmation entry:** if no pullback occurs, wait until after the October 25 runoff and
   the first credible economic-team/fiscal-policy signals. Do not buy another large gap immediately.
4. **Second tranche:** increase to the 1.0% maximum only after a Bolsonaro victory plus a credible finance
   minister, spending-control plan or 2027 budget path. Election victory alone is insufficient.
5. Use a **limit order**, especially in FLBR or EWZS. Recheck live NAV spreads and fundamentals before
   every purchase; the reference prices are not standing orders.

### Funding

Funding remains a separate user decision. The cleanest portfolio construction would eventually be to
carve the position from the existing 5% EM/China sleeve—normally from VWO—rather than add another country
bet on top. Replacing 0.5% of VWO with 0.5% EWZ would keep the total sleeve unchanged while making the
Brazil overweight explicit. Do not reduce gold, healthcare or the global-equity core for this trade.

### Exit and review

- Exit or do not initiate if Lula wins and the position was entered solely for the election catalyst.
- Exit/reassess if Flávio wins but appoints an economically non-credible team, expands unfunded tax cuts
  and infrastructure spending, abandons fiscal adjustment, or escalates institutional conflict.
- If the election produces another 10%–15% surge without corresponding fiscal evidence, harvest part of
  the tactical gain rather than automatically converting it into a permanent holding.
- Review after the economic team is named, the 2027 budget path is visible, and the first 100 days have
  provided evidence on implementation.
- Convert to a strategic holding only if inflation expectations, real rates and projected debt begin to
  decline together. If that happens, FLBR becomes the preferred long-duration vehicle.

## What Would Change the Recommendation

### Upgrade above 1%

All of the following would be needed: credible fiscal appointments, legislated spending reform, a
falling projected debt path, disinflation without recession, and attractive entry valuation after the
election. None is established today.

### Cancel the trade

- Flávio loses the runoff;
- fiscal promises remain unfunded or the projected debt path worsens;
- institutional conflict dominates the first policy agenda;
- inflation expectations reaccelerate and prevent meaningful rate cuts; or
- EWZ continues vertically higher before evidence catches up.

### Prefer no dedicated Brazil allocation

If the goal is merely broad emerging-market diversification, continue funding VWO. A Brazil position is
justified only when Hooman intentionally wants the election/fiscal/rate/BRL bet.
