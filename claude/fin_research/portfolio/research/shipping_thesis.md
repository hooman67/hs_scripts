## 1. Does shipping FIT the macro thesis? — sub-sector by sub-sector

Shipping is not one asset. The four sub-sectors have almost nothing in common in a stagflation. Here is how each maps to Hooman's Phase-1 (oil spike / inflation) → Phase-2 (demand destruction / recession) framework.

| Sub-sector | Cargo / driver | Phase 1 (oil spike, disruption) | Phase 2 (recession) | Net fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) CRUDE TANKERS** (VLCC/Suezmax) | Oil trade, ton-miles | **STRONG WIN** — Hormuz rerouting, floating storage, sanction "shadow fleet", oil in backwardation → more voyages | **Mixed** — oil demand destruction cuts volumes, but supply-driven high oil + long routes can persist; historically tankers hold up better than bulk/box | **BEST FIT** |
| **(b) PRODUCT TANKERS** (MR/LR2) | Refined products (diesel/jet/gasoline) | **STRONG WIN** — refinery dislocation, long-haul diesel arbitrage, Russia rerouting still in force | **Mixed-to-weak** — recession cuts fuel demand, but refining geography keeps ton-miles long | **STRONG FIT** |
| **(c) DRY BULK** (iron ore/coal/grain) | China construction + global industrial | **Partial win** — commodity supercycle + rerouting help; but tied to China property (weak) | **LOSE** — industrial recession crushes it; deeply cyclical | **WEAK/PARTIAL** |
| **(d) CONTAINER** (consumer goods) | Retail imports, discretionary | Neutral-to-win *only* via Red Sea rerouting; volumes fragile | **BIG LOSE** — this IS the demand-destruction trade; consumer goods collapse in a recession | **ANTI-FIT** |
| **LNG carriers** (bonus, gas trade) | LNG transport | Rerouting helps ton-miles | **Structural GLUT** regardless of phase | **DOESN'T FIT (supply glut)** |

**Key insight tested and CONFIRMED:** geopolitical disruption → longer routes (ships around the Cape of Good Hope instead of Suez; Hormuz avoidance; Panama drought) → **more ton-miles per barrel/box = fewer effective ships = higher rates.** This is real and it is happening right now (see §2). It maps directly onto Hooman's disruption thesis. **BUT** the same insight cuts the other way in Phase 2: container and dry bulk get hit by demand destruction that swamps the ton-mile benefit. **Only tankers plausibly survive both phases**, because an oil *shock* keeps crude moving on long routes even as demand softens — and because the tanker fleet is old (see orderbook §3).

**Why tankers specifically fit Hooman's book:**
- He is **long energy and expects an oil shock.** Crude/product tankers are a *leveraged, second-derivative* play on exactly that — they profit from oil *dislocation and rerouting*, not just the oil price. In an oil-supply-shock they can outperform the oil majors he already owns.
- He believes in **geopolitical disruption (Iran/Hormuz, tariffs, protectionism).** Tankers are the most direct listed beneficiary of Hormuz/Red Sea/Black Sea disruption — arguably more direct than any energy equity.
- **USD debasement / commodity supercycle:** shipping rates are real-asset, hard-currency cash flows (ships are dollar-priced steel that inflates with replacement cost). Mild fit.

**Where it does NOT fit:** Hooman is **quality-first**. Shipping is the opposite of a quality compounder — it is capital-destructive across the cycle (see §2 cyclicality). So the fit is *thematic/tactical*, and it fights his core style. That tension is why sizing must be small and entry disciplined.

### Why shipping is capital-destructive long-term (the trap mechanics)

- **The order-book reflex kills every boom.** High rates → owners order ships → 2-3 years later the newbuilds arrive → glut → rates collapse. The global order book just hit a **17-year high** (BIMCO via gCaptain, Apr 9 2026). Crude tanker orderbook is now **~22% of fleet**, product **~19%**, container **~37%**, **LNG ~40%** (BIMCO Q1 2026 — VERIFIED-as-reported). Only **dry bulk (~11%)** still has a clean supply picture.
  - **Important correction to a common prior:** the tanker orderbook is **no longer historically low** — owners chased the boom and it's now ~22%. That erodes part of the classic "tankers can't add supply" bull case. The offset: **21% of the crude fleet and 17% of the product fleet is 20+ years old** (BIMCO, Apr 2026) → scrapping potential once rates fall. Net supply is less tight than the 2023 bull case assumed.
- **Low tangible book compounding.** Ship values themselves are cyclical; NAV inflates at the peak and craters at the trough. P/B > 2-3 at a peak (FRO 3.4, NAT 3.1, DHT 2.3) means you're paying up for peak-inflated asset values.
- **BIMCO explicitly warns of a 2027 unwind** if the geopolitical rerouting reverses (VERIFIED-as-reported). A Hormuz ceasefire + Suez reopening + newbuild deliveries is a triple supply shock to rates.

**Conclusion on entry timing: this is LATE-cycle, not early. It is a bad entry for a buy-and-hold, and a mediocre-to-poor entry even for a trade.** The asymmetry favors *waiting*.

## 4. Verdict + sizing

### Does shipping deserve a place in Hooman's book?
**Yes — but as a small, tactical, tanker-only satellite, and NOT at today's price.** It is the most direct listed expression of his geopolitical-disruption + oil-shock thesis, and tankers are the one sub-sector that plausibly survives both phases of his stagflation. But it violates his quality-first, buy-and-hold instinct (shipping destroys capital over a full cycle), and the entry today is at a war-driven rate peak with stocks at 5-year highs. Those two facts cap it at a small tactical allocation with strict discipline.

### Which sub-sector / vehicle
- **Sub-sector: CRUDE + PRODUCT TANKERS only.** Skip container (Phase-2 casualty), LNG (structural glut), and treat dry bulk as optional/China-dependent.
- **Vehicles (quality-first): STNG and TNK are the two highest-quality (both net cash, disciplined).** INSW/TRMD for more direct yield + fit. **FRO is the purest Hormuz play but the most expensive on P/B — use only on a pullback.** **Avoid DHT-for-yield, NAT, ZIM.** No good tanker-only ETF exists; BOAT dilutes the thesis.

### Sizing
- **2-4% of the growth bucket (Bucket C), maximum.** This is a satellite/trade, not a core holding. Given ~$120k in Bucket C, that's roughly **$3-5k** — enough to matter if the war-disruption thesis extends, small enough that a cyclical reversal doesn't hurt. Split across 2 quality names (e.g., STNG + TNK, or STNG + INSW) rather than one.
- **It partly overlaps his existing energy/oil-shock exposure** — so it is not additive diversification, it is a *higher-beta expression* of a bet he already holds. That argues for the low end (2%) unless he wants to lean into the rerouting angle specifically.

### Is NOW the time, or wait?
**WAIT. Do not chase the peak.** Concrete triggers to buy:
1. **A rate pullback / de-escalation crack.** The moment a durable Hormuz ceasefire holds or Suez reopens, spot rates fall and the *stocks fall with them* — that pullback (not the peak) is the entry, IF you still believe disruption recurs.
2. **The Phase-2 washout.** In his expected recession, cyclicals including shipping will get sold indiscriminately. That is where you buy the *survivors* (net-cash STNG/TNK) at trough P/B — the classic "buy shipping when it's hated, at high P/E / low rates" contrarian entry. Pre-position the watchlist now; deploy the waiting cash then.
3. **Do NOT buy** while every name is at 89-100% of its 5-year range and VLCC rates are $500k/day on an active war. That is selling-the-news territory, not buying.

### The honest risk to this whole idea
If Hormuz stays disrupted for years (a real possibility given the 2026 war), rates stay high and today's "peak" isn't a peak — you'd miss it by waiting. That's the cost of discipline. But the risk-reward at a 5-year-high entry into a war-premium cyclical is unattractive for a quality-first investor with 49% cash and no need to force the trade. **Waiting is the correct default; the asymmetry favors patience.**
