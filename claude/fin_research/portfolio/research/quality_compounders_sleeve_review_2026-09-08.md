## What the sleeve is supposed to accomplish

### Valid portfolio function

This portfolio is not a conventional 60/40 portfolio. Its target allocation includes 15% gold, 9% energy, 3.5% uranium/nuclear, 3% agriculture, 3% copper/industrial metals, 1% silver/platinum, and several narrower equity themes. Those exposures can perform very well in an inflationary or commodity-supercycle regime, but many depend on commodity prices, capital spending, regulation, project execution, or geopolitical outcomes.

A quality-factor sleeve supplies a different return engine:

- consistently profitable operating companies;
- healthier balance sheets and less dependence on refinancing;
- more stable earnings than cyclical-resource producers;
- capital-light platforms and branded businesses with reinvestment capacity;
- broad participation in long-run U.S. nominal growth.

That is a defensible reason to hold the sleeve even though the securities already exist inside VTI. The sleeve is a **deliberate overweight**, not a new asset class.

### What it does not accomplish

- **It is not safe house capital.** In the February–March 2020 selloff, QUAL lost roughly one-third peak-to-trough, close to the total U.S. market. JQUA was only modestly more resilient. Cash, T-bills, insured deposits, or short-duration government instruments—not quality equities—protect a purchase that must occur on schedule.
- **It does not eliminate equity-duration risk.** High-quality businesses can become poor investments when purchased at excessive prices. QUAL's current 30.08x reported P/E is the clearest current warning.
- **It is not an AI mandate.** AI exposure is substantial today because many profitable mega-cap and semiconductor companies score well. The index may remove them if their accounting quality deteriorates or other companies score better.
- **It is not a portfolio of permanently held hand-selected compounders.** All of these quantitative vehicles turn holdings over. `Quality factor` is a more accurate label than `quality compounders`.

### Is it redundant with the broad core?

At the security level, almost completely: VTI already owns virtually every U.S. company in QUAL and JQUA. At the factor level, no: a 5.5% sleeve alongside an 11% VTI target materially increases the weight of profitable, stable, lower-leverage companies.

The sleeve remains justified while the portfolio deliberately retains large commodity and thematic allocations. It should **not exceed 5.5%**. If the broad global-equity core later becomes substantially larger than 20%, the simplest portfolio would fold this sleeve back into VTI/VXUS rather than keep adding factor products.

## QUAL in detail

### Methodology

QUAL tracks the MSCI USA Sector Neutral Quality Index. The index begins with U.S. large- and mid-cap companies and scores them using:

1. high return on equity;
2. low earnings variability;
3. low financial leverage.

Each holding is weighted using its quality score multiplied by its parent-index weight. Sector weights are then normalized to the parent index, and issuers are capped at 5% at rebalancing. The index rebalances semiannually. Weight drift can take a company above 5% between rebalances; Microsoft, Apple and Nvidia were each above 6.5% on 2026-09-04.

This is a sensible, transparent quality definition, but it has limitations:

- ROE can be inflated by buybacks, low book equity, goodwill accounting, or leverage.
- Low historical earnings variability can favor mature incumbents and miss younger future compounders.
- Sector neutrality prevents quality from expressing a strong sector view and still inherits the parent index's high technology weight.
- There is no explicit valuation safeguard. A wonderful company can enter at a very demanding price.
- The index describes current financial quality, not an analyst's assessment of moat duration, reinvestment runway, management, or disruption risk.

## Tax structure

QUAL, JQUA, SPHQ, VFQY, DUHP, QGRO and MOAT are conventional U.S.-domiciled equity ETFs registered under the Investment Company Act—not commodity pools or partnerships. They generally issue Form 1099-DIV rather than Schedule K-1. Distributions can include qualified dividends, ordinary dividends and capital-gain distributions.

For a U.S. tax resident:

- a U.S.-listed ETF in a U.S. taxable account is the simplest reporting implementation;
- holding the same U.S.-listed ETF in a Canadian non-registered account does not turn the ETF into a Canadian PFIC, but the account and transactions remain subject to U.S. tax and potentially FBAR/FATCA reporting;
- an eligible RRSP may receive treaty-based U.S. tax deferral, but account-specific reporting and withdrawal consequences should be confirmed with a cross-border tax professional;
- JQUA's 19% and QUAL's 21% reported turnover are more attractive for taxable implementation than QGRO's 165%, although ETF in-kind creation/redemption can reduce—not eliminate—capital-gain distributions.

## Follow-up: why JQUA despite the AI concern

JQUA is **not a small-cap fund**. It selects from the Russell 1000, had a weighted-average market
capitalization of approximately $586 billion, and still owned Apple, Microsoft, Broadcom, Alphabet,
Nvidia, AMD and Meta among its largest positions. Choosing JQUA therefore does not abandon the
large-cap AI winners. It reduces the amount of the sleeve whose outcome depends on a handful of
those companies and on today's elevated valuations.

The diversification benefit is mostly **within U.S. equities**, not a new asset class:

- QUAL's top ten holdings were 44.24% of the fund versus 18.5% for JQUA.
- QUAL's information technology plus communication-services exposure was 47.46%; JQUA's technology
  plus telecommunications exposure was approximately 42.5%.
- QUAL's reported portfolio P/E was 30.08x versus JQUA's 20.95x, although issuer calculation methods
  are not perfectly comparable.
- If AI succeeds, JQUA should participate through its substantial semiconductor, platform and
  software holdings. QUAL should outperform only if today's concentrated mega-cap winners continue
  to dominate enough to overcome their higher starting valuations.
- If the AI trade disappoints, neither fund is a hedge and both can fall. JQUA's lower top-ten
  concentration and lower reported valuation should, however, reduce the damage from a narrow
  mega-cap valuation unwind. It does not guarantee protection in a broad recession.
