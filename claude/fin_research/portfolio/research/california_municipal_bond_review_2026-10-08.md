### Why California tax does not improve VTEC relative to VGIT

California FTB Publication 1001 states that:

- direct U.S.-obligation interest is exempt from California income tax;
- fund dividends attributable to U.S. obligations or California state/municipal obligations are exempt
  when at least 50% of fund assets satisfy the state's test; and
- interest attributable to non-California state and local bonds is taxable by California.

The latest completed-year Vanguard tax supplement reported VGIT's income as 100% U.S.-government-source.
Therefore, California's 10.3%–11.3% marginal rate is not an incremental advantage for VTEC or CMF over
VGIT. It *is* an advantage versus corporate bonds, bank interest and national municipal funds holding
mostly out-of-state bonds.

### Why VTEC is a less reliable recession hedge

VTEC can perform well in an orderly, disinflationary recession. The concern is not that municipal bonds
must fall whenever the economy contracts. The concern is that VTEC has a second moving part that VGIT
largely avoids.

- **VGIT yield is essentially the Treasury curve.** In a recession, inflation expectations and policy
  rates commonly fall, global investors seek liquid safe assets, and intermediate Treasury yields can
  decline. With no corporate or municipal spread to widen, most of that yield decline becomes a price
  gain.
- **VTEC yield is the tax-exempt base curve plus municipal credit, liquidity, supply and tax-demand
  spreads.** A recession can lower the base curve while simultaneously widening the municipal spread.
  The two moves offset each other.
- Municipal spreads can widen even without defaults. Recession pressure reduces income, sales and other
  tax receipts; weakens transport, utility, hospital and other revenue-bond cash flows; increases budget
  pressure; and raises downgrade concerns. Investors may redeem municipal funds precisely when dealers
  have little balance-sheet capacity, forcing bonds to clear at wider spreads.
- Treasuries are global reserve assets and core collateral in the world's deepest government-bond
  market. California municipal bonds are fragmented, less frequently traded securities purchased mainly
  for U.S. tax reasons. That liquidity difference matters most during a panic.
- Many municipal bonds are callable. When rates fall, issuers can refinance and return principal near
  the call price, limiting appreciation and forcing VTEC to reinvest at lower yields. VGIT's Treasury
  notes and bonds are noncallable.
- VTEC is also exposed to one state's fiscal, political, economic and catastrophe risks. VGIT is not.
  Vanguard explicitly classifies VTEC as a **nondiversified** fund under the Investment Company Act and
  lists municipal-market, credit, liquidity, call, extension and state-specific risks in its disclosure.

The first-order scenario math below uses current effective duration and assumes parallel shifts. It is
illustrative, before income, curve changes, convexity and tracking effects.

| Scenario | Treasury-curve move | Municipal-spread move | Approximate VGIT price effect | Approximate VTEC price effect |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Orderly disinflationary recession | -100 bp | 0 bp | **+4.9%** | **+6.9%**, before call effects |
| Recession plus muni stress | -100 bp | +100 bp | **+4.9%** | approximately **0%** |
| Severe muni liquidity/fiscal stress | -100 bp | +150 bp | **+4.9%** | approximately **-3.5%** |
| Inflationary recession / stagflation | +100 bp | 0 bp | approximately **-4.9%** | approximately **-6.9%** |

This is why VTEC is not simply “VGIT with tax-free extra yield.” In the best recession regime VTEC can
outperform because it has more duration. In the regime where protection is most valuable—a disorderly
selloff with a demand for immediate liquidity—its municipal spread can erase the Treasury rally.

### What the extra 1% is buying—and risking

At current yields, VTEC provides approximately **1.01 percentage points** more annual after-tax income
than VGIT. That equals about **$101 per year for each $10,000 invested**.

Because VTEC's duration is 6.9 years, an adverse yield/spread move of only about **15 basis points** causes
approximately a 1.0% price decline, enough to erase one year of the extra carry. A 100-basis-point adverse
move costs roughly 6.9%, or almost seven years of the current carry advantage. These are mark-to-market
effects; a default is not required.

The March 2020 proxy makes the tail risk concrete. VTEC did not yet exist, so CMF is used as the closest
long-history broad California-municipal ETF:

- February 19 through March 23, 2020: **CMF -13.50%, VGIT +5.01%**.
- March 9 through March 23, 2020: **CMF -14.57%, VGIT +0.20%**.
- Full calendar 2020 after recovery: **CMF +4.50%, VGIT +7.48%**.

The Global Financial Crisis shows the same weaker-hedge pattern. VGIT had not launched, so IEI—the
iShares 3–7 Year Treasury ETF—is used as a duration-comparable Treasury proxy:

- Full calendar 2008: **CMF +0.64%, IEI +12.22%**.
- October 11, 2007 through the March 9, 2009 equity trough: **CMF +2.78%, IEI +16.38%**.

On $10,000, the February-to-March relative gap was approximately $1,851—more than 18 years of the current
$101 annual carry advantage. Municipal markets later recovered, but the shortfall occurred at the exact
moment a recession hedge was supposed to provide liquidity and offset equity losses.

VGIT is not principal-stable: its own maximum drawdown was approximately 16% from the 2020 rate low into
the 2022 tightening cycle. Its advantage is narrower: it is the cleaner hedge when recession and
disinflation cause Treasury yields to fall. Neither VGIT nor VTEC protects well against inflation-driven
rate increases.

## Other Implementations

### Direct California municipal bonds

The State of California's October 6, 2026 GO pricing showed initial reoffering yields of 3.52% for bonds
due in 2029 and 3.57% for bonds due in 2030. Direct bonds provide:

- a known maturity and par repayment if held to maturity and the issuer pays;
- no recurring ETF expense ratio; and
- a way to match the house horizon more closely than a perpetual-duration ETF.

They also create retail markups/markdowns, minimum denominations, call and market-discount tax details,
poor diversification at a 1%–2% portfolio size, and uncertain sale proceeds if liquidity is needed early.
At a 1.5% allocation, a one-time 0.5% dealer spread would consume roughly one year of the yield advantage
over VGIT. Direct bonds are therefore preferable only when Hooman can buy a clearly disclosed high-grade,
AMT-free issue at a competitive spread and is likely to hold it to maturity.

### CALI: short-term California muni ETF

CALI has a 3.08% SEC yield, 3.62% YTM, 1.24-year duration, 164 holdings and a 0.20% net expense ratio.
Its approximately 3.08% tax-free yield is almost identical to VGIT's modeled 3.05% after-tax yield while
taking much less duration risk. It is a reasonable taxable-cash substitute, but it gives up the capital-
gain potential that motivated the VGIT allocation. It should not replace the recession-hedge portion.

### Target-maturity municipal ETFs

The iShares iBonds term-muni lineup provides maturity certainty at the fund level:

- IBMR (2029): 3.28% SEC yield, 2.35-year duration and 0.10% expense ratio.
- IBMS (2030): 3.43% SEC yield, 3.34-year duration and 0.10% expense ratio.

These are national AMT-free municipal funds. California taxes the out-of-state portion, so their current
after-California-tax income is only approximately 2.91%–2.94% for IBMR and 3.04%–3.08% for IBMS at
Hooman's likely 10.3%–11.3% state rate. They do not currently beat VGIT clearly enough to justify losing
Treasury quality and recession behavior. There does not appear to be a California-specific term-maturity
ETF; a direct California ladder is the cleaner version of that idea.

## Recommendation

### Best portfolio construction

1. **Keep the accepted 5% recession-hedge target in VGIT.** The original target was approved for
   recession protection and capital gains when intermediate Treasury yields fall, not merely income.
2. **Do not fund VTEC from VGIT solely for its approximately 1% current after-tax carry advantage.** A
   modest muni-spread move can erase years of that advantage, and historical stress performance is worse
   at the exact time the hedge is needed.
3. **If a separate tax-efficient income objective is later approved, prefer VTEC over CMF and cap the
   initial VTEC position at 1.0%–1.5%.** Do not own both; their exposure is redundant.
4. **Use any municipal allocation in a U.S. taxable account only.** Reconfirm tax residency and account treatment before any move to
   Canada or another state.
5. **CALI and direct 2029–2030 California GO bonds solve different problems.** CALI is a taxable-cash
   substitute; direct bonds provide maturity matching when held. Neither replaces VGIT's safe-haven role.

### Entry discipline

No purchase should be driven by headline tax-equivalent yield alone. Before execution:

- refresh SEC yield, duration and municipal/Treasury ratios;
- compare VTEC's tax-free SEC yield with `VGIT SEC yield × 0.612`;
- require at least approximately 0.50 percentage point of after-tax carry advantage to compensate for
  the extra municipal duration, liquidity and California concentration;
- check the current premium/discount and use a limit order; and
- verify the annual sponsor tax statement rather than assuming every distribution is fully exempt.

At the October 6–7 snapshot, VTEC's advantage is approximately 1.01 percentage points, so the carry gate
passes. The portfolio-role and drawdown gates—not the tax math—are the limiting factors.
