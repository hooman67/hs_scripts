# Iran/Hormuz Fertilizer-Shortage Thesis

**As of:** 2026-08-27 (America/Los_Angeles)  
**Portfolio context:** Agriculture/fertilizer sleeve target 3.0%; user-supplied current weight
2.5% ($15,661), or 0.5 percentage point / $2,896 below target. The broader portfolio already
has 33.5% in energy, gold, uranium/power, copper, agriculture and silver/platinum. The cash
sleeve is designated recession/house dry powder and is not authorized for equity deployment.

## Bottom line

The thesis is **directionally correct but too broad**.

- A severe physical disruption has already occurred in Gulf exports of urea, ammonia and
  sulfur. This is not merely a narrative.
- The most direct fertilizer shortage is **nitrogen** (urea/ammonia). Phosphate is affected
  indirectly because Gulf sulfur and ammonia are important inputs. Potash is comparatively
  well supplied and has little direct Iran/Hormuz exposure.
- The relevant risk is the **Gulf/Hormuz system**, not Iran in isolation. Iran matters both as
  a producer and as the conflict center around the chokepoint, but Qatar, Saudi Arabia, the
  UAE and Bahrain account for much of the exposed trade.
- “Severe global shortage of all fertilizer” is not supported. The evidence supports acute,
  nutrient-specific and region-specific scarcity, high prices and delivery risk. Substitution,
  Chinese exports, land rerouting, demand destruction and restored Gulf production can limit
  or reverse the price spike.
- The equity trade is no longer undiscovered. Since 2026-02-27 through 2026-08-27, adjusted
  market-price data show CF up about 27.5% and UAN about 38.7%. The World Bank fertilizer
  index fell 4.3% in July after the earlier surge. The shortage can remain real while a stock
  is already fully or partly priced for it.
- **No urgent portfolio purchase is required today.** The existing 2.5% weight is within a
  reasonable 2.25%-3.75% rebalance band around a 3% target. Hold the existing CF/PDBA mix.
  If the sleeve is deliberately filled to 3% using verified long-horizon risk capital, CF is
  the cleanest security for this exact thesis; cap CF around 1% of the total portfolio and use
  PDBA for the other roughly 2%. Do not fund the trade from protected cash.

## What the evidence establishes

### The disruption is real

The International Fertilizer Association estimated that the affected Gulf trade normally
represents approximately:

- 34% of global urea trade;
- 23% of global ammonia trade;
- 49% of global sulfur trade; and
- 18% of global MAP/DAP trade.

Source: [IFA 2026 Medium-Term Outlook](https://www.fertilizer.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/2026_IFA_Medium_Term_Outlook_Report_English.pdf).

UN/ITC trade data for April reportedly showed export volumes from Hormuz-dependent economies
down 83% for urea and 75% for ammonia year over year. FAO had separately estimated that 3-4
million tonnes of fertilizer trade per month were stalled early in the conflict. Those are
severe physical-flow disruptions, even though one month's year-over-year comparison is not a
forecast of the permanent loss.

Sources: [AMIS August market summary](https://www.amis-outlook.org/) and
[FAO conflict briefing](https://digital-media.fao.org/archive/CONFLICT-IN-MIDDLE-EAST--AGRIFOOD-IMPLICATIONS-2A6XC5LF6G3C.html).

FAO warns that missed fertilizer application windows can lower yields and tighten food supply
in the second half of 2026 and into 2027, especially in import-dependent and financially weak
countries. This validates the agricultural consequence, but it does not imply that every
fertilizer producer or every crop future must appreciate.

Source: [FAO fertilizer-scarcity warning](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/strait-of-hormuz-crisis--fertilizer-scarcity-will-affect-next-harvests-and-food-supplies--fao-warns/en).

### The price response is not one-way

The World Bank reported that urea exceeded $850/tonne in April, about 80% above February, and
forecast a roughly 60% average increase for 2026 before easing in 2027 as exports and new supply
recover. It also noted that the spike was smaller than 2021-22 because Northern Hemisphere
farmers had already secured significant supply, natural-gas prices rose less, and trade was
being rerouted. In July, the World Bank fertilizer index declined 4.3% month over month.

Sources: [World Bank fertilizer analysis](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/fertilizer-prices-surge-as-strait-of-hormuz-disruptions-tighten-) and
[World Bank July Pink Sheet summary](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/commodity-prices-broadly-unchanged-in-july---pink-sheet).

CF Industries said nitrogen prices had returned to pre-conflict levels by the end of Q2 as
Northern Hemisphere demand seasonally fell and the market expected some Gulf supply to return.
Nutrien subsequently said urea strengthened again in Q3 amid outages, disrupted trade, energy
costs and Indian/Brazilian demand. The correct description is therefore **tight and volatile**,
not a continuously accelerating shortage.

Sources: [CF Q2/H1 2026 results](https://ir.cfindustries.com/Investors/news/news-details/2026/CF-Industries-Holdings-Inc--Reports-First-Half-2026-Net-Earnings-of-1-34-Billion-Adjusted-EBITDA-of-2-18-Billion/default.aspx) and
[Nutrien Q2/H1 2026 results](https://www.nutrien.com/news/press-releases/nutrien-reports-second-quarter-2026-results-1753).

## Nutrient-by-nutrient verdict

| Nutrient/input | Hormuz exposure | Thesis verdict | Best listed expression |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Urea/ammonia/nitrogen | Very high | Strongest and most direct. Gulf export losses and high marginal European gas costs support North American producers. | CF; NTR as a diluted/diversified alternative |
| Sulfur | Very high | Physical shortage is real, but sulfur producers/logistics are not represented cleanly in the current sleeve. High sulfur prices can hurt phosphate producers. | No simple core vehicle recommended |
| Phosphate (DAP/MAP) | Medium/high, mainly through sulfur and ammonia | Product prices can rise, but producer margins may fall when sulfur/input cost rises faster. | Avoid treating MOS as a simple beneficiary |
| Potash (MOP) | Low direct exposure | Constructive demand is possible, but no Iran/Hormuz shortage thesis. Canada/Russia/Belarus and new capacity dominate. | NTR/MOS only for a separate potash thesis |
| Crop commodities | Second-order | Missed fertilizer can reduce yields and support crop prices, but weather, inventories, acreage, exports and farmer demand also dominate. | PDBA, understood as an indirect hedge |

## Security review

### CF Industries (CF): best fit, but not an undiscovered bargain

**Verdict: HOLD; conditional ADD only inside the existing 3% sleeve.**

Why it fits:

- It is a focused North American nitrogen producer, so the thesis maps directly to ammonia,
  urea and UAN margins.
- North American natural gas remains structurally advantaged relative to marginal European
  production.
- Q2 EPS was $4.73; trailing-12-month operating cash flow was $2.98B and company-defined FCF
  $1.82B. Production operated at 98% of available ammonia capacity.
- Management estimates the conflict removed 4.0-4.5 million tonnes of traded Middle East urea
  and about 1 million tonnes of ammonia to date.

Why not chase it:

- At about $125.40 on August 27, CF was up about 27.5% from February 27 and only about 8% below
  its one-year adjusted high. The headline 9.3x trailing P/E capitalizes cyclical, conflict-
  elevated earnings and includes a litigation settlement in H1.
- CF itself says nitrogen prices returned to pre-conflict levels at Q2 end before strengthening
  again. Earnings are highly sensitive to product prices, gas, outages and crop economics.
- Yazoo City remains out until an expected H1 2027 restart, and Blue Point requires capital.

Implementation:

- Cap CF at approximately **1.0% of the total portfolio**. It is a satellite equity, not safe
  commodity collateral.
- With CF near $125, do not buy simply because the thesis is in the news. A pullback toward the
  approximately $110-$115 area would provide a better starter/add level; this is a risk-control
  zone, not an intrinsic-value estimate. CF itself repurchased Q2 shares near an average $115.
- If immediate full-target implementation is required, use no more than the existing 0.5-point
  sleeve shortfall ($2,896) and split it into at least two tranches. The stronger default is to
  wait because the sleeve is already within band.

Source: [CF Q2/H1 2026 results](https://ir.cfindustries.com/Investors/news/news-details/2026/CF-Industries-Holdings-Inc--Reports-First-Half-2026-Net-Earnings-of-1-34-Billion-Adjusted-EBITDA-of-2-18-Billion/default.aspx).

### Nutrien (NTR): better diversification, less pure thesis

**Verdict: WATCH/HOLD; use instead of CF if diversification is the priority.**

- Potash, nitrogen and retail diversify single-product and single-asset risk.
- H1 adjusted EBITDA included $1.24B from Potash and $1.12B from Nitrogen; retail adds a more
  stable but lower-margin business.
- Low-cost North American nitrogen and Canadian potash assets are strategically strong.
- At about $73.04, the stock was roughly flat since February 27, so it has not reacted like CF.

Counterpoints:

- Potash is not a Hormuz-shortage commodity; including it dilutes the thesis.
- Retail customers can be hurt by fertilizer affordability and credit stress.
- Q2 nitrogen volume was down materially due to shutdowns/maintenance/deferred purchases, and
  phosphate margins were hurt by sulfur.
- The balance sheet carries material debt and the company is still optimizing/divesting some
  assets.

NTR is the better choice for a long-term multi-nutrient franchise; CF is the better choice for
this specific nitrogen-dislocation thesis. A 3% total sleeve does not need both unless each is
kept very small. Nutrien repurchased shares around $67-$70 in H1/early Q3; below roughly $70 is
a more attractive review zone than chasing above $73, but this is not a formal fair-value floor.

Source: [Nutrien Q2/H1 2026 results](https://www.nutrien.com/news/press-releases/nutrien-reports-second-quarter-2026-results-1753).

### Mosaic (MOS): wrong instrument for the simple thesis

**Verdict: AVOID as a Hormuz-shortage purchase.**

- MOS produces phosphate and potash, not nitrogen.
- Q2 reported a $273M net loss, adjusted EBITDA fell to $407M from $566M, operating cash flow
  fell to $167M and company-defined FCF was negative $153M.
- Phosphate gross margin was negative $4/tonne even with DAP selling prices up, because sulfur
  availability/affordability and low operating rates damaged economics.
- Potash remains reasonably healthy, but that is a separate thesis.

MOS could become a recovery investment **after sulfur normalizes** and phosphate margins repair.
That is nearly the opposite timing of a purchase based on continued severe Hormuz disruption.

Source: [Mosaic Q2 2026 SEC-filed release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1285785/000128578526000109/pressreleaseq22026-ex991.htm).

### CVR Partners (UAN): pure but unnecessarily risky

- At about $126.43, adjusted market data show a roughly 38.7% gain since February 27 and a price

Source: [CVR Partners Q2 2026 results](https://investors.cvrpartners.com/news/news-details/2026/CVR-Partners-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx).

### PDBA: good broad-agriculture diversifier, not fertilizer exposure

**Verdict: HOLD; do not call it a fertilizer trade.**

PDBA uses agriculture futures and collateral. It can benefit if lower fertilizer use reduces
future crop yields, but it also owns exposure to livestock, grains/oilseeds and soft commodities
whose returns depend on many unrelated variables. At about $38.50 it was at its one-year adjusted
high and roughly 11% above February 27, so there is no need to chase it.

The current net fee is 0.59% under a waiver scheduled through at least 2026-08-31, versus 0.75%
gross. Recheck the fee after September 1. PDBA remains preferable to DBA for a no-K-1 structure
and current lower cost, but its futures roll/collateral/tax behavior is not the same as owning
farmland, crops or fertilizer.

Source: [Invesco commodity ETF page](https://www.invesco.com/us/en/solutions/invesco-etfs/commodity-investing.html).

### Broad agribusiness ETFs and fertilizer futures

- MOO and VEGI are broad producer baskets, not focused fertilizer exposure. MOO's recent
  holdings included only about 6% NTR and 5% CF, alongside equipment, seeds, processors, animal
  health and other agribusinesses. They dilute the thesis and add overlap.
- The old Global X Fertilizers/Potash ETF, SOIL, was liquidated in 2020. Do not rely on current-
  looking third-party pages that still describe it.
- CME fertilizer futures are economically direct, but standard contracts can represent 100
  tons and are leveraged/industrial hedging instruments. They introduce margin, liquidity,
  curve and basis risk and are inappropriate for this small strategic sleeve.

Sources: [VanEck MOO holdings](https://www.vaneck.com/us/en/investments/agribusiness-etf-moo/holdings),

## Portfolio implementation

### Preferred 3% structure

For the stated strategic allocation, the cleanest structure is:

- **2.0% PDBA:** broad agricultural-commodity/food-inflation exposure;
- **1.0% CF:** explicit nitrogen/fertilizer-producer exposure.

This deliberately separates two different bets. PDBA is a crop/food commodity futures sleeve;
CF is a cyclical operating company. Do not count them as interchangeable.

If company-specific risk is unacceptable, replace the 1% CF allocation with NTR. Do not add
CF + NTR + MOS + UAN inside a 3% sleeve; that is false diversification because all remain
fertilizer-cycle equities and the positions become too small to manage meaningfully.

## What would invalidate or strengthen the trade

### Strengthen / allow an add

- sustained Gulf urea/ammonia export volumes remain far below normal into Q4;
- Q3/Q4 benchmark urea strengthens while North American natural-gas spreads remain favorable;
- Chinese exports fail to offset the Gulf loss or new export restrictions appear;
- India/Brazil tenders remain strong without major demand destruction;
- CF maintains high utilization and the share price offers a materially better entry.

### Weaken / trigger no-add or trim review

- Hormuz transit and Gulf production normalize faster than expected;
- Chinese/other supply, land routes and inventories close the deficit;
- urea prices fall despite continuing headlines, showing supply substitution/demand destruction;
- grain prices/farmer margins fall enough to reduce fertilizer demand;
- North American gas rises enough to erode CF/NTR's cost advantage;
- the total agriculture sleeve exceeds 3.75% or the aggregate resource block grows further.

## Decision

1. **Validate the macro thesis:** yes for nitrogen and sulfur scarcity; partly for phosphate;
   no for a broad potash/all-fertilizer shortage.
2. **Validate the portfolio area:** yes, a 3% agriculture/fertilizer satellite is reasonable;
   no, the evidence does not justify increasing it when the portfolio already has a 33.5%
   resource/inflation block.
3. **Buy now:** no urgent purchase. Hold existing CF/PDBA. If exact-target rebalancing is
   explicitly desired and funded from risk capital, CF is the single best add, limited to the
   current 0.5-point shortfall and a 1% total-portfolio cap. Prefer a staged add or a pullback
   toward $110-$115 rather than chasing at approximately $125.
