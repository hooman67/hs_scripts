# Research learnings

- A nonimmigrant classification is not the same thing as admission under a nonimmigrant **visa**. Federal firearm law and the current Form 4473 use the latter test.
- The missing visa foil is evidence but not the legal test. The decisive fact is whether the most recent admission actually used a visa.
- An I-797A approval notice is not itself a visa. Its attached I-94 documents status/authorized stay.
- “Resident” is context-specific. ATF's 27 CFR § 478.11 Example 4 treats an alien working for three years and making a home in a state as a resident of that state; IRS tax residence and immigration permanent residence are separate classifications.
- A hunting license is a federal exception for certain people admitted under a nonimmigrant visa. It does not fix California's separate “Federal Limits Apply” document rule and does not justify an inaccurate Form 4473 answer.
- UG Imports' generic page contains language that appears internally inconsistent for this case. Its reply linking that page was not individualized confirmation.
- A California utility bill may establish handgun residency, but it does not necessarily solve every federal or “Federal Limits Apply” identity-document requirement.
- An unexpired EAD (Form I-766) is on California DMV's REAL ID identity-document list, creating a clean route to remove the “Federal Limits Apply” trigger. The EAD is not itself listed in 11 CCR § 4045.1(b) as a direct supplement for the existing marked license.
- Filing Form I-765 or receiving its receipt/approval notice does not itself solve the California document issue. The practical path requires delivery of the physical unexpired I-766, issuance of the hard-copy California REAL ID, and then FFL pre-clearance.
- San Jose's gun-harm-reduction fee could not be verified as currently collectible. Its insurance and attestation requirements remain codified.
- California's residence storage rule changed for 2026. Secure storage is now the default whenever a firearm is not carried or readily controlled by a lawful authorized user.
- San Jose and the Santa Clara County Sheriff both publish CCW routes. The county route currently publishes lower agency fees and an application that expressly collects nonimmigrant/I-94 information, although its portal also contains contradictory citizenship wording.
