# Lawful firearm acquisition in San Jose for a Canadian in TN status

**Current through:** 2026-10-14  
**Scope:** General legal and administrative research based on the stated facts. It is not a personal eligibility opinion and does not replace the firearm lawyer's written advice.

**User-confirmed next route:** The user plans to file Form I-765. A filed or pending I-765 is not sufficient for the California documentation problem. The usable route begins after USCIS approves the application and delivers a physical, unexpired Form I-766 EAD card: use I-766 to obtain a hard-copy California REAL ID, then obtain FFL pre-clearance before DROS.

## The most important conclusion

Do **not** order a firearm, send one to a dealer, pay a nonrefundable transfer charge, or begin DROS yet.

The federal sources strongly support lawful federal eligibility on the stated facts because the most recent Canadian TN admission was visa-exempt. The immediate practical problem is different: California's rule for a driver's license marked “Federal Limits Apply” does not clearly list a visa-exempt Canadian passport with I-94/I-797A as an accepted supplemental document. The selected FFL must confirm in writing that it can process the exact documents before money changes hands.

## Facts and assumptions used

The research assumes all of the following are accurate:

- The buyer is at least 21 and is not prohibited for any independent criminal, restraining-order, mental-health, substance, dishonorable-discharge, fugitive, or other reason.
- The latest entry was a lawful Canadian TN admission without a visa, followed by an in-country I-797A extension, with no later international travel or re-entry.
- The current I-94/TN authorization remains valid and TN conditions continue to be maintained.
- The California driver's-license address is the current San Jose residence and matches the DROS address.
- Any hunting license and FSC described as current are actually issued and unexpired.

Five facts remain material: the firearm lawyer's actual conclusion; the hunting-license and FSC expiry dates; the complete post-entry immigration/travel and adjustment history; whether any independent firearm prohibition exists; and who in the home must be prevented from accessing the firearm. These do not prevent the document pre-clearance work below, but they must be resolved before purchase or possession.

## 1. Federal TN analysis

The exact quotation-and-location evidence chain for this conclusion is preserved in `research/san-jose-home-defense/TN_VISA_EXEMPT_EVIDENCE.md`.

### General rule — VERIFIED

18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(5)(B) applies to an alien admitted under a **nonimmigrant visa**, subject to § 922(y)(2). DOJ/ATF's 2012 final rule says the disability does not apply to nonimmigrants who lawfully entered without a visa, and such people do not need the hunting-license exception.

Canadian citizens ordinarily do not need a visa for TN admission. USCIS likewise says Canadian TN applicants may establish eligibility at a port of entry without applying for a TN visa at a consulate. An I-797A is an approval/replacement I-94, not a visa, and an in-country extension is not a new admission.

Tax residence and firearm-law state residence do not change that visa analysis. Under 27 CFR § 478.11, an alien who comes to work for three years, rents a home, moves personal possessions there, and resides there with family is a resident of that state. The rule separately says an alien merely visiting on vacation is not a state resident. Thus a TN professional can simultaneously be a TN nonimmigrant, a visa-exempt entrant, a California resident for an FFL transaction, and a U.S. resident for tax purposes. Each classification answers a different legal question.

On the stated facts, these sources strongly point to:

- Form 4473 question 20: enter the current I-94/admission number.
- Question 21.m.1 (“admitted ... under a nonimmigrant visa?”): **No**.
- Question 21.m.2: leave blank.
- No hunting-license exception documentation is federally necessary for that visa-exempt admission.

This conclusion is preserved as the current research-based Form 4473 position: **answer 21.m.1 “No,” enter the current I-94/admission number in question 20, and leave 21.m.2 blank**, assuming the facts remain unchanged. Filing Form I-765 or possessing an EAD does not itself create a visa or a new admission. Re-check the answer before signing if the person travels, re-enters on advance parole or a visa, loses or changes status, uses adjustment-based employment authorization in a way that affects TN maintenance, or becomes a permanent resident.

The buyer must answer personally and truthfully. Obtain the firearm lawyer's case-specific written confirmation before signing; an FFL cannot give the final legal answer and should never instruct a buyer to use an inaccurate answer.

### Hunting-license fallback — VERIFIED but conditional

If counsel instead concludes that the buyer was admitted under a nonimmigrant visa, 18 U.S.C. § 922(y)(2)(A) provides an exception while the person possesses a valid, unexpired U.S. government-issued hunting license or permit. The dealer must inspect and retain supporting information. Because § 922(g) covers continuing possession of firearms and ammunition, a person relying on this exception should keep the qualifying license continuously valid.

The hunting license does not solve California's separate identification rule.

### Immigration changes — HIGH-IMPORTANCE RECHECK

A pending I-485 does not itself grant permanent-resident status. Get fresh written advice before continued firearm or ammunition possession after international travel, advance-parole re-entry, TN expiration or violation, employment/status change, or adjustment approval. Once permanent residence is approved, the nonimmigrant-visa prohibition no longer applies to an LPR.

## 2. California's “Federal Limits Apply” problem

### Current regulation — VERIFIED; application to this document set UNRESOLVED

11 CCR § 4045.1 requires a buyer presenting a California license marked “Federal Limits Apply” to supply an additional listed document. The official list includes a foreign passport **with a valid U.S. immigrant visa and approved I-94**. It does not expressly list a Canadian passport plus I-94/I-797A for a visa-exempt TN admission.

California DOJ's general FAQ separately says a noncitizen should show lawful presence with an A-number or I-94. That broader FAQ does not erase the narrower “Federal Limits Apply” regulation. This is why dealer pre-clearance and California firearm counsel are necessary.

Getting a California REAL ID might eventually remove this particular marker issue, but DMV's current public checklist also does not clearly describe a visa-exempt Canadian TN document combination. Do not assume DMV can issue one without first confirming the route.

### What the current TN documents do and do not satisfy

As of 2026-10-14, the literal text of 11 CCR § 4045.1(b) lists seven acceptable supplemental documents. The only foreign-passport option is a valid, unexpired foreign passport **with a valid U.S. immigrant visa and approved I-94**. The following documents are not separately listed and should not be treated as substitutes without written DOJ confirmation:

- Canadian passport without a visa foil;
- I-94 by itself;
- I-797A with attached I-94;
- California hunting license;
- FSC;
- utility bills, lease, deed, or vehicle registration; or
- an EAD card presented directly to the firearm dealer.

Some of those documents serve other purposes. The hunting license may support a federal exception if one is needed; utility/lease/government records may prove handgun residence; and an EAD may qualify the holder for a REAL ID. They do not appear in § 4045.1(b)'s own supplemental-document list.

California DOJ's Final Statement of Reasons confirms that a commenter warned the regulation could block lawfully present nonimmigrants who are federally eligible. DOJ responded that such people could establish lawful presence with supplemental documentation, but the final list does not identify a visa-exempt Canadian passport plus I-94/I-797A. This creates a genuine textual and administrative gap; it is not safe to assume that a dealer may improvise an unlisted document.

- Final Statement of Reasons, pages 2–3, Comment/Response 2: https://oag.ca.gov/sites/all/files/agweb/pdfs/firearms/regs/id-fa-ammo-fsor.pdf

### Resolution routes, in recommended order

1. **If an unexpired physical EAD card (Form I-766) is available, obtain a California REAL ID.** California DMV's current checklist expressly accepts an unexpired EAD as the identity document for REAL ID. Once the driver license is REAL ID compliant and no longer marked “Federal Limits Apply,” § 4045.1(b)'s supplemental-document trigger does not apply. The EAD itself is not listed as a direct substitute at the firearm counter while the marked license remains in use.
2. **If there is no EAD, ask DMV for a written determination on the Canadian TN documents.** Ask whether DMV can issue REAL ID using a Canadian passport, current visa-exempt TN I-94, and I-797A through SAVE despite the public checklist. The published DMV list generally requires a foreign passport with a valid U.S. visa and I-94; passport plus I-94 without a visa is publicly listed only for citizens of the Freely Associated States. Do not count on this route until DMV confirms it.
3. **Ask California DOJ Bureau of Firearms for an administrative document-acceptance answer.** The narrow question is which § 4045.1(b) document a visa-exempt Canadian TN holder may use. Request a written answer through https://oag.ca.gov/firearms/contact; the published telephone number is 916-210-2300. Ask the selected FFL to raise the same exact question through its authenticated DES support route.
4. **If DMV and DOJ both say the current documents are insufficient, wait for or obtain a listed document.** A green card directly satisfies § 4045.1(b)(7). An unexpired EAD creates the DMV REAL ID route. A pending I-485, receipt notice, I-797A TN approval, or hunting license does not appear to do so by itself.
5. **If a prompt purchase remains necessary and the agencies cannot resolve the gap, retain California administrative/firearm counsel to seek written agency clarification or assess a challenge.** Do not use a failed DROS as the experiment because fees and transaction complications are avoidable.

### How to determine whether an EAD exists

An I-797A TN extension approval is not an EAD. An EAD normally results from a separate **Form I-765, Application for Employment Authorization**, and the approved document is a physical photo card identified by USCIS as **Form I-766**.

1. Sign in at https://my.uscis.gov/ and review **My Cases**. Look specifically for `I-765 — Application for Employment Authorization`. An I-485, I-129, I-131, or I-797A is not the same form.
2. Open the I-765 case, if present. A status such as `Card Was Produced`, `Card Was Mailed`, `Card Was Delivered`, or an approval notice means USCIS approved an EAD. `Case Was Received` or another pending status means there is no approved card yet.
3. Check paper and electronic USCIS notices for an I-797C or I-797 notice whose form type is **I-765**. Its receipt number is 13 characters—typically three letters such as IOE, MSC, NBC, LIN, SRC, WAC, or EAC followed by ten digits. Do not confuse it with the separate I-485 or I-129 receipt number.
4. If the case is not linked in the online account, use the I-765 receipt number at https://egov.uscis.gov/ or add the paper-filed case to the USCIS account. USCIS says paper-filed cases can be linked; an IOE case may require the Online Access Code mailed by USCIS.
5. Search physical files and mail for a wallet-sized photo card labeled `Employment Authorization Card`. The front generally identifies it as Form I-766 and shows a `Card Expires` date. For this DMV route, the safest document is the actual unexpired card.
6. If no I-765 case or receipt notice can be found, it was probably not filed with the adjustment package. Confirm directly with USCIS through Emma/live chat at https://www.uscis.gov/tools/meet-emma-our-virtual-assistant or the USCIS Contact Center at 800-375-5283. Ask: `Is there a Form I-765 case associated with my A-number or pending I-485, and if so, what is its receipt number and current status?`

USCIS states that Form I-765 is the application used to request an EAD and that, after approval, the EAD card is produced and mailed. Sources:

- Form I-765: https://www.uscis.gov/i-765
- USCIS online account: https://my.uscis.gov/
- Case Status Online: https://egov.uscis.gov/
- Account benefits, including linking paper-filed cases: https://www.uscis.gov/file-online/benefits-of-a-uscis-online-account
- USCIS Contact Center: https://www.uscis.gov/contactcenter

### I-765-to-REAL-ID sequence

1. File Form I-765 under the correct eligibility category. For a pending adjustment application this is commonly an adjustment-based category, but immigration counsel should confirm the correct category and whether using the resulting employment authorization affects maintenance of TN status.
2. Preserve the I-765 receipt notice privately. A receipt or pending-case status does not satisfy the DMV identity-document list.
3. Wait for USCIS approval and delivery of the physical Form I-766 Employment Authorization Card. Verify the name and expiration date.
4. Use the unexpired I-766 as the identity document in a California REAL ID application. Also bring the Social Security and California-residency documents required by DMV's current checklist.
5. Wait for the hard-copy REAL ID driver license. Do not rely on the temporary/interim paper license for DROS.
6. Send the selected FFL a copy-free document list for written pre-clearance: hard-copy California REAL ID, Canadian passport, current I-94, I-797A with attached I-94, FSC, and handgun-residency evidence. Provide copies of private immigration documents only through a secure channel if actually requested.
7. Re-check Form 4473 question 21.m.1 against the travel/status facts existing on the day it is signed. If nothing material changed, the preserved research-based answer remains No.

## 4. Ordinary California purchase steps after written pre-clearance

### Before going to the dealer

1. Confirm the exact handgun make, model, caliber, barrel length, and SKU is on California DOJ's handgun roster: https://oag.ca.gov/firearms/certified-handguns/search
2. Confirm FSC validity. An FSC costs $25, requires 23 correct answers out of 30, and is valid for five years. Official FAQ: https://oag.ca.gov/firearms/fscfaqs
3. Assemble originals in a folder:
   - California driver license.
   - Canadian passport.
   - latest CBP I-94 printout.
   - original I-797A with attached I-94.
   - current hunting license as backup or if counsel says it is required.
   - FSC.
   - at least two strong handgun-residency documents with matching name/address, such as a fixed-utility bill dated within three months plus current vehicle registration, signed lease, deed, or qualifying government license. A mobile-phone bill is not the safest choice.
   - the FFL's written acceptance and the lawyer's concise conclusion, if requested.
4. Ask for an itemized price including firearm, sales tax, California excise tax, DROS/state fees, dealer/transfer fee, lock or safe-device requirement, and any storage fee if pickup is delayed.

### At the first dealer visit

1. Complete DROS and Form 4473 truthfully. Read every question; do not let anyone answer for you.
2. The dealer verifies identity, residency, FSC, roster status, and immigration/admission number, then submits the background check.
3. Current state transaction charges total $37.19 ($31.19 DROS, $1 Firearms Safety, and $5 Safety and Enforcement), plus dealer, lock, tax, excise-tax, and transfer charges.
4. The statutory waiting period is 10 full 24-hour periods after DOJ acceptance/DROS number. The dealer gives the earliest lawful pickup time.

### Pickup

1. Return only after the exact earliest pickup time and within the dealer's 30-day delivery window. Missing the window usually means starting again and paying again.
2. Bring the requested originals again, the FSC, and the dealer-approved lock/device or buy one there as instructed.
3. Complete the safe-handling demonstration with the firearm and sign the required affidavit.
4. Before leaving, verify the serial number and model match the paperwork and obtain copies/receipts.

California DOJ dealer FAQ: https://oag.ca.gov/firearms/dlrfaqs

## 5. San Jose duties before bringing it home

### Insurance and attestation — VERIFIED

San Jose requires residents who possess a firearm in the city to maintain homeowners, renters, or firearm-liability coverage for accidental firearm-caused death, injury, or property damage. Ask the insurer in writing whether the personal-liability coverage applies to firearm accidents in the home and whether any firearm exclusion applies.

Complete and sign San Jose's insurance attestation before pickup. Keep it with the firearm wherever the firearm is stored or transported; do not submit it to the city unless lawfully requested. The separate annual fee remains in the municipal code, but no current collection portal, payable amount, or designated nonprofit could be verified from a current official page. Do not send money unless the city publishes a current official payment process.

### Home storage — VERIFIED

Since January 1, 2026, Penal Code § 25145 requires every firearm in a residence to be securely stored whenever it is not being carried or readily controlled by a lawful authorized user. Use a California-approved locking device or a secure gun safe that meets the statutory standards. A quick-access safe that is anchored to the building is prudent because it resists removal, although the state and San Jose provisions reviewed do not say that every safe must be anchored.

Keep the firearm inaccessible to children, visitors, prohibited persons, and anyone who has not been authorized and trained. Storage violations become more serious with repeat violations or access by a child/prohibited person.

- Penal Code § 25145: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=25145
- California DOJ safety-device search: https://oag.ca.gov/firearms/fsdcertlist

### Transport from dealer — obtain counsel confirmation

Penal Code §§ 25505 and 25525 appear to cover a legal resident taking an unloaded handgun in a locked container directly from lawful receipt to the residence, with only reasonably necessary deviations. Because some DOJ summaries use “U.S. citizen” language while § 25525 says citizen **or legal resident**, ask counsel to confirm that “legal resident” covers current TN status.

Until confirmed, use the conservative method: unloaded firearm, locked container other than the glove box, ammunition separate for simplicity, direct trip home, no unnecessary stops. A vehicle trunk may qualify as a locked container, but a dedicated locked case is clearer.

- California DOJ travel page: https://oag.ca.gov/firearms/travel
- Relevant Penal Code chapter: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?division=5.&chapter=2.&part=6.&lawCode=PEN&title=4.

## 6. Ammunition

California ammunition sales use a separate eligibility/background-check process, and the “Federal Limits Apply” document issue may arise again. Ask the pre-clearing dealer whether the same documents will work for ammunition. Avoid ordering ammunition online or bringing it into California until the dealer and counsel explain the lawful process for this case. Use only California-lawful magazines; for a straightforward purchase, stay at 10 rounds or fewer.

## 7. Concealed carry comes later

A home firearm does not authorize concealed public carry. The CCW process should begin only after a handgun is lawfully acquired and recorded to the applicant in California DOJ records.

### Most practical current route — Santa Clara County Sheriff, subject to pre-clearance

The county's current application explicitly collects citizenship, nonimmigrant status, I-94 number, nonimmigrant-visa status, and any § 922(y) exception. It does not publish citizenship as a standalone eligibility rule, but its portal contains contradictory citizenship wording. Before paying, email `ccw@shf.sccgov.org` and ask whether it will process a visa-exempt Canadian TN applicant, which immigration documents it needs, and whether the “Federal Limits Apply” license plus its residency documents is acceptable.

The county currently publishes $488 at initial submission and $488 at issuance, plus card fees, Live Scan, psychological assessment, 16-hour training, range, and ammunition. The payments are nonrefundable. It permits up to three listed handguns and requires at least one. After the application, the sequence includes interview, references, fingerprints/background investigation, psychological assessment, agency authorization to train, then 16 hours with an approved provider and qualification with every listed handgun.

- County application/process: https://scso.permitium.com/ccw/start
- County policy: https://permitium-downloads.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/SCSO_POLICY.pdf
- County CCW contact: `408-808-4942`, `ccw@shf.sccgov.org`

### San Jose Police route

SJPD also accepts applications from city residents through https://sjpdpermits.eprovalcloud.com/. Its current fee page lists a $1,591 city initial-application fee, with 20% due at filing and the balance at issuance, plus the state fee and separate psychology/training costs. Because this is materially more than the county's published agency fee, compare current written totals immediately before choosing.

CCW location restrictions are extensive and are still affected by litigation. Training is not a substitute for checking the current issuing-agency policy and California DOJ bulletin immediately before carrying. Never carry under a pending application; carry only after the physical license is issued, only a listed firearm, and only in lawful places and conditions.

## 8. Minimum legal rule for defensive force

Owning a firearm does not authorize using it to protect property alone. California's home-intruder presumption in Penal Code § 198.5 is limited to specified unlawful and forcible entries by a non-household/non-family intruder while inside the home. In general, deadly force requires a reasonable belief of imminent death or great bodily injury, a reasonable belief that immediate force is necessary, and no more force than reasonably necessary. It ends when the imminent threat ends.

- Penal Code § 198.5: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=198.5
- California criminal jury instructions: https://courts.ca.gov/criminal-jury-instructions-resource-center

Take a California-specific lawful-use-of-force course before relying on any firearm for defense. Continue using exterior lighting, reinforced doors/strike plates, alarms, cameras, and a family emergency plan; those measures help prevent a legal and physical emergency from developing.
