## Why This Cycle Failed

1. **Step 1 was harsh** (50% rejection): Many technologies explored were either too early (TRL 3-4: photonic logic, 3D chips, 90nm LEDs, plastic-to-hydrogen) or already well-known (event sensors — commercializing since 2020)

2. **Step 2 was devastating** (80% rejection): Every technology with commercial potential already has well-funded incumbents:
   - Event sensors: Prophesee + Sony + Samsung
   - PFAS removal: CycloPure already sells DEXSORB
   - Satellite water detection: ASTERRA deployed globally
   - AI chip design: Synopsys + Cadence dominate

3. **Step 4 killed the last survivor**: Focused microwave bioelectronics is genuinely novel (no competitors) but the path to market goes through FDA Class III device approval (5-7 years, $20M+) — incompatible with startup economics

---

## Key Patterns Learned

- **Regulatory-gated markets are startup death traps** unless you have $20M+ and 5+ years of runway. Medical devices, nuclear, and novel materials all require qualification timelines that exceed typical startup funding cycles.

- **The "clean tech" pattern**: Water, environment, energy — these domains attract massive R&D funding but commercialization requires infrastructure partnerships (utilities, municipalities) that move slowly. Best entered via a wedge product that doesn't require system-level integration.

- **Semiconductor/computing innovations** are nearly impossible for startups unless they're software-on-existing-hardware (like Cycle 002's alloy software). The fab/foundry requirement kills capex-constrained ventures.

- **Sensor innovations** are mature enough that incumbents dominate. Better to build APPLICATIONS on novel sensors than to make the sensor itself.

---

## Notable Technologies for Future Cycles

- **Event sensors as enabling tech**: While we can't make the sensors, applications built ON event sensors for power-constrained edge AI (agriculture monitoring, structural health, wearable wellness) could be explored in a future cycle as APPLICATION plays.

- **Focused microwave circuit printing**: If reframed as a RESEARCH TOOL (not medical device), the shorter regulatory path + smaller market might work as a Phase 1 stepping stone. Worth revisiting if regulations change or a non-medical application emerges (soft robotics, agricultural sensors on plants).
