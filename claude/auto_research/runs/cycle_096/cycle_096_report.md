# Cycle 096 Report — Ag Drainage-Tile (3/4): SAM-Expansion Escapes (Broaden the Buyer Base)

**Date**: 2026-07-09. **Round**: 4. **Domain**: #11 Ag Drainage-Tile.
**Cycle focus**: 095 killed the contractor-only plant on SAM (~$4–9M). This cycle tests whether
broadening the buyer base rescues it: (a) the WIDER underground-utility as-built market, (b)
subscription to the FARMER/landowner at scale, (c) a One-Call/811-style locate service for buried
tile, (d) transaction-driven demand (land sales / drainage disputes).
**Kill-risks**: farmer as-built already free in OEM clouds; 811/One-Call is a utility/government
mandate function (instant-reject buyer); broadening dilutes the moat.

---

## Step 1: SAM-expansion angles (4)

1. **Broaden to all buried private infrastructure** — irrigation, private utilities, effluent lines
   as-builts (not just tile). TRL 7.
2. **Landowner atlas at scale** — sell to the ~2M US farms, not the 2K contractors. TRL 7.
3. **Ag "One-Call" locate plant** — pre-excavation tile-locate lookup (811 doesn't cover private
   ag tile). TRL 6.
4. **Land-transaction drainage due-diligence** — as-built + drainage-status report at farmland sale
   (recurring, transaction-driven like TitlePlant). TRL 7.

**Step 1: 3 PROMOTED** (drop #1 — dilutes into the crowded general utility-as-built/GIS market:
Esri, ProStar PointMan, Bentley — density 4/5).

---

## Step 2: Incumbent / Buyer Check per angle

- **#2 Landowner atlas (REJECT):** the as-built is **already captured free** in the farmer's Ops
  Center / AgFiniti account (VERIFIED 094). Farmers won't pay a third party for what the OEM cloud
  gives them free; and reaching 2M farms is a consumer-scale distribution problem with no moat.
- **#3 Ag One-Call locate (REJECT — buyer check):** a locate/811 service is a **utility/government-
  mandate function**; the payer/mandator skews public-utility (12–24mo procurement, instant-reject).
  Private ag tile isn't covered by 811 precisely because there's no mandate → no non-deferrable
  demand and no institutional payer that fits the founder.
- **#4 Land-transaction due-diligence (BORDERLINE→REJECT on SAM):** genuinely TitlePlant-shaped
  (transaction-triggered, recurring). BUT US farmland *sale* transactions are relatively infrequent
  (~1–2% of ~900M acres turn over/yr), the drainage due-diligence line item is small, and the
  buyer/title/appraisal channel is thin. Even at a few thousand relevant transactions/yr × a
  modest report fee, SAM stays well under $100M and requires building the underlying plant first
  (chicken-and-egg cold-start with no anchor customer).

**Step 2: ALL REJECTED.**

---

## Step 3: Market note

None of the expansions crosses $100M without either (a) entering the crowded general utility-GIS
market (loses the niche moat + faces Esri/Bentley/ProStar) or (b) a public-utility buyer
(instant-reject). The niche that gives the moat is the same niche that caps the SAM.

## Summary

| Stage | In | Promoted | Rejected |
|-------|----|----------|----------|
| Step 1 | 4 | 3 | 1 |
| Step 2 | 3 | 0 | 3 |

**Graduates: 0.** Every SAM-expansion path either dilutes the moat into a crowded incumbent market,
hits an instant-reject public buyer, or still can't clear $100M. The moat and the market size are
in irreconcilable tension for this domain.
