# Round 3 Report — Casting a Wider Net (Cycles 041–050)

**Date**: 2026-07-01
**Author**: Claude (Opus 4.8), synthesizing the round-3 funnel run.
**Scope**: 10 new cycles across 10 previously under-explored, software-native domains,
run with three new mandatory gates: (1) Incumbent/OEM check, (2) Moat gate (does the
defensible asset accrue to the founder or to the system-of-record owner?), (3) Growth-rate check.
**Benchmarks to beat**: CutLog **8.85** (round-1 winner, already in flight);
non-CutLog #2 Compressed Air Leak Detection **7.65**; Fire Sprinkler Inspection **6.70**
(best defensible, diversification-friendly, pure-SW idea from the original 30).

## 1. Executive Summary — Did the wider net pay off?

**No. Casting the net across 10 software-native domains produced exactly ONE graduate
in 100 ideas scanned, and it does not beat what we already have.**

- **100 ideas scanned, 1 graduate** (TitlePlant AI, cycle 041). Nine of ten domains
  returned **zero graduates** — honest, correct zeroes, not lazy draws.
- The single graduate — **TitlePlant AI** (AI title-search/curative automation for
  independent title agents, built on a self-accumulating title-data plant) — scored a
  self-assessed **6/6** (founder-fit 6, feasibility 6) at the cycle stage. That is
  *below* CutLog (8.85), *below* Compressed Air (7.65), and *below* Fire Sprinkler (6.70).
  And every one of the 19 round-2 graduates that entered deep research at "8–9/10" got
  cut to ~5.1 — so a **cycle-stage 6/6 should be expected to grade in the low-5s** after
  deep research, once the same incumbent discount is applied.

**Why the net came back nearly empty — and why that is itself the finding.** The three new
gates worked exactly as designed. Software-native domains are the *most* capital-flooded,
incumbent-dense arenas in tech precisely *because* they are cheap to build and high-CAGR —
the two traits that attract maximum VC and maximum cloning. In every one of the ten domains
the moat gate was fatal by construction: the defensible asset (proprietary data, a system
of record, a certificate/credential network, a regulatory lock-in, GPU-scale compute, or a
rights-holder relationship) is **structurally owned by the incumbent/OEM, not acquirable by
a solo founder.** This is round-2's core lesson (moat 2.8 vs 5.4) reproduced at extreme scale.

**The one genuinely useful positive signal** is the *shape* of the sole survivor: TitlePlant
graduated for the one reason nothing else did — it is the rare white-collar niche where a
solo founder can **uniquely accumulate** a proprietary data asset (a title plant / chain-of-
title + defect graph) rather than layering AI on data an incumbent SaaS-of-record already
owns. That is structurally the same moat class as CutLog's parameter flywheel. But it grades
a middling 6/6 with real capital ($300–500K), E&O/legal-liability exposure, an ASSUMED
(403-blocked, unverified) growth rate, a housing-cyclical market, and — unlike CutLog — **no
owner-operator / Facebook-group distribution edge.** It does not clear CutLog or the #2/#3.

**Recommendation (see §4):** Do **not** run more cycles — this confirms domain exhaustion and
the round-2 conclusion. Do **not** deep-research any consumer, fintech, health, education,
climate, robotics, genAI-media, trust-safety, or bio-informatics idea from this round; all
nine domains are dead ends for this founder. **TitlePlant AI is the *only* candidate with a
real moat answer** and is the *only* thing in round 3 that could warrant a Step-6 deep dive —
but only if Hooman wants a fully-independent (non-fab) #2 and is willing to accept the E&O
exposure and verify the growth rate first. The safer, already-established answer remains
unchanged: keep CutLog as #1 and treat the **fab-shop platform** (CutLog + free Springback
calculator, Plasma held as first true 2nd product) as the primary #2 path.

## 5. New Patterns / Learnings (append-worthy to LEARNINGS.md)

1. **Software-native domains are the *most* incumbent-saturated, not the least — inverting the
   naive intuition.** Casting a wider net into pure-software verticals (fintech, health, edu,
   climate-SW, robotics-SW, genAI, trust-safety, bio-info, consumer) produced 1 graduate in
   100 ideas. The very traits that make a domain founder-friendly (cheap to build, high CAGR,
   no hardware) are what attract maximum VC and maximum cloning. **Low build cost is an
   anti-signal for defensibility at the domain level, not just the idea level.**

2. **The moat gate is the single highest-leverage filter ever added to the funnel.** Reframing
   the question from "is there competition?" to **"does the defensible asset accrue to the
   founder or to whoever owns the system of record / the data?"** killed 9 of 10 domains
   instantly and correctly. In software-native domains the answer is almost always "the
   incumbent," because the SaaS-of-record already sits on the flywheel data.

3. **"Founder-market fit is perfect" is a trap flag in crowded domains.** GenAI media (047)
   and fintech (042) are Hooman's *strongest*-fit domains and are the *worst* for
   defensibility — perfect fit means the space is obvious to every other capable founder too.
   Fit should be read as necessary-but-dangerous, never as a green light.

4. **Growth rate was never the constraint in round 3 — every one of 10 domains cleared the
   >15% CAGR bar.** Confirms the round-2 note that market size/growth is the *least*
   discriminating dimension. High CAGR is a *crowding cause* (it draws VC and OEM feature
   investment), not a green flag. Stop leading with market size.

5. **Distribution OEMs weaponize "free" to defend the core product.** In genAI media, trust &
   safety, education, and consumer, the platform that owns distribution (YouTube, Amazon,
   Meta, Google, Adobe, Unity, Epic, Intuit, Cloudflare, Skool) ships the exact feature *for
   free* to retain users — capping any standalone price at ~$0. Same structural cap seen in
   round 2 (Testo/Evergreen TAB apps), now confirmed as a domain-level pattern in software.

6. **The winning archetype is confirmed narrower than "pure software."** It is specifically
   **"a proprietary data flywheel the solo founder can bootstrap and uniquely own, in an
   underserved niche the incumbents aren't watching."** TitlePlant survived *only* because it
   is the one white-collar niche where the founder can *accumulate* proprietary data (a title
   plant) rather than layer AI on data an incumbent already owns. This is the CutLog moat class,
   and it is rare precisely because in most software verticals the SaaS-of-record owns the data.

7. **Adobe's own standards work forecloses one whole domain.** Content provenance (C2PA) is a
   free open standard governed by a 6,000-member coalition Adobe co-leads — Hooman's employer
   owns the "moat," so trust-safety provenance is a non-starter for him specifically.

8. **Domain exhaustion is now confirmed at the *category* level, not just the cycle level.**
   Round 2 flagged exhaustion at ~35 cycles within industrial niches; round 3 extended to 10
   entirely fresh, broad software domains and still returned 1 graduate. The leverage is no
   longer in scanning more domains — it is in deep-validating the handful of moat-bearing
   ideas already found (CutLog, the fab-adjacent cluster, Fire Sprinkler, possibly TitlePlant).
   **Recommendation: stop running new cycles.**
