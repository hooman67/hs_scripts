# Track B — Buy & Operate a Small BORING Physical/Local Business

**Shape owner:** research agent (Opus 4.8) · **Date:** 2026-07-01
**Mandate:** secondary income $5–30K/mo profit, part-time (10–20 hr/wk) or delegable, $25–150K of
Hooman's cash in (SBA leverage OK but keep his cash in range), AI-safe / boring / durable, with a
PROVEN path to success (real people already earn this money).

---

## TL;DR

The "buy a boring business" shape is legitimately one of the strongest fits for the Track-B mandate:
these are cash-flowing, recession-resistant, AI-can't-clone-a-laundromat businesses that trade at
low multiples (SDE 2–4x for most; storage/car-wash on cap-rate real-estate math). The whole
category is **the opposite of the swarmed VC/AI arena** — the moat is physical location, existing
cash flow, and the fact that most sellers are retiring boomers with no succession plan (the
"Silver Tsunami" — ~2.9M boomer-owned US businesses, ~$10T in value, coming to market this decade).

**The hard truth on capital fit:** most *good* cash-flowing physical businesses that throw off
$5–30K/mo cost **$300K–$1.5M** at market multiples. Hooman's $25–150K cash is a **down payment**,
not the whole price — the play is **SBA 7(a) with 10% down** (SBA lowered the min injection; ~10%
is standard) on a business with an existing manager, or buying a **route / vending / ATM / small
laundromat** outright at the low end. This is fine and normal, but it means the honest framing is
"$25–150K down + SBA leverage + a personal guarantee," not "buy a $30K/mo business for $100K cash."
That personal guarantee is the single biggest downside risk in this shape and is called out per-idea.

**Best sub-plays for Hooman specifically (ranked):**
1. **Self-storage (small/secondary-market facility, manager-run)** — most passive, real-estate-backed
   downside, boringest, AI-proof. Weighted 8.0.
2. **Laundromat (established, card-system, part-time attendant)** — proven 25–35% ROI, semi-absentee,
   biggest owner community. Weighted 7.9.
3. **Bread / FedEx / distribution route** — lowest capital, highest cash-on-cash, but a "job you own"
   unless you hire a driver; delegable. Weighted 7.6.
4. **Car wash (self-serve/IBA, not full-labor tunnel)** — high margin, real estate backed, but bigger
   check. Weighted 7.2.
5. **Vending / ATM route** — truly passive and cheap, but low ceiling and grind to scale. Weighted 7.0.

## Why this whole SHAPE fits the mandate (Step 2 — Crowding & Durability)

- **Not swarmed by VC/AI.** No YC startup is cloning your laundromat. The "search fund / boring-business"
  scene (Codie Sanchez "Contrarian Thinking," WSB / "HoldCo" Twitter, r/smallbusiness) is loud, but that's
  *content* about buying — actual small-business acquisition stays fragmented and local. AI is a **tailwind**
  (cheaper bookkeeping, remote management software, dynamic pricing for storage) not a threat.
- **Durable 5–10 yr.** People will wash clothes, store junk, eat bread, and pull cash from ATMs. These
  categories have existed for 50+ years and are consolidating slowly, not disrupting.
- **Structural reason competition stays low:** it's *boring and illiquid.* Deals are found via brokers,
  cold outreach to retiring owners, and local networks — not a marketplace anyone can arbitrage. That
  friction is the moat.
- **Silver Tsunami tailwind (buyer's opportunity):** millions of boomer owners with no successor; supply
  of sellers > supply of competent operator-buyers. This is the demographic engine behind the entire play.

**MARKET REALITY on multiples (proven, cited):** BizBuySell Q1 2026 Insight Report — overall US
small-business median sale price **$350K**, median cash flow **$165K**, average **cash-flow multiple 2.7x**;
Service businesses (42% of all transactions) median sale **$350K**, median cash flow **$167K**. So a
"$150–250K cash flow" business typically costs ~$400–700K — SBA territory, not all-cash. (Source: BizBuySell
Insight Report Q1 2026.)

## The Track-B Funnel applied to each sub-shape (Steps 1–4, with REAL listings/evidence)

### 1) Self-storage (small facility, secondary market) — TOP PICK
- **What:** buy a 20–60K sq ft self-storage facility (or convert/expand a small one). Own the dirt +
  the operation. Software (Storable/SiteLink) + a part-time manager or fully remote/automated (kiosk +
  cameras) run day-to-day; you handle pricing and capex.
- **Real listings (LoopNet, 2026):** Aspen Storage, Montrose CO **$995K @ 5.32% cap**; 3650 S Classen
  Blvd, Norman OK **$2.995M @ 8.00% cap**; Heartland Storage, Fruita CO **$4.9M @ 5.87% cap**; Center
  Point Storage, AL **$1.5M / 17,025 sq ft**. LoopNet shows **597 facilities for sale**; cap rates
  **4.76%–8.00%**.
- **Economics:** self-storage runs **~55–65% NOI margin** (expense ratio ~35–45%) — one of the highest of
  any real-estate class; breakeven occupancy is low (~40–50%). A sub-$1M secondary-market facility at an
  8% cap = ~$80K NOI ≈ **$6.7K/mo** before debt; with expansion/RM (revenue management) upside. Hooman's
  $25–150K = ~10–15% down on a $1M facility via SBA 504/7(a).
- **Fit:** most passive of the physical shapes (remote-manageable, low labor), real-estate collateral
  cushions downside, boring and AI-proof. Analytical/infra brain is a real edge on dynamic pricing +
  automating the front office.

### 2) Laundromat (established, card system) — TOP PICK
- **What:** buy an existing, stabilized laundromat (ideally card/app-based, newer high-efficiency machines,
  long lease). Run semi-absentee with one part-time attendant; owner does vendor mgmt, collections
  oversight (now digital), and marketing.
- **Evidence people make the money:** Laundromat Resource (largest owner community + podcast + forum)
  and industry data: **typical ROI 25–35%**; a **$250K laundromat returns ~$62.5–87.5K/yr** (~$5.2–7.3K/mo).
  Existing laundromats sell for **~3.5–4.5x net operating income**. Public success stories (e.g., Hyacinth
  Tucker, featured by Laundromat Resource, scaling to multi-store). Owner community is the highest-signal
  validation channel and confirms these numbers repeatedly.
- **Economics:** a stabilized store doing ~$25–35K/mo revenue at ~30% margin nets **$7.5–10K/mo**; two
  stores → $15–20K/mo. Card systems (CCI/ESD, PayRange, Cents) make it genuinely semi-absentee.
- **Fit:** proven playbook, huge owner community, learnable, delegable. Watch: lease terms, utility costs,
  neighborhood/demographics (renter-heavy = good).

### 3) Bread / FedEx / distribution route — LOWEST CAPITAL, HIGHEST CASH-ON-CASH
- **What:** buy a protected/semi-protected distribution route (Bimbo, Flowers/Wonder, Campbell's Snacks,
  Utz) or a FedEx Ground contract territory. You (or a hired driver) deliver/merchandise a fixed territory.
- **Real listings (RoutesForSale.net, 2026):**
  - **Flowers Bread, Calera AL: $99,000 asking / $77,759 cash flow** (≈0.79x price!) 
  - **Flowers Bread, Pensacola FL: $500K / $231,718 cash flow**
  - Bimbo, Gadsden AL: $150K / $74,906; Bimbo, Willcox AZ: $200K / $124,227
  - Campbell's Snacks, Yakima WA: $524K / $151,564
  - **FedEx Ground, Eastern KY: $1.1M / $308,186 cash flow**; Peoria IL: $1.2M / $267K
- **Economics:** bread routes commonly trade at **~1.5–3x cash flow** (far below "real" businesses because
  they're closer to a job) → several here are within Hooman's cash range **outright** and yield
  **$44–231K/yr**. FedEx routes yield more but cost $1M+ (SBA) and need multiple drivers.
- **Fit:** cheapest entry, but a single bread route without a hired driver is a 5-day-a-week job (breaks the
  part-time gate). The part-time version = **hire a driver / buy a multi-route territory with drivers in
  place**, which lowers your net but keeps it delegable. FedEx routes are explicitly manager/driver-run.

### 4) Car wash (self-serve or in-bay automatic; NOT full-labor tunnel)
- **What:** buy a self-serve or in-bay-automatic (IBA) car wash — low labor vs. a full express tunnel.
- **Real data (BizBuySell car-wash category, 419 listings):** median asking **$1.475M**, median revenue
  **$750K**, median SDE **$177K**, **earnings multiple 6.46x** (real-estate-heavy), revenue multiple 2.26x.
  Lower-quartile SDE **$107K** (~$8.9K/mo). Entry-level self-serve options under **$100K** exist.
- **Economics:** express tunnels can net huge (a CA multi-site listed at $13.5M / $930K cash flow) but need
  full-time labor. **Self-serve/IBA** = high margin, minimal staff, real estate backed — better semi-absentee
  fit, ~$5–12K/mo net at the small end.
- **Fit:** good margins + real-estate downside protection, but bigger check and the express-tunnel version
  is labor-heavy. Self-serve/IBA only for the part-time mandate.

### 5) Vending route (snack/drink or micro-market)
- **What:** buy an established vending route (machines + locations + contracts).
- **Real data (BizBuySell vending, 112 listings):** median asking **$140K**, median revenue **$154K**,
  median SDE **$76,105** (~$6.3K/mo). Examples: Birmingham AL **$215K / $60K SDE / 65 machines / 21
  locations**; Fort Worth TX **$250K / $105K SDE / 24 machines**; Chicago **$239K / $74,856 / 24 coffee
  machines**; Tampa **$749,900 / $210K SDE / 250 machines**.
- **Economics:** within cash range at the low/mid end; **$6–9K/mo** net is realistic for a solid route.
  Micro-markets (unattended retail) are the growth/upgrade path.
- **Fit:** learnable, delegable (route drivers), low downside (machines have resale value, no real estate
  lease exposure if placed on host locations). Ceiling is lower and scaling = a grind of restocking logistics.

### 6) ATM route
- **What:** buy an ATM route (machines placed in bars, c-stores, etc.); revenue = surcharge per withdrawal.
- **Real data (BizBuySell ATM, 42 listings):** median asking **~$300K**, median cash flow **~$70K**
  (~$5.8K/mo). Examples: Seattle Metro **$135K / 24 units / $62,400 cash flow**; Houston **$750K / 119 units
  / $300K**; New Orleans **$1.4M / 275 units / $500K**. Marketed as **"hands-off / vendor-loaded"** passive.
- **Economics:** genuinely among the most passive; a small route in cash range nets **$5–6K/mo**. Main
  risks: cash-handling logistics, declining cash usage over 5–10 yr (a real durability question), location
  churn.
- **Fit:** most passive + cheap, but **cash-usage secular decline** is the durability yellow flag here.

### 7) Semi-absentee franchise (adjacent, mentioned for completeness)
- **What:** brands explicitly built for absentee owners with a GM — e.g., car-wash franchises, laundromat
  franchises (Tide Cleaners), fitness, some service brands.
- **Note:** franchises add fees/royalties and territory constraints; investment for absentee-model brands is
  usually **$200K–$1M+**, above Hooman's cash but SBA-financeable. The independent buys above (storage,
  laundromat) generally beat franchises on economics for a capable operator; franchise = pay a premium for a
  playbook Hooman can learn anyway. Kept as a fallback, not a top pick.

## Step 3 — Path-to-Success: concrete first 90 days (generic to the shape)

1. **Weeks 1–3 — Pick lane + get pre-qualified.** Choose 1–2 sub-shapes (recommend storage + laundromat).
   Get SBA pre-qualification from an SBA-preferred lender (Live Oak Bank is the largest SBA 7(a) lender and
   specializes in these categories) so you know your buying power ($100K cash ≈ ~$1M purchase at 10% down).
2. **Weeks 2–8 — Deal flow.** Set BizBuySell / LoopNet / Crexi alerts; contact 5–10 business brokers in
   target metros; for laundromats join Laundromat Resource forum; for storage use broker networks. Do
   direct-mail/cold outreach to retiring owners for off-market deals (the Silver Tsunami edge).
3. **Weeks 6–12 — Underwrite + LOI + diligence.** Verify seller financials (tax returns, utility bills,
   collection logs, rent roll for storage). Confirm lease length (laundromat/vending) or fee-simple real
   estate (storage/car wash). Model debt service against NOI/SDE; require DSCR > 1.3.
4. **Close + install management.** Line up the manager/attendant/driver BEFORE closing; keep the seller on
   30–90 days for transition. Instrument everything with software (Storable, Cents/Spyder Wash, VendSys) so
   you run it in <10 hr/wk.

**Validation before capital:** shadow an operator (Laundromat Resource meetups; storage owner forums), and
put your model in front of the target's actual bank statements. Never buy on the broker's pro-forma.

## Step 4 — Fit & downside for Hooman

- **Capital:** $25–150K cash = down payment on a $250K–$1.5M business via SBA (10% down + PG), OR an
  outright buy of a low-end route/vending/ATM/small laundromat. Both are inside the mandate.
- **Time:** all are 10–20 hr/wk **once a manager/attendant/driver is in place** and software is installed.
  Single bread route or full-labor tunnel WITHOUT staff = full-time → excluded unless delegated.
- **Unfair advantage:** infra/analytics brain → dynamic pricing (storage), remote monitoring & automation,
  clean bookkeeping/ops dashboards; capital + W-2 income → easy SBA qualification. Mech-eng friends help
  vet laundry/car-wash equipment.
- **THE downside risk (shape-wide):** SBA loans require a **personal guarantee** and often a lien on the
  home. A bad deal can cost more than the $25–150K — it can threaten personal assets. Mitigate: keep LTV
  modest, DSCR > 1.3, prefer real-estate-backed deals (storage/car wash) where the dirt limits loss, and
  don't over-leverage. This is why cash-heavy / real-estate-backed picks score higher on downside safety.
