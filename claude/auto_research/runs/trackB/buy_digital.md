# Track B — Buy & Operate an Existing Cash-Flowing Digital Business

**Shape owner:** Hooman Shariati. **Researched:** 2026-07-01 by Claude (Opus 4.8, 1M).
**Mandate:** secondary/lifestyle income, $5–30K/mo profit, $25–150K of own capital, ~10–20 hrs/wk
(or delegable), AI-safe / not-swarmed, CLEAR PROVEN PATH TO SUCCESS.

**Scope of this file:** Acquire a profitable small digital business (content/niche site, SaaS,
newsletter, ecommerce brand, or app) in the $25–150K range via Empire Flippers / Acquire.com /
Flippa / MicroAcquire, then run and improve it. Realistic multiples, what actually sells, the
improve-it playbook, and the risk register.

---

## 0. Why this shape fits the Track-B mandate

Buying an *already-cash-flowing* business is the single most direct answer to Track B's #1
filter ("clear, concrete, PROVEN path to success"): the business already prints the money on
day one. You are not betting on a build succeeding — you are buying a P&L and trying to hold
or grow it. Downside is bounded to the purchase price (all-cash, no personal guarantee), which
directly satisfies the "can't lose more than $25–150K" gate. It is delegable: portfolios of
these are run by part-time operators and by roll-up funds (Onfolio, WebStreet) precisely
because they don't need a full-time founder.

Founder-fit is strong-to-neutral: Hooman's analytical/engineering skills map well to diligence
(reading traffic analytics, verifying revenue, spotting fake metrics) and to *improving* a site
with automation. His AI skills are a **genuine post-acquisition tailwind** (cutting content
production cost, automating ops) — but AI is also the biggest *threat* to one sub-shape
(SEO content sites), which is the core tension in this file.

## 1. How the market actually works (multiples & mechanics)

Small digital businesses are priced on a **multiple of monthly (or annual) net profit / SDE**
(Seller Discretionary Earnings = net profit + owner's add-backs).

**Verified benchmark multiples (mid-2026):**

| Asset type | Multiple (monthly profit) | ≈ Annual profit multiple | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content / affiliate sites | 30–45× monthly | **2.5–3.75×** annual | The Website Flip, 2026 avg (thewebsiteflip.com) |
| Amazon FBA | 20–36× monthly | ~1.7–3× annual | Empire Flippers live listings |
| Small SaaS | 25–40× monthly | ~2–3.5× annual (higher for growing/low-churn) | Empire Flippers live listings; FE International |
| Micro-PE roll-up buy price | — | **3–4× free cash flow** | Onfolio investor materials (onfolio.com) |
| Empire Flippers overall | 20–60×+ monthly | 1.7–5×+ annual | EF "how much is my website worth" page |

**Implication for a $25–150K budget:** at ~2.5–3.5× annual profit, that buys a business earning
roughly **$8K–$60K/year net = ~$650–$5,000/mo**. To land in the $5–30K/mo target you either
(a) buy near the top of budget ($100–150K) a business already doing ~$3–5K/mo and grow it, or
(b) buy 2–3 smaller businesses and run them as a mini-portfolio, or (c) buy one and grow it
over 12–24 months toward the target (the flip playbook below).

## 2. The improve-it playbook (how operators grow an acquired asset)

Real, documented flips from The Website Flip (thewebsiteflip.com case studies):

- **Dating content site:** bought early-2019 for **$32,000** (~$1,000/mo). Grown to **$9,000/mo**;
  now valued **$270K–$360K**. ~10× money over several years.
- **Outdoor content site:** bought Apr-2020 for **$23,000** (~$300/mo). Scaled to **$3,000–$6,000/mo**;
  valued **$140K–$160K**.
- **Wellness site:** bought Jul-2020 **$5,650** ($300/mo) → sold Dec-2020 **$30,100** ($936/mo). 5 mo flip.
- **B2B aged-domain (3,000 email list, no revenue):** bought May-2023 **$3,000** → sold Oct-2023 **$15,000**. 5 mo.

Repeatable growth levers operators use:
1. **On-page SEO + content refresh** on existing ranking pages (raise CTR/position).
2. **Add/optimize monetization** — swap to higher-CPM ad network (Mediavine/Raptive), add
   affiliate offers, add a digital product or email capture.
3. **Publish into proven topical clusters** (the site already ranks → expand the winning cluster).
4. **Cut cost / automate ops** — AI-assisted content ops, VA for publishing, automate reporting.
5. For SaaS: **fix churn, raise price, add an annual plan, plug a leaky funnel** — usually the
   prior indie owner never optimized pricing or did any marketing.

## 3. The concrete opportunities (sub-shapes), ranked

I treat each *type* of acquirable business as an opportunity, because the mandate is "buy one
of these." Ranked best-fit first.

### A. Small B2B / vertical micro-SaaS (Acquire.com / EF), $25–150K
Buy a boring vertical SaaS doing $2–8K MRR from an indie who is tired of it. Highest
founder-fit (Hooman can read the code, own the roadmap, and *improve* it), most durable
(recurring revenue, switching costs, not one-Google-update-from-zero). Evidence: EF/Acquire
listings above; Acquire.com $500M+ closed, 2,000+ sold. **This is the top pick.**

### B. B2B / SaaS-adjacent content site (not consumer SEO), $25–150K
A niche site serving a *profession/industry* (leads, sponsorships, a small tool, an email list),
less exposed to Google's consumer content purge. Evidence: the B2B aged-domain flip; the
"Information/Business" EF affiliate listings.

### C. Newsletter / paid community in a boring niche, $25–150K
Owned audience (email = not Google-dependent), sponsorship + subscription revenue, very
delegable. Multiples ~2.5–3.5× annual profit. AI-resistant (trust/curation moat).

### D. Buy 2–3 small assets as a mini-portfolio (the Onfolio/WebStreet model, solo)
Spread $100–150K across a content site + a micro-SaaS + a newsletter to diversify the
single-point-of-failure risk. Evidence: Onfolio (3–4× FCF) and WebStreet (8–12% yield, 20% IRR).

### E. Established Amazon FBA brand with a moat (NOT commodity churn), $25–150K
Only a *branded, differentiated, review-moated* FBA business — never a generic dropship/clone.
Evidence: EF FBA listings at $2–4K/mo. Lower rank: supplier/China/platform risk, thin durability.

### F. Consumer SEO / display-ad content site, $25–150K — **CAUTION / mostly reject**
The classic flip (dating/outdoor case studies show it *worked* historically) but now the most
AI-and-Google-exposed sub-shape. Include only for a *recovered/diversified* site bought cheap.

## 4. Crowding & durability

- **Buying** small digital businesses is a real but *not swarmed* activity — it's operationally
  heavy diligence work that most people won't do; the roll-up funds are the "crowd" and they
  buy at 3–4×, leaving plenty of $25–150K deals for a diligent solo buyer.
- **Durability by sub-shape:** SaaS/newsletter/community (recurring, owned-audience) = durable
  and AI-tailwind. Consumer SEO content = **structurally threatened** by Google's Helpful
  Content Update (2023–24 wiped 50–90% traffic off many affiliate sites) and by AI Overviews /
  answer engines eating informational clicks. This is the biggest durability risk in the shape
  and the reason to weight toward SaaS/newsletter/B2B over consumer SEO.

## 5. 90-day start plan (generic, tune per deal)

1. **Days 1–20:** Pick sub-shape (recommend SaaS or newsletter/B2B). Create free accounts on
   Empire Flippers, Acquire.com, Flippa. Set watchlists/filters: $25–150K, ≥12 mo history,
   recurring or diversified revenue. Read EF/FE valuation guides. Join operator communities
   (r/juststart, r/SaaS, Dynamite Circle, Trends/Indie Hackers acquisition threads).
2. **Days 20–50:** Diligence 15–25 listings; shortlist 3–5. Verify traffic (Google Analytics
   read access, GSC), verify revenue (Stripe/ad-network/Amazon screenshots + bank), check
   traffic concentration, backlink/keyword risk, churn, code quality. Use EF's migration/escrow.
3. **Days 50–75:** Make offers below ask; negotiate seller financing / earnout to reduce
   cash-at-risk and align seller. Close via escrow (EF/Acquire handle this). Complete asset +
   ops handover with a 30–60 day transition support clause.
4. **Days 75–90:** Take over ops (aim <10 hrs/wk), install analytics/reporting, ship the first
   1–2 improvement levers (pricing tweak for SaaS; content refresh + ad-network upgrade for
   content; sponsorship outreach for newsletter). Hire a VA/contractor for recurring tasks.

## 6. Risk register

- **Fraudulent/inflated metrics** (esp. Flippa): faked revenue, purchased traffic, hidden
  Google penalty. Mitigant: buy via curated EF/Acquire, escrow, verify at the source.
- **Google dependency (content sites):** one algo update = -50–90% overnight. Mitigant: avoid
  single-traffic-source consumer SEO; prefer recurring/owned-audience.
- **AI substitution:** AI Overviews cut informational-content clicks; AI clones cheap SaaS.
  Mitigant: buy defensible SaaS (integrations, switching costs, B2B) or owned-audience assets.
- **Post-sale decline / key-person risk:** revenue leaves with the founder's relationships or
  the seller re-competes. Mitigant: non-compete, transition period, earnout.
- **Overpaying:** paying 4–5× for a flat/declining asset. Mitigant: buy ≤3× on flat, demand
  seller financing, model downside.
- **Downside cap:** all-cash means max loss = purchase price. No SBA personal guarantee needed
  at this size. Satisfies the "can't lose more than the $25–150K" gate.

---

## 7. Verdict

Strong fit for the mandate. It is the *most literal* proven path (buy existing cash flow), it is
delegable/part-time, and downside is capped at cash in. **Best sub-play: buy a boring B2B
micro-SaaS ($2–6K MRR, $80–150K) with an underserved indie owner, then apply
pricing/marketing/AI-ops improvements** — highest founder-fit, most durable, AI as tailwind not
threat. Second: a niche newsletter/paid community. **Avoid** consumer SEO content and commodity
FBA. Realistic outcome: land at $3–5K/mo net at purchase, grow toward $8–15K/mo over 12–24 mo,
i.e. inside the $5–30K target, with a 25–35% cash-on-cash yield mirroring what WebStreet/Onfolio
achieve at scale.
