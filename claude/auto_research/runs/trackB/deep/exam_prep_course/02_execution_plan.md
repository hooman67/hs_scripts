**For**: Hooman Shariati (Adobe eng manager; ~10–20 hrs/wk; $25–150K capital available; target $5–30K/mo; already building CutLog).
**Slug**: `exam_prep_course`. **Date**: 2026-07-01. **Author**: Claude (Opus 4.8).
**Verdict feeding this plan**: PASS, MEDIUM confidence — but *conditional*. See `01_research_validation.md`.

---

## 0. Read this first — what this plan is NOT

The original pitch ("build a course on a mandated exam, 85% margin, near-passive after 3–6 months, $5–25K/mo")
is **rewritten here around the validated reality**:

- **It is a DISTRIBUTION business, not a passive-income business.** Content build is the easy 20%. Ranking (SEO/YouTube/ads) against focused incumbents is the whole game and is ongoing 15–20 hrs/wk work.
- **The headline number is a top-decile outcome, not the median.** Median new course in an owned niche makes ~$0–3K/mo. $5–25K/mo is "if you win the ranking war."
- **The load-bearing assumption is UNVERIFIED.** No "large + rising + weak-incumbent" niche was found in research. Every attractive mandated exam (Part 107, insurance, EPA 608, NABCEP, ham, pilot) is already owned by an entrenched specialist. **Finding a real gap IS the first bet.**
- **This attacks Hooman's one documented weakness** (fragmented-buyer distribution grind) while CutLog is still in its own 0→1 grind.

**Therefore this plan is front-loaded with a cheap KILL GATE.** ~$0–500 and ~15–20 hours of Hooman's time are spent proving a niche exists *before* any course is built. If Gate 0 fails, STOP. Do not build.

## 1. The specific target

Research found **no verified viable niche**. The plan does not pretend otherwise. Instead:

**Phase 0 selects the target via primary data.** The target is defined by a filter, not a guess:

> A mandated/legally-required certification exam with **≥30–50K new candidates/year** (from the certifying body's own published test-taker stats — not aggregators), a **defensible $150+ price point**, and **top-3 incumbents that are demonstrably weak** (dead/dated sites, no recent exam-update, poor reviews, no refund guarantee, no active YouTube/SEO presence).

**Least-bad bias if a tie:** a **narrow, genuinely technical exam adjacent to Hooman's EE/RF/wireless/cloud skills** — where he can produce *visibly better technical content* than a generalist incumbent, giving a thin content moat a generalist can't clone as fast. Candidate hunting-ground exams to *probe* (NOT pre-committed; must pass Gate 0):
- RF/wireless operator or radio-frequency safety certs
- Specific networking/cloud/security operator certs that are *employer-mandated* and where incumbents are stale
- Emerging-tech operator licenses (new enough that incumbents haven't colonized, old enough to have volume)

**Explicitly BANNED targets** (saturated — auto-reject): FAA Part 107 / drone, insurance producer/adjuster, EPA 608, NABCEP, FAA pilot (private/instrument), PMP, CPA, ham radio (free HamStudy caps price).

## 2. Cheapest-possible validation BEFORE money — Gate 0 (Weeks 1–3)

This is the entire first bet. **Cost: ~$0–500. Time: ~15–20 hrs total.** No course built yet.

**Deliverable: a one-page "Niche Dossier" scoring 8–12 candidate exams** on four hard filters. Each filter is pass/fail; a niche must pass all four.

| Filter | How to check (cheap) | Pass bar |
|---|---|---|
| **Volume** | Certifying body's *own* published test-taker/pass stats (FOIA-style pages, annual reports). NOT third-party estimators. | ≥30K new candidates/yr |
| **Price** | What do existing courses charge? Is there a strong FREE option? | Sustainable ≥$150; no dominant free competitor |
| **Incumbent weakness** | Manually audit top-3 Google/YouTube results: last content update, review quality, refund guarantee, ad presence (check if they bid on keywords via a $50 Google Ads test or SpyFu free tier) | ≥2 of top-3 are dated/absent/weak |
| **Founder edge** | Can Hooman produce *better technical* content than incumbents? | Yes for technical exams; No = deprioritize |

**Gate 0 decision:**
- **≥1 niche passes all four filters → GO to Phase 1** with that niche.
- **Zero niches pass → HARD STOP.** Kill the idea. Reallocate hours to CutLog. This is the expected outcome per research and is a *win* — you spent <$500 and ~20 hrs to avoid a 12–24 month distribution grind that loses to incumbents.

> **Do NOT rationalize a marginal niche through Gate 0.** The research already found that borderline niches lose. If nothing clears cleanly, stop.

## 4. Months 4–12 — path to revenue + delegation (MUST stay part-time)

The core constraint: **Hooman must not become the operator.** His weekly role caps at ~5 hrs (technical content QA + exam-change monitoring + strategic decisions). Everything else is delegated.

**Delegation stack (this is how it stays part-time):**

- **Video editor** (per-project, ~$500–1K/mo): repurpose lessons into YouTube/shorts.
- **Media buyer / VA** (~$500–1.5K/mo, ramp when CAC is proven positive): manage paid ads.
- Hooman: technical accuracy, exam-change updates (non-delegable), P&L decisions.

**Revenue path (realistic, incumbent-adjusted):**

| Month | New buyers/mo | Price | MRR-equivalent | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 5–10 | $200–300 | $1–3K | Paid-ads-driven, CAC-heavy, near break-even |
| 6 | 15–30 | $200–300 | $3–8K | SEO/YouTube starting to contribute organic buyers (lower CAC) |
| 9 | 25–50 | $200–300 | $6–15K | Rank climbing IF niche gap was real; otherwise plateau at ~$3K |
| 12 | 40–80 | $200–300 | $10–24K | **Top-decile / "won the ranking war" outcome.** Median realistic = $3–8K/mo. |

> Model both. **Base case: $3–8K/mo by month 12** (meets the low end of the target with a small ceiling). **Upside: $10–24K/mo** only if the niche gap was genuinely underserved AND organic rank compounds. Do not budget on upside.

**Buy-once dynamics:** revenue = annual new-cohort volume × capture rate × price. There is no recurring subscription base — you must re-win each year's cohort. Add upsells (team licenses, refresher/recert bundles, adjacent cert) to raise LTV.

## 6. Go/No-Go decision gates

| Gate | When | GO if | NO-GO → action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gate 0** (the real bet) | End Week 3 | ≥1 niche passes all 4 filters AND smoke-test CAC < 30% of price | **KILL the idea.** Spent <$500. Return hours to CutLog. |
| **Gate 1** | End Week 13 | 10–25 paying buyers AND blended CAC < 40% of price AND ≥1 organic signal (a ranking article gaining impressions) | Pause paid spend; try 4 more weeks of organic only. If still flat, kill. |
| **Gate 2** | Month 9 | ≥ break-even (15–25 buyers/mo) AND organic buyers growing | Wind down. Keep course as-is passively; stop all spend/contractors. Cut losses (<$40K). |
| **Gate 3** | Month 12 | ≥$5K/mo net AND trend up | Continue + consider a second adjacent cert. Below $5K → keep passive or sell the asset. |

**Cheapest validation principle:** every gate is designed so the *maximum* loss if you're wrong is small (Gate 0 <$500; Gate 1 <$10K; Gate 2 <$40K). No debt, no personal guarantee, no inventory.

## 7. How it coexists with CutLog (Hooman's scarce hours)

**The honest recommendation:** CutLog is the priority. This idea should only proceed as a **capital-and-delegation play, never a focus play.** The research is explicit that opening a second 0→1 *distribution* front while CutLog is still in its own 0→1 grind is the trap the master report warns against.

**Rules for coexistence:**
1. **Gate 0 first, and it's cheap enough to run without hurting CutLog** (~15–20 hrs over 3 weeks, spread thin). If Gate 0 fails — the likely outcome — you've lost almost nothing and CutLog keeps 100% of your focus.
2. **If Gate 0 passes, run this as a delegated operation, not a build.** Hooman's cap is ~5 hrs/wk (technical QA + exam monitoring + decisions). The distribution grind is *bought*, not personally executed — that is exactly why the budget carries contractors from month 3.
3. **CutLog always wins a time conflict.** If both need Hooman in the same week, exam-prep waits. Structure contractors so the exam business survives Hooman being absent for 2–3 weeks.
4. **Sequence, don't parallelize the hard part.** Do not start the exam-prep SEO grind until CutLog is either self-sustaining or clearly stalled. Two simultaneous distribution grinds guarantees both starve.
5. **Kill-readiness.** Because the moat is thin and founder-fit generic, treat this as a disposable experiment. Be willing to shut it at any gate without sunk-cost attachment.
