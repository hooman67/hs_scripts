# Execution Plan — Royalty Licensing (inventRight model)

**Date**: 2026-07-01. **Author**: Claude (Opus 4.8). **Owner**: Hooman Shariati.
**Status**: PASS as a *near-zero-cost background bet* — NOT as a reliable $5–30K/mo income engine.
**Companion doc**: `01_research_validation.md` (read it first; this plan is built on its verdict).

---

## 0. The honest framing (read this before anything else)

The validation research is unambiguous, and this plan does not fight it:

- **This will not reliably produce $5–30K/mo.** At 5% of *wholesale* (≈2.5% of retail), a single
  product must sell **~$4.8M/yr retail** to net $10K/mo. That is a *hit*. The base case for a
  licensed product is **$0–$6K/YEAR** (Stephen Key's own drum-key example). Per-idea odds of
  licensing at all are **<~10%**, and most of those earn trivial money.
- **Time is Hooman's binding constraint, not capital** — and this activity spends the *exact same*
  scarce evening hours as CutLog, while leaning on his one documented weakness (cold outreach to
  fragmented buyers) rather than his EE/AI strength.
- **Therefore this is run as a lottery ticket, not a business.** The correct posture: minimize
  cash AND time spent, cap total exposure hard, and treat any royalty as a windfall — never a plan.

**What this plan optimizes for:** the *cheapest, lowest-time-cost* way to buy a small number of
real lottery tickets (licensed-product shots) without (a) sinking money into inventRight coaching,
(b) stealing focus from CutLog, or (c) letting sunk-cost drag it past its kill-gates.

**If you want a $5–30K/mo secondary income, this is the wrong idea in the study** — a buy-and-operate
cash-flowing asset (capital + delegation) fits Hooman's profile far better than a persistence/sales
game. This plan exists only to run royalty licensing *correctly if run at all*: small, cheap, bounded.

## 1. The specific target (chosen niche)

From the research, the only defensible framing:

- **Category**: workshop/tools, EDC (everyday-carry), or pet products — mature, un-sexy,
  high-unit-volume, hundreds of mid-size OEMs running open-submission programs. **Pick ONE** category
  Hooman personally uses and has taste in (default recommendation: **workshop/tools** — he tinkers,
  understands the products, and can judge a good improvement).
- **Product type**: a *simple incremental improvement* to an existing high-volume product — NOT a
  from-scratch invention, NOT deep-tech, NOT anything needing regulatory approval or custom
  manufacturing to prove out.
- **Channel**: one-page **sell sheet** + **provisional patent** → pitched to **8–15 manufacturers**
  via **MarketBlast** (~1,500 companies seeking ideas) + direct OEM open-submission portals.
- **Explicitly avoid**: toys (Spin Master/Hasbro gatekeeping + trend risk), regulated products,
  anything requiring you to prove manufacturability, and **inventRight paid coaching** ($3.5K–$18K).
- **Hooman's real edge here** (small): capital to file real provisionals cheaply, analytical
  filtering of prior-art-clear high-volume ideas, and the ability to run it with zero income pressure.

**Concrete commitment for the pilot**: land **2–3 product improvements** through the full funnel
(idea → provisional → sell sheet → pitch to 10+ OEMs each) within **12 months**, spending
**≤$6K cash total** and **≤4 hrs/week**, using only free resources (Key's book *One Simple Idea* +
free YouTube). The pilot's job is to *test the funnel and Hooman's stomach for it* — not to hit income.

## 2. First 90 days — week-by-week

**Cadence rule: ≤3 hrs/week, and it goes to ZERO in any week CutLog needs the time.** This is the
backstop bet; CutLog is the priority.

**Weeks 1–2 — Learn the model for $0 (validation before any spend).**
- Hooman: read *One Simple Idea* (Stephen Key) + watch ~5–8 hrs of the free inventRight YouTube
  library. **Do not buy coaching.**
- Deliverable: a 1-page written summary of the process (idea → prior-art → provisional → sell sheet
  → pitch → negotiate) so the method is internalized, not re-learned later.
- **GATE 0 (stomach check)**: after this, honestly answer *"Am I willing to cold-pitch 30+ strangers
  over a year?"* If no → **STOP HERE, $0 spent.** This is the cheapest possible off-ramp.

**Weeks 3–4 — Pick the category + generate an idea list.**
- Choose ONE category (default: workshop/tools). Brainstorm 15–20 candidate improvements to
  existing high-volume products. Log them in a simple sheet (`ideas_log.md`/spreadsheet).
- Filter to the 5 most promising on: high unit volume, obvious pain, simple to describe on one page.

**Weeks 5–6 — Prior-art scan (the real kill filter, free).**
- For each of the top 5: free Google Patents + USPTO + Amazon/retail search. Kill anything already
  patented or already on shelves. Expect 3–4 of 5 to die here. **This is where cost is saved** —
  never file on an idea you didn't prior-art scan.
- Deliverable: 1–2 surviving ideas that look prior-art-clear.

**Weeks 7–8 — File first provisional(s) + first sell sheet.**
- File **1 provisional patent** (self-file via USPTO ~$65–130 micro-entity, or ~$300 with a light
  attorney review) on the single best idea. **Spend real money on ONE idea first, not all.**
- Commission a sell sheet from a design student / Fiverr (~$80–200). Optional: a cheap 3D-printed
  prototype photo (~$50–150).
- Running cash total to date: **~$300–650.**

**Weeks 9–10 — Build the pitch list + start outreach.**
- Set up a free MarketBlast account; submit the idea. Identify 10–15 direct OEM open-submission
  portals in the category.
- Begin cold outreach: submit/pitch to the first 5 targets. Track every touch in a CRM-lite sheet
  (company, contact, date, response).

**Weeks 11–12 — Finish first pitch wave + first review.**
- Complete pitches to 10+ manufacturers for idea #1. File provisional + sell sheet for idea #2 if
  time and budget allow (adds ~$300–650).
- **GATE 1 (90-day review)**: metrics = # ideas prior-art-cleared, # provisionals filed, # OEMs
  pitched, # of *any* reply (even "no"), and — honestly — hours actually spent + whether it hurt
  CutLog. Cash to date should be **≤$1.5K.**

## 3. Months 4–12 — the (deliberately modest) path forward

There is **no revenue-target ramp to promise** — the research forbids it. The month 4–12 goal is
purely to **complete the pilot** (2–3 ideas fully pitched) and reach a clean go/no-go, not to hit a
dollar number.

- **Months 4–6**: Keep idea #1 and #2 pitches alive (follow-ups, respond to any OEM interest). Add
  idea #3 through the full funnel. Provisional patents last 12 months — decide per idea whether to
  let it lapse (default) or convert to a non-provisional (~$1–3K, **only if a manufacturer is
  actually engaged** — never speculatively).
- **Months 7–12**: If any OEM shows real interest → negotiate (5% of wholesale ask 7%/settle 5%;
  insist on a minimum annual guarantee + an **audit clause**, given the documented royalty-shorting
  risk). If nothing lands → let provisionals lapse, harvest learnings, and stop generating new ideas.

**Hiring / delegation** (keeps it part-time — this is the ONE lever that makes it non-time-toxic):
- **Delegate the low-skill, time-heavy pieces with cash, not hours:** sell-sheet design (Fiverr,
  ~$100/ea), 3D-print prototype photos (~$100/ea), and — the big one — **prior-art search + outreach
  list building** to a **VA** (~$8–20/hr, ~$150–300 per idea batch).
- **Hooman keeps only**: idea selection, the go/no-go judgment, and the actual negotiation. That
  compresses his personal load to **~2 hrs/week**, which is the only way this coexists with CutLog.
- Do **not** hire anyone to *replace the cold-outreach persistence itself* early — a VA can build
  lists and send template submissions, but genuine OEM relationship follow-through needs the founder
  until a deal is warm.

## 4. Budget (itemized, hard-capped)

| Item | Low (DIY) | High (delegated) |
|---|---|---|
| inventRight coaching | **$0 (skip — use the book)** | $0 (still skip) |
| Book *One Simple Idea* | $15 | $15 |
| Provisional patents (2–3 ideas) | $200 (self-file, micro-entity) | $900 (light attorney review) |
| Sell sheets (2–3) | $200 | $600 |
| Prototype photos/3D prints (2–3) | $150 | $450 |
| MarketBlast / submission fees | $0–100 | $300 |
| VA (prior-art + outreach, 3 idea batches) | $0 (DIY) | $900 |
| Contingency (1 non-provisional IF a deal warms) | $0 | $3,000 |
| **TOTAL (12-month pilot)** | **~$600** | **~$6,200** |

- **Hard cap: $6K cash and 12 months.** This is *far* below the $25–150K study range — deliberately.
  The whole point is that the downside is capped at a few thousand dollars + time.
- **Monthly burn**: ~$100–500, lumpy (spikes at each provisional filing).
- **Break-even**: undefined/irrelevant by design. A *single* licensed product doing even ~$500K/yr
  retail (5% wholesale ≈ ~$6K/yr) repays the entire pilot; but base case is **$0 return**, and that
  outcome is acceptable and pre-accepted.

## 5. Revenue model + realistic ramp

- **Model**: 5% of *wholesale* (≈2.5% of retail) per licensed unit, ideally with a modest annual
  minimum guarantee + audit rights. Advances are typically small and against future royalties.
- **Realistic ramp (base case)**: Year 1 = **$0** (pilot; probably nothing licensed yet). Years 2–3 =
  **$0–low-thousands/yr** *if* one modest product licenses. A meaningful $5–15K/mo requires a genuine
  hit that **most inventors never get**, arriving lumpy and back-loaded on a **3–5 year (or never)**
  horizon.
- **Deals-over-time (honest)**: pitch ~30+ OEM touches across 2–3 ideas in year 1 → expect **0–1**
  license. Portfolio of ~10–15 lifetime ideas → maybe **1–2** ever license → maybe **0–1** earns
  more than pocket change. Plan around **zero**; treat anything else as a windfall.

## 6. Go/No-Go gates (cheapest validation first)

- **GATE 0 (Week 2, $0)** — *Stomach test.* After learning the model free: is Hooman willing to
  cold-pitch 30+ strangers/yr and accept mostly-"no"? **No → STOP, $0 spent.** (Cheapest possible
  validation, before any money.)
- **GATE 1 (Day 90, ≤$1.5K)** — *Funnel test.* Did idea #1 get prior-art-cleared, provisional'd,
  sell-sheeted, and pitched to 10+ OEMs? Did it stay ≤3 hrs/wk and NOT hurt CutLog? **Any "no" on the
  CutLog-impact question → STOP.**
- **GATE 2 (Month 6)** — *Signal test.* Across 2 ideas / 20+ pitches, has **any** OEM given a
  substantive reply (request for more info / sample / terms)? **Zero substantive interest → do NOT
  file more provisionals; finish existing pitches and wind down.**
- **GATE 3 (Month 12)** — *Continuation test.* Is any deal warm? **Yes → negotiate + optionally
  convert one provisional.** **No → let all provisionals lapse, close the pilot, write up learnings.**
  Total spend by here should be **≤$6K.**

**Absolute stop-loss**: if cash exceeds $6K OR Hooman's personal time exceeds ~3 hrs/wk for two
consecutive months OR CutLog momentum measurably slips → **halt immediately**, regardless of gate.

---

## 7. How it coexists with CutLog

The research is explicit: Hooman's constraint is **time/focus, not capital**, and this idea competes
for the *same* evening hours CutLog needs — its single biggest kill-risk. Rules:

1. **CutLog always wins the hour.** Royalty work is the *residual* — only the hours CutLog doesn't
   need that week. Some weeks that's zero, and that's fine.
2. **Buy down the time with money, not the reverse.** Every delegable task (design, prints,
   prior-art, list-building, template outreach) goes to Fiverr/VA. Hooman's personal footprint stays
   **≤2 hrs/wk** — idea selection, go/no-go, negotiation only.
3. **Hard-capped and pre-committed to stop.** $6K / 12 months / 3 gates. It cannot metastasize into a
   focus-draining second job.
4. **No coaching, ever.** The $3.5K–$18K inventRight upsell is the trap that turns a cheap bet into a
   sunk-cost spiral. Free book + free YouTube only.

**Bottom line**: run this as a bounded, mostly-delegated background lottery ticket that respects
CutLog's priority — or don't run it at all. It is a *nice-to-have windfall bet*, explicitly **not** the
secondary income stream Hooman is looking for.
