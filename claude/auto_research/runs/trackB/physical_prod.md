# Track B — Physical Products / DTC / Licensing in a Defensible Niche

**Shape owner:** Physical products / DTC / licensing. **Researched:** 2026-07-01 by Claude (Opus 4.8).
**Mandate:** secondary/lifestyle income, $5–30K/mo PROFIT, part-time (~10–20 hrs/wk or delegable),
$25–150K capital, AI-safe / not-swarmed, CLEAR PROVEN PATH. NOT commodity Amazon/dropship churn.

Hooman's edge for this shape: EE bachelor's + real ML/product-systems chops + mech-eng friends +
capital. That maps directly onto **engineered/electronics products** (things most Etsy/DTC sellers
can't build) and onto **licensing a design** (an engineer's core output is a design).

---

## The shape, honestly

Physical products is the shape where the reject list bites hardest: most of it IS commodity
Amazon-FBA churn and dropshipping — races to the bottom with no moat, exactly what the mandate
forbids. The defensible subset is narrow but real:

- **Moat comes from the PRODUCT being hard to make** (electronics, engineered mechanism, patent,
  a design that took skill) — not from marketing spend or a first-mover Amazon listing.
- **Or moat comes from a brand/community in an enthusiast niche** (musicians, EDC, hobbyists) where
  buyers pay a premium and are loyal, and Chinese clones can't easily reach the audience.
- **Or you skip operations entirely and LICENSE the design** for a royalty — the capital-lightest,
  most-delegable version, and the best founder-fit for a busy engineer.

AI is largely a **tailwind** here (cheaper CAD, marketing copy, listing images, CS) and NOT a
clone threat — you can't 3D-print a physical splatter guard or solder a guitar pedal with a
prompt. That is precisely why this shape is "AI-safe."

**Capital reality:** $25–150K comfortably funds a first production run (tooling/injection mold
$5–40K, or an inventory buy of $10–60K), a small parts inventory for electronics, or a fleet of
3D printers ($15–40K). It does NOT fund a big-ad-spend DTC brand — so the durable plays here are
niche/enthusiast/engineered, not paid-acquisition consumer brands.

## Opportunity 1 — License an engineered product design for royalties (inventRight model)

**What it is:** Invent/design a simple physical product (a mechanism, gadget, tool improvement),
file a provisional patent (~$150 self-filed), build a "sell sheet" + prototype, and license it to
an established manufacturer for a **3–6% royalty on wholesale** (per UpCounsel; inventRight teaches
5%+). You never manufacture, hold inventory, or ship. This is the most delegable, most capital-safe
version of "physical products" — and the design work is literally what an engineer does.

**Evidence people make real money:**
- inventRight (Stephen Key's program) publishes named licensors: **Mohamed Ali (iLock): $100M in
  sales, 70+ licensed products**; **Ryan Bricker (Whiskey Wedge): 1M+ units**; **Dennis Diener:
  700K units**; **Keanu Alfaro: 60+ products licensed to 10 companies in 4 years**; **Veronica R:
  400K+ units / 12 designs** (inventright.com/testimonials).
- Royalty math: on a product doing $2M/yr wholesale, a 5% royalty = $100K/yr = **$8.3K/mo passive**
  per successful license. Stack 2–3 modest licenses ($400–800K wholesale each) to reach $5–15K/mo.
- Classic proof-of-shape: Larami's "Super Soaker" (Lonnie Johnson, an engineer) — licensed,
  became a billion-dollar line (inventionhome.com).
- Rate benchmark: "average royalty for licensing an invention is 3 to 6 percent of wholesale"
  (UpCounsel).

**Economics:** capital <$5K per idea (provisional patent, prototype, sell sheet). Royalty income
is pure margin. Ramp is slow (6–18 mo to a signed deal; many ideas get zero deals) but downside is
tiny. Realistic target after 18–36 mo of consistent submissions: **$5–15K/mo across a portfolio.**

**Crowding/durability:** Un-swarmed — it's unglamorous, slow, and requires domain product taste,
so VC/AI ignore it entirely. Durable for decades; AI helps (faster CAD/prototyping/prior-art
search). The bottleneck is idea quality + persistence, not competition.

**90-day start:** Pick a category Hooman understands (workshop/electronics/EDC/home). Watch
inventRight's free YouTube + read Key's *One Simple Idea*. Generate 10 candidate improvements to
existing products; do a quick patentability/prior-art scan; pick 1–2; file provisional(s) yourself;
build a sell sheet + cheap prototype (3D print / breadboard); start cold-emailing manufacturers.

**Downside:** Most ideas never license — it's a numbers game. But max loss is a few thousand
dollars per idea. No inventory, no personal guarantee. Lowest capital-risk play in the whole shape.

**Fit:** Excellent. Designing is an engineer's native skill; it's the most delegable/part-time
option; capital-safe. Weakness: royalty income is lumpy and back-loaded.

## Opportunity 2 — Boutique guitar/effects pedals & pro-audio electronics (EE-native brand)

**What it is:** Design and sell analog/digital effects pedals or niche pro-audio/synth hardware
under your own brand, DTC + a few dealers. This is a **thriving cottage industry of EE founders** —
several literal one-person garage starts. The moat is design taste + brand + an enthusiast community
that actively distrusts mass-market clones.

**Evidence people make real money:**
- **EarthQuaker Devices** — Jamie Stillman started it solo in his basement (2004); by 2019, 50
  employees, 15,000 sq ft, exporting to 47 countries, SBA Small Business Exporter of the Year (Wikipedia).
- **Chase Bliss Audio** — founded 2013, ~21 employees, est. ~$4M revenue (Kona Equity).
- **JHS, Wampler, Keeley, Cusack, Electronic Audio Experiments** — all solo/garage founder starts;
  notably **John Snyder of EAE has an EE doctorate** and started building pedals to solve his own
  band's problem (search-verified founder bios). This is the exact founder profile.
- Unit economics: analog pedal components "cost a few dollars or even less" (Range of Sounds);
  boutique pedals retail $150–350. A solo builder selling ~40–80 units/mo DTC at ~60% margin nets
  well into the $5–15K/mo range before scaling to contract assembly.

**Economics:** capital $15–60K (parts inventory, PCB runs, enclosures, a reflow/soldering setup, or
a contract assembler). DTC gross margin ~55–70%; dealer channel ~35–45%. Caveat (guitar.com): at
scale, B2B/dealer margins compress and PCB spend balloons — so keep it boutique/DTC to protect margin.

**Crowding/durability:** The pedal market is competitive but **not VC/AI-swarmed** — it's a
taste-and-community business immune to software clones. Durable (guitarists have bought pedals for
60 years). AI is neutral-to-helpful. Risk: it's a passion market with many small players, so you
compete on design voice, not volume.

**90-day start:** Design 1–2 distinctive circuits (or license a proven topology and voice it
uniquely); prototype on perfboard; get small PCB + enclosure runs (JLCPCB/PCBWay + powder-coated
enclosures); build 10 units; seed to YouTube demo channels (the genre's kingmakers); sell DTC via
Shopify + Reverb. Validate demand before a big parts buy.

**Downside:** Inventory of parts/enclosures is the main capital at risk ($10–40K), all recoverable-ish
and no personal guarantee. Main failure mode: a product with no distinctive voice gets ignored.

**Fit:** Very strong founder-market fit (EE + analog/mixed-signal design). Part-time-feasible early;
assembly is delegable to a contract house. This is arguably the single best EE-fit brand play.

## Opportunity 3 — 3D-printed niche products (design-led print farm / Etsy+Shopify)

**What it is:** Use a small fleet of 3D printers to make and sell niche, hard-to-source items you
design yourself (novelty/decor, hobby accessories, functional parts, replacement/adapter parts).
Moat = your original designs + speed + long-tail niches too small for injection molders.

**Evidence people make real money:**
- **Austin Ziegler (ZiggyZigDesigns, Etsy)** — 27-yr-old engineering manager (medical devices);
  Business Insider **verified via Etsy dashboard**: **$16,000 in September 2023**, $10K in one week
  in Aug 2023, up from ~$1,400/mo avg early 2023. Runs **12 printers**; bestseller is a "gas can
  ornament"; keeps a list of hundreds of product ideas. (businessinsider.com) — note: this is an
  engineer moonlighting, exactly Hooman's situation.
- Print-farm operators: one landed a **$7,000 contract and hit 70% profit by month 2**
  (3dprinterly.com). Community benchmarks: part-time 1–2 printers → $500–2K/mo; full-time 3–5
  printers → ~£2,500–8,000/mo gross (search-verified ranges).
- Etsy income distribution (richtactic.com): most sellers <$100/mo, but **top sellers $10K–50K+/mo**
  — the spread is entirely about original design + niche selection, not luck.

**Economics:** capital $15–40K (10–15 printers + filament + racking). Consumable cost per part is
tiny (grams of filament + electricity); margins 70%+ on original designs. Highly delegable — a
part-timer manages the queue, printing runs unattended.

**Crowding/durability:** The generic-STL end is a race to the bottom (avoid). The **original-design**
end is durable and not AI-swarmed (AI can't print/ship). Seasonal/novelty products decay, so a
pipeline of designs is required. Etsy platform-risk is real (algorithm/fees) → diversify to Shopify.

**90-day start:** Buy 3–4 printers; pick a niche Hooman knows (workshop/maker/EDC/auto); design
5–10 original products in CAD; list on Etsy; reinvest into more printers as winners emerge; add a
Shopify storefront for the top SKUs. Ziegler's path is the literal playbook.

**Downside:** Low — printers resell, filament is cheap, no personal guarantee. Failure mode is
"designs don't sell," which costs time more than money.

**Fit:** Strong. CAD + iteration + systems thinking are Hooman's wheelhouse; a proven engineer-
moonlighter case study exists; delegable and part-time. Ceiling per operation is modest ($10–20K/mo)
without scaling to a real farm.

## Opportunity 4 — Enthusiast-electronics accessories / mechanical-keyboard niche

**What it is:** Design and sell accessories/kits for an enthusiast hardware community (mechanical
keyboards, retro-computing, ham radio, e-bike/EV DIY, RC, drones). These communities pay premiums,
share group-buys, and are hostile to faceless commodity sellers. EE lets you make the *hard* parts
(PCBs, controllers, adapters) most sellers can't.

**Evidence people make real money:**
- **TechKeys (Brad Agdern, 1 person)** — mechanical-keyboard parts/accessories, **$60K/yr ($5K/mo)
  revenue, $1,000 startup cost**, built by organizing group buys then a retail store
  (starterstory.com/ideas/mechanical-keyboard-business/success-stories). Small but a clean solo
  proof at the bottom of the target band.
- **Ken (Shenzhen)** — low-profile carbon-fiber/magnesium keyboards; "many big brands overlook this
  small category, which has become our opportunity" (eu.36kr.com) — validates the "boring
  overlooked sub-niche" thesis.
- Broader enthusiast-hardware brands (Keychron, etc.) started small in the same community; the
  group-buy model de-risks inventory (customers prepay).

**Economics:** capital $10–50K; **group-buy/pre-order model means customers fund the production
run** — a rare capital-safe structure in physical products. Margins 40–60%. TechKeys shows $5K/mo
is reachable solo; a broader accessory line + a signature PCB/controller product can push higher.

**Crowding/durability:** Community-gated → not AI/VC-swarmed. Durable as long as the hobby exists
(keyboards, ham radio, retro have been durable for decades). Risk: trend-sensitivity within a hobby;
pick a hobby with staying power.

**90-day start:** Embed in a community Hooman already relates to (maker/EE-adjacent). Find an unmet
accessory/kit need; design it (Hooman's PCB edge shines here); run a pre-order/group-buy to fund the
run; fulfill; expand the catalog. Zero speculative inventory if pre-order-funded.

**Downside:** Very low with the pre-order model — customers fund production, so capital-at-risk is
minimal. Failure = a group-buy that doesn't hit MOQ (refund and move on).

**Fit:** Strong EE fit + capital-safe pre-order structure. Ceiling is modest solo (TechKeys = $5K/mo),
so treat as a base to layer a signature engineered product on top of.

## Opportunity 5 — Single engineered consumer gadget, DTC + Amazon (patent-protected)

**What it is:** Design ONE genuinely novel, patentable physical product that solves a real everyday
problem, protect it, and sell DTC + Amazon + retail. The moat is the patent + being first + a real
manufacturing/tooling barrier — the opposite of me-too FBA.

**Evidence people make real money:**
- **Frywall (Yair Reiner, Gowanus Kitchen Lab, 2015)** — a silicone splatter guard he invented from
  a kitchen mishap; sells online + retail; his rule of thumb: "production cost must stay below 25%
  of retail" (i.e., **~75% gross margin**); landed cost ~$5, retails ~$20+ (inventionschool.tech,
  NYT essay). A one-person-conceived, single-SKU engineered product with durable margins.
- **Instant Pot (Robert Wang, an engineer, unemployed at the time)** — sold **215,000 units in a
  single Amazon Prime Day**; started as one inventor's design (CNBC).
- **Rotimatic (Pranoti Nagarkar, mechanical engineer)** — 35+ patents on an automated roti maker;
  proves engineer-invented single products can be heavily defensible.

**Economics:** capital $30–150K (injection-mold tooling $10–40K + first production run $20–60K +
patent $5–15K). Gross margins 60–75% on a proprietary product. One good SKU doing 1,000–2,000
units/mo at $10–15 profit each = **$10–30K/mo**.

**Crowding/durability:** A patented single product in a boring category is not VC/AI-swarmed. The
real risk is **clones after the patent gap / tariff exposure** (Frywall's own cautionary tale on
tariffs). Durability depends on patent strength + brand + continuous iteration.

**90-day start:** Identify a specific everyday friction Hooman has personally felt; validate the
problem is common; sketch + prototype a solution (3D print); file provisional; get manufacturing
quotes (Alibaba + a domestic backup for tariff hedging); pre-sell via a landing page or small
Kickstarter to validate demand before tooling spend.

**Downside:** Highest capital-at-risk in this shape — tooling + inventory ($30–100K) can be largely
lost if the product flops. Mitigate with pre-sell/crowdfund validation BEFORE tooling. No personal
guarantee needed, but this is the play where you can actually lose a chunk of the $25–150K.

**Fit:** Good product-design fit; higher capital risk and more hands-on (sourcing, QC, logistics,
Amazon ops) than the others — but delegable to an ops contractor/3PL once running.

## Opportunity 6 — Buy a cash-flowing niche physical-product brand (small acquisition)

**What it is:** Skip the 0-to-1 risk and **buy** an already-profitable niche physical-product
business in the $25–150K range on Empire Flippers / Flippa / Acquire, then improve/operate it
part-time. Best targets: a defensible brand with repeat customers and a moat (proprietary product,
loyal niche, subscription/consumable), NOT a generic FBA arbitrage store.

**Evidence people make real money (real listings, verified):**
- **Empire Flippers marketplace, live listings (July 2026):**
  - Home-products FBA/ecom brand — **$3,640/mo net profit**, asking $101,892 (~28x).
  - Beauty/cosmetics FBA brand — **$3,971/mo net**, asking $80,044 (~20x).
  - Multi-category ecom — **$2,048/mo net**, asking $59,388 (~29x).
- These are pre-vetted, "earns from day one" businesses; multiples ~20–29x monthly = **~2–2.4 year
  payback**. Buying 2–3 in the band, or one larger with a follow-on earn-out, can stack toward the
  $5–15K/mo target. (empireflippers.com/marketplace)

**Economics:** capital = the purchase price ($25–150K all-cash, or SBA/seller-note to keep cash in;
mandate says keep his cash at $25–150K). Immediate cash flow, no ramp. Profit = the acquired net,
which Hooman then grows via his product/ops/analytics skills.

**Crowding/durability:** Buying is less crowded than building (most people build). Durability depends
entirely on the specific asset's moat — **must reject pure FBA-arbitrage/commodity listings**
(they're the swarmed, race-to-bottom trap the mandate forbids) and buy only genuinely defensible
niche brands. This is a due-diligence problem, not a shape problem.

**90-day start:** Open Empire Flippers / Acquire accounts; define a buy-box (niche physical brand,
repeat/consumable revenue, <30x, verifiable Amazon+Shopify P&L, transferable supplier); review
listings weekly; do diligence (traffic sources, supplier concentration, review authenticity,
seasonality); make an offer with an escrow + migration period; hire a VA/3PL to run ops.

**Downside:** You can lose the purchase price if the business declines post-sale (supplier loss,
Amazon suspension, ad-cost creep). Mitigate with tight diligence, escrow, and seller earn-outs.
Moderate capital risk; no personal guarantee if all-cash.

**Fit:** Good — buying compresses the ramp and is highly delegable, but requires diligence rigor
(Hooman's analytical strength) and accepting that the "moat" is only as good as the specific asset.
