## 2. Accountability check — what survived, what got busted

The original `TRACKB_REPORT.md` was systematically optimistic. Specifically:

**BUSTED — the headline income numbers.** Every "$5–25K/mo" / "$5–15K/mo" figure was **gross read as
profit** and/or a **top-decile outcome sold as a base case**. Validated profit is roughly **one-third to
one-half** of the pitch across the board:
- Exam-prep "$5–25K/mo, near-passive after 3–6mo build" → **median $0–3K, 15–20 hrs/wk ONGOING, plus a
  12–24mo distribution grind the pitch never mentioned.** The proof (Drone Launch $50K/mo) is a 2016
  early-mover outlier, partly insurance not courses, in a now-closed niche.
- Managed hosting "$5–25K/mo" → **$2–8K in 12–24mo**; and the flagship niche (UniFi) is **double-covered by
  HostiFi + Ubiquiti's own cloud since ~2022**; n8n is license-gated. "No clean best target exists."
- Bookkeeping "$5–15K/mo, under-served SaaS niche" → **$3–6K net at maturity**, and the "under-served" claim
  is **false**: Kruze, Graphite, Zeni, Fondo, Pilot already own SaaS finance-ops — it's the *most*
  AI-penetrated corner, not an open lane.
- Royalty licensing "$5–15K/mo across a portfolio in 18–36mo" → **$0 yr 1; a lottery ticket** (one product
  must sell ~$4.8M/yr retail to net $10K/mo; per-idea license odds <10%). Both cited proofs collapse
  (Rotimatic is VC-funded, not licensed; Johnson had to sue Hasbro for $73M).
- Buy-SaaS "$3–6K/mo → $8–15K/mo in 12–24mo" → **entry $2–4K, base case flat-to-$3–5K**; and the
  multiple/quality contradiction (want 3–3.5x SDE *and* sticky low-churn — that quadrant is nearly empty;
  clean assets get taken at 4–5x by cash-ready micro-PE).

**BUSTED — founder-fit for the software ideas.** The report leaned on "his infra/AI skills are the moat."
Validation inverted this for the two "his wheelhouse" ideas: managed hosting's real moat is
**community/sales (his weakness)**, not infra; bookkeeping's edge is **relationships/trust**, not code.
Micro-SaaS churns on support/domain trust, so "fix the ugly code" creates near-zero new revenue.

**BUSTED — "near-passive / delegable to <5 hrs/wk."** The non-delegable work is exactly the judgment/
subject-matter/QA/relationship work that a $15–30/hr VA can't do. Realistic personal load is
**8–15 hrs/wk sustained** on every idea, landing in the same evening hours CutLog needs. The
delegation-affordability math on buy-SaaS is the clearest failure: a $2–4.5K/mo VA+dev spend consumes
**all** the profit on a $1.5–2.5K/mo asset.

**SURVIVED — the downside-safety story.** Every idea genuinely has **low, capped downside**: no debt, no
personal guarantee, loss bounded by capital + time. That part of the report held everywhere.

**SURVIVED — capital fit.** All five fit the $25–150K envelope; three (bookkeeping, licensing, and hosting-
if-built) are dramatically *cheaper* than pitched. Capital is not the constraint.

**SURVIVED (and got stronger) — §5's core thesis.** The single most important call in the original report —
**do NOT open a second 0→1 distribution front against CutLog; a secondary stream must consume capital +
delegation, not focus** — is now *reinforced* by every deep dive. Three of the five ideas route directly
INTO his documented distribution weakness; two are the *only* ones that could be capital+delegation bets.

## 3. Ranked recommendation (filtered by TIME, not score)

**Capital + delegation bets (can *theoretically* coexist with CutLog):**
1. **Buy a cash-flowing micro-SaaS** — the only idea whose primary input is *capital*, not his hours. But
   validation exposed the delegation-affordability trap: at his $150K ceiling, affordable assets can't be
   delegated and delegatable assets exceed budget, so months 1–6 are still 20–25 hrs/wk of *his* time. It's
   a capital bet that behaves like a focus bet for the first 6 months.
2. **Managed hosting via *acquisition* (not build)** — same logic, worse odds; ~40–50% chance of $0, and the
   coexistence hinge (a cheap VA who owns infra on-call) is a false economy — either he keeps the pager or a
   real on-call makes a $5K-MRR business margin-negative.

**Focus bets (compete head-on with CutLog — do NOT run in parallel):**
3. **Vertical bookkeeping** — sales-gated, his known weakness, and the target niche is already saturated.
4. **Exam-prep course** — routes straight into a 12–24mo SEO/YouTube grind vs entrenched specialists.
5. **Royalty licensing** — cheap and low-downside, but as *reliable secondary income* it's $0; it's a
   right-skewed lottery, and even the "2 hrs/wk background" claim didn't survive (judgment work can't be
   delegated).

**Sequence:** Do nothing new until CutLog clears its own 0→1 (reaches product-market signal or a clear
stall decision). *If and only if* capital-deployment appetite exceeds focus appetite, the buy-SaaS **Gate 0**
("can I spare 15–20 hrs/wk for 6 months without hurting CutLog?") is the honest first filter — and its most
probable correct answer today is **no**.

## 4. If you do ONE thing

**Do nothing new right now. Protect CutLog's focus.** That is the recommendation the validation
supports most strongly — none of the five clears a clean bar for a founder who is already in a 0→1 grind,
and three of them actively worsen it.

**If you insist on deploying the $25–150K into a second stream anyway,** the *only* shape that fits the
capital+delegation test is **buying a cash-flowing micro-SaaS** — accepting the honest, revalidated
economics: ~$2–4K/mo entry, base case flat-to-$3–5K at 24 months, ~$0–2.5K/mo *true* net after your time,
capped downside, and a hands-on first 6 months.

**Concrete first step (costs ~$0, no commitment):** open Acquire.com / Empire Flippers, filter for vertical
B2B system-of-record SaaS at **$2–4K/mo source-verified net, ≥18–24mo history, <20% customer concentration,
owner <10 hrs/wk**, and do a **2–3 listing dry-run diligence** to answer the real Gate 0 question — *not*
"is there a deal?" but **"can I honestly spare 15–20 hrs/wk for 6 months without slowing CutLog?"** If the
honest answer is no, defer entirely rather than split focus.
