# Track B — Productized Services (done-for-you, fixed scope/price)

**Researched**: 2026-07-01 by Claude (Opus 4.8). Owner: Hooman Shariati.
**Shape**: Niche productized services — fixed scope, fixed price, systematized and delegable.
Start solo, hire contractors as it grows. Target $5–30K/mo profit, part-time compatible.

---

## What a "productized service" is (and why it fits the Track-B mandate)

A productized service packages a repeatable done-for-you deliverable into a fixed-scope,
fixed-price offer (often a monthly subscription). Instead of custom consulting proposals, you
sell a menu: "$X/mo, submit unlimited requests, one active at a time, 48h turnaround." This
converts freelance labor into a **quasi-SaaS cash-flow business** with predictable MRR that
can be delegated to contractors as it scales — exactly the "start solo, hire out" model.

**Why it's AI-safe / durable enough:** These businesses' moat is NOT the tool (AI can do the
task). It is (a) the **trust/relationship/brand** with a repeat B2B buyer who does not want to
manage freelancers or prompt an AI, (b) **QA + accountability** (someone to fire if it's wrong),
and (c) **a specific vertical's domain judgment**. AI is a **cost tailwind** (it makes each
delivery cheaper, widening margin) far more than a threat — the buyer is paying for the outcome
delivered reliably, not for keystrokes. The danger zone is the **generic, undifferentiated**
version (generic logo/design subscriptions are now swarmed — see crowding notes). The safe zone
is a **narrow vertical no VC bothers with**.

## Crowding & durability screen (which sub-niches to AVOID)

- **AVOID generic design/logo subscriptions.** DesignJoy's success spawned dozens of clones —
  comparison articles now list "12 DesignJoy alternatives" (Penji, Kimp, Design Pickle, Draftss,
  Cider, Endless, etc.). The generic tier is a race to the bottom and is the closest to being
  eaten by AI image tools. If entering design at all, go **vertical** (e.g. only pitch decks for
  VC-backed SaaS, or only packaging for CPG).
- **AVOID generic "unlimited content writing"** — most exposed to commodity LLM output.
- **PREFER niches with (a) a technical/regulated deliverable, (b) a specific vertical buyer, and
  (c) a reason a human must sign off.** These stay un-swarmed because they're unglamorous and
  require domain judgment, not just a tool.

## The opportunities (6, screened & scored)

### 1. Webflow / no-code site build + maintenance subscription — VERTICAL (e.g. SaaS or law firms)
- **What**: Fixed-price site builds ($3–8K) + a monthly care/edits subscription ($300–1,500/mo).
- **Evidence**: Flowout $850K/yr, Hilvy $74K/yr solo, 180Sites $950K/yr — all web-design subs
  (ManyRequests/Starter Story). Retainer maintenance is the recurring layer.
- **Fit**: High. Hooman can build the delivery system; delegate builds to Webflow contractors
  ($30–60/hr). AI = tailwind (speeds builds).
- **Risk**: Design-adjacent = some crowding; mitigate by picking ONE vertical and owning it.

### 2. Managed technical hosting / DevOps-lite subscription (HostiFi model)
- **What**: Managed hosting of a specific self-hosted tool for non-technical operators — e.g.
  UniFi controllers (HostiFi's exact play), or n8n/self-hosted-analytics/status-pages/WordPress
  for a vertical. Flat $/mo per instance.
- **Evidence**: HostiFi (Reilly Chase) — managed UniFi hosting, bootstrapped solo to $100K/yr
  then ~$1M ARR, later acquired. Pure recurring, high margin, delegable to a small ops team.
- **Fit**: VERY high — this is Hooman's exact skill set (cloud/infra, systems). Strong moat via
  operational reliability + support relationship. AI-safe (buyers want it managed, not to learn).
- **Risk**: Requires a niche tool with a large non-technical install base; must not compete with
  the tool's own cloud offering.

### 3. Vertical bookkeeping / financial-ops subscription (one industry only)
- **What**: Monthly bookkeeping for ONE vertical (e.g. e-commerce Shopify sellers, dental
  practices, law firms, agencies). $300–1,500/client/mo, recurring, sticky.
- **Evidence**: Industry sources (Jetpack Workflow, Content Snare, Double) document solo/AI-
  assisted bookkeeping firms at **$3,000–15,000+/mo**; ~10–20 clients at $500–1,000 = target
  band. Extremely sticky (switching a bookkeeper is painful) and recurring.
- **Fit**: High feasibility (learnable; QuickBooks ProAdvisor cert is a weekend). Delegates
  cleanly to trained bookkeepers/VAs while Hooman owns sales + QA. AI = strong margin tailwind.
- **Risk**: Low downside; requires learning accounting basics and building trust. Durable/boring.

### 4. Cold-outreach lead-list / research subscription (ColdLytics model) — vertical B2B
- **What**: Deliver verified, enriched lead lists + outreach data monthly to B2B sales teams in
  a niche. $500–3,000/mo retainers.
- **Evidence**: ColdLytics $300K/yr (ManyRequests). Data-as-a-service angle: "do the work once,
  sell access many times."
- **Fit**: High — Hooman can automate enrichment/scraping (his data/infra strength) and delegate
  verification to VAs. AI = tailwind (enrichment/classification cheaper).
- **Risk**: Moderate crowding + deliverability/regulatory (CAN-SPAM/GDPR) hygiene; pick a
  vertical where data is hard to assemble.

### 5. Podcast / video post-production subscription (Record Edit / VideoHusky model)
- **What**: Fixed monthly editing of podcasts or short-form video for creators/B2B teams.
  $500–2,500/mo per client.
- **Evidence**: VideoHusky $1.2M/yr, Record Edit Podcast $302K/yr, MixBloom $636K/yr
  (ManyRequests). Proven, recurring, highly delegable to editor contractors.
- **Fit**: Medium — not Hooman's domain, but purely a systems/ops + hiring problem, which suits
  a generalist EM. AI = mixed (auto-editing tools exist; premium human QA still sells).
- **Risk**: More commoditized than the technical plays; differentiate by vertical + turnaround.

### 6. Managed docs / API-documentation subscription for dev-tool & B2B-SaaS companies
- **What**: Ongoing technical documentation + API reference upkeep as a monthly retainer for
  SaaS/dev-tool companies who chronically under-invest in docs. $1.5–6K/mo.
- **Evidence**: Rate data — specialized API/technical writers bill **$100–175/hr**; $1/word is
  standard for API guides (jobbers.io, earnifyhub.com). A retainer bundling this reaches the
  $5–30K/mo band with 4–8 clients. Financial models project six-figure EBITDA at scale.
- **Fit**: VERY high founder-fit — Hooman's exact world (AI infra, dev tools, generative models);
  he can sell credibly to CTOs and QA output an AI drafts. Delegate drafting to tech writers.
- **Risk**: Most AI-exposed deliverable of the six — but the *value* is engineering judgment +
  accountability + keeping docs in sync with shipping code, which AI alone can't own. Position as
  "docs that stay correct," not "docs generated."

## Cross-cutting 90-day start playbook (applies to all six)

1. **Weeks 1–3**: Pick ONE vertical + ONE deliverable. Define fixed scope, one or two price
   tiers, and turnaround SLA. Stand up a one-page site + Stripe + a request board (Trello/
   ManyRequests). Copy the DesignJoy structure literally.
2. **Weeks 4–8**: Land 2–3 founding clients via direct outreach + one niche community
   (LinkedIn, a vertical Slack/FB group). Deliver personally to learn the system and build
   case studies/testimonials.
3. **Weeks 9–12**: Document the delivery SOP. Hire the first contractor (offshore/onshore) to
   take over production; Hooman keeps sales + final QA (~5–10 hrs/wk). Raise price on the next
   cohort. Reinvest into paid acquisition once CAC/LTV is known.

**Capital**: $25–75K covers a year of contractor float + tools + paid acquisition — well inside
the $25–150K band, and most of it is *optional* (many of these bootstrap from client cash).
**Downside**: Very low — no inventory, no debt, no personal guarantees; worst case is lost time
and a few thousand in ads. This is among the safest Track-B shapes.
