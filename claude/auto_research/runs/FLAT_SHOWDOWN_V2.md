## 2. Did either Track-A idea beat the buy-SaaS pick (5.2)?

**No. Decisively no.** Both came in at **3.6**, a full **1.6 points below** buy-SaaS and below
CutLog, hosting, the fab-shop platform, and bookkeeping. Each collapsed on the *same* pattern
that has cut every red-teamed idea in this project:

- **False "empty quadrant / low competition" premise.** Fire Sprinkler's headline "zero
  competition" is simply false — Inspect Point (15K users, 4.5M inspections, $28M raised),
  ServiceTrade (1,300+ customers), and BuildingReports (13M+ inspections) already own the
  NFPA-25 workflow, the photo/deficiency capture, the labeled corpus, *and* branded AI. Compressed
  Air faces Atlas Copco SMARTAIRNET, Kaeser KMT, UE Systems, Fluke, CS Instruments, Prosaris —
  plus free utility-rebate audits and free dealer surveys.
- **Gross read as profit.** Both pitches quoted top-line ($200/mo × N + kit; $199–399/seat) as if
  it were net. Kit is mostly COGS/support; realistic seat price is $50–80 (or $0 office / ~$75/tech).
- **Top-decile treated as base case.** Fire Sprinkler pitched $1.79M ARR yr1 (off by ~1000x);
  Compressed Air pitched a $1.6K capital build (real is $45–150K).
- **Founder-fit / capital reality.** Fire Sprinkler needs $200K+ and *two* hires (iOS + domain
  expert) for skills he lacks — not a solo build; the non-delegable hard part (trade trust,
  distribution) is exactly his weakness. Compressed Air needs a solo hardware/RF/FCC build outside
  his domain plus daytime industrial cold-sales — incompatible with a 10–20 hr/wk evening constraint.

Buy-SaaS remains #1 because its lever is **capital, not his scarce evening hours**, and its
downside is **capped** by an all-cash + earnout/escrow structure (no SBA/personal guarantee).

## 4. Final recommendation — the one venture slot

**Nothing changed the v1 call. If anything, it is now firmer.** The two Track-A ideas were the last
unverified rows and the only remaining candidates that could have out-ranked buy-SaaS. Both were
red-teamed to **3.6 / PASS** — well below the buy-SaaS **5.2**. There is now **no build idea in the
field that out-ranks buying a cash-flowing micro-SaaS**, and specifically **the honest "build" pick
named in v1 (Fire Sprinkler) is now dead** — it needs $200K+ and two hires and faces three
entrenched platforms.

**Recommendation (unchanged, now with the details Hooman asked for):**
1. **Do not treat CutLog as the serious-venture slot.** Demote it to a ≤2 hrs/wk hobby/optionality
   project; keep the ~15% industrial upside alive without letting it claim the slot.
2. **The merit pick for the $25–150K is the buy-a-micro-SaaS path** — the only candidate whose lever
   is capital, not his scarce evening hours, with capped downside and a written 90-day playbook.
3. **Freeing CutLog's focus is what makes buy-SaaS viable** — the two decisions are linked, because
   Gate 0 requires 20–25 hrs/wk for 6 months.
4. **Run the ~$0 dry-run first.** Confirm on 2–3 real listings that a fair-priced $2–4K/mo asset can
   be run at <10 hrs/wk without a VA+dev spend that eats the profit. If the dry-run shows the
   delegation trap is unbeatable, buy-SaaS collapses toward the hosting idea's ~40–50% chance of $0
   and *no* idea in this field earns a full GO — in which case the honest answer is "don't deploy
   the slot yet."

**Bottom line**: On an honest, sunk-cost-free scale, **buy a cash-flowing micro-SaaS (5.2)** remains
the best use of the one venture slot; **free CutLog's focus to enable it**; **do the dry-run before
committing capital**. Compressed Air and Fire Sprinkler are confirmed PASSES — not the secondary
stream Hooman wants.
