# Round 4 Domain Map — Launch Plan for Discovery Cycles 051–100

**Date:** 2026-07-09. **Author:** Claude (Opus 4.8, 1M), read-only research strategist.
**Purpose:** Chart genuinely fresh territory for a NEW round of 50 discovery cycles (051–100)
and maximize survival through the deep-research **moat gate** — the filter that killed 9 of 10
domains in Round 3 and cut every Round-2 graduate from 8–9/10 to ~5.1.

**The single organizing principle for Round 4** (distilled from LEARNINGS.md + round3_report.md):

> Hunt ONLY for the CutLog / TitlePlant class: **a proprietary data flywheel a solo software
> founder can bootstrap and uniquely OWN, in an underserved/fragmented skilled trade the
> incumbents aren't watching, with a regulatory-mandate or hard-ROI demand driver.**

Two structural rules learned the hard way:

1. **Moat must accrue to the FOUNDER, not to a system-of-record (SoR) incumbent.** In every
   software-native domain the SaaS-of-record already sits on the flywheel data, so "layer AI on
   the incumbent's data" is dead on arrival. **STRONGLY PREFER "digitize a manual/paper process
   in a fragmented trade where the operator accumulates their OWN data"** (CutLog's parameter
   flywheel, TitlePlant's self-built title plant) over "add an AI button to someone's existing SoR."
2. **Low build cost is an anti-signal at the DOMAIN level.** Cheap-to-build + high-CAGR = maximum
   VC + maximum cloning. Round 3's broad software verticals (fintech/health/edu/genAI/etc.)
   backfired for exactly this reason. Fresh Round-4 domains are deliberately **unglamorous
   licensed trades** that VC ignores and where the buyer is a fragmented private operator (NOT a
   government/utility — the government is only the mandate SOURCE, never the customer).

## Part 2 — FRESH DOMAINS (12) for cycles 051–100

Selection filter applied to every candidate below:
- NOT in Part 1 (genuinely un-mined).
- Fits the CutLog/TitlePlant class: **the operator accumulates their OWN proprietary data** as a
  by-product of doing the mandated job — so the flywheel accrues to the founder's platform, not
  to an incumbent SoR.
- A **regulatory mandate or hard, invoice-visible ROI** drives demand (buyer can't say "we'll wait").
- **The customer is a fragmented PRIVATE licensed operator** (tester, driller, surveyor,
  inspector, contractor) — the government is only the mandate SOURCE. This keeps us clear of the
  gov/utility-BUYER instant-reject while still capturing the "regulatory tailwind."
- Explicitly AVOIDS: the instant-reject list AND the generic software-native trap.

The domains are ordered by conviction (strongest founder-accruing moat first).

### 1. Backflow-Prevention Assembly Testing
- **Thesis:** ~every commercial/irrigation water connection in North America must have its backflow
  preventer tested **annually by a certified tester** and the report filed with the water purveyor.
  Testers still run paper test-kit forms and chase renewals by memory. A mobile app that captures
  the test (gauge-paired), auto-generates the purveyor's PDF, and owns the **device+site test
  history** becomes the tester's book of business.
- **Why the moat accrues to the FOUNDER:** the tester accumulates a proprietary, compounding
  **device-level history** (every assembly's serial, model, location, pass/fail trend, next-due
  date) across all their accounts — a private register no purveyor and no field-service SoR holds
  in one place. Renewal-scheduling on that owned dataset is the recurring-revenue flywheel.
- **Biggest kill-risk to check first:** **SwiftComply / Aqua Backflow / TokenOfTrust-style purveyor
  portals may already own the report-submission SoR** (utilities push testers to a mandated portal),
  which would relocate the data moat to the purveyor's vendor. Verify testers still choose their own
  tool and that no portal has bundled a free tester app.

### 2. Septic / Onsite-Wastewater Inspection & Service
- **Thesis:** 60M+ US homes on septic; most states mandate inspection at property transfer and
  periodic operating-permit inspections for advanced systems. Pumpers/inspectors work off paper
  and clipboard diagrams. Digitize the inspection + as-built system diagram + pump/service log.
- **Why the moat accrues to the FOUNDER:** the operator builds a proprietary **per-property system
  history + as-built graph** (tank size, drainfield location, component ages, failure history) that
  compounds with every visit and is portable across the region — nobody else holds it. Real-estate
  and health-dept demand is non-deferrable.
- **Biggest kill-risk:** confirm the market isn't already consolidated by a septic-specific
  field-service SoR (e.g. **Ritam/Summit, ServiceCore, or state e-permit portals**) that owns the
  as-built record; and that the county doesn't mandate a specific portal (moat → county vendor).

### 3. Water-Well Drilling & the private "Well Plant"
- **Thesis:** water-well drillers must file a **state-mandated well-completion / lithology log** for
  every well. A driller who digitizes their logs accumulates a proprietary **subsurface database**
  (depth-to-water, yield, formation, casing) — the direct groundwater analog of TitlePlant's
  title plant. Sell drilling-time/yield prediction and permit auto-filing back to the trade.
- **Why the moat accrues to the FOUNDER:** the driller *uniquely accumulates* local subsurface data
  no SaaS-of-record owns; density in a county compounds into a real predictive/valuation asset.
  Same moat CLASS that made TitlePlant the only Round-3 survivor.
- **Biggest kill-risk:** **many states publish well logs as open public data** (USGS/state GWIS),
  which can commoditize the "plant" — verify the *value-add* (interpretation + prediction +
  workflow) can't be replicated off the free public dataset, and that incumbents (WellSites,
  gINT/Bentley) don't already serve drillers.

### 4. Cathodic-Protection / Corrosion Survey (AMPP-mandated)
- **Thesis:** buried/immersed steel (tanks, pipelines, well casings, water systems) must have
  **annual cathodic-protection surveys** per AMPP/NACE + regulatory codes. CP technicians record
  structure-to-soil potentials on paper and rectifier readings by hand. Digitize the survey and own
  the multi-year potential time-series per structure.
- **Why the moat accrues to the FOUNDER:** the CP tech accumulates a proprietary, **time-series
  corrosion-history per structure** — trend/anomaly detection on that owned longitudinal data is
  the flywheel; no generic SoR holds it.
- **Biggest kill-risk:** **the buyer may skew toward pipeline/utility operators** (12–24mo
  procurement = instant-reject), and incumbents (Bass Engineering "MyCPmaps", ProGauge, American
  Innovations "Bullhorn") may own the space. Verify a fragmented **private CP-service-contractor**
  buyer exists who pays directly.

### 5. Commercial Kitchen Exhaust / Hood Cleaning (NFPA 96)
- **Thesis:** restaurant hood/duct systems must be cleaned on an NFPA-96 schedule with
  **before/after documentation + a certification sticker** (fire-code + insurance mandated).
  Hood-cleaning contractors are highly fragmented and paper/photo-based. Digitize the job:
  photo-documented cleaning report, sticker/QR, and next-due scheduling.
- **Why the moat accrues to the FOUNDER:** contractor accumulates a proprietary **site-history +
  photo-evidence register** across all restaurants they service, which powers automated
  recompliance scheduling (recurring revenue) — the AHJ and insurer don't hold it.
- **Biggest kill-risk:** horizontal field-service SoRs (ServiceTitan/Jobber/Housecall Pro) can add
  a hood-cleaning template; verify the **NFPA-96-specific documentation + compliance-scheduling** is
  enough of a wedge that generic FSM doesn't already suffice, and no niche incumbent (e.g. hood-
  cleaning-specific apps) has locked it.

### 6. Marine / Boat Surveying
- **Thesis:** insurers and lenders **require a marine survey** for most vessel purchases/renewals.
  Surveyors write long narrative reports by hand from paper checklists. Digitize the survey
  (structured checklist + photos + valuation) and accumulate a **vessel-condition database**.
- **Why the moat accrues to the FOUNDER:** surveyor accumulates a proprietary **per-hull condition +
  valuation history** and comparable-vessel dataset; over time this becomes a defensible valuation/
  depreciation model no insurer SoR holds. Demand is insurance-mandated (non-deferrable).
- **Biggest kill-risk:** market may be **too small** (a few thousand accredited surveyors — SAMS/
  NAMS) to clear SAM>$100M at software prices; verify seat-count × ACV, and that no incumbent
  (e.g. TidewaterSurvey/ generic inspection apps) already serves it.

### 7. Arborist Tree-Risk (TRAQ) + Pesticide-Application Compliance
- **Thesis:** commercial arborists perform **ISA-TRAQ tree-risk assessments** and must keep
  **pesticide-application records** (state-mandated). Work is clipboard + photos. Digitize the
  risk assessment + spray log + per-tree work history.
- **Why the moat accrues to the FOUNDER:** the arborist accumulates a proprietary **per-tree
  longitudinal history** (species, defect progression, treatments, risk rating) across all client
  properties — a compounding urban-forest dataset. Regulatory (pesticide) + liability (risk-tree)
  demand drivers.
- **Biggest kill-risk:** **municipal tree-inventory SoR vendors already exist** (PlanIT Geo
  TreePlotter, ArborNote, ArboStar, SingleOps) and may own the record; verify the *risk-assessment +
  private-arborist* angle is unserved rather than a feature of an existing SoR.

### 8. Legal-Metrology / Calibration-Service Records ("weights & measures")
- **Thesis:** commercial scales, fuel dispensers, tank gauges, and lab instruments must be
  **calibrated and certified on a legal schedule** (NIST Handbook 44 / ISO-17025 / state W&M).
  Independent calibration-service techs record readings on paper cert forms. Digitize the cal
  event + generate the accredited certificate + own the drift history.
- **Why the moat accrues to the FOUNDER:** the cal-service company accumulates a proprietary
  **instrument-level calibration + drift time-series** across all customers, enabling predictive
  "this scale will drift out of tolerance before its next due date" — recurring-revenue flywheel on
  owned data. Mandate is legal (can't skip).
- **Biggest kill-risk:** enterprise **calibration-management SaaS already owns the record** on the
  customer side (GAGEtrak, Beamex CMX, ProCalV5, Qualer); verify the **service-provider-side**,
  fragmented independent cal-lab buyer is unserved and won't be captured by the customer's CMMS.

### 9. Propane / LPG System Inspection & Leak Check (NFPA 58 / "Gas Check")
- **Thesis:** propane dealers must perform **NFPA-58/54 system inspections and leak checks** on
  delivery and at defined triggers. Documentation is paper. Digitize the inspection + own the
  per-tank/per-site system history and out-of-gas/leak-check compliance state.
- **Why the moat accrues to the FOUNDER:** the dealer's field techs accumulate a proprietary
  **per-account system + appliance history** and compliance-due state; liability + regulatory demand.
- **Biggest kill-risk:** propane-industry **ERP/dispatch incumbents may own it** (ADD Systems,
  Cargas Energy, Blue Cow) as part of the delivery SoR — verify the compliance/inspection module
  isn't already bundled, and that independent dealers (vs. the big MLPs) are a reachable fragmented buyer.

### 10. Radon Measurement & Mitigation (real-estate-transaction driven)
- **Thesis:** radon testing is disclosure-mandated/customary at real-estate transactions and
  regulated in many states; measurement providers and mitigators run continuous-radon-monitor (CRM)
  test cycles and file reports. Digitize test-report generation + own the **geographic radon dataset**.
- **Why the moat accrues to the FOUNDER:** as providers run tests across a region, the platform
  accumulates a proprietary **address/zip-level radon dataset + mitigation-outcome history** — a
  geographic "plant" (TitlePlant analog) with predictive/valuation value nobody else aggregates.
- **Biggest kill-risk:** **CRM-device OEMs may own the data feed** (AirThings, Radalink, Sun Nuclear
  push their own cloud/report tools) — verify the report/workflow layer isn't already free from the
  device vendor, capping price at ~$0 (the Round-3 "OEM ships it free" pattern).

### 11. Agricultural Drainage-Tile & the private "as-built subsurface plant"
- **Thesis:** farm drainage-tile contractors install buried tile networks and capture RTK/GPS
  as-installed lines, but as-builts live in trucks and heads. Digitize the as-built + own a private
  **subsurface tile-map plant** contractors and farmers pay to access before digging/replacing.
- **Why the moat accrues to the FOUNDER:** the contractor *uniquely accumulates* proprietary
  **as-built underground maps** the moment they're installed — a private locate/records asset (a
  fragmented, ag-specific "One-Call plant") that compounds geographically. Hard ROI: avoid cutting
  existing tile; faster re-work bids.
- **Biggest kill-risk:** **ag-precision incumbents / RTK-OEM ecosystems may already capture as-builts**
  (Trimble WM-Drainage, John Deere Operations Center, AGREM) — verify the as-built record isn't
  already owned by the equipment OEM's free platform, and that the buyer is the fragmented contractor
  (not the OEM's captive channel).

### 12. Locksmith Master-Key Systems — the private "key plant"
- **Thesis:** commercial locksmiths design and maintain **master-key bitting systems** for
  facilities — proprietary keying charts still kept in binders/spreadsheets. Digitize the keying
  system, key-issuance/authorization records, and rekey planning; own the **facility keying plant**.
- **Why the moat accrues to the FOUNDER:** the locksmith accumulates a proprietary, high-switching-
  cost **per-facility keying database + issuance ledger** (who holds which key, pinning charts) — a
  private "key plant" that locks in the account. Direct CutLog-class own-data flywheel; security +
  liability demand.
- **Biggest kill-risk:** **market may be too small / DIY-satisfied** (existing niche tools: InstaKey,
  MasterKing, ProMaster Key Manager, Excel) — verify enough commercial locksmiths pay a SaaS ACV to
  clear SAM>$100M, and that the incumbent key-management tools haven't already locked the segment.

**Fan-out guidance (per CLAUDE.md §5.3 — background + parallel, don't over-parallelize):**
- Launch domains 1–10 as **10 independent background agents** (A–J), each owning its cycle block
  end-to-end (Steps 1–5 of `research-plan.md`) so the main session stays free.
- Domains 11–12 (drainage-tile, locksmith) are the two speculative/lightest; either run as 2 extra
  background agents (12 total) or fold them into the two highest-conviction agents (A/B and C) once
  those finish, to hold the concurrent fan-out at ~10.
- **Serialize the deep-research (Step 6) stage** — it depends on knowing which domains produced
  graduates. Do NOT fan out deep research until the discovery agents report back.

**Mandatory gates every agent must apply at the CYCLE stage** (carry forward from Round 3, they are
what make this round worth running):
1. **Incumbent/OEM check** — search for the niche SoR/ERP AND the invisible entrenched player
   (European QC firm, industry-specific ERP, device-OEM free cloud, utility/AHJ-mandated portal)
   BEFORE promoting. Each domain's "biggest kill-risk" above names the specific one to check first.
2. **Moat gate** — for every candidate, ask: *does the defensible data asset accrue to the FOUNDER's
   platform, or to whoever owns the system-of-record / the mandated portal / the device feed?* If
   the operator can't UNIQUELY ACCUMULATE their own compounding data, reject.
3. **Growth-rate + SAM check** — SAM > $100M at realistic SaaS ACV × reachable seat count. (Growth
   was never the binding constraint; size/reachability is — several of these trades may be too small.)
4. **Buyer check** — the paying customer must be the **fragmented private licensed operator**, never
   the government/utility/AHJ (that's an instant-reject 12–24mo procurement buyer). The regulator is
   only the demand SOURCE.

**Honest expectation to set going in:** given the Round-2/3 base rate (cycle scores inflate ~2–4 pts;
red-team haircut lands everything at 3.0–5.5), the realistic goal of Round 4 is to surface **1–3
genuinely moat-bearing, own-data-accumulating trade-digitization ideas** that survive the deep-
research gate — not a flood of graduates. If a domain returns an honest zero (as 9 of 10 did in
Round 3), that is a valid, valuable result. The prize is a defensible #2 in the CutLog/TitlePlant
class, reached by hunting where VC isn't looking: unglamorous licensed trades whose operators
generate their own proprietary data every working day.
