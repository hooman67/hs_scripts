# Customer Validation Plan: AI-Powered Crane Load Monitoring Retrofit

## First 5 Target Customers

### Customer 1: Sterling Crane (Houston, TX)
- **Fleet**: ~300 cranes (mobile/truck, rough terrain, crawler)
- **Profile**: Regional player in Gulf Coast market. Mix of owned and rented-out equipment. Strong Texas/Louisiana presence (petrochemical construction, energy infrastructure).
- **Why them**: 
  - Gulf Coast = high OSHA inspection frequency (Region 6 is aggressive)
  - Fleet likely includes significant pre-2015 equipment needing retrofit
  - Large enough to be meaningful revenue ($200K+ potential deal) but small enough to lack in-house engineering solutions
  - Texas market is relationship-driven but pragmatic on cost
- **Entry strategy**: Industry advisor introduction or cold outreach to VP of Safety/Fleet Operations
- **Deal size potential**: 50-100 cranes x $4K = $200-400K hardware + $4-8K/mo SaaS

### Customer 2: TNT Crane & Rigging (Houston, TX)
- **Fleet**: ~200 cranes, heavy lift specialty
- **Profile**: Mid-size player focused on industrial/petrochemical projects. Acquired by Manitex International (public company) -- has corporate compliance pressure.
- **Why them**:
  - Public parent company = heightened compliance sensitivity
  - Heavy lift specialty means LMI is critical safety requirement
  - Corporate parent may mandate fleet-wide monitoring
  - Houston-based = accessible for demos and pilots
- **Entry strategy**: Through Manitex corporate safety/compliance team, or direct to TNT fleet operations
- **Deal size potential**: 80-150 cranes x $4K = $320-600K + monthly SaaS

### Customer 3: Crane Rental Corporation (CRC) (multiple locations, Southeast US)
- **Fleet**: ~100-150 cranes
- **Profile**: Pure rental company (rents cranes to contractors without operators). They are liable for equipment condition but cannot control how operators use the cranes.
- **Why them**:
  - Pure rental = maximum liability exposure (they provide equipment but cannot supervise use)
  - Monitoring gives them visibility into how renters use their equipment
  - Fleet utilization tracking is a side benefit they will value
  - Smaller company = faster decision cycles, more accessible decision makers
- **Entry strategy**: Trade show introduction (ConExpo, SC&RA Annual Conference) or direct outreach to owner/GM
- **Deal size potential**: 40-80 cranes x $4K = $160-320K + SaaS

### Customer 4: Bay Crane (NYC metro area)
- **Fleet**: ~200 cranes (including tower cranes), heavy urban construction focus
- **Profile**: Major NYC crane operator. NYC Department of Buildings has strictest crane regulations in the US. Recent crane accidents in NYC have increased scrutiny.
- **Why them**:
  - NYC DOB regulations exceed OSHA requirements -- compliance pressure is maximum
  - Recent high-profile crane accidents (2024-2025) have increased insurance and regulatory pressure
  - Urban operations = higher liability per incident (dense population)
  - Willing to pay premium for compliance documentation
- **Entry strategy**: Through NYC crane safety consultant community (small, tight-knit group)
- **Deal size potential**: 50-100 cranes x $5K (NYC premium pricing) = $250-500K + SaaS

### Customer 5: Deep South Crane & Rigging (Gulf Coast, multiple states)
- **Fleet**: ~60-80 cranes
- **Profile**: Smaller regional player covering Alabama, Mississippi, Louisiana. Mix of older and newer equipment. Cost-sensitive but compliance-aware.
- **Why them**:
  - Ideal "design partner" size -- small enough for CEO/owner to make decisions quickly
  - Likely has highest percentage of non-compliant older equipment (cost-sensitive, deferred upgrades)
  - Owner-operated = single decision maker, fast pilot approval
  - If we solve their problem, they become a testimonial for larger companies
- **Entry strategy**: Direct outreach to owner. Offer free pilot on 3-5 cranes in exchange for testimonial.
- **Deal size potential**: 20-40 cranes x $3.5K (discount for early adopter) = $70-140K + SaaS

## Discovery Questions (for customer interviews)

### Current State & Pain
1. "How many cranes in your fleet currently have working LMI/RCI systems? What percentage are non-compliant?"
2. "When an LMI system fails on an older crane, what do you currently do? Replace OEM ($20K+), operate without, or retire the crane?"
3. "Have you received OSHA citations related to crane load monitoring in the past 3 years? What was the cost (fines + remediation)?"
4. "What does your annual spend on crane safety compliance look like? (inspections, certifications, documentation)"
5. "How do you currently generate OSHA-required inspection documentation and load test records?"

### Decision Process
6. "Who makes the decision to purchase safety equipment for your fleet? (Owner/CEO, VP Safety, Fleet Manager, Committee?)"
7. "What is your typical budget approval process and timeline for a $100K-$500K fleet-wide investment?"
8. "Do you have preferred vendors/dealers you buy crane equipment from? Would you buy safety equipment from a new company?"
9. "What would make you trust a new entrant in crane safety? (Certifications, insurance backing, peer references, pilot program?)"

### Value & Pricing
10. "If a system could provide OSHA-compliant load monitoring at $3,500-$5,000 per crane (vs. $20K+ for OEM replacement), how many cranes would you retrofit?"
11. "Would you pay $79-$99/month per crane for automated compliance documentation and fleet monitoring? What value does that represent vs. manual record-keeping?"
12. "Does your insurance carrier offer premium discounts for monitored cranes? Have they asked about monitoring capabilities?"
13. "Would real-time fleet visibility (which cranes are working, utilization, load history) be valuable beyond safety compliance?"

### Installation & Operations
14. "How much crane downtime can you tolerate for a retrofit installation? (hours vs. days)"
15. "Do you have in-house mechanics who could install equipment, or would you need the vendor to provide installation?"
16. "What cellular coverage is like at your typical job sites? Do you have connectivity concerns?"
17. "How do your operators feel about monitoring systems? (Resistance? Acceptance? Indifference?)"

### Competition & Alternatives
18. "Have you evaluated any load monitoring retrofit solutions? What options have you been quoted?"
19. "Are you aware of any affordable retrofit LMI solutions in the market? (testing for competitive awareness)"
20. "What would a 'perfect' solution look like for you? What features are must-have vs. nice-to-have?"

## Demand Signals to Watch For

### Strong Positive Signals (validates product-market fit)
- "We have XX cranes sitting idle because LMI failed and replacement is too expensive"
- "Our insurance carrier is requiring monitoring or raising premiums"
- "We received an OSHA citation for crane monitoring deficiency"
- "We would install this on every crane in our fleet tomorrow if it existed"
- "Can we be your first customer / pilot partner?"
- Customer asks about timeline and pricing unprompted
- Customer offers to pay for a pilot installation
- Multiple companies report same pain point independently

### Moderate Positive Signals (market exists but need more validation)
- "Yes, this is a problem, but we have workarounds" (what workarounds? how painful?)
- "We would consider it if it were certified" (certification is the blocker, not demand)
- "Our fleet is mostly newer, but we know companies with this problem" (referral opportunity)
- "We spend $X per year on compliance paperwork" (quantified pain)

### Negative/Warning Signals (pivot or de-risk)
- "We already have a solution from [competitor]" (market already served?)
- "Our operators would refuse to use monitoring systems" (adoption risk)
- "We would never buy safety equipment from a startup" (credibility gap too large)
- "$5K per crane is still too expensive" (price point wrong)
- "OSHA rarely inspects us / we handle citations as cost of business" (compliance pressure insufficient)
- "We are already budgeted to replace LMI with OEM systems" (market timing off)

### Kill Signals (abort this idea)
- 5+ conversations all say "this is not a real problem for us"
- Insurance carriers say monitoring data does not affect premiums
- OSHA enforcement is actually declining, not increasing
- ASME certification for retrofit systems is impossible or requires crane-by-crane approval

## Outreach Plan

### Phase 1: Warm Introductions (Weeks 1-4)

**Goal**: 5-8 conversations through warm channels

| Channel | Target | Action |
|---------|--------|--------|
| LinkedIn (direct) | Fleet managers, safety directors at target companies | Personalized connection request + message referencing their fleet/OSHA activity |
| Industry advisors | Recruit 1-2 retired crane safety professionals | Post on LinkedIn targeting "crane inspector" "fleet safety manager" retired professionals. Offer $500/intro meeting + $2K/month advisory. |
| Crane inspection companies | Companies that do annual crane inspections (they see non-compliant cranes daily) | Partner pitch: "We send you installation revenue, you send us customer referrals" |
| Trade associations | SC&RA (Specialized Carriers & Rigging Association), NCCCO (National Commission for Certification of Crane Operators) | Attend local chapter meetings, ask for introductions to fleet operators |

### Phase 2: Trade Shows & Events (Months 2-4)

| Event | Timing | Investment | Goal |
|-------|--------|-----------|------|
| SC&RA Annual Conference | April (annual) | $3,000-$5,000 | 10+ conversations with fleet operators |
| ConExpo-Con/Agg | March (every 3 years, next: 2029) | $5,000-$10,000 | 50+ leads, brand visibility |
| Regional Crane & Rigging events | Quarterly | $1,000-$2,000 each | 5-8 conversations per event |
| OSHA Crane Safety Training events | Ongoing | $500-$1,000 | Meet safety professionals, build credibility |

### Phase 3: Cold Outreach (Weeks 4-8)

**Target list building:**
1. Crane rental companies from CraneNetwork.com directory (largest crane marketplace)
2. OSHA citation database (publicly searchable) -- find companies cited for crane violations
3. SC&RA member directory
4. State DOT crane permit databases (identifies active crane operators)

**Cold outreach script** (email/LinkedIn):
```
Subject: Cutting your crane LMI replacement costs by 80%

Hi [Name],

I'm building an OSHA-compliant load monitoring retrofit system that installs on older cranes 
in under 2 hours for $3,500-$5,000 (vs. $20K+ for OEM LMI replacement).

I'm talking with fleet managers about whether this is a real pain point. Do you have cranes 
in your fleet with failed or absent LMI systems?

I'd love 15 minutes to hear how you currently handle this. Not selling anything -- just 
validating whether this problem is worth solving.

[Name]
```

**Target**: 50 cold outreach messages -> 10-15 responses -> 5-8 conversations

### Phase 4: Pilot Recruitment (Months 3-6)

**Pilot offer:**
- Free installation of prototype system on 3-5 cranes
- 90-day monitoring with full data access
- Company provides: crane access, operator feedback, testimonial if satisfied
- Company gets: free load monitoring for pilot period, option to purchase at 20% discount

**Pilot criteria (ideal):**
- Company has 5+ cranes without working LMI
- Located within driving distance (initially Gulf Coast / Texas)
- Has a safety-conscious fleet manager willing to champion internally
- Willing to provide installation access and operator access for feedback

## Validation Timeline & Decision Gates

| Week | Activity | Decision Gate |
|------|----------|--------------|
| 1-4 | 5-8 discovery conversations | GATE 1: Do 4+ companies confirm this is a top-5 pain point? |
| 4-8 | 10-15 total conversations, pricing validation | GATE 2: Would 3+ companies pay $3.5-5K per crane? Any pilot commitments? |
| 8-12 | Pilot agreements signed, prototype demo'd | GATE 3: 2+ signed pilot agreements with deposit? |
| 12-16 | Pilot installations running | GATE 4: System works reliably in field? Positive operator feedback? |

**Gate 1 fail** = Pivot or abandon (problem is not acute enough)
**Gate 2 fail** = Adjust pricing or feature set (problem exists but value prop is off)
**Gate 3 fail** = Credibility gap too large -- need industry co-founder or stronger advisory board
**Gate 4 fail** = Technical challenges exceed estimates -- reassess feasibility and timeline
