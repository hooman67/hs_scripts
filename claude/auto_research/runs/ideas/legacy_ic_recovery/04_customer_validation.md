# Customer Validation Plan: Legacy IC Recovery-as-a-Service

## First 5 Target Customers

### Customer 1: Sims Lifecycle Services (E-Waste Recycler -- Host Site)

**Company**: Sims Lifecycle Services (subsidiary of Sims Limited, ASX: SGM)
**Revenue**: ~$500M (Sims Metal division is $7B+)
**Location**: Multiple US facilities (Nashville, TN; La Vergne, TN; Roseville, CA)
**Role to target**: VP of Operations or Director of IT Asset Disposition (ITAD)
**Why them**:
- Process 100,000+ tons of e-waste/year in the US
- Already sort PCBs by grade (data center boards vs. consumer)
- Have existing relationships with enterprise clients (data center decommissions = high-value boards)
- Publicly committed to circular economy (ESG narrative aligns)
- Large enough to tolerate pilot; small enough division to make fast decisions

**What we offer them**:
- Zero-capex robotic cell installed in their facility
- New revenue stream: 40% of recovered IC sales (potentially $50-200K/month per cell)
- They continue to shred the board after IC extraction (no change to existing metal recovery)
- ESG/sustainability story enhancement for their corporate reporting

**Entry point**: Their innovation/sustainability team or ITAD division leadership

### Customer 2: Rochester Electronics (Legacy IC Distributor -- Buyer)

**Company**: Rochester Electronics (Private)
**Revenue**: Estimated $200-500M
**Location**: Newburyport, MA
**Employees**: 700+
**Role to target**: VP of Supply Chain or Director of Sourcing
**Why them**:
- World's largest authorized aftermarket semiconductor source
- They have 70,000+ device types but cannot cover the full universe of obsolete parts
- Their re-manufacture lead time is 6-18 months; recovered ICs ship immediately
- They already have the customer base (defense, aerospace, industrial)
- A recovered IC supply source fills gaps in their catalog without competing

**What we offer them**:
- Authenticated, AS6171-traceable recovered ICs for part numbers they cannot re-manufacture
- Immediate availability (vs. 6-18 month re-manufacture cycle)
- Lower cost basis than wafer-level re-manufacture for small quantities
- First-look purchasing rights on recovered inventory matching their demand list

**Discovery question**: "What percentage of customer part number requests can you NOT fulfill today, and what's the average value of those unfulfilled orders?"

### Customer 3: DLA (Defense Logistics Agency) / DMSMS Program Office (Government Buyer)

**Company**: US Department of Defense - DLA Land and Maritime
**Location**: Columbus, OH
**Role to target**: DMSMS (Diminishing Manufacturing Sources and Material Shortages) Program Manager
**Why them**:
- DLA spends $1B+/year managing obsolete component issues for weapon systems
- Every military platform (F-16, M1 Abrams, Aegis, etc.) has hundreds of obsolete ICs
- Current solution: expensive re-designs ($500K-$5M per chip replacement) or form/fit/function replacements
- Government is actively seeking alternative supply sources for obsolete parts
- Published requirement: all replacement parts must meet AS6171 authentication

**What we offer them**:
- Authenticated recovered ICs at fraction of re-design cost
- Full AS6171 compliance with X-ray, electrical test, provenance documentation
- Faster than re-manufacture (weeks vs. months)
- Supports DLA's sustainability/circular economy mandates (Executive Order 14057)

**Entry point**: DMSMS annual conference, DLA Industry Day events, GIDEP (Government-Industry Data Exchange Program)

## Discovery Questions (for each customer category)

### For E-Waste Recyclers (Hosts):

1. "What's the current revenue per ton for PCBs you process? How does that compare to your highest-value waste stream?"
2. "Do you currently sort PCBs by type/origin (server, telecom, automotive)? What's the labor cost of that sorting?"
3. "Have you ever been approached by anyone wanting to recover individual components before shredding? What was your response?"
4. "What's your available floor space for a new processing cell? What are your facility constraints (power, ventilation, safety)?"
5. "What revenue share model would make this interesting enough to dedicate space and board flow?"
6. "What's your biggest concern about hosting third-party equipment in your facility?"
7. "How much advance notice do you have before high-value board lots come in (e.g., telecom decommission, data center refresh)?"

### For Legacy IC Buyers (Distributors/OEMs):

1. "What percentage of obsolete part requests can you NOT fulfill today? What's the dollar value of those lost sales?"
2. "What's your average incoming inspection cost per lot from non-authorized sources?"
3. "What counterfeit detection rate are you seeing on parts from the open market?"
4. "If a source provided ICs with full provenance (board photos, thermal extraction records, X-ray, electrical test), would that reduce or eliminate your incoming inspection?"
5. "What part categories are hardest to source? (mil-spec logic, analog, memory, microcontrollers?)"
6. "What's the typical premium you can charge for authenticated vs. unverified parts?"
7. "Would you commit to purchasing X quantity per month at Y price if we could provide guaranteed-authentic recovered parts?"

### For Defense/Government:

1. "How many DMSMS cases are currently open across your program portfolio?"
2. "What's the average cost of a chip-level redesign when you can't source the original part?"
3. "Would recovered/authenticated parts from field-returned boards meet your qualification requirements if AS6171-compliant?"
4. "What testing standard would recovered ICs need to meet for your specific applications (MIL-STD-883, commercial, automotive)?"
5. "Are there specific part families where you have critical shortages today?"

## Key Assumptions to Validate

| # | Assumption | How to Validate | Kill Criteria |
|---|-----------|----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Average PCB contains >$25 in recoverable IC value | Analyze BOM of 50 real server/telecom boards vs. aftermarket prices | If <$10/board average, unit economics fail |
| 2 | Recyclers will dedicate floor space + board flow | Discovery calls + pilot MOU | If 0/10 recyclers show interest after demo |
| 3 | Buyers will pay premium for recovered-authenticated vs. gray market | Price comparison + buyer LOIs | If authenticated recovered ICs cannot command >50% of Rochester pricing |
| 4 | 200+ chips/hour is achievable | Lab prototype validation | If sustained rate <50/hour, economics don't work |
| 5 | Thermal damage rate is manageable (<10%) | Lab extraction runs | If >25% damage rate across package types |
| 6 | IC identification accuracy is high enough | Vision system testing | If <80% correct identification, manual review cost kills margin |
