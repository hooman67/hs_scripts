# Cycle 097 Report — Ag Drainage-Tile (4/4): Final Domain Verdict

**Date**: 2026-07-09. **Round**: 4. **Domain**: #11 Ag Drainage-Tile "as-built subsurface plant".

## Domain #11 — FINAL VERDICT: HONEST ZERO (near-miss on moat, killed on SAM)

This is the most structurally interesting of the three tail domains, and the reason it fails is
worth recording precisely.

**What it got RIGHT (rare in Round 4):**
- **A real, founder-accruing moat exists** — the contractor-owned, brand-agnostic, **cross-farm**
  as-built plant is genuinely not held by any incumbent, because the OEM clouds (JD Operations
  Center, Trimble Farmer Core, Ag Leader AgFiniti) are **farmer-account-siloed and brand-siloed**
  (VERIFIED 094). A contractor working mixed fleets across dozens of farms has no single portable
  records asset today.
- **Clean private buyer** — fragmented tile contractors, not government/utility (passes the buyer gate).
- **Real tailwind** — tile-drainage acreage is expanding (yield/climate driven).

**What KILLED it (cycle 095, confirmed 096):**
- **SAM is ~$4–9M** — only ~1,000–3,000 US tile contractors (many jobs are farmer-self-tiled), ×
  ~$1–3K/yr ACV. That is **10–25× below the $100M floor.** A countable buyer universe is a red
  flag (same shape as Round-2 Wireless TAB, ~$9–18M SAM).
- **Every SAM-expansion escape fails (096):** farmer-facing = free in OEM cloud; ag-One-Call =
  public-utility buyer (instant-reject); land-transaction due-diligence = too-infrequent + cold-start;
  broaden-to-all-utility = crowded GIS incumbents (Esri/Bentley/ProStar).

**The lesson (durable):** this domain is the cleanest illustration of the Round-4 tension — *the
narrow, un-watched trade that gives you a defensible founder-owned moat is often too small to clear
SAM.* Moat and market size pull in opposite directions in the deep tail. The as-built-plant thesis
was sound; the trade is simply too small.

**No defensible #2 candidate in ag drainage-tile.** Valid, expected honest zero.
