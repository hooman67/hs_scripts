# Customer Validation Plan: Remote Dryland Farm Soil Monitoring (Satellite)

## First 5 Target Customers

### Customer 1: Large-scale Australian grain farm (Western Australia wheat belt)
- **Profile**: 3,000-8,000 hectare broadacre wheat/canola operation in WA's eastern wheatbelt
- **Why them**: Zero cellular coverage across 70%+ of paddocks. Makes planting decisions worth $200K+ based on "finger in the dirt" soil tests. Already uses precision ag (variable rate seeding) but lacks real-time soil data.
- **Decision maker**: Farm owner/manager (often family-run, 1-3 decision makers)
- **Budget authority**: Yes — typical precision ag spend $10-50K/year on technology
- **Probe deployment**: 20-40 probes across paddocks (1 per 100-200 hectares for zoning)
- **Annual value**: $1,200-2,400/year in subscriptions + $2,000-4,000 hardware
- **How to reach**: GRDC (Grains Research & Development Corporation) events, Ag-Grow field days, local agronomist referrals

### Customer 2: US Great Plains cattle rancher with cropland (Montana/Dakotas)
- **Profile**: 5,000-20,000 acre operation combining dryland wheat/hay with cattle grazing. Remote location, patchy cellular at best.
- **Why them**: Needs to decide: when to move cattle between pastures (grass moisture = growth), when to plant winter wheat (soil moisture at seeding depth), and when to cut hay (soil conditions).
- **Decision maker**: Ranch owner
- **Budget authority**: Yes — operations generating $500K-2M revenue
- **Probe deployment**: 15-30 probes (mix of pasture and cropland)
- **Annual value**: $900-1,800/year subscriptions
- **How to reach**: Montana Stockgrowers Association, USDA Extension offices, ranching podcasts (The Art of Range, Working Ranch Magazine)

### Customer 3: East African commercial farm aggregator (Kenya/Tanzania)
- **Profile**: Company managing 5-20 smallholder farms (50-500 hectares total), producing maize/sorghum for market. Provides inputs + tech to smallholders in exchange for crop purchase agreements.
- **Why them**: Virtually no cellular in rural farmland. Crop failure from planting into dry soil is the #1 loss factor. Data enables better input timing (when to apply fertilizer relative to moisture).
- **Decision maker**: Aggregator company management (not individual smallholders)
- **Budget authority**: Yes — the aggregator bears technology cost, recoups from yield improvement
- **Probe deployment**: 10-20 probes across managed farms
- **Annual value**: $600-1,200/year subscriptions
- **How to reach**: AGRF (Africa Green Revolution Forum), Mercy Corps AgriFin, AgDevCo portfolio companies

### Customer 4: Australian pastoral company (large-scale cattle stations)
- **Profile**: 100,000+ hectare cattle station in Queensland/NT. Manages water points and grazing rotation across vast distances.
- **Why them**: Already buys satellite-connected technology (Ceres Tag for cattle, satellite phones). Understands the "no connectivity" problem viscerally. Soil moisture indicates pasture recovery after grazing.
- **Decision maker**: Station manager + head office (corporate pastoral companies: AACo, Paraway Pastoral)
- **Budget authority**: Centralized tech investment — can deploy 50-100 probes per station
- **Probe deployment**: 50-100 probes per property
- **Annual value**: $3,000-6,000/year per property
- **How to reach**: Direct approach to AACo, Consolidated Pastoral Company, Paraway. Beef Australia expo. Northern Territory Cattlemen's Association.

### Customer 5: Carbon credit verification company
- **Profile**: Company helping farmers register for soil carbon credits (programs like ERF in Australia, Verra globally). Needs continuous soil moisture documentation to validate carbon sequestration claims.
- **Why them**: Carbon credit protocols increasingly require CONTINUOUS soil monitoring data (not just annual samples). Current monitoring cost is prohibitive ($50-100K per property using manual methods). Our probes provide the data trail needed for credit verification at 1/10th cost.
- **Decision maker**: Head of monitoring/verification
- **Budget authority**: Yes — charges farmers for credit registration, can bundle monitoring cost
- **Probe deployment**: 10-30 probes per farm enrolled in carbon program
- **Annual value**: Could be $10K-50K across portfolio of enrolled farms
- **How to reach**: Soil Carbon Solutions, AgriProve, Indigo Ag (carbon program), Regen Network

## Discovery Questions (for initial farmer conversations)

### Understanding the problem (open-ended)
1. "How do you currently decide when to plant / move cattle / cut hay?" (Looking for: "I just look at the weather" or "I dig a hole and feel the soil")
2. "In the last 3 years, have you had a crop failure or poor germination that you attribute to soil moisture misjudgment?" (Looking for: specific loss stories, quantified if possible)
3. "How much does one bad planting decision cost you in a typical year?" (Looking for: $10K-100K loss quantification)

### Understanding current solutions
4. "Do you currently use any soil monitoring technology? If so, what? If not, why not?" (Looking for: "no cellular coverage" or "too expensive" or "never heard of anything")
5. "Have you looked at soil sensors before and decided against them? What stopped you?" (Looking for: barriers we can overcome vs. fundamental disinterest)

### Value and pricing
6. "If you could see your soil moisture at multiple points across your property — updated daily — on your phone, what would that change about your decisions?" (Looking for: specific actions they would take differently)
7. "What would that improved decision-making be worth to you per year?" (Looking for: anchor their value perception before we say price)
8. "I am exploring a product at $99 per probe with $5/month data service. No cellular needed — works via satellite. For a farm like yours, how many probes would be useful and would that price work?" (Looking for: honest reaction — enthusiasm, hesitation, price objection)

### Logistics and deployment
9. "How often do you drive out to different parts of your property?" (Looking for: accessibility for installation, how remote it really is)
10. "Would you want to install probes yourself or have someone come out?" (Looking for: self-install feasibility)

### Competition / alternatives
11. "If a product like this existed today, where would you expect to find it? Who would you trust to sell it to you?" (Looking for: channels — ag supply store, agronomist, direct online)
12. "What else have you tried for remote monitoring on your property? Satellite phones, tracking devices, weather stations?" (Looking for: technology adoption level, budget precedent)

## Demand Signals to Look For

### Strong positive signals (indicate real demand)
- Farmer can quantify cost of moisture-blind decisions (>$10K/year in losses)
- Farmer has already tried solutions that failed due to connectivity
- Farmer immediately asks "when can I get one?" or "how do I sign up for the pilot?"
- Farmer volunteers additional use cases we had not considered
- Farmer offers to pay for pilot participation (even nominal amount)
- Multiple farmers in same region express interest (network effect potential)

### Weak/neutral signals (not enough alone)
- "That sounds interesting" without follow-up questions
- Theoretical interest but no quantified pain
- Interest contingent on very low price (<$2/month)
- Only interested if bundled with weather/other data

### Red flags (demand may not exist)
- "I would just use the BoM radar / weather forecast" (free alternatives sufficient)
- "Soil moisture does not change my decisions — I plant on calendar dates"
- "My agronomist handles this" (outsourced decision, not farmer's problem)
- "$5/month per probe is too much for dryland — margins are too thin"
- "I tried soil sensors before and stopped looking at the data after 2 months"

## Outreach Plan

### Phase 1: Warm introductions (Weeks 1-4)

**Target**: 10-15 conversations with Australian and US dryland farmers

| Channel | Action | Expected Yield |
|---------|--------|----------------|
| GRDC (Grains Research & Development Corp) | Attend or sponsor a GRDC Updates event in WA. Network with advisors who introduce farmers. | 3-5 farmer conversations |
| AgTech meetups (Perth, Adelaide) | Present concept at Perth NW AgTech meetup or similar | 2-3 interested farmers |
| LinkedIn outreach (farm managers) | Cold outreach to farm managers at large pastoral companies (AACo, Paraway) with specific value prop | 2-3 conversations |
| USDA Extension (Montana, ND) | Contact county extension agents who work with dryland operations. Ask to be introduced to farmers. | 2-3 US farmer conversations |
| Reddit r/farming, r/agriculture | Post asking about soil moisture monitoring pain points. Identify interested parties. | 1-2 conversations |
| Farming podcasts as guest | Reach out to The Art of Range, Growing SA, or similar | Long-term brand building |

### Phase 2: Structured discovery (Weeks 4-8)

**Target**: Deep interviews with 5 most promising leads from Phase 1

- 30-45 minute structured interviews using questions above
- Offer: "We are building this. Would you be willing to trial 10-20 probes on your property for free for 3 months in exchange for feedback?"
- Document: pain severity (1-10), price sensitivity, deployment logistics, channel preference

### Phase 3: Letter of Intent / Pilot commitment (Weeks 8-12)

**Target**: 3-5 signed pilot agreements

- Non-binding LOI: "I agree to deploy X probes for 3 months and provide feedback. I intend to purchase at $99/probe + $5/month if the pilot demonstrates [specific value]."
- This is the REAL demand signal. Talk is cheap. Written commitment is signal.

### Phase 4: Paid pilot (Weeks 16-28)

- Charge $49/probe for pilot (50% discount) + $5/month from day 1
- Paying pilot customers = strongest possible demand validation
- Revenue from pilot: ~$5K-10K (not material but validates willingness to pay)

## Key Metrics for Validation Success

| Metric | Target | Minimum Viable |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| Farmer conversations completed | 15 | 10 |
| Farmers who quantify >$10K/yr in moisture-blind losses | 8/15 (>50%) | 5/15 (>33%) |
| Farmers who say $5/month is "acceptable" or "cheap" | 9/15 (>60%) | 6/15 (>40%) |
| Signed pilot LOIs | 5 | 3 |
| Probes committed for pilot | 100 | 50 |
| Farmers willing to pay for pilot | 3 | 2 |

### Decision framework
- **STRONG GO**: 5+ LOIs, 100+ probes committed, >60% say price is acceptable, clear quantified pain
- **WEAK GO**: 3-4 LOIs, 50-100 probes, price sensitivity exists but manageable
- **PIVOT**: <3 LOIs, <50 probes, or majority say "not enough value for dryland"
- **STOP**: <2 conversations lead to any interest, or universal response is "dryland does not need this"

## Carbon Credit Angle (Secondary Value Proposition)

If pure moisture monitoring demand is weak among dryland farmers, the carbon credit documentation angle may be stronger:

- Australian Emissions Reduction Fund (ERF) soil carbon method requires ongoing monitoring
- Verra VM0042 requires demonstration of soil moisture conditions for permanence claims
- A network of probes providing continuous moisture data could be REQUIRED infrastructure for carbon credit programs
- In this model, the BUYER is the carbon credit company, not the farmer

**Validation**: Approach AgriProve, Soil Carbon Solutions, and Indigo Ag's Australian operations. Ask: "Would continuous soil moisture data from our probes satisfy your monitoring requirements? Would you pay $5/probe/month to have this data for your enrolled properties?"

This could flip the business model from B2C (sell to farmer) to B2B (sell to carbon platform that deploys on farmer land).
