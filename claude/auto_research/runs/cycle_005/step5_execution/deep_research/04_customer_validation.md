## Discovery Interview Questions

### Problem Validation Questions (10 minutes)

1. "Walk me through your current QC workflow for a batch from fermentation to packaging. Where do you measure dissolved oxygen, and what do you do with those readings?"
   - *What we're learning:* Current workflow, pain points, decision-making around DO

2. "In the past 12 months, have you had a batch where oxidation/staling was detected after packaging? What happened? How did you find out?"
   - *What we're learning:* Frequency and cost of staling events; detection lag

3. "When you get a high DO reading, what do you actually do? How confident are you that the batch will or won't stale?"
   - *What we're learning:* The gap between DO measurement and staling certainty

4. "Have you ever wished you could measure something OTHER than dissolved oxygen to predict freshness? What would that look like?"
   - *What we're learning:* Awareness of DO limitations; appetite for better tools

5. "What's your biggest fear about freshness as you consider expanding distribution / shelf presence?"
   - *What we're learning:* Future pain amplification; distribution-driven urgency

### Solution Validation Questions (10 minutes)

6. "If I told you a handheld device could measure the actual free radicals causing staling — not just oxygen, but the actual damage molecules — in 30 seconds at the tank, how would that change your process?"
   - *What we're learning:* Perceived value; workflow integration potential

7. "Where in your process would you most want to take a radical measurement? (e.g., pre-transfer, post-transfer, at packaging, in-package, in-market)"
   - *What we're learning:* Highest-value measurement point; workflow integration

8. "What would you need to see to trust a new measurement tool? (Correlation studies? Brand endorsement? Peer brewery validation?)"
   - *What we're learning:* Adoption barriers; credibility requirements

9. "How much do you currently spend per year on QC instrumentation and consumables? What's the most expensive single instrument you've purchased?"
   - *What we're learning:* Budget framing; price anchoring

10. "If this device existed today at $5,000 with $2.50/test cartridges, would you buy it this quarter? What would make you say no?"
    - *What we're learning:* Willingness to pay; specific objections

### Demand Signal Questions (5 minutes)

11. "Would you be willing to be a beta tester for 6 weeks? We'd provide the device free in exchange for weekly feedback and usage data."
    - *What we're learning:* Actual commitment signal (not just polite interest)

12. "Do you know 3-5 other breweries that struggle with this problem? Would you introduce us?"
    - *What we're learning:* Referral willingness; problem universality; network access

13. "If we built this and it worked as described, would you be willing to be quoted in marketing materials / a case study?"
    - *What we're learning:* Enthusiasm level (case study = strong signal)

## Demand Signals to Track

### Strong Positive Signals (weighted heavily)

| Signal | Weight | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| "I would buy this today" (unprompted) | 5x | The gold standard |
| Offers to pre-pay or put down a deposit | 5x | Money > words |
| Asks "when can I get one?" | 4x | Urgency signal |
| Describes specific batch loss due to staling | 4x | Pain is real and costly |

| Offers warm introductions to other breweries | 3x | Would not risk reputation unless genuinely excited |
| Asks detailed technical questions about accuracy/calibration | 3x | Evaluating for purchase, not just curious |
| Mentions existing budget line item for QC tools | 3x | Budget already allocated |

### Moderate Positive Signals

| Signal | Weight | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| "That's interesting / I can see the value" | 2x | Polite interest — need more probing |
| Asks about price (curiosity) | 2x | Engaging but not committed |
| Describes DO meter frustrations | 2x | Problem awareness |
| Mentions competitor products they have evaluated | 2x | Active shopper |

### Negative / Cautionary Signals

| Signal | Weight | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| "My DO meter tells me everything I need" | -3x | Satisfied with proxy measurement |
| "We don't have budget for new QC tools" | -2x | Budget constraint |
| "Our sensory panel catches everything" | -2x | Human-centric process; skeptical of instruments |
| "I'd need to see 2+ years of peer data first" | -2x | Slow adopter; not a launch customer |
| "Sounds like a solution looking for a problem" | -4x | Fundamental value rejection |
| "I've never had a staling issue" | -3x | Problem does not exist for them (or they don't know it does) |

### Go / No-Go Criteria from Customer Discovery

| Outcome | Threshold | Decision |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| Strong GO | 4/5 interviews show strong positive signals; 2+ offer to beta test; 1+ offers deposit | Proceed to prototype |
| Conditional GO | 3/5 show moderate-to-strong interest; concerns are addressable (price, proof) | Proceed with caution; address concerns first |
| NO-GO | 3/5 show negative signals; "my DO meter is fine" is dominant response | Pivot or abandon |

## Outreach Plan

### Phase 1: Warm Introductions (Weeks 1-2)

**Channels:**
1. **Brewers Association / Craft Brewers Conference**: Largest US craft brewing event (10,000+ attendees). Networking opportunity for face-to-face introductions. Next CBC: typically May/June.

2. **Master Brewers Association of the Americas (MBAA)**: Technical brewing organization. Members are QC-focused. District meetings are smaller and more accessible.

3. **LinkedIn**: Brewing professionals are active on LinkedIn. Direct outreach to head brewers and QC managers with a compelling hook.

4. **Local brewers guilds**: Oregon Brewers Guild, Vermont Brewers Association, Massachusetts Brewers Guild, Colorado Brewers Guild. Attend technical meetings.

5. **University connections**: UC Davis Brewing Science program, Oregon State Fermentation Science, Siebel Institute alumni network.

### Phase 2: Cold Outreach Campaign (Weeks 2-4)

**Email template:**

Subject: Quick question about staling detection at [Brewery Name]

---

Hi [Name],

I'm researching a new approach to measuring beer freshness — specifically, detecting the free radicals that cause staling in real-time (30 seconds, handheld device), rather than relying on dissolved oxygen as a proxy.

The technology is based on miniaturized EPR spectroscopy (the gold-standard lab technique for radical detection, shrunk to handheld size).

I'm interviewing 10 QC-focused craft breweries to understand whether this would genuinely solve a problem or whether current DO monitoring is sufficient.

Would you have 20 minutes for a call this week or next? I'm not selling anything — purely gathering honest feedback on the concept.

[Name]

---

**Target list:** 25 breweries (to yield 10+ conversations at ~40% response rate)

### Phase 3: Conference Presence (Months 2-4)

| Event | Date (typical) | Approach |
|-------|----------------|----------|
| Craft Brewers Conference (CBC) | May | Attend; network at QC seminars; host informal meetup |
| MBAA Annual Conference | October | Present technical poster on EPR for brewing QC |
| GABF (Great American Beer Festival) | September | Network with brewers; not a technical venue but good relationship-building |
| Local guild technical meetings | Monthly | Present concept; gather feedback; recruit beta testers |

### Phase 4: Beta Program Recruitment (Months 3-5)

**Beta program offer:**
- Free device for 6 weeks
- Free cartridges (100 included)
- Weekly 15-minute check-in call
- We provide: device, cartridges, support, calibration
- They provide: honest feedback, usage data, permission to compare with their DO readings
- Optional: permission to use their name/quote in marketing (strong commitment signal)

**Target: 10 beta breweries** (expect 5-7 to actually use it regularly)

| **Total 5-year hardware revenue** | | | | **~$7.2M** |
| **Annual consumable revenue (at maturity)** | | | | **~$2M/yr** |

**Confidence: LOW-MEDIUM** — Penetration rates are speculative. The $7-9M total addressable revenue over 5 years suggests this is a $2-3M/year business at maturity, NOT a $50M+ venture-scale opportunity unless adjacencies (wine, spirits, pharma, cooking oils) are successfully penetrated.

### Key Validation Questions for Market Size

1. Is 15% penetration of larger craft breweries realistic? (Requires: strong product-market fit + peer validation + affordable price)
2. Will international markets adopt? (Requires: CE marking, international distribution, localization)
3. Can adjacencies 10x the market? (Requires: separate validation for wine/spirits/food oxidation)
