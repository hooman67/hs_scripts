# Customer Validation Plan: AI Fire Sprinkler Inspection App

## First 5 Target Customers

### Customer 1: Mid-Size Fire Protection Company, Southern California

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Profile** | 12-20 inspectors, $3-8M revenue, primarily commercial sprinkler inspection |
| **Why them** | CA has aggressive fire code enforcement. LA/Orange County commercial real estate boom means more buildings to inspect. Tech-forward market (iPads already in use). High labor costs make efficiency gains more valuable. |
| **Decision maker** | Operations Manager or VP of Service |
| **Pain point** | Cannot hire experienced inspectors fast enough. Junior techs miss deficiencies on first pass, requiring expensive re-inspections. Report writing consumes 40% of inspector time. |
| **Willingness to pay signal** | Already paying for Inspect Point or ServiceTrade (proves SaaS budget exists). Frustrated with current tool's lack of intelligence. |
| **How to find** | NFSA member directory (Southern California chapter). LinkedIn search: "fire protection" + "operations manager" + "California". AFSA (American Fire Sprinkler Association) directory. |

## Discovery Questions

### For Operations Managers (Customers 1-4)

**Understanding Current Process:**
1. Walk me through a typical sprinkler inspection from start to finish. What does your inspector do when they arrive at a building?
2. How long does a typical 100-head inspection take? What about 500+ heads?
3. What percentage of that time is spent on documentation vs. actual inspection?
4. How do your inspectors currently identify sprinkler head types and temperature ratings?
5. What tools/apps do your inspectors currently use in the field?

**Understanding Pain:**
6. What's your biggest operational bottleneck right now?
7. How often do inspectors miss deficiencies that get caught on re-inspection or by AHJs?
8. What does a missed deficiency cost you? (Re-inspection trip, lost contract, liability?)
9. How long does it take to train a new inspector to be "production-ready"?
10. What's your inspector turnover rate? Why do they leave?

**Validating Solution:**
11. If an AI could identify sprinkler head types and flag potential deficiencies from a photo, would your inspectors trust it? Why/why not?
12. What accuracy level would the AI need to achieve before your inspectors would rely on it? (e.g., "if it's right 9 out of 10 times...")
13. Would your inspectors see this as helpful or threatening to their jobs?
14. How much time per inspection would AI need to save to justify a $299/month subscription?
15. Would AI-generated reports with annotated photos help you win contracts over competitors?

**Pricing/Buying:**
16. What do you currently spend per inspector per month on software tools?
17. Who makes the purchasing decision for new field technology?
18. What would make you say "no" to trying this even if it were free for 30 days?
19. If this existed today, would you buy it at $299/inspector/month? What about $199? $399?
20. Would you be willing to participate in a beta program where we provide the app free in exchange for feedback and image data?

### For Solo Inspectors (Customer 5)

1. How many inspections do you do per week? What limits you from doing more?
2. How many hours per week do you spend on report writing?
3. What would you do with 10 extra hours per week?
4. Have you ever missed a deficiency? What happened?
5. Would you trust AI suggestions, or would you feel it undermines your expertise?
6. What would you pay for a tool that cuts your report time in half?

## Demand Signals to Look For

### Strong Positive Signals (Validate Market)

| Signal | Where to Find | Significance |
|--------|--------------|--------------|
| "I'd pay for that today" | Customer interviews | Direct purchase intent |
| Asking when it launches | Customer interviews | Urgency and unmet need |
| Offering to be beta tester | Customer interviews | Willingness to invest time = real pain |
| Willing to share training images | Customer interviews | Solves cold-start problem AND validates partnership potential |
| Currently hiring inspectors (multiple job posts) | Indeed, LinkedIn | Capacity constraint = AI augmentation appeal |
| Complaints about Inspect Point's lack of intelligence | Reddit, forums, reviews | Gap confirmation from users of incumbent |
| Insurance companies requiring better documentation | Industry news, customer interviews | External forcing function driving adoption |
| AHJs rejecting reports as insufficient | Customer interviews | Pain that automation directly solves |

### Weak/Negative Signals (Reconsider)

| Signal | Where to Find | Significance |
|--------|--------------|--------------|
| "My inspectors won't use apps" | Customer interviews | Technology resistance may be too high |
| "We already have Inspect Point and it's fine" | Customer interviews | Satisfaction with current tooling |
| "AI can't replace experience" (dismissive) | Customer interviews | Trust barrier may be fundamental |
| "NFPA 25 is too complex for AI" | Domain experts | Scope may be larger than estimated |
| Very low willingness to pay (<$100/mo) | Customer interviews | Value perception too low for SaaS economics |
| "We'd need it to be perfect" (zero tolerance for errors) | Customer interviews | Unrealistic accuracy expectations |

## Outreach Plan

### Phase 1: Warm Introductions (Weeks 1-2)

**Target:** 10 conversations, expect 3-5 to convert to deep interviews

| Channel | Action | Expected Response Rate |
|---------|--------|----------------------|
| LinkedIn (direct outreach) | Connect with 30 fire protection operations managers. Message: "Researching how AI might help with sprinkler inspection efficiency. Would you spare 20 minutes for a call? Not selling anything -- just learning." | 15-20% response rate |
| NFSA local chapter meetings | Attend next meeting in nearest metro area. Introduce yourself as "building AI tools for fire protection." Ask for 1-on-1 follow-ups. | High -- in-person is best for this industry |
| Fire protection subreddits/forums | Post: "Building an AI tool for sprinkler inspection -- would love to hear from inspectors about their biggest pain points." | Variable -- may get 5-10 responses |
| Industry contacts | Ask any existing network connections in construction/facilities for intros to fire protection people | Highest quality leads |

### Phase 2: Structured Interviews (Weeks 3-4)

**Target:** 5 deep interviews (45-60 minutes each) with decision-makers at target profiles

| Activity | Details |
|----------|---------|
| Schedule | Book 45-min video calls with respondents from Phase 1 |
| Format | Semi-structured interview using discovery questions above |
| Recording | Ask permission to record (for notes, not sharing) |
| Incentive | $50 Amazon gift card for their time (optional -- fire protection folks often happy to talk about their work) |
| Deliverable | Interview notes, key quotes, pain severity ratings |

### Phase 3: Prototype Demo & Feedback (Weeks 5-8)

**Target:** Show working prototype to 5 interviewees, get purchase commitment signals

| Activity | Details |
|----------|---------|
| Build demo | Even a non-functional prototype (screen recording of expected workflow) is valuable. Or: take photos of sprinkler heads, run through ML model manually, show results. |
| Demo format | 15-minute Zoom showing: (1) camera pointed at sprinkler head, (2) AI detection overlay, (3) generated report page |
| Key question | "If this worked as shown, would you sign up for a pilot program?" |
| Commitment ladder | Free beta → paid beta ($99/mo) → launch pricing ($299/mo). See where they drop off. |

### Phase 4: Beta Recruitment (Weeks 9-12)

**Target:** 3-5 companies committed to beta program

| Offer | Details |
|-------|---------|
| What they get | Free access to beta app for 3 months |
| What you get | Real-world usage data, labeled images (with permission), feedback, testimonials |
| Agreement | Simple MOU: they provide feedback + allow image use for training. You provide free access + priority support. |
| Selection criteria | Must have iPads, do 10+ inspections/month, willing to provide weekly feedback |

## Kill Criteria (When to Stop)

Stop pursuing this idea if ANY of the following are true after 4 weeks of outreach:

1. **Cannot get interviews:** <3 conversations out of 30 outreach attempts (indicates market is unreachable or uninterested)
2. **No pain confirmation:** Interviewees rate inspection pain <5/10 (indicates problem is not severe enough)
3. **Universal AI distrust:** 4/5 inspectors say they would never trust AI for deficiency detection (indicates fundamental adoption barrier)
4. **Cannot get training data:** Zero companies willing to share inspection images (indicates cold-start problem is unsolvable)
5. **Price ceiling too low:** No one will pay >$100/month/inspector (indicates SaaS economics don't work)
