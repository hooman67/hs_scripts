## Why This Cycle Failed

1. **Construction tech is a graveyard of innovation**: Both construction-related ideas (modular bricks, metallic glass motors for buildings) failed at Step 4 due to: building code certification timelines (2-5 years per country), extreme industry conservatism, long sales cycles, and high capital requirements. Katerra's $3B failure is the cautionary tale.

2. **Environmental/sustainability sensors are a crowded space**: Wildfire detection (Dryad Networks, Pano AI) and miniature spectrometers (Spectral Engines, Si-Ware) both rejected at Step 2 due to well-funded existing players.

3. **Energy/materials innovations are too early**: Battery tech, biogas, and plastic-to-fuel all rejected at Step 1 or Step 2 for long timelines or incumbent dominance.

4. **University-stage hardware needs 2-3 more years**: Metallic glass motors (Saarland), DUV stickers (Hanyang), photothermal panels (UMass) — all interesting science but 24-36 months from even a prototype suitable for customer deployment.

---

## Key Patterns Learned

- **Construction industry should be avoided entirely**: Certification timelines, conservatism, long sales cycles, and capital intensity make it incompatible with startup economics (under $3M, under 18 months).
- **Hardware-heavy environmental sensors need a platform play**: The successful approach is building on existing sensor platforms (like Cycle 005's satellite IoT play), not creating new sensor hardware.
- **"EU mandates" ≠ market pull**: Regulatory tailwinds are necessary but not sufficient. If the industry is structurally resistant to change, mandates take 5-10 years to create actual purchase orders.
