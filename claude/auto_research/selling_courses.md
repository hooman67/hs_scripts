# Selling AI / Engineering Courses — Viability Research

**Decision date:** 2026-09-10  
**Founder:** Hooman Shariati  
**Constraints:** $5K–$30K/month target; 10–20 hours/week; $25K–$150K maximum capital; willing to teach under his real name and on video; may truthfully reference Adobe employment and general enterprise-AI experience, but never clients, confidential information, internal systems, or proprietary details.

## Bottom line

**Conditional GO — but as a premium, live training business, not as a passive prerecorded-course business.**

The best version is a narrow course for engineering leaders who must ship reliable production AI, sold first as a **paid live workshop**, then as a four-week cohort, and finally as a **corporate workshop/license**. LinkedIn should be the audience-building and outbound channel; Maven can provide course infrastructure and some discovery. LinkedIn Learning, Udemy, YouTube, Pluralsight, O'Reilly, and Coursera should not be the core revenue model.

- **$5K/month:** credible after validation. One $7.5K corporate half-day every 4–6 weeks, or 8–10 cohort seats at $750–$1,250, can clear it before taxes. **[ASSUMED; model, not a platform promise]**
- **$10K–$15K/month:** possible, but requires repeatable corporate distribution or a warm professional audience. This is a small expert-training business, not passive income. **[ASSUMED]**
- **$30K/month:** a bull case, not a responsible base case within 10–20 hours/week. It would require roughly two $12.5K corporate workshops plus product sales monthly, or 25–30 premium cohort seats every month. **[ASSUMED]**
- **Prerecorded marketplace royalties alone:** unlikely to reliably reach even $5K/month. Udemy's marketplace economics are price-sensitive, LinkedIn Learning/Pluralsight/O'Reilly are selective and do not publish enough compensation data to underwrite the target, and Coursera is primarily a partner-institution channel. **[VERIFIED for platform structures; ASSUMED for Hooman's outcome]**

The fastest honest test is **not filming a course**. Presell a 90-minute live workshop, “The Engineering Leader's Production-AI Readiness Review,” at $249 per individual or $2,500 for a private team. Build only after at least eight individuals from four employers pay, or one company pays for a private session.

## What is actually being sold

Generic “learn AI,” prompt-engineering, model-news, and framework walkthrough courses are already commodities. The defensible product is a costly work outcome:

> Help an engineering manager turn an AI prototype into a production launch plan—complete with eval strategy, reliability gates, cost controls, incident readiness, ownership, and a 30/60/90-day roadmap.

This positioning uses the uncommon intersection in Hooman's background: AI infrastructure + generative models + management of real engineering delivery. It does **not** depend on disclosing anything about Adobe's implementations.

The durable assets are the diagnostic, templates, exercises, judgment, feedback, alumni network, and corporate relationships. Recorded lectures are supporting material, not the product.

## Why demand exists—and why that does not guarantee a business

Demand for AI capability is real. The World Economic Forum's 2025 employer survey ranks AI and big data as the fastest-growing skills and says 39% of workers' existing skill sets are expected to be transformed or become outdated by 2030. **[VERIFIED]** [WEF, Future of Jobs Report 2025](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/digest/)

At the same time, learners need help distinguishing demos from reliable systems. Stack Overflow's 2025 developer survey found more developers distrust AI-tool accuracy than trust it, even while use is widespread. That supports training in evaluation, reliability, and engineering judgment rather than another tool tutorial. **[VERIFIED]** [Stack Overflow 2025 Developer Survey — AI](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai/)

Supply is also intense:

- Maven's live catalog currently shows **6 AI-evals cohort courses plus 70 free lessons** and **12 LLM-operations cohort courses plus 137 free lessons**. **[VERIFIED; catalog counts observed 2026-09-10]** [Maven AI Evals for Engineers](https://maven.com/courses/for-engineers/ai-evals), [Maven LLM Operations](https://maven.com/courses/for-engineers/llm-ops)
- Existing specialist courses have strong credentials and premium prices: “AI Evals for Engineers & PMs” lists $4,200 and 500 students; “Beyond Evals” lists $2,999; “Build AI Agents for Enterprises” lists $950. **[VERIFIED; displayed prices observed 2026-09-10]** [AI Evals for Engineers & PMs](https://maven.com/parlance-labs/evals), [Beyond Evals](https://maven.com/aishwarya-kiriti/evals-problem-first), [Build AI Agents for Enterprises](https://maven.com/skanda/ai-agents-enterprises)

Therefore “AI is growing” is not a go-to-market plan. Hooman must own a **specific buyer, job-to-be-done, and distribution path**.

### Important platform conclusion

Platforms solve **hosting, checkout, and sometimes credibility**. None reliably solves Hooman's core risk: getting a narrowly defined buyer to pay. Even Maven—the strongest fit—advertises average first-year instructor earnings of only $10K. The plan must work when platform discovery contributes zero.

## Recommended first product

### Offer

**The Engineering Leader's Production-AI Readiness Review**

**Audience:** engineering managers and directors at 50–2,000-person software or digitally enabled companies that have at least one generative-AI prototype but no trusted production launch process. **[ASSUMED ICP; validate]**

**90-minute paid pilot outcome:** each participant scores one project across:

1. use-case and failure-cost clarity;
2. offline and online evaluation;
3. data/privacy/security boundaries;
4. latency, cost, and capacity;
5. observability and incident response;
6. human override and ownership;
7. staged rollout and stop conditions.

They leave with a one-page readiness scorecard and the top three gaps to close. Use public or synthetic examples only.

**Pilot price:** $249/person; 10-seat cap. Private-team alternative: $2,500 for up to 12 people. **[ASSUMED]** After two successful pilots, move to $399/person / $5K private. A four-week cohort can later be $1,200 early-bird / $1,500 standard. Corporate half/full-day target after proof: $7.5K/$12.5K. **[ASSUMED; pricing must be tested]**

### Why this wedge instead of a full course

- Buyer feedback arrives in days, before 60–100 hours of recording.
- Live diagnosis and feedback are harder for free videos or an LLM to replace.
- Every pilot exposes the vocabulary, objections, and artifacts needed for the eventual course.
- The same artifact can become an assessment, lead magnet, cohort backbone, and corporate sales tool.
- Corporate buyers pay for shared team alignment and reduced project risk, not hours of video.

## Customer acquisition: the real work

### The initial funnel

1. **Warm-network interviews:** 15 engineering leaders outside Adobe's customer/confidential context.
2. **LinkedIn proof:** two useful posts per week for four weeks—one diagnostic, one failure pattern—with a neutral disclaimer that views are personal.
3. **Direct invitations:** 30–50 carefully selected contacts receive a personal note asking for feedback or a paid seat; no automated spam.
4. **One free 30-minute briefing:** deliver the diagnostic's first layer and offer the paid workshop. Capture opt-in email.
5. **Paid workshop:** collect completion, rating, before/after confidence, artifacts produced, and permissioned testimonials.
6. **Corporate conversion:** offer attendees a private team session with tailored exercises, not merely a bundle of seats.

### Funnel math to test

To fill 10 seats at $249:

- 50 genuinely relevant warm/direct contacts × 20% paid conversion = 10 seats; or
- 200 qualified landing-page visitors × 5% = 10 seats. **[ASSUMED]**

For a $1,200 cohort, assume only 1–3% of qualified email subscribers buy on a launch until Hooman has evidence otherwise. Filling 12 seats may require 400–1,200 qualified subscribers, or a much smaller warm network with high trust. **[ASSUMED]** Do not model followers as leads or waitlist signups as customers.

### Acquisition cost guardrails

- Do not buy ads for the first two launches.
- After proof, paid customer-acquisition cost must remain below **20% of first-purchase revenue** unless corporate follow-on value is measured.
- Include refunds, failed payments, platform fees, contractor support, captioning, and software in contribution margin.
- Record source for every buyer: warm introduction, LinkedIn post, direct message, Maven discovery, partner, or paid channel.

## 30-day action sequence

### Days 1–3: clear the boundary

- Review current Adobe outside-business, conflict-of-interest, confidentiality, invention-assignment, brand/social-media, and approval rules. Seek written internal clearance if required. **Do this before using the Adobe name in a sales page.**
- Create a clean-room rule: personal equipment, accounts, repositories, datasets, slides, and time only.
- Select the single ICP and promise above; do not build multiple courses.

### Days 4–10: interview and presell

- Conduct 15 structured 20-minute interviews with non-Adobe engineering leaders.
- Ask about the last production-AI launch, failure costs, training budget owner, prior training, desired artifact, and whether they would pay $249 personally or $2,500 as a team.
- Publish a one-page checkout/landing page with date, deliverable, who it is not for, refund terms, and instructor disclaimer.
- Invite 30–50 relevant warm contacts. Charge immediately or collect a refundable deposit; “interested” does not count.

### Days 11–21: build only what sold

- If the payment gate clears, build the scorecard, 25–35 slides, synthetic case, workbook, and two exercises.
- Deliver live; record only with explicit attendee consent.
- Collect before/after measures, rating, NPS, completion, the artifact produced, and testimonial permission.

### Days 22–30: decide

- Offer a second workshop and private-team version within 48 hours.
- Interview buyers and non-buyers.
- Choose one: proceed to a four-week cohort, keep it as a monthly workshop, reposition once, or kill.
- Only now apply to Maven and LinkedIn Learning or buy a course platform subscription.

## Days 31–90

- Run at most **two more paid iterations** of the same promise.
- Publish six to eight derivative LinkedIn posts and two short YouTube clips from material already created; every piece points to an email list or next session.
- Ask every satisfied attendee for one introduction to an L&D leader, VP Engineering, or engineering director.
- Sell three private-team pilots; target at least one paid corporate booking by day 60 and a repeat/referral by day 90. **[ASSUMED target]**
- Package the validated version as a four-week cohort only if buyers request deeper implementation.
- Hire part-time production/admin help only after cumulative collected revenue exceeds $10K; protect Hooman's time for teaching, IP, and sales conversations.
- Apply selectively to LinkedIn Learning, O'Reilly, and Pluralsight using the paid workshop recording and learner outcomes as proof. Treat acceptance as bonus distribution.

## Kill and pivot criteria

Kill the course line—or pause it for six months—if any of these occurs:

1. **Payment failure:** after 30 qualified interviews and two distinct presale windows, fewer than eight individuals total pay at least $199 and no company pays at least $2,500.
2. **Weak outcome:** after the second delivery, average rating is below 4.3/5, NPS below 40, completion below 80%, or fewer than 70% finish the promised readiness artifact.
3. **No B2B bridge:** no paid private-team engagement, credible procurement process, or qualified corporate referral by day 90.
4. **Time failure:** the third delivery still needs more than 20 hours of preparation/admin per event, or total workload exceeds 20 hours/week for four consecutive weeks.
5. **Economics failure:** direct acquisition cost exceeds 20% of collected revenue, refund rate exceeds 10%, or cash contribution before Hooman's labor stays below 60% after the third run.
6. **Employer/IP conflict:** Adobe does not approve the outside activity or the necessary positioning cannot be separated cleanly from confidential/proprietary work. Stop; do not work around the restriction.

Allow **one** positioning pivot based on interview evidence—for example from generic production AI to engineering directors managing agent reliability. Do not respond to failed demand by recording more content or adding platforms.

## Legal, IP, and conflict-of-interest guardrails

This is operational risk guidance, not legal advice.

### Must verify before selling

- **Outside-business approval and conflicts:** current Adobe policies and Hooman's agreements are not in this research file. Their exact requirements are **UNVERIFIED**. Obtain the applicable policy/approval in writing before launch.
- **Employer identification:** “Engineering manager at Adobe” can be a factual biographical line only if policy permits. Add “Views are my own; this course is independent and is not sponsored or endorsed by Adobe.” Never place Adobe in the course title, logo, domain, or implied credential.
- **Clean-room creation:** no Adobe laptop, accounts, paid time, repositories, prompts, datasets, metrics, slides, screenshots, architecture diagrams, customer examples, vendor contracts, incidents, or internal terminology. Use public and synthetic examples.
- **Invention assignment:** California Labor Code §2870 limits assignment of qualifying employee inventions created entirely on the employee's own time without employer resources, but it has exceptions for work related to the employer's business or resulting from work performed for the employer. It is not a blanket exemption. **[VERIFIED statute; application to Hooman is UNVERIFIED]** [California Labor Code §2870](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=2870.&lawCode=LAB)
- **No confidential “war stories”:** abstraction or anonymization may still disclose strategy or enable re-identification. If an example originated at work, do not use it; construct a clean synthetic case instead.
- **Teaching agreements:** check exclusivity, reuse rights, ownership of raw recordings, name/image licenses, takedown rights, refunds, tax collection, and whether learner contact data can be exported before accepting any platform or corporate contract.

### Normal business hygiene

- Form an appropriate entity and separate bank/accounting stack after the first paid validation, with CPA/legal advice.
- Use terms covering educational—not professional or guaranteed-results—services, refunds/rescheduling, acceptable behavior, recording, and ownership of templates.
- Obtain consent before recording participants; provide captions/transcripts and accessible materials.
- License all code, screenshots, fonts, images, music, datasets, and third-party course excerpts.
- Disclose affiliate/vendor compensation clearly. The FTC provides disclosure guidance for social-media endorsements. **[VERIFIED]** [FTC Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)
- For corporate training, avoid receiving or retaining the client's confidential project data; use their redacted example or a synthetic case.

## What not to do

- Do not spend 60–100 hours filming before paid validation.
- Do not launch “AI for everyone,” prompting 101, ChatGPT tips, generic agent-building, or a tool-specific course.
- Do not count Maven/Udemy/LinkedIn discovery as the customer-acquisition strategy.
- Do not use YouTube ad revenue in the business case.
- Do not claim “enterprise-proven” methods unless every example is publicly supportable and independently created.
- Do not advertise an Adobe affiliation as endorsement.
- Do not build separate courses for AI, cloud, management, and electrical engineering simultaneously.
- Do not infer willingness to pay from likes, poll votes, free-webinar attendance, or waitlist signups.

## Final recommendation

Run one **30-day, under-$2K validation sprint** for “The Engineering Leader's Production-AI Readiness Review.” Use LinkedIn and personal professional relationships for distribution, Maven or a simple checkout/Zoom stack for delivery, and a paid threshold rather than a waitlist.

If it clears the payment and outcome gates, evolve it in this order:

1. repeatable live workshop;
2. private corporate workshop;
3. four-week cohort;
4. reusable templates and selected self-paced modules;
5. optional LinkedIn Learning/O'Reilly/Pluralsight exposure.

That sequence preserves the strongest economics and learns from buyers before content production. The evidence does **not** support treating Udemy royalties, YouTube ads, or platform discovery as a credible path to the stated income target.
