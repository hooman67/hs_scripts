## 2. SDE bridge & the manager add-back — THE deal-decider

This is where the new detail overturns the category file's conservative assumption. The category file
(written before the detail page was parsed) assumed you subtract a **full $100–110K CA foreman/GM
wage** from the $220K SDE. The listing detail shows that is **too harsh for L1** — and correctly harsh
for L2.

### The margin-ratio tell (diligence alpha)
| Deal | Rev | SDE | **SDE % of rev** | Read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L1** | $868,553 | $220,000 | **25.3%** | **Healthy, delegable-shaped.** A maintenance company with paid crews + 68% GM + 25% SDE margin is earning a *business* return, not paying the owner for manual labor. |
| **L2** | ~$744,000 | $341,600 | **45.9%** | **Owner-sweat trap.** A 46% SDE margin on a labor business means the "profit" is largely the owner's own uncompensated field/design labor. Subtract a real wage and it collapses. |

A 25% SDE margin on a route/maintenance service business is exactly the profile of a company where
the owner is doing *management*, not *the work* — consistent with the "20 hrs/week + self-managing
crews" claim. A 46% margin is not.

### L1 add-back scenarios (all magnitudes ASSUMED; ratios VERIFIED)
The owner's role is **~20 hrs/week of white-collar work**: scheduling, client comms, design. That is
the entire management add-back — there is no owner-in-the-field labor to replace. So:

- **Scenario A — Hooman operates it himself (20 hrs/wk).** This is highly compatible with keeping the
  Adobe day job at first (scheduling/client comms are async/evening-able; design is the one piece that
  may need daytime presence). SDE stays ~**$220K**. If you charge his own labor at a *pro-rated* GM
  rate (~$110K full-time GM × ½ time ≈ **$55K**), the **owner-independent SDE ≈ $165K**, and
  **all-cash yield ≈ $165K / $690K ≈ 24%** (~$13.7K/mo of true return on capital, on top of the
  ~$55K notional wage). This is a genuinely good all-cash number and clears the $5–30K/mo target.
- **Scenario B — fully delegate (hire a GM).** A full-time CA landscape GM/operations manager runs
  **~$81–118K in the Bay Area** (VERIFIED wage data: Glassdoor $81K CA foreman / Comparably $118.7K
  San Jose foreman / Salary.com ~$86K). Because the role is only 20 hrs/wk of management today, a
  **$70–100K office/operations manager** almost certainly covers it (be conservative and model $100K).
  **Post-manager SDE ≈ $220K − $100K = $120K**, **all-cash yield ≈ $120K / $690K ≈ 17.4%** (~$10K/mo,
  fully hands-off). **The SDE SURVIVES the add-back.** This is the pass/fail test, and L1 passes.

### Contrast — L2 fails the same test
$341.6K SDE − a $100K manager (and L2 already lists an "operations manager," so the owner's $341K is
*on top of* a paid manager — meaning the owner is doing large personal billable labor/design) → the
honest delegable SDE is plausibly **$150–220K on a smaller $744K revenue base**, and the 46% margin
says a big chunk is the seller's own hands. **L2 is cheaper (1.3x) for exactly the reason the project's
P8/P9 learning warns about: you're buying one person's unreplaceable sweat, not an annuity.** Do not
be seduced by the low multiple.

**Verdict on the decider:** **L1's $220K SDE substantially survives a realistic manager add-back**
(down to ~$120K fully-delegated / ~$165K if Hooman keeps the 20 hrs). Owner-independent all-cash yield
of **~17–24%** on a durable recurring book is a real, fundable secondary income. **This is the single
strongest fact in the deal** and it is the opposite of the L2/L3 trap.

## 4. California cost structure & the C-27 gate

- **Workers' compensation — the CA-specific margin compressor (VERIFIED rates).** Landscaping is NCCI
  **class code 0042**. California median **~$5.41 per $100 of payroll**, typical range **$5.50–$13.00**
  vs. a **$4.39 national average** — CA is materially above national, as expected. On 8 employees with
  an assumed ~$350–450K field payroll, workers' comp is roughly **$20–35K/yr**. **This is already
  inside the reported SDE** (it's an operating expense), so it doesn't re-haircut the number — but
  **pull the actual policy + EMR (experience mod)**: an EMR >1.0 signals injury history and higher
  future premiums; a clean EMR (<0.9) is a quiet asset.
- **Wages (VERIFIED Bay Area data):** landscape laborers/foremen are expensive here — foreman
  ~$81–118K, laborers well above other states' rates. This is why CA SDE margins are structurally
  thinner than the OKC/TX exemplars, and it is *already reflected* in L1's 25% margin. The risk is
  **wage inflation + CA minimum-wage / overtime / meal-break compliance** (PAGA exposure is a real CA
  litigation tail for field-labor businesses) → confirm timekeeping and pay-stub compliance in diligence.
- **C-27 Landscape Contractor license — THE structural gate (VERIFIED CSLB rules, ASSUMED for this co.):**
  Work over $500 requires a **C-27 license** held via a qualifying individual (the owner as
  **RMO/sole owner-qualifier**, or an **RME** employee). **Two hard facts Hooman must internalize:**
  1. **A license does NOT automatically transfer** when a business is sold. On a change of ownership,
     the new entity generally needs its own qualifier.
  2. **To personally qualify, CSLB requires ~4 years of journey-level landscaping experience** — which
     **Hooman does not have and cannot manufacture on a deal timeline.** So he **cannot be his own
     C-27 qualifier at close.**
  → **The only workable paths:** (a) **retain the seller as RMO/RME** through a transition period
  (12–24 mo), or (b) **promote/retain a licensed foreman as RME**, or (c) hire a qualifier. An RME must
  be a **bona fide employee** (working ≥32 hrs/wk or ≥80% of the business's operating hours) — you can't
  rent a name part-time. **This is directly analogous to the pest "qualifying-manager Operator"
  dependency flagged in the category file, and it is a genuine key-person risk.** **Kill-check:** who
  holds the current C-27, will they stay as qualifier, and is there a second licensed employee as backup?
- **Customer & geographic concentration — UNVERIFIED, must confirm:** premium residential across an
  East-Bay service area is usually **well-diversified** (hundreds of small accounts, no single client
  >a few %) — a *strength* vs. a commercial book with 2–3 anchor contracts. Confirm no single customer
  >10% and that the route geography is dense (dense = margin + Hooman's optimization lever; sprawled =
  windshield time eats the SDE).
- **Crew retention risk (the real daily grind):** 8 long-tenured staff is a plus, but CA field-labor
  turnover + wage pressure is the #1 operational risk and **no software fixes it** (LEARNINGS: "hiring/
  retaining CA field crews is raw exposure"). Confirm crew tenure, comp, and whether key foremen sign
  retention/non-compete-adjacent agreements at close.

## 5. Hooman-fit — where his software edge is a REAL lever vs. irrelevant

**REAL levers (margin, not vanity):**
- **Route-density / scheduling optimization — the highest-value lever.** 8 staff across an East-Bay
  maintenance route: denser day-routes = more stops/crew/day = direct margin, and it is the *one* lever
  that fights CA's high labor cost. A 3-decade paper/QuickBooks shop almost certainly schedules by
  habit, not optimization. Realistic, defensible upside.
- **Churn / renewal prediction + save campaigns.** The whole asset is a retention annuity. An at-risk-
  account model + proactive save outreach protects the LTV that IS the business. Direct fit.
- **Dispatch + review-generation + local-SEO automation.** Turning a referral-only shop into one that
  systematically harvests Google reviews lowers CAC and feeds the "turning away business" demand. Cheap,
  high-ROI, squarely his skill set.
- **The owner's 20-hr/wk white-collar role IS automatable** — scheduling and client comms are exactly
  what software compresses, which is what makes the delegation math (§2, Scenario B) *better* over time,
  not just at close.

**IRRELEVANT / raw exposure (no algorithm helps):**
- **Hiring/retaining CA crews, workers'-comp/PAGA compliance, the C-27 qualifier dependency, and
  physical local presence.** These are the core of the job and consume evening/focus hours — the
  opposite of a remote SaaS. This is hands-on NorCal operating.
- **The owner's DESIGN function** (the project-revenue rainmaker) is a *taste/relationship* skill, not
  a software problem; if project revenue matters, Hooman must either learn/hire that or let project
  revenue fade to the maintenance core (which is fine — the maintenance core is the durable asset).

**Honest hours/week:** **~15–25 hrs/wk** to run it himself in the owner's current shape (scheduling,
client comms, vendor/crew management), spiking during transition and hiring events. This is
**day-job-compatible at first** if crews truly self-manage — but plan for the first 6–12 months to be
heavier (learning the trade, the C-27 transition, meeting every key client and crew lead). It is **not**
5 hrs/wk passive. If fully delegated to a GM (§2 Scenario B), owner oversight drops to ~5–10 hrs/wk at
the cost of ~$100K of the SDE.

## 6. Downside + capital fit

**Capital fit:** $690K ask vs. ~$500K cash → **$190K gap**. All-cash-only, no SBA/PG. Structures:
- **$500K cash down + ~$190K seller note** (say 5–7 yr, 6–8%, ~$3–3.7K/mo P&I). A **retirement seller
  is the ideal note-holder**, and a seller note is also a **quality signal + alignment** (they only get
  paid if the book holds). This is the base-case structure. Note service (~$40K/yr) comes out of SDE →
  cash-on-cash on the $500K equity is *higher* than the unlevered yield (seller-note leverage), but the
  note is a fixed claim ahead of Hooman's return — model it.
- **Negotiate the price.** At 3.14x for a business with a real C-27/qualifier dependency and an
  ASSUMED-not-VERIFIED recurring %, **~2.6–2.8x ($570–620K) is a defensible target**, which also
  shrinks the note toward what $500K cash can nearly cover.
- **Reserve:** even at $500K down + note, keep **$50–100K working-capital/transition reserve** for
  payroll timing, a crew-retention bonus pool, and the qualifier arrangement. If the full $500K goes to
  the down payment with no reserve, that's a **structural fail** — don't do it.

**Downside scenarios:**
- **Base case:** book holds, ~17–24% owner-involved yield, ~$10–14K/mo. Meets the target.
- **Bear case — key-person unwind:** seller's design/client relationships + C-27 qualifier leave, a
  foreman quits and takes accounts (CA non-competes are largely unenforceable → **route/customer
  non-solicit + retention bonuses are the only protection**), 2–3 top clients churn. Recurring base
  could drop 15–30%, SDE toward ~$120–150K *before* the manager cost — thin but not fatal, because the
  maintenance annuity is sticky and diversified.
- **Worst case:** you overpay on headline SDE, the recurring % turns out to be ~40% (project-heavy), the
  design revenue evaporates with the seller, and you're left running a labor business at CA cost with a
  note to service. This is the **L2 outcome imported into L1** — avoidable purely by verifying the
  recurring split and the manager add-back *before* signing.
