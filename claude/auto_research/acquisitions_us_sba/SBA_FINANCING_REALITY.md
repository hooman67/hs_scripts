## 1. SBA 7(a) for a Business Acquisition — current terms (2026)

The 7(a) is the standard vehicle for buying an existing operating business (a "change of ownership").

| Parameter | Current value | Confidence / source |
|---|---|---|
| **Max loan amount** | **$5,000,000** (SBA-guaranteed 7(a) cap) | VERIFIED — sba.gov/funding-programs/loans/7a-loans |
| **Down payment / equity injection** | **Minimum 10% of total project cost** (price + closing + working capital), for a full change of ownership, under SOP 50 10 8 (eff. 6/1/2025) | VERIFIED — SOP 50 10 8; regaliscapital, starfieldsmith, whitefordlaw alerts |
| **Seller-note-as-equity** | A seller note may count for **at most HALF the required injection = 5% of project cost**, and ONLY if on **FULL STANDBY (zero principal AND interest) for the entire loan term (~10 yrs)**. The buyer must still inject **≥5% of project cost in genuine, non-borrowed cash.** The old 24-month standby rule is **obsolete.** | VERIFIED — SOP 50 10 8; regaliscapital seller-note guide, commerciallendingx |
| **Interest rate** | Variable, **Prime + spread**; prime ≈ **6.75%** (2026), spread capped at **+2.75%** for loans >$50K with terms >7 yrs → **~9.5%**, though many acquisition loans price **~9.5–11.5% APR** all-in | VERIFIED — SBA rate caps; guidant/baystreet/ct-acquisitions ranges |
| **Maturity / amortization** | **10 years** for a goodwill/business-only acquisition (no real estate); **up to 25 yrs** if ≥51% of proceeds fund owner-occupied real estate. **No balloon** — fully amortizing. | VERIFIED — ebitcommunity, ct-acquisitions |
| **Personal guarantee** | **Unlimited PG required from every 20%+ owner.** SBA takes a **lien on personal real estate (the home) when the loan is not fully collateralized by business assets** and the owner has available equity. | VERIFIED — SOP 50 10 8 collateral rules; standard 7(a) requirement |
| **DSCR minimum** | **≥ 1.15x** business operating cash flow to debt service (historical and/or projected), plus **≥ 1.0x on a global basis** (business + personal). Lender overlays commonly push to **1.20–1.35x.** | VERIFIED — SOP 50 10 8 OCF/DS rule |
| **SBA guaranty / fees** | SBA guarantees ~75–85%; borrower pays an upfront guaranty fee (~2.77–3.5% of the guaranteed portion for larger loans) — roll into the loan. Closing costs run ~2–4% of price. | ASSUMED — standard 2025 fee schedule; confirm with lender |

**Practical read**: on a $1.5M all-goodwill deal, the *headline* down payment is **$150K (10%)**, of
which **as little as $75K need be his own cash** (the other $75K can be a full-standby seller note),
plus ~$45–60K of closing/guaranty/working-capital costs. **He can control a $1.5M business for
~$150–225K cash in.** [VERIFIED mechanics]

## 2. SBA 504 — for real-estate-heavy deals (when it beats 7(a))

The 504 is a **two-loan structure for owner-occupied real estate and long-life equipment**, not for
goodwill. Relevant to the physical, RE-backed categories the prior search demoted: **self-storage,
car wash with the dirt, laundromat that owns its building.**

| Parameter | 504 value | Confidence / source |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | **50% conventional bank loan / 40% CDC (SBA-backed) / 10% borrower down** | VERIFIED — axiantpartners, fundingfred |
| **Down payment** | **10%** standard; **15%** for a **single-/special-purpose property** (car wash, self-storage, gas station are special-purpose); **20%** if special-purpose AND a startup | VERIFIED (10%); ASSUMED 15%/20% overlay — standard 504 rule, confirm per-deal |
| **CDC portion max** | **$5.0M** ($5.5M for manufacturing / energy-efficient) — total project can be larger since the bank half stacks on top | VERIFIED — SBA 504 limits |
| **Rate** | **Fixed, ~5.9–6.0%** on the CDC portion (Apr 2026: 25-yr fixed ~5.942%, 20-yr ~5.983%) — **below** the ~9.5–11% variable 7(a) | VERIFIED — stacking.capital 2026 |
| **Term** | **25 years** (RE) fully amortizing on the CDC portion | VERIFIED |

**When 504 beats 7(a):** pick 504 when **≥85% of the proceeds go to real estate (or 10+ year
equipment)** and you want a **low, FIXED, 25-year rate.** [VERIFIED — axiantpartners decision tree]

- **Self-storage / car wash / laundromat that INCLUDES the real estate** → **504 wins**: the RE
  collateralizes a 25-yr fixed loan at ~6% vs. 7(a)'s ~10% variable over 10 yrs. Lower payment,
  rate certainty, and the asset is backed by hard collateral (less reliance on the PG/home lien).
- **A pure operating business / goodwill deal, or a mixed deal** → **7(a) wins**: 504 can't fund
  goodwill or working capital, and 7(a) blends RE + goodwill + working capital in one loan.
- **Common hybrid**: 504 for the building + a small 7(a) for goodwill/working capital.

## 3. Purchase-Power Math — what $500K actually buys under 7(a)

Two constraints govern how big a deal he can do. **Whichever is tighter wins.**

**(A) The equity/reserve constraint.** He must inject ≥10% of project cost and should keep a
reserve. His ~$500K cash gives him plenty of room:

| Deal size | 10% equity injection | Min own cash (½ can be standby seller note) + ~3% costs | Reserve left from $500K |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1.5M | $150K | ~$75K cash + ~$45K costs ≈ **$120K** | **~$380K** |
| $1.8M | $180K | ~$90K cash + ~$54K costs ≈ **$144K** | **~$356K** |
| $3.0M | $300K | ~$150K cash + ~$90K costs ≈ **$240K** | ~$260K (thinner) |

Even at $1.8M he keeps a large reserve. Equity is **not** what caps him at $1.5–2M — his own
prudence (reserve + not over-levering) is.

**(B) The DSCR constraint — the bank sizes the loan to cash flow.** The loan must be serviceable at
**≥1.15x** by the business's own cash flow. Debt-service math at **10.5% / 10-yr amortization**
(conservative; ~9.5% is achievable) → **~$0.162 of annual debt service per $1 borrowed.**

Rearranged, the bank will lend up to about **(SDE ÷ 1.15) ÷ 0.162 ≈ 5.4× SDE** at these terms.
Since normally-priced small businesses trade at **~3–3.5× SDE**, DSCR leaves comfortable headroom —
**for a healthy deal, DSCR is not binding.** It only bites on thin-margin, over-priced, or
declining assets (exactly the ones his diligence brain should be killing anyway).

### Worked example 1 — $1.5M price, ~3.5× SDE (SDE ≈ $429K/yr)
- Loan = 90% = **$1.35M**. Monthly P&I @10.5%/120mo ≈ **$18,200/mo → ~$218K/yr** debt service.
- **DSCR = $429K / $218K = 1.97x** — well above 1.15x. Approvable. [VERIFIED math]
- **Owner cash flow AFTER debt service ≈ $429K − $218K = ~$211K/yr pre-tax** (~$17.6K/mo), before
  his own salary/taxes. Cash-on-cash on ~$150–225K cash in = **very high** (the leverage amplifies
  the ~3.5x-SDE yield). [modeled — ASSUMED SDE multiple]

### Worked example 2 — $1.8M price, ~3.5× SDE (SDE ≈ $514K/yr)
- Loan = 90% = **$1.62M**. Monthly P&I @10.5%/120mo ≈ **$21,900/mo → ~$262K/yr** debt service.
- **DSCR = $514K / $262K = 1.96x** — approvable. [VERIFIED math]
- **Owner cash flow after debt ≈ $514K − $262K = ~$252K/yr pre-tax** (~$21K/mo), before salary/taxes.

### Sensitivity — where it gets tight
If the same $1.8M deal were priced richer (say 4.5× → SDE only $400K), debt service $262K →
**DSCR = 1.53x** (still fine). It only fails DSCR near ~6× SDE / thin margins. **Takeaway: at 10%
down the leverage is aggressive; a debt-service hit of ~$18–22K/mo means a revenue shock hurts far
more than in an all-cash deal.** The upside (10x+ cash-on-cash) and the downside (fixed nut that
doesn't shrink when sales dip) are two sides of the same lever. [VERIFIED mechanics]

## 4. The Personal-Guarantee / Capped-Downside Tradeoff — stated plainly

This is the real cost of the leverage, and it directly contradicts a **prior standing preference**:
`US_STATUS_AND_LEARNINGS.md` §5 (learning #5) and `LEARNINGS.md` recorded *"AVOID SBA / personal-
guarantee financing even when it becomes available"* because it destroys the capped, cash-only
downside. **That preference should now be re-weighed as a tradeoff, not treated as a hard rule** —
that is a decision for Hooman (flag it in DECISIONS.md; don't assume he's changed his mind).

**What he can lose:**
- **All-cash deal**: maximum loss = **price − resale/liquidation value.** Downside is *capped at the
  cash he put in.* The house is never at risk.
- **SBA 7(a) deal**: he signs an **unlimited personal guarantee**, and SBA typically takes a **lien
  on his home.** If the business fails and its assets don't cover the loan, he is personally on the
  hook for the **remaining loan balance — up to ~$1.35M (Example 1) or ~$1.62M (Example 2)** — and
  the lender can pursue the home to satisfy it. **He can lose materially MORE than his cash in.**
  [VERIFIED — SBA PG + collateral rules]

**How to frame it (risk to weigh, not a blocker):**
- The PG is the price of turning $500K into ~$1.5–2M of earning power. The question is whether the
  *durability* of the asset justifies exposing the home. His whole edge is diligence — a durable,
  recession-safe, low-technical-obsolescence asset (laundromat-with-RE via 504, entrenched
  recurring-route service, sticky vertical SaaS) is exactly the profile where the PG risk is lowest.
- **Mitigants worth pricing in**: (a) larger equity / lower LTV shrinks the exposed balance; (b)
  **504 on RE-backed deals** is collateralized by the building, reducing reliance on the home lien;
  (c) negotiate a **seller note** to reduce the SBA balance; (d) some states / structures limit home
  exposure — LAWYER territory; (e) key-man / business interruption insurance.
- **The honest asymmetry**: leverage multiplies *both* the ~high cash-on-cash return *and* the
  worst case. All-cash = sleep-at-night, smaller asset. 7(a) = bigger asset, home on the line.
