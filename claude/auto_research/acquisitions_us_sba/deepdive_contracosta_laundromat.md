## 2. The utility-regression diligence plan (Hooman's signature edge — made concrete)

Financials are undisclosed, so **the water/sewer/PG&E bills ARE the audited financials.** Water
volume is the one input a cash-skimming seller cannot fake: every wash cycle consumes a
physically-metered quantity of water that the utility bills independently of whatever the seller
claims the registers took in. This is the highest-leverage $0 test in the whole deal.

### 2.1 The core identity

```
Metered water (gallons/yr, from EBMUD bill)
      ÷  blended gallons-per-cycle (from the machine mix)      →  IMPLIED WASH CYCLES / yr
      ×  blended vend price per wash (from the price board)    →  IMPLIED WASH REVENUE
      ×  (1 + dryer-revenue ratio) [+ vending/WDF]             →  IMPLIED TOTAL REVENUE
                                                    ── compare to ──►  seller's CLAIMED revenue
```

If claimed revenue **materially exceeds** utility-implied revenue → the excess is **either skimmed
cash the seller is inventing, or a claim you're paying a multiple on that isn't real.** If claimed
revenue is **at or below** implied → the numbers are credible (and there may even be understated
cash working in the buyer's favor). Water can only *under*-count real revenue via non-water income
(vending, WDF, ATM) — it essentially never *over*-counts, which makes it a hard **ceiling** on
believable wash revenue.

### 2.2 The three inputs Hooman must obtain (LOI diligence checklist)

1. **12 consecutive months of EBMUD (or local water district) bills** showing **CCF consumed** per
   billing period. (1 CCF = 748 gallons — VERIFIED, standard.) Insist on the utility's own bills,
   not a seller spreadsheet.
2. **12 months of PG&E electric + gas bills** (kWh and therms) — a cross-check on water and the
   direct input to the true utility expense line.
3. **The machine price board + capacity of every washer** (photo of the store + a walk-count).
   Vend prices and capacities set the $/cycle and gal/cycle constants.

### 2.3 The constants (VERIFIED where possible, else conservative ASSUMED)

| Constant | Value used | Confidence / source |

### 2.4 A worked example (ASSUMED illustration — shows the mechanics, not a claim about this store)

Suppose the EBMUD bills show an average of **~200 CCF/month** consumed:

- 200 CCF × 748 = **149,600 gal/month**
- Assume a blended washer ~30 lb capacity (mix of 20–60 lb) at ~**55 gal/cycle** (≈1.8 gal/lb, between modern and conservative):
  - 149,600 ÷ 55 = **~2,720 wash cycles/month**
  - Sanity check: 2,720 ÷ 22 washers ÷ 30 days = **~4.1 turns/day → "healthy"** ✅ (matches a live, real store)
- Wash revenue: 2,720 × ~$4.50 blended vend = **~$12,240/mo**
- Add dryers at ~35% of wash revenue → total **~$16,500/mo ≈ $198K/yr gross** (ASSUMED)

**Now the test:** if the seller *claims* $198K and the water implies $198K → **credible, proceed.**
If the seller claims **$300K** on 200 CCF/month, that would require **either ~6 turns/day at $4.50
(possible but top-quartile — verify the price board and traffic) or skimmed/invented cash.** The
gap between "what water allows" and "what's claimed" is exactly where the fraud or the over-payment
hides. **Run the same regression on electric (kWh/cycle) and gas (therms/cycle for hot-water + dryer
heat) as an independent second and third estimate** — three physical meters that must all corroborate
the same turn count. If water says 4 turns/day but the register tape says 7, the extra 3 aren't real.

### 2.5 How to execute it (concrete, actionable)

1. In the LOI, make **"delivery of 12 months of EBMUD + PG&E bills AND 3 years of tax returns / T-12"**
   a diligence contingency. Undisclosed financials are normal for laundromats; **refusal to produce
   utility bills is a walk-away signal** (utilities can't be gamed, so a clean seller has no reason to withhold).
2. Enter each billing period's CCF, kWh, therms into a spreadsheet. Compute implied cycles three ways
   (water / electric / gas). They should agree within ~15–20%.
3. Take the **lowest** of the three implied-revenue figures as the conservative truth.
4. **Do a live count on two different days** (one weekday evening, one weekend) — physically watch
   turns for an hour, extrapolate. Compare to the utility-implied turns. Cross-check against a
   remote-monitoring read if any machines are networked.
5. Only after utility-implied revenue is in hand do you compute the yield (Section 5). **The claimed
   number is irrelevant until the meters confirm it.**

> **This is the whole deal.** Everything else (lease, equipment, price) is a modifier on a revenue
> number that must first survive the meter. Do not proceed to valuation on any seller-stated figure.

## 3. The lease as life-expectancy (the #1 laundromat killer)

**LEARNINGS + category file are emphatic: the lease is the business's life expectancy.** You don't
own the dirt; a laundromat is a 15–20 year capital commitment (plumbing, drains, machines bolted in)
sitting on a lease. A non-renewal or an option-period rent reset can zero the SDE overnight.

**What B1 has (VERIFIED from listing):** lease **expires 10/31/2029** (~3.3 yrs of committed term
left as of mid-2026) **+ a stated 10-year renewal option.** This is the **best lease structure on the
Bay Area shelf** — B2 Pinole's extension is merely "negotiable," B3 Oakland's term is "unspecified,"
B5's is a weaker 5-yr option. B1's 10-yr option, *if real and exercisable on capped terms*, gives a
~13-yr total runway that comfortably covers the equipment's remaining life.

**But "a 10-yr option" is worth $0 until you read the actual clause.** Verify in the executed lease:

| Must verify in writing | Why it matters | Confidence today |
|---|---|---|
| **Is the option exercisable at the tenant's sole election?** | An option "subject to landlord consent" or "at then-market rent TBD" is not an option — it's a re-negotiation. | UNVERIFIED — not seen |
| **The rent-escalation clause during the current term AND the option period** | At Bay Area rent-to-revenue of 23–32%, a single 3–5%/yr escalator compounds; an uncapped "market reset" at 2029 can add 30–50% to rent overnight. This is the yield-killer. | UNVERIFIED — **model both a capped (~3%/yr CPI) and an uncapped market-reset case** |
| **The full NNN load** (property tax, CAM, insurance, and Prop-13 reassessment risk if the building sells) | The $2,640 base is low; NNN is where the real occupancy cost hides. A building sale triggers Prop-13 reassessment → higher tax pass-through. | UNVERIFIED — get the current NNN $/mo and the assessed value |
| **Assignability / transfer** | Can the seller assign the lease to Hooman without the landlord re-trading terms or demanding a personal guarantee? | UNVERIFIED |
| **Who owns the building? Any sale pending?** | If the landlord is selling, reassessment + a new landlord's renewal posture is a wildcard. | UNVERIFIED |
| **Relocation / demolition / redevelopment clause** | Bay Area retail redevelopment can eject a tenant mid-option. | UNVERIFIED |

**Rent-to-revenue gate (apply after Section 2):** healthy = 15–20% of gross; **>25% is a margin trap
and a renewal bomb.** Compute base+NNN as a % of *utility-implied* gross, then re-compute it assuming
the 2029 option is exercised at (a) CPI-capped and (b) uncapped-market rent. **If the uncapped case
pushes rent-to-revenue >28–30%, the option is not protection — it's a deferred kill-trigger.**

## 4. Equipment — remaining life, re-equip reserve, upgrade levers

**Anchor:** store "established 2021," so a substantial share of the fleet is **~5 years old** — the
**newest on the Bay Area shelf** (vs. B2's 1993 Wascomat/ADC and B3's ~1990 fleet, which carry a
$150–300K re-equip bomb). This is B1's biggest structural advantage.

- **Remaining useful life:** commercial laundry machines run **~15–20 years** with maintenance
  (ASSUMED, industry norm). A 2021 fleet has **~10–15 yrs left** → its remaining life **covers the
  full 2029+10-yr lease option**, meaning **no forced major re-equip inside the ownership horizon.**
  This is the single best thing about the asset.
- **⚠️ The four-brand mix caveat (from §1):** Speed Queen + Dexter + Huebsch + **Maytag** at a store
  "established 2021" suggests **not all 50 machines are 2021-new** — some may be inherited/older.
  **Diligence item:** photograph the serial/date plate on every machine; Speed Queen/Dexter/Huebsch
  serials decode to a build year. Budget the re-equip reserve to the *oldest cohort*, not the 2021 date.
- **Re-equip reserve to hold in the $500K:** even with a young fleet, reserve for rolling
  replacement + repairs. **ASSUMED $30–60K** set-aside over the hold (vs. $150–300K for the old-fleet
  comps) — a fraction of the competitors' liability. Confirm against the actual fleet-age audit.
- **Payment tech (the primary value-add lever):** listing itself flags **cashless payment** as
  upside → implies the store is **still coin-heavy.** Retrofitting **card/app payment** (CCI /
  Laundroworks / SpyderWash / ESD, or a full app like Cents/CleanCloud) typically **lifts revenue
  ~15–25%** via higher effective vend + convenience (ASSUMED, operator-reported range — verify), and
  is a **capital + light-labor** upgrade, exactly Hooman's preferred lever (not scarce focus).
- **Remote monitoring — Hooman's engineering edge:** networked payment systems expose a **real-time
  turns/revenue-per-machine API**, which (a) makes the semi-absentee model genuinely absentee-capable
  (alerts on machine faults, no daily site visit) and (b) gives him **his own live version of the
  utility regression** — he'd see actual turns vs. water draw continuously and can dynamic-price by
  daypart. This converts his diligence edge into an *operating* edge post-close. **AI-safe:** demand
  for clean clothes is not disrupted by AI; the improvement lever is ops software he can build.

  so protecting the SDE (rent + volume) protects the exit. AI-safe and recession-resilient (people
  wash clothes in downturns), which supports durability — the category's genuine merit.
- **Distance:** Contra Costa County is **~40–60 min from San Jose** (ASSUMED, varies by city) — drivable
