## 0. The financing math that reframes everything (vs. the prior all-cash scan)

The prior WIDE_SEARCH treated Hooman as a non-US Canadian → no SBA → capped at a ~$500K
all-cash asset. **Under the corrected US-person assumption, SBA 7(a) changes the whole board.**

- **SBA 7(a): ~10% down, ~90% financed, ~10.5% rate, 10-yr term.** Debt service ≈ **16.2% of
  the loan balance per year** (VERIFIED annuity math: monthly factor 0.01349 at 10.5%/120mo).
- With **~$500K cash**, a ~10–12% down payment + working-capital reserve reaches a
  **~$1.5–2.0M business** — 3–4x the SDE the all-cash plan could touch.
- **The number that matters is SDE minus annual debt service** = Hooman's pre-tax owner cash
  (pay + return). I compute it per deal below. Rule of thumb this session:
  - $1.0M loan → ~$162K/yr debt service
  - $1.35M loan → ~$219K/yr
  - $1.8M loan → ~$291K/yr

**Trap carried forward from the prior scan:** single-platform Amazon FBA = platform
single-point-of-failure (account suspension, fee/PPC creep, copycats). This scan **demotes any
listing where Amazon is the dominant channel** and rewards real B2B reorder relationships +
diversified channels. Print/commercial-print substrate decline is treated as a second AI/secular
risk axis.

## 4. Category-level findings & honest caveats

1. **SBA reframes the category from "backstop" to "front-runner."** As a US person, Hooman can
   reach $1.5–2M B2B businesses throwing off **$13–25K/mo after debt service** — squarely in his
   $5–30K target — while keeping a ~$300–390K cash reserve. The prior all-cash scan's pessimism
   about this category was mostly a financing artifact, now removed.

2. **The best two deals split along a risk/return axis.** #1 (Building Products) = maximum cash +
   maximum use of his forecasting/pricing edge, but a $2M house-cycle-exposed price. #2 (Print/
   Promo) = smaller, safest coverage, biggest reserve, cleanest owner-op, but a secularly
   declining/AI-exposed substrate. **These are the two to open diligence on.**

3. **"Multi-channel product brand" delivered exactly one real anti-FBA candidate (#5), and it
   carries a category tax** (adult/novelty → payment-processor + platform-policy risk). The mandate's
   thesis (diversified reorder + no platform concentration) is sound, but such brands are rare on
   public listings — most "multi-channel" e-com is still Amazon-dominant. Verify channel % before
   believing any diversification claim.

4. **Diligence gates common to the whole set (all ASSUMED until verified):** (a) SDE is
   un-audited seller math — get 3 yrs of tax returns; (b) **customer concentration is undisclosed
   on every listing** — the single most important unknown for durability; (c) lease terms (month-
   to-month on #3, showroom on #1) are fragility points; (d) inventory treatment (included vs. on
   top — it's *on top* for the reference-tier bath/kitchen deal, inflating true cost).

5. **DSCR is the SBA gate.** Lenders want ≥1.25x coverage. #1 (~2.0x), #2 (~2.2x), #3 (~1.7x) pass
   easily; **#5 (~1.4x) is thin** and would want a seller note; **#4 (~1.04x) fails** — which is
   why its price is really the real estate, not the earnings.

6. **Blocked/gated sources to pursue directly:** Quiet Light and businessesforsale.com both
   returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch this session. Quiet Light in particular is where aged,
   diversified multi-channel product brands (the ideal profile) concentrate — worth a manual /
   NDA pass. Fire/safety inspection and specialty-distribution deals with recurring contracts
   remain largely broker-gated/off-market (noted in the prior scan too).

**Bottom line:** This category is a **genuine front-runner under SBA**, not a backstop. Lead
candidates: **#1 Premier Home Design & Building Products (CA)** for maximum cash + best fit, and
**#2 B2B Print/Promo (VA)** for the safest coverage and cleanest all-in-cash fit. Both need
customer-concentration and audited-SDE diligence before believing the numbers. Demote #3 (weak
edge, rich multiple), #5 (category tax), and #6 (no recurring revenue); pass on #4 (no earnings).
