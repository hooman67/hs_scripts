## 0. THE HEADLINE — read this first (brutally honest)

**The national scan's ~30–37% all-cash yield exemplars (Fords NJ $285K/$95K/3.0x) DO NOT EXIST in
the Bay Area.** Every real Bay Area laundromat I could pull today trades at **4.8–5.4× SDE**, not 3×,
and **rent eats 23–32% of gross revenue** — the highest in the country. Net all-cash pre-tax yield on
the diligenceable Bay Area deals is **~18–21% gross of owner labor**, roughly **half** the national
exemplars, and the lease (short terms, weak/"negotiable" options, no dirt owned) is materially more
fragile than the report's national assumption of "confirm 10+ yr options."

Concretely, the shelf splits into three unappealing buckets:
1. **In-budget but low-yield / lease-fragile / old equipment** (Pinole $320K, Oakland $449.9K,
   Santa Cruz $399K — all 4.85–5.4×, all with a lease or equipment-age problem).
2. **In-budget but financials undisclosed** (San Jose/Santa Clara Co $220K, Value-Add CC $400K —
   can't underwrite; the $220K one has a *$9,630/mo* rent).
3. **Genuinely good economics but 3–7× over the all-cash ceiling** (SF 2-store chain $1.65M @
   $242K EBITDA; East-Bay newly-built absentee $2.95M @ $421K EBITDA; SF laundromat + owned RE +
   rentals $1.61M — the only lease-risk-free one, and it's unbuyable all-cash).

**Verdict up front:** As a *pure NorCal, all-cash* play, this category is **materially worse than the
national laundromat thesis** — the good economics are priced out of reach and the reachable deals are
priced for ~5× SDE with 4–5 yr leases. The single deal worth a diligence hour is the **Value-Add
Contra Costa ($400K, 2021-built equipment, 10-yr renewal option)** — but *only if* its (currently
undisclosed) T-12 and utility bills clear Hooman's regression. If Hooman insists on local, he should
expect **~15–22% all-cash yield, not ~33%**, and treat the lease as the deal's life expectancy.

## 1. All-cash scoring basis (same as national file)

All-cash, yield ≈ **SDE ÷ price** (≈ 1/multiple), *before* deducting the owner's own labor
(small for a genuinely semi-absentee unattended mat; large for a fully-attended one). No debt math.
Fit ceiling: ~$500K all-cash, ~$650–700K with a seller note. Rent-to-revenue ratio is the Bay Area
tell: healthy laundromats run ~15–20% of revenue; **>25% is a margin trap and a renewal-hike bomb.**

## 5. VERDICT — is a LOCAL Bay Area laundromat a GO all-cash? WEAK / CONDITIONAL.

- **The national ~30–37% yield thesis does not survive the geography.** Bay Area mats are **4.8–5.4×
  SDE with 23–32% rent-to-revenue and 4–5 yr leases** → **~18–21% all-cash gross yield before owner
  labor**, ~half the Fords-NJ exemplar. The report's own #1 category-killer (the lease) is *worse*
  here: no owned dirt, short terms, options that are merely "negotiable." (VERIFIED across B1–B5.)
- **The only genuinely good NorCal economics are 3–7× over the all-cash ceiling** (SF chain $1.65M;
  East-Bay newly-built $2.95M; SF laundromat+RE $1.61M). Staying all-cash locks Hooman out of them.
- **The one worth a diligence hour is B1 (Value-Add, Contra Costa Co, $400K):** newest equipment on
  the shelf (**2021** Speed Queen/Dexter → smallest re-equip reserve) and the **only committed
  long-tail lease (exp 2029 + a real 10-yr renewal option)**. Its financials are *undisclosed*, which
  is exactly where **Hooman's edge pays off**: request the T-12 + pull water/sewer/PG&E bills and
  regress against claimed revenue before believing any number. If the utility-implied volume supports
  a ≥15% yield after a real rent-escalation assumption, it's the local pick; if not, walk.
- **Everything else is compromised:** B2 Pinole (sale pending + 1993 machines), B4 Santa Cruz (in
  contract + "not passive" + 1hr+ drive), B3 Oakland (fully attended, $150K/yr rent), B5 San Jose
  ($9,630/mo rent-trap, no financials).

### Recommendation
1. **B1 Contra Costa Value-Add — request T-12 + utility bills; run the regression. Only local GO
   candidate.** Confirm the NNN load and the 10-yr option is exercisable at a capped escalation.
2. **Be honest with Hooman:** the *national* laundromat winners (Fords NJ / Allentown PA, ~33%, 3×)
   are structurally cheaper and lease-safer than anything drivable from San Jose. If he'll only buy
   local, he should accept **~15–22% all-cash yield and a lease that is the business's life
   expectancy** — or reconsider the geographic constraint for this specific category.
3. **NorCal self-serve car washes: pass all-cash** (all RE-bundled, over ceiling).

### Open items to VERIFY (Hooman's diligence)
- **B1 T-12 + water/sewer/PG&E vs. revenue regression** (the kill/keep test) and the exact NNN.
- **Lease options in writing** for any deal — "negotiable" (B2) / unspecified (B3) options are worth $0.
- **Equipment age & re-equip reserve** — B2/B3 (1990–93 Wascomat) carry a $150–300K hidden liability.
- **Rent-escalation clauses** — at 23–32% rent-to-revenue, a single option-period hike can zero the SDE.
