  1. **Leverage** — the whole point of a franchise is SBA-financeability. He's declining it.
     All-cash he pays full price for cash flow he could rent for ~10% down elsewhere.
  2. **His edge** — franchisors deliberately remove the product/pricing/tech/software levers where
     Hooman creates value. He'd be an *operator*, not a *builder*; his AI/infra skill is stranded.
- **What IS genuinely better now vs. the prior report:** he can legally run a US unit himself, so

  single owner-operated unit. But that's true of every category, not a franchise-specific edge.
- **If he insists on this category all-cash, exactly ONE sub-archetype is defensible:** an
  **asset-light B2B recurring-contract management franchise** — concretely **City Wide Facility
  Solutions** (commercial-cleaning *broker/manager*, not the cleaner) — where the model is a
  contract book + subcontractor coordination (route/CRM/scheduling, i.e. where his data edge is
  at least *marginally* real) rather than food/fitness labor. Everything else (food multi-unit,
  boutique fitness, single-unit QSR) is a pass for this buyer all-cash.
- **Recommendation: keep this category as a FALLBACK, not a lead vein.** It does not beat the
  wide-search leaders (verified laundromat / recurring-route pest / vertical B2B SaaS) on
  risk-adjusted all-cash return. If he wants "buy cash flow + operate hands-on," a **non-franchise
  recurring-route service business** dominates a franchise on the same dollars (no royalty drag,
  no franchisor approval, he keeps the pricing/margin levers).

**Net:** 2 of the 3 strikes are unchanged; the 1 that improved (operating legality) is not unique to
franchises. So the category's *relative* rank vs. non-franchise cash-flow businesses barely moves.
The franchisor-control strike is the one Hooman-specific structural problem and it is permanent.

**Cash-on-cash reality (all-cash, unlevered), CONFIRMED against current listings below:**
franchise resales cluster at **~2.0-3.0x SDE**, i.e. **~33-50% headline unlevered yield** — but that
headline **embeds the owner's full-time wage in SDE** (these are SDE, not EBITDA). Net the owner's
labor (~$70-90K for a hands-on GM-equivalent role) and the true *return on capital* on a single
owner-operated ~$450-600K unit is **~15-25%**, ~$6-10K/mo — *before* the ~6-8% royalty+ad drag that a
non-franchise business of the same cash flow wouldn't carry. A non-franchise service business at the
same 2.5x SDE yields the same headline **without royalty drag and without franchisor approval on
resale** — which is why, dollar-for-dollar, the franchise wrapper is a net negative here.
*(Yield method: cash-on-cash = SDE ÷ all-cash price; wage-adjusted return = (SDE − owner-labor) ÷ price. VERIFIED arithmetic on listing figures; owner-labor $70-90K = ASSUMED market GM rate.)*
