## SBA financing math used throughout (ASSUMED, verify with a lender)

- **SBA 7(a):** up to $5M, ~10% equity injection (seller can carry part of it on full standby).
  Business-only loans amortize **10 yr**; deals with real estate blend toward **25 yr**.
- **Rate (2026):** prime (~7.5%) + ~2.75% ≈ **10.5%** variable. VERIFY at close.
- **Debt service (business-only, 10 yr, 10.5%):** ≈ **$16.2K per $1M borrowed per year**
  (≈ $1.35/mo per $1). So a **$1.5M** deal at 90% LTV → ~$1.35M loan → **~$218K/yr** debt service.
  A **$780K** deal → ~$700K loan → ~$114K/yr. **Real estate in the loan drops these sharply**
  (25-yr amort ≈ $121K/yr per $1M borrowed).
- **Key implication:** at ~$218K/yr debt service, a deal needs **≥ $300–350K SDE** to leave
  Hooman a full owner salary AND positive cash-on-cash. The sweet spot is **$300K+ SDE at ≤4.5x**,
  or a smaller deal where RE in the loan cuts the payment. Cabinet/RE-included and $340–350K-SDE
  CNC shops clear this; the awning and low-SDE-high-ask deals do not.

## Category economics (honest, quantified)

- **Fair multiples:** CNC/precision machining trades **3.5–4.5x SDE (roughly 4–7x EBITDA)**;
  sign/graphics and small fab **2.4–2.8x SDE**; specialty product mfrs (awning, adhesive) **3–3.6x
  SDE**. The RI CNC shop (4.4x SDE / 6.8x EBITDA), Indiana (3.7x EBITDA), and cabinet (3.1x SDE incl
  RE) are the fair-to-cheap ones.
- **SBA changes the game vs. the old all-cash scan:** with ~$155–200K equity in, Hooman controls a
  $1.3–1.5M / $340K-SDE asset and nets **~$120–150K/yr (~$10–12.5K/mo) after debt service** while
  drawing the operator role — versus ~$155K SDE all-cash at Buncombe. Same cash out of pocket,
  **~2x the SDE.** This is the core reason to prefer the SBA-range CNC shops.
- **Owner-hours:** **FULL-TIME, hands-on (40–50 hr/wk)** for owner-operated shops (RI #1, cabinet #6,
  Buncombe #9). The **manager-run/near-absentee** exceptions worth prizing: NJ fab #3 (remote CEO),
  HSC MN #4 (owners work 3 days/wk), KY plastics #7 (absentee-capable). Those let the day job survive.
- **Durability / AI-safety:** **HIGH** — physical precision manufacturing is not AI-displaceable and
  has deep switching costs (qualified suppliers, tooling, tolerances, defense/aero quals). The risks
  are *demand-cyclical* (motorsports, residential cabinetry) and *equipment-obsolescence*, not
  software disruption. This category is squarely AI-safe, unlike content/SEO/e-com.
