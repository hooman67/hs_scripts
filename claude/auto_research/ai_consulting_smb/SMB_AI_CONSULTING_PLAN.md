genuine ~6.5/10 — but it fails the "secondary income that doesn't steal focus" test, and it's

The five agents split exactly along the line this project has drawn all along:

is a defensible wedge. The difference between 4/10 and 6.5/10 is entirely **discipline and narrowness.**

## ⚠️ GATE 0 — resolve BEFORE anything else (surfaced independently by all 5 agents)

**The Adobe conflict-of-interest / IP question.** Consulting in his exact day-job domain (generative
AI) while a full-time Adobe employee creates four exposures:
1. **Outside-employment / COI policy** — likely requires disclosure + written approval for same-field work.
2. **IP assignment** — CA Labor Code §2870's carve-out does NOT protect work *related to the employer's
   business*; generative-AI consulting is squarely Adobe's business. High-severity.
3. **Confidentiality / non-solicitation** — Adobe's clients (Coca-Cola/Home Depot/Ford/Tesco/Humain)
   and Firefly/internal tools are strictly off-limits.
4. **Non-compete** — VERIFIED good news: CA bans employee non-competes (§16600). Does NOT rescue 1–3.

**Action:** read his signed Adobe employment/IP agreement + spend ~$500–1.5K on an employment attorney
BEFORE taking a single client. **The bitter irony:** his deepest edge (generative media) is his MOST
conflicted with Adobe's business — he may have to steer toward agentic/ops automation and away from
gen-media, OR keep it truly clean (own time/equipment, no Adobe clients/IP, disclosed & approved).
**Everything below is contingent on this clearing.**

## The honest tension you should weigh (from `05` + this project's whole history)

This is the **shape that looks most like his day job** — and this project has repeatedly found that's
the seductive trap (it looks like perfect fit precisely because it's obvious to every capable person).
Three hard truths:
1. **It's maximally focus-consuming.** "Portfolio of many small clients" directly fights "part-time
   solo." Each tiny deal has the sales + support cost of a big one. This is the *opposite* of the
   capital-and-delegation secondary-income shape the project concluded was right for a busy founder.
2. **The moat erodes.** "Ex-Adobe" opens the meeting but doesn't lock the client, and it fades the day
   he leaves Adobe. Durable edge = enterprise delivery judgment + agentic depth, which SMBs often
   can't *value*, vs. the n8n plumbing they can.
3. **Tools are eating the middle.** Value is migrating to the two ends (enterprise governance; deep
   vertical specialists). The undifferentiated solo generalist implementer is the squeezed slice.

**But the counter-case is real too:** unlike the build-side ideas (all zeros) and the acquisition path
(needs capital he's cautious to leverage), this needs **near-zero capital**, leverages his single
most valuable and current asset (enterprise AI delivery), and has a fast, cheap validation loop (talk
to 20 owners, sell 3 audits). Even if it never becomes the "portfolio," it could de-risk a future
full independence or a productized micro-SaaS born from a repeated client need.

## Recommendation & first-30-days (if Gate 0 clears)

**Treat it as a cheap, high-learning VALIDATION sprint, not a committed business — narrow hard, and
let the market tell you.** Concrete sequence:

**Week 0 (the gate):** Read the Adobe IP/COI/moonlighting agreement; book an employment attorney.
Decide the clean structure (own time/equipment, no Adobe clients/IP, disclosed+approved; likely
agentic-ops-first, gen-media only if cleared). **If this doesn't clear cleanly, STOP — or defer the
whole play until he's ready to leave Adobe.**

**Weeks 1–2:** Pick ONE vertical (recommend regional home/field services). Rewrite LinkedIn to the
positioning line. Mine a warm list of ~50 owners + referral super-nodes. Draft the $1,500 Audit offer
+ one flagship solution spec. Do NOT build a website yet.

**Weeks 3–4:** Start 20 conversations (warm-first, then personalized outbound). Goal = **sell 1–3 paid
Audits**, not close retainers. Build one free "AI teardown" of a real local business as proof. Stand up
a 1-page Framer site only once you have something to point to.

**The kill/keep gate at day 30:** Did ≥2 owners pay for an Audit (or credibly commit)? If yes →
productize the repeated finding into a fixed build + retainer, and consider whether it's worth the
focus cost. If no → the reachability/WTP thesis is weaker than hoped; keep it as background optionality,
don't quit anything.

**Biggest reason it fails:** no moat + race to the bottom + it eats the focus a secondary stream
shouldn't. **Biggest reason it works:** his scarce delivery judgment + gen/agentic depth, IF he
de-scopes to one vertical with outcome-based retainers and refuses commodity work — and clears Adobe.
