## PART 1 — The vertical pick

**Lead vertical: Regional home & field services** — HVAC, plumbing, roofing, electrical, pest control,
landscaping companies with ~20–200 staff and multiple crews. (Top-ranked in `01_target_segments.md` on
reachability × budget × pain × his-edge.)

**Why this one, specifically:**
- **Owner-reachable & decisive** — one owner/GM signs; no enterprise committee (the exact thing he's
  escaping from Adobe).
- **Real, arithmetic ROI** — a missed lead or a mis-scheduled crew is money they can *count*. Easy to
  prove value in dollars, which shortens the sale.
- **Manual & un-modernized** — most still run on paper, phone, whiteboards, and a bookkeeper. Huge
  automation surface, low incumbent-software saturation vs. tech verticals.
- **Zero Adobe overlap** — no gen-media, no conflict with his day job.
- **He already has recon** — this project (CutLog, the acquisition search, the Round-4 trades) mapped
  where these owners congregate (vertical Facebook groups, trade associations) — a distribution head start.
- **Fragmentation is a feature here** — many similar small firms with the same workflows = the exact
  condition for a repeatable product to emerge.

**Two backup verticals** (if home-services access proves slow): multi-location **healthcare-adjacent**
groups (dental/vet/med-spa — fat margins, but keep it to ops/marketing-ops, NOT clinical data = HIPAA)
and regional **B2B distributors / light manufacturers** (strongest pure agentic-automation fit, bigger
deals, slightly harder to reach). Pick ONE to start; don't spread.

**Anti-targets:** solo operators (can't pay), single-location micro-shops (too DIY), anything requiring
gen-media as the core deliverable (Adobe zone), franchisors (multi-stakeholder = enterprise motion again).

## PART 2 — The "Discovery Engagement" offer (designed to surface a product, not just deliver a service)

This is the heart of the plan. A normal consultant sells a fix and moves on. Hooman's engagement is
**structured as R&D**: priced to be an easy yes, scoped to force him across the client's whole operation
so he *sees* the repeatable pain, and contracted so he keeps the right to build a product from what he learns.

### The offer: "AI Operations Teardown + First Automation" — ~$2,500–4,000, fixed, ~2–3 weeks
Deliberately a bit richer than a pure audit, because he needs to actually *work inside the ops* to spot
the product — a report alone won't reveal the repeatable workflow.

**Structure:**
1. **Week 1 — Operations map (the discovery).** Sit with the owner + one person from each function
   (sales/intake, scheduling/dispatch, back-office/billing, marketing). Document every manual,
   repetitive, human-in-the-loop workflow. Deliverable: a ranked "automation opportunity map" with a
   dollar estimate per opportunity. *(This is the data-collection instrument — see Part 3.)*
2. **Week 2–3 — Build ONE quick win.** Implement the single highest-ROI, lowest-effort automation live
   (e.g. inbound-lead → CRM auto-entry + instant response; or job-notes → invoice draft). This proves
   value, earns trust, and is the wedge to a retainer.
3. **The upsell built in:** the opportunity map *is* the backlog. End every engagement with "here are
   the next 3 things, and I can run them on a monthly basis" → retainer ($500–2.5K/mo).

**Why priced/scoped this way:** cheap enough that an owner says yes without a committee; rich enough that
he's *inside the operation* long enough to spot the pattern; produces a tangible quick win so he's not
selling on promises; and every engagement deposits a structured ops-map into his own dataset (Part 3).

### The contract clause that protects the whole strategy (non-negotiable)
Include an **IP/tooling clause**: the client owns their specific configuration and data; **Hooman retains
ownership of any reusable methods, templates, and tools** he develops. Standard consulting language, and
it's what lets him legally productize the repeated workflow later instead of having given it away 4 times.
*(Separate from — and additional to — the Adobe question, which he's chosen to manage via gen-media
avoidance + own-equipment.)*

## PART 3 — The signals that tell you WHICH workflow to productize

Every engagement feeds one running document: a **"Repeatable Pain Log"** — one row per manual workflow
seen, across all clients. After ~3–4 engagements, the product reveals itself. Score each candidate
workflow on these six signals; productize the one that scores highest on ALL of them:

| # | Signal | The question | Why it decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Repetition** | Did the SAME workflow appear at ≥3 of the first ~4 clients, in nearly the same shape? | The #1 signal. Repetition = a market, not a one-off. If it's bespoke each time, it's a service, not a product. |
| 2 | **Painful & dollar-countable** | Can the owner name what it costs them (missed leads, overtime, errors, days of delay)? | Willingness-to-pay follows countable pain. Vague "efficiency" doesn't sell a subscription. |
| 3 | **Solvable with a productizable core** | Can ONE configurable tool serve all of them with ~10% per-client tweaking, not a rebuild each time? | This is the line between a product and a consulting deliverable. If each needs custom work, it won't scale off his time. |
| 4 | **Data moat / compounding** | Does the tool get better as it runs (accumulated data, benchmarks, a workflow library) so a copycat can't trivially clone it? | The lesson from every prior round: no moat = race to the bottom. Prefer workflows where usage builds a proprietary asset. |
| 5 | **Not owned by an incumbent SoR** | Does their existing software (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro, QuickBooks) already do this, or bundle it free? | The Round-2/3/4 killer. If the vertical SaaS-of-record ships it, walk. Target the gaps BETWEEN their tools. |
| 6 | **Retainer-shaped** | Is it an ongoing need (runs monthly) vs. a one-time fix? | Recurring revenue is the whole portfolio thesis. One-time tools don't compound. |

**The decision rule:** productize the FIRST workflow that scores high on all six — especially #1
(repetition), #4 (moat), and #5 (not incumbent-owned), because those three are what killed every prior
idea in this project. If nothing clears #5 (the SoR already owns it all), that's a real signal to switch
vertical, not to build anyway.

**Concrete hypotheses to watch for in home/field services** (unvalidated — the engagements confirm or
kill them): inbound-lead capture + instant-response + CRM auto-entry across phone/text/web/forms;
job-completion-notes → invoice/quote draft; crew scheduling/dispatch optimization; review-request +
reputation automation; supplier/parts reorder agents. The one that repeats + isn't already in their
ServiceTitan/Jobber stack is the product.

## PART 4 — Sequencing (part-time, ~10–15 hr/wk)

- **Weeks 1–2 — Set up to sell, not to build.** Rewrite LinkedIn to the positioning line
  ("enterprise-grade AI ops automation, for home-service companies that can't hire a Fortune-500 team").
  Mine a warm list of ~40–50 home-service owners + referral super-nodes (accountants, trade-group
  contacts, suppliers). Draft the Teardown offer one-pager + the standard contract with the IP clause.
  **No product, no fancy website yet.**
- **Weeks 3–6 — Land the first 1–2 engagements.** Warm outreach first, then personalized cold (his own
  AI does the personalization). Goal: sell 1–2 paid Teardowns. Start the Repeatable Pain Log on client #1.
- **Weeks 7–14 — Deliver + pattern-hunt.** Run the engagements, bank quick wins, convert at least one to
  a small retainer, and — critically — fill the Pain Log. Aim for 3–4 total engagements before deciding.
- **The productize gate (~engagement #3–4):** if one workflow scores high on all six signals →
  productize it (narrow tool, first 2–3 clients pre-committed from the engagements). If not → either run
  more engagements or switch vertical. **Do NOT build until the six signals say so.**

## Success / kill markers
- **Green (proceed to product):** ≥3 engagements sold; ≥1 workflow hitting all six signals; ≥1 retainer live.
- **Yellow (keep consulting, keep hunting):** engagements sell but no workflow repeats cleanly yet.
- **Red (rethink):** can't sell 2 paid Teardowns in ~8 weeks → the reachability/WTP thesis is weaker than
  hoped; treat as background optionality, don't quit anything, don't build.

## The one discipline that makes or breaks this
**Treat every engagement as R&D toward a product, not as the business.** The failure mode is becoming a
busy, well-paid consultant who never productizes and just sells more hours. The Pain Log + the six-signal
gate are the mechanism that keeps you honest — review them after every engagement.
