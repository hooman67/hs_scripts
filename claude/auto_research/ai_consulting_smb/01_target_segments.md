# SMB Target Segments for a Solo AI-Consulting Practice

**Author:** market-research agent · **Date:** 2026-08-28
**For:** Hooman Shariati — solo AI-consulting practice targeting SMBs (portfolio of many small clients, not one enterprise)
**Founder edge to exploit:** Adobe Foundry enterprise credibility (Coca-Cola, Home Depot, Ford, Tesco, Humain) · image & video generation · agentic workflows · ML/AI, cloud, data engineering · business-understanding + AI-integration.

---

## 0. How to read this / methodology

Ranking score = **Reachability × Budget × Pain × Founder-edge** (each 1–5, product normalized to /10 shown per segment).

- **Reachability** = can he get the owner/decision-maker on a call, and is there a channel (association, trade show, referral loop)?
- **Budget** = can they pay a $10–60K project + retainer without a committee?
- **Pain** = is the manual work expensive, recurring, and felt by the owner personally?
- **Founder-edge** = do his *specific* skills (enterprise cred, image/video gen, agentic automation) beat the commodity Fiverr/agency/off-the-shelf-SaaS alternative?

**Confidence tags:** VERIFIED = pulled from a cited source this session. ASSUMED = industry-standard reasoning / founder-domain knowledge, not independently verified here. Treat all dollar willingness-to-pay bands as ASSUMED unless tagged otherwise — I could not source SMB-specific AI-project price benchmarks directly (search engines were network-blocked in this environment); the anchor I *did* verify is agency pricing (below), which brackets the ballpark.

**Key verified anchors (used across segments):**
- Digital-marketing agencies charge SMBs **$100–$149/hr** (US, specialized) and typical monthly engagements run **$10,000–$49,999/mo**; general work $25–49/hr. [VERIFIED — Clutch 2026, clutch.co]
- **42%** of marketing & sales departments now "regularly use" generative AI (55% in tech); product/service dev **28%**. So AI is already normalized in the two functions Hooman wants to target — buyers won't need convincing that AI is real, only that *he* can deliver it. [VERIFIED — McKinsey State of AI]
- CX/customer-service demand is strong but **only ~20% of agents have access to gen-AI tools** and **62% of CX leaders say they're behind** on instant experiences — a large unmet-implementation gap. [VERIFIED — Zendesk CX statistics]

## RANKED SEGMENTS

### #1 — Regional multi-location home & field services (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, pest, landscaping, garage doors) — 20–200 staff, $3M–$30M revenue
**Score ≈ 8.7/10** (Reach 5 · Budget 4 · Pain 5 · Edge 4)

- **The manual pain:** Owners live in the CRM/FSM gap. Dispatchers re-key jobs by hand; techs write illegible paper/voice notes that someone re-types; missed-call and after-hours leads go to voicemail and die; review-response and rebooking is manual; quotes are built in spreadsheets. The two Hooman-favored pains show up hard here: **(a) sales/CRM updated by hand** (call logs, follow-ups, pipeline in ServiceTitan/Jobber/Housecall Pro entered manually), and **(b) marketing content run manually** (seasonal promos, before/after photos, Google Business posts).
- **AI solution:** Agentic voice/SMS lead-capture + auto-CRM-update, call transcription → structured job record, automated review-response + rebooking, and image-gen for before/after and seasonal marketing creative.
- **Why reachable + budget + pain:** Owner-operator makes the call in one meeting — no committee. They already pay ServiceTitan/agencies real money, so budget is proven. A single missed $8K HVAC-replacement lead is a visceral, quantifiable loss → **ROI is arithmetic they do in their head.** Reachable via trade associations (ACCA, PHCC), Facebook contractor groups (same channel that worked for CutLog), and franchise/dealer networks.
- **Founder edge:** Agentic workflows (lead → CRM → dispatch) are exactly his lane; image/video gen produces the marketing creative these shops can't make. Enterprise cred ("built AI for Home Depot" — a *brand these contractors literally buy from*) is uniquely potent here.
- **WTP:** $8–25K build + **$1.5–4K/mo** retainer. Portfolio of 10–15 = strong solo income. [ASSUMED, bracketed by verified agency $10–50K/mo]
- **Skeptic's note:** Getting crowded — ServiceTitan, Jobber, and a wave of "AI receptionist" startups (Avoca, Rilla, Signpost) target this. Edge is *integration + custom agentic glue across their actual tool stack*, not another point tool. Don't sell a chatbot; sell "your CRM fills itself."

### #2 — Specialty & multi-location healthcare-adjacent groups: dental, vet, med-spa, dermatology, optometry, physio — 3–30 locations, 20–150 staff
**Score ≈ 8.3/10** (Reach 4 · Budget 5 · Pain 5 · Edge 4)

- **The manual pain:** Front-desk drowns in phone-tag scheduling, insurance/eligibility paperwork, recall/reactivation calls, and manual patient-record and referral data entry. Marketing (especially med-spa/derm/ortho) is heavily **visual** and run manually or via pricey agencies — before/after galleries, treatment explainer videos, Instagram content.
- **AI solution:** AI phone/scheduling agent + no-show reduction, document/insurance intake automation, patient-recall agentic campaigns, and **image/video-gen for treatment marketing** (Hooman's exact strength).
- **Why reachable + budget + pain:** High per-patient LTV and fat margins → real budget; med-spa/derm/dental are notorious heavy marketing spenders. Owner (a doctor) or practice manager decides. Multi-location groups (DSOs, vet roll-ups) are big enough to pay but small enough to skip procurement.
- **Founder edge:** Visual content-gen at scale is a genuine differentiator vs generic marketing agencies. Agentic recall/scheduling is high-ROI.
- **WTP:** $15–40K + **$2–5K/mo**; med-spa marketing retainers can run higher. [ASSUMED]
- **Skeptic's note:** **HIPAA / PHI is a real constraint** — anything touching patient data needs BAAs and careful data handling; adds friction and liability. **Mitigation: lead with the *marketing/image-gen* wedge (no PHI)** to land the client, then expand into ops. Also somewhat agency-saturated on marketing — the ops-automation + enterprise-grade-data-handling angle is the moat.

### #3 — Regional B2B distributors / wholesalers / light manufacturers (industrial supply, building materials, food service, auto parts) — 30–250 staff, $10M–$100M
**Score ≈ 8.1/10** (Reach 4 · Budget 4 · Pain 5 · Edge 5)

- **The manual pain:** Quoting from PDFs and emails by hand; RFQ → line-item entry; product catalog/spec-sheet creation; order data re-keyed between email/ERP; **sales reps updating CRM manually** (or not at all — pipeline lives in reps' heads). Document processing is the bleeding wound: unstructured POs, invoices, spec sheets.
- **AI solution:** Agentic quote/RFQ automation (email in → structured quote out), document-extraction pipelines, ERP/CRM auto-sync, and product-content generation (images + descriptions) for e-commerce catalogs at scale.
- **Why reachable + budget + pain:** These businesses run on thin-margin volume where a 2-day quote turnaround loses deals — **ROI is direct revenue.** Owner/VP-sales decides. They have IT budget but weak internal AI talent → won't DIY.
- **Founder edge:** **This is the strongest founder-edge fit on the list** — data engineering + agentic document workflows + catalog image-gen are precisely his enterprise-grade skills, and unstructured-doc automation is hard enough that Fiverr/off-the-shelf can't touch it. His Tesco/retail-supply-chain-adjacent cred lands directly.
- **WTP:** $20–60K build + **$2–6K/mo**. Highest project sizes on this list. [ASSUMED]
- **Skeptic's note:** Longer sales cycle (more stakeholders than a 1-owner shop), and ERP integration can be a swamp. Less "get owner on a call in a day." But the deals are bigger and stickier. Scoop-and-run risk if you over-promise ERP integration — scope tightly.

### #4 — Boutique / franchise marketing & creative agencies themselves (10–50 staff) — as an AI "content-ops" supplier
**Score ≈ 7.8/10** (Reach 5 · Budget 4 · Pain 5 · Edge 5)

- **The manual pain:** Agencies **run the entire content pipeline by hand** — this is Hooman's literal example. Designers/editors produce social creative, product photos, short-form video, and variations manually; it's their margin killer. They resell to their SMB clients.
- **AI solution:** A managed image/video-gen + agentic content pipeline (brief → 50 on-brand variations → scheduled) they white-label to clients.
- **Why reachable + budget + pain:** Agency owners are extremely reachable (they sell for a living, answer LinkedIn/DMs), they *feel* content-production cost daily, and they have budget because they bill it forward. **Channel multiplier:** one agency = access to *their* 20–50 SMB clients.
- **Founder edge:** Image/video generation at production quality is his single most differentiated skill — agencies can't build the pipeline themselves and are desperate for it. Enterprise cred = instant trust.
- **WTP:** $10–30K + **$2–5K/mo** (or rev-share per asset). [ASSUMED]
- **Skeptic's note:** **Most saturated / most DIY-able zone** — agencies are actively adopting Midjourney/Runway/Adobe Firefly (his own employer's product!) themselves, and a flood of "AI for agencies" tools exists. Edge must be *bespoke agentic pipeline + brand-consistency at scale + fine-tuning*, not "I'll run Midjourney for you." Real risk of being commoditized in 12 months. High reach, thinner moat — treat as a fast cash + referral-engine play, not the anchor.

### #5 — Real estate: brokerages, property-management firms, mortgage brokers — 10–100 agents/staff
**Score ≈ 7.5/10** (Reach 5 · Budget 4 · Pain 4 · Edge 4)

- **The manual pain:** Listing copy + photo enhancement + virtual staging done manually or outsourced; **CRM/lead follow-up is famously neglected** (agents don't update it); document-heavy (leases, disclosures, applications); tenant/lead comms are manual.
- **AI solution:** Virtual staging & listing-video generation (image/video gen), agentic lead-nurture that auto-updates CRM, lease/application document extraction, tenant-comms agent.
- **Why reachable + budget + pain:** Brokers and PM owners are hyper-reachable and marketing-obsessed; commission-driven so ROI on a closed deal is obvious. Property managers have recurring document/comms pain.
- **Founder edge:** Virtual staging = pure image-gen; agentic follow-up = his lane.
- **WTP:** $8–25K + **$1–3K/mo**. [ASSUMED]
- **Skeptic's note:** **Heavily tooled already** — virtual staging (BoxBrownie, REimagineHome), CRM (Follow Up Boss, kvCORE), and AI-lead tools are everywhere and cheap. Individual agents are too small/DIY; **target the brokerage/PM firm (the owner with 20+ agents), not the solo agent.** Moderate moat.

### #6 — Professional services SMB firms: accounting/bookkeeping, insurance agencies, law (small firms), consultancies — 10–75 staff
**Score ≈ 7.3/10** (Reach 4 · Budget 4 · Pain 4 · Edge 4)

- **The manual pain:** Document processing is the core job — invoices, receipts, claims, contracts, intake forms — much of it manual. Client comms, proposal generation, and CRM upkeep are manual.
- **AI solution:** Document-extraction + classification agents, proposal/report drafting, client-intake automation, agentic email/CRM.
- **Why reachable + budget + pain:** Bill by the hour → they *understand* the value of time saved and have budget. Owner/partner decides.
- **Founder edge:** Data engineering + agentic doc workflows fit well; enterprise-grade accuracy/compliance framing beats generic tools.
- **WTP:** $12–35K + **$1.5–4K/mo**. [ASSUMED]
- **Skeptic's note:** Accounting/legal have **strong vertical incumbents** (Intuit, Thomson Reuters CoCounsel, Ironclad, a dozen "AI for accountants" startups) and conservative, risk-averse buyers (accuracy/liability). Harder to differentiate; regulatory caution slows deals. Insurance agencies (less tooled, high doc pain, owner-led) are the sweet spot within this band.

### #7 — Multi-location hospitality & food/beverage: restaurant groups, breweries, boutique hotels, catering — 3–20 locations
**Score ≈ 7.0/10** (Reach 5 · Budget 3 · Pain 4 · Edge 4)

- **The manual pain:** Social/menu content produced manually and constantly (food photography, seasonal promos, reels); reservations/reviews/comms manual; catering-quote and event-inquiry handling manual.
- **AI solution:** Food/menu **image & video-gen** at scale, agentic review-response + reservation/inquiry handling, automated local-marketing content calendar.
- **Why reachable + budget + pain:** Owners are reachable and marketing-hungry; visual content is a perpetual, painful treadmill for them.
- **Founder edge:** Image/video-gen shines (appetizing food creative at scale); enterprise cred (Coca-Cola — a *beverage brand they stock*) resonates.
- **WTP:** $6–20K + **$1–3K/mo**. [ASSUMED]
- **Skeptic's note:** **Thin margins → budget is the weak link;** single-location restaurants can't pay — must target 3+ location *groups*. Content is also DIY-able with consumer AI apps. Fun/reachable but lower-value; a volume/marketing-wedge play.

### #8 — Regional e-commerce & DTC brands (Shopify-scale) — $2M–$50M revenue, 5–60 staff
**Score ≈ 6.8/10** (Reach 4 · Budget 4 · Pain 4 · Edge 5)

- **The manual pain:** Product photography, model shots, lifestyle imagery, and ad-creative variations produced manually/expensively; product descriptions and SEO content manual; catalog scale is brutal (thousands of SKUs); customer-support tickets manual.
- **AI solution:** Product image/video-gen (on-model, lifestyle, variations), agentic content/SEO generation across the catalog, support-automation.
- **Why reachable + budget + pain:** DTC founders are AI-forward and reachable; creative + support are their two biggest recurring costs.
- **Founder edge:** Image/video-gen at catalog scale + fine-tuning to brand = strong, and few can do it well.
- **WTP:** $10–30K + **$1.5–4K/mo**. [ASSUMED]
- **Skeptic's note:** **Most AI-mature, most DIY-prone buyer on the list.** They already use Klaviyo AI, Gorgias AI, product-image tools (Flair, Photoroom), and many founders prompt Midjourney themselves. High commoditization risk. Win only on *bespoke, brand-fine-tuned pipelines + integration*, not generic gen. Reachable but a knife-fight.

### #9 — Specialty trades / niche manufacturers needing technical-doc + visual work: sign shops, custom fabrication, cabinetry/millwork, promotional products — 10–80 staff
**Score ≈ 6.6/10** (Reach 4 · Budget 3 · Pain 4 · Edge 4)

- **The manual pain:** Custom quoting from drawings/specs, product mockups/renderings done by hand, catalog/proof generation, RFQ handling. (Adjacent to Hooman's proven CutLog/fabrication domain — the Facebook shop-owner channel is *known-good*.)
- **AI solution:** Mockup/rendering image-gen, agentic quote-from-drawing, proof automation, catalog content.
- **Why reachable + budget + pain:** **He already knows how to reach these people** (fabrication FB groups from CutLog). Custom-mockup generation is a daily grind.
- **Founder edge:** Image-gen for mockups/proofs + agentic quoting; existing domain familiarity de-risks discovery.
- **WTP:** $8–25K + **$1–3K/mo**. [ASSUMED]
- **Skeptic's note:** Budgets are lumpier and shops are conservative/hands-on; some overlap with CutLog — could confuse focus. Good *warm-start* segment (leverage existing channel) but not the biggest-money.

### #10 — Franchise systems / franchisors (as a HQ-level buyer that pushes to franchisees) — 20–500 units
**Score ≈ 6.5/10** (Reach 3 · Budget 5 · Pain 4 · Edge 4)

- **The manual pain:** Franchisor HQ must supply local marketing content, brand-consistent creative, lead-routing, and support playbooks to every franchisee — currently manual and inconsistent.
- **AI solution:** Centralized brand-locked image/video-gen + agentic local-marketing + lead-capture rolled out to all units.
- **Why budget:** **One sale = many units of recurring revenue;** franchisors have real budgets and mandate tools top-down (matches the "regulatory-mandate-to-buy = gold" learning — here it's a *franchise mandate*).
- **Founder edge:** Brand-consistency at scale via fine-tuned gen + enterprise cred (he's shipped brand-safe gen for Coca-Cola/Home Depot — *exactly* the franchisor's fear).
- **WTP:** $30–80K + **$3–10K/mo** (enterprise-ish deal size). [ASSUMED]
- **Skeptic's note:** **Reachability is the killer** — franchisor HQ is a multi-stakeholder, slower B2B sale that looks a lot like the enterprise motion Hooman is trying to *escape*. High value, low fit-with-stated-strategy. Include as an opportunistic upsell path *from* a franchisee win (#1/#5/#7), not a cold target.

## Segments to AVOID (look good, aren't)

- **Solo practitioners / micro-businesses (<5 staff, <$1M):** Reachable and painful, but **can't/won't pay** enough for a portfolio to work; DIY with $20/mo tools. Skip unless franchised.
- **Generic "SMB marketing content" as a standalone offer:** Saturated by agencies + consumer AI apps + his own employer's Firefly. Use it as a *wedge*, never the whole pitch.
- **Restaurants (single-location), individual real-estate agents, individual insurance agents:** Too small / too tooled / too DIY. Always go up a level to the *group/firm/brokerage owner*.
- **Regulated-core workflows (legal drafting, medical/PHI, financial advice):** Liability + conservative buyers + entrenched vertical incumbents. Enter only via the *non-regulated marketing/ops wedge*, then expand.
- **Anything requiring per-client custom heavy integration with no reuse:** A solo practice dies on bespoke one-offs. **Productize a repeatable core** (agentic CRM-updater, doc-extraction pipeline, brand image-gen pipeline) and re-skin per client — otherwise the portfolio model doesn't scale for one person.

## Bottom line / recommended focus

**Anchor on #1 (home & field services) and #3 (B2B distributors/light mfg):** best blend of owner-led reachability, proven budget, arithmetic-obvious ROI, low DIY risk, and strongest fit for agentic + document + image-gen skills. **Use #4 (agencies) and #9 (fabrication shops, warm channel) as fast cash + referral engines.** Treat visual/marketing-content gen as the *wedge that lands the client*, then expand into the stickier agentic-ops and document-automation work — that's where the moat, the retainers, and the enterprise-grade edge actually live.

**Two structural rules for a solo operator (both derived from the founder profile + this analysis):**
1. **Sell "your system runs itself," not "a chatbot"** — integration/agentic glue across the client's real tool stack is the defensible part; point-tools get commoditized.
2. **Productize one repeatable core and re-skin per vertical** — a portfolio of bespoke builds will crush one person; a portfolio of the same pipeline re-branded will fund him.
