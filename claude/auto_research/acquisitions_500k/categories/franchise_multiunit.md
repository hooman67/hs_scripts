# Franchises / Multi-Unit / Semi-Absentee Ownership — Acquisition Scan

**Category owner:** side-quest agent. **Date:** 2026-07-03.
**Mandate:** best ~$500K acquisition for Hooman (Canadian citizen, no SBA). See `../README.md`.

Scope covered: semi-absentee franchise ownership (fitness, home services, food, senior care,
education) and small multi-unit operators; franchise **resales** (existing, cash-flowing units)
rather than new-franchise fees.

---

## 1. Key distinction up front: RESALE vs NEW FRANCHISE

Most "franchise for sale" listings on BizBuySell/BizQuest are **new franchise fees** (Jani-King
$25K, Le Macaron $100K, Christian Brothers Auto $85K, School of Rock $150K). Those are a
franchise *fee to start from zero* — no revenue, no cash flow, high build-out risk. **Not
acquisitions.** We want **resales**: an existing operator selling a unit (or units) that already
throws off SDE. The best marketplaces for resales are:

- **FranchiseFlippers.com** — "leading franchise resale exchange in N. America," ~400 live
  resale listings, cash-flow bands shown. Best single source for this category.
- **BusinessesForSale.com** (us.businessesforsale.com) — ~682 franchise-resale listings, shows
  revenue + cash-flow bands.
- **BizBuySell** — mixed (new + resale), 1,500+ listings; broker directory flags "SBA Loan
  Eligible" (irrelevant to Hooman).

## 3. Category economics (real, quantified)

- **Multiples:** Small franchise resales trade at **~2.0–3.0x SDE** (Duck Donuts ~2.7x on $187K;
  Koala ~1.9x; Budget Blinds ~2.7x). Franchise resales tend to be **cheaper than independents**
  of the same cash flow because of royalty drag (typically 5–8% of revenue) + transfer fees +
  the franchisor's approval right on any buyer.
- **Market context:** BizBuySell Q1-2026 data — median small-business sale **$350K on median
  cash flow $165,256** (~2.1x). Franchise resales cluster right around this.
- **Cash yield:** At ~2.5x SDE all-cash, unlevered yield is **~35–40% of purchase price** *before*
  the owner's own labor. But most sub-$500K franchise units are **owner-operated, NOT truly
  semi-absentee** — the "$187K cash flow" on Duck Donuts *includes* the owner's full-time labor
  (SDE, not EBITDA). Subtract a ~$70–90K manager salary to run it hands-off and the passive
  yield drops sharply.
- **Owner-hours reality:** Genuine **semi-absentee** ($500K buys a manager-run single unit or a
  small multi-unit with a GM) requires paying a manager out of that SDE. For a ~$500K buy you
  typically get EITHER (a) one owner-operated unit at full SDE, OR (b) a multi-unit where a
  manager already exists but net owner cash flow is thinner. Duck Donuts (#1) is the rare one
  that's already 3 units with 25–50 staff and store managers — closest to true semi-absentee in
  this price band.

## 4. Financing reality — CANADIAN citizen, NO SBA

- **SBA 7(a)/504 is OFF the table.** SBA requires US citizen or LPR (green card). A ~$500K
  franchise resale that a US buyer would grab with **10% down (~$50K) + SBA** is, for Hooman,
  a **full ~$500K all-cash** commitment. This is the single biggest structural disadvantage in
  this category — franchise resales are *priced assuming SBA-leveraged US buyers*, so all-cash
  Hooman is competing at a cash-flow-on-invested-capital disadvantage.
- **What works for him:**
  - **All-cash within $500K:** Duck Donuts ($500K), Budget Blinds ($425K), Home Inspection
    ($399K), Pool Maintenance ($240K), and everything below fit. Koala ($649K) and Houston Auto
    ($550K) do NOT fit all-cash.
  - **Seller note / vendor take-back:** Common on franchise resales (10–30% seller-financed,
    3–5 yr). Realistic path to stretch into the $600–700K range or preserve cash. Franchisor must
    still approve the buyer regardless of financing.
  - **Canadian BDC / bank:** BDC and Canadian banks finance business acquisitions, but they
    strongly prefer the **target be in Canada** (or a Canadian entity). A US-located franchise
    unit is hard to finance from a Canadian lender. **Cross-border ownership also creates
    immigration/tax friction** — running a US-based franchise hands-on as a Canadian likely needs
    an E-2 (treaty investor) visa if he wants to operate it himself on the ground; passive/remote
    ownership + a US manager is cleaner but then it must be truly manager-run.
- **FLAG:** The cleanest fit for Hooman is a **Canadian franchise resale** financed via BDC/bank
  with a seller VTB — but the deals captured here are US listings. A dedicated Canadian scan
  (Sunbelt Canada, businessesforsale.com/canada, franchise resale via CFA members) is warranted
  before committing.

## 5. Hooman-fit

- **Where his edge helps:** Diligence alpha (he can read an FDD, cohort-analyze recurring
  revenue, and price royalty drag correctly). Data/ops-heavy models — **home services with
  route density, scheduling, and CRM** (Pool Maintenance, Budget Blinds, Koala, floor covering)
  are where analytics + marketing automation can genuinely lift a sleepy unit. Multi-unit food
  (Duck Donuts) benefits less from his AI/data skills — it's execution/labor/local-marketing.
- **Where his edge is irrelevant:** The franchisor controls the product, pricing, brand, and
  often the tech stack. Franchise ownership deliberately **removes the levers** where Hooman
  creates value; he'd largely be an operator, not a builder. His software/AI skillset is
  **mostly wasted** in a food/fitness/senior-care franchise.
- **Time:** Sub-$500K franchise units are overwhelmingly **owner-operated, hands-on**. To keep
  his Adobe job he must buy a genuinely manager-run multi-unit (few at this price) or accept
  going full-time. Semi-absentee is more marketing promise than reality in this band.

## 6. Honest category verdict

**Franchises/multi-unit is a MEDIUM vein for the best ~$500K buy — real cash flow exists, but it
is a structurally poor fit for THIS buyer.** Three headwinds stack up:

1. **No SBA = he pays full price all-cash** while the market is priced for leveraged US buyers —
   worst-affected category in the whole side quest because franchise units are the canonical
   SBA-financed asset class.
2. **Royalty drag + franchisor control** cap upside and neutralize his AI/data edge.
3. **"Semi-absentee" is largely a myth sub-$500K** — most units need a hands-on owner; true
   manager-run multi-units cost more and yield thinner net-of-manager cash flow.

**Best sub-play if he pursues this anyway:** a **home-services franchise resale with recurring
revenue, route density, and a team already in place** — where his analytics/marketing-automation
can actually add value and physical presence is optional. Concretely:
- **Budget Blinds (St. Cloud, MN, $425K, ~$159K SDE)** — fits all-cash, low overhead, mobile,
  relationship-driven; improvable with better lead-gen/CRM.
- **Duck Donuts 3-store (Cary, NC, $500K, ~$187K SDE, $1.9M rev)** — best true multi-unit /
  closest to semi-absentee, award-winning operator, but food/labor-heavy and his skills add little.
- **Pool Maintenance (Chantilly, VA, $240K)** — cheapest recurring-revenue home-services entry.

**Recommendation:** Treat this category as a fallback, not a lead vein. If he wants it, prioritize
a **Canadian home-services resale** (financeable via BDC + seller VTB, no visa/tax friction) over
any US listing here. **Avoid boutique fitness entirely** (chronic low cash flow, high resale churn).
