## Category economics (honest, quantified)

- **Typical SDE multiple:** 2.0–3.0x SDE for owner-operated niche manufacturers, sign shops, and
  small distributors. Distributors of commodity products and route businesses skew higher on ask
  (4–5x seen above) but that's often overpriced — pure distribution has thin, fragile margins.
- **Cash yield at a fair buy:** a $370–550K all-cash purchase at ~2.4–2.75x SDE yields **$155K–$200K
  SDE/yr = ~$13K–$17K/month** pre-tax owner cash flow. On $500K cash that's a **~31–40% cash-on-cash
  yield** IF you run it yourself (SDE includes your salary — a big chunk is really wages for full-time
  owner labor, so treat maybe half as "return on capital" and half as "wage for your labor").
- **Owner-hours:** FULL-TIME, hands-on. These are 40–50 hr/wk owner-operator jobs (sales, quoting,
  production supervision, hiring, AR/AP). Sign/print shops especially are sales-and-service treadmills.
  Not passive. Route/distribution can be leaner (~20–30 hr) but lower ceiling.
- **Durability:** Sign & graphics and print/promo are mature, fragmented, local-relationship moats —
  durable but **low growth and partly AI/print-decline exposed** (commercial print is secularly
  shrinking; signage/large-format is more resilient). Niche distribution durability depends entirely on
  supplier + customer concentration (diligence-critical). Franchise-backed ones (Minuteman, national
  sign brands) trade support/brand for ~5–6% royalty drag on margin.

## Financing reality for a Canadian buyer (no SBA)

- **SBA 7(a)/504 is effectively OFF THE TABLE** — requires US citizen or LPR. Several of these
  listings (metal distribution, many sign franchises) are **SBA-pre-approved / SBA-marketed**, meaning
  their pricing assumes a US buyer putting 10% down and borrowing the rest. Hooman **cannot use that
  leverage**, so his real universe is **all-cash ≤$500K or all-cash + seller note.**
- **Seller financing is the key lever here** and is available on the two best deals (#1 Sign NM, #2
  Minuteman Print). A typical structure: 60–70% cash down + 30–40% seller note over 3–5 yr at 6–8%.
  That lets the $600K Minuteman deal fit inside $500K cash (e.g., $420K down + $180K seller note).
- **Franchise transfer approval:** franchised listings (Minuteman, national sign brands, metal-dist
  franchise) require **franchisor approval of the buyer** — a non-resident foreign buyer who intends to
  own but may need a US-based operator/manager is an added hurdle; verify each franchisor's stance and
  whether a US work-authorization / E-2 visa path is needed to actively operate on-site.
- **Immigration is the real gate, not the loan:** to full-time operate a US business on the ground,
  Hooman likely needs an **E-2 treaty-investor visa** (Canada is an E-2 treaty country — this is a
  genuine, well-trodden path; ~$500K comfortably meets "substantial investment"). This is the single
  most important diligence item for any US on-the-ground acquisition and applies to this whole category.
- **Canadian alternative:** if he'd rather stay in Canada, BDC / bank acquisition financing exists for
  Canadian-domiciled businesses, but this scan was US-listing-heavy. A parallel Canadian search (sign
  shops, distributors in ON/BC) would let him use BDC leverage AND avoid the visa problem.

## Hooman-fit (AI/data/EE, willing full-time)

- **Where his edge helps:** these businesses are almost universally **run on spreadsheets, paper
  quotes, and tribal knowledge.** An AI/data-engineering owner can build quoting automation, CRM +
  reorder/route optimization, demand forecasting for the distributor's inventory, and marketing
  automation — genuine value-creation levers that a retiring owner never touched. The signage-materials
  + CNC fabricator (#3) even overlaps his CutLog/laser-cutting domain (CNC, wide-format, plastics
  cutting) — real operational familiarity.
- **Where his edge is IRRELEVANT / a mismatch:** the core job is **local B2B sales, walk-in service,
  labor management, and physical production** — none of which is his strength or interest. He'd be
  buying a hands-on trade-services job, not a software company. His analytical/diligence ability is
  worth more here in *picking a good deal cheaply* than in *running it.*
- Net: he can improve margins 10–20% with software, but he'd spend most of his week on non-technical
  operator work. Founder-market fit is **moderate**, not high.

## Category verdict

**A solid, cheap-multiple vein — but a moderate, not top-tier, fit for THIS founder.** The economics are
genuinely good (2.0–2.75x SDE, 30–40% owner-operator cash yield, seller financing available), and it's
the classic recession-resilient "boring cash flow" play. The best single deals in this scan:

1. **Signage Materials + Custom Fabrication, Buncombe NC (#3)** — $370K / $155K SDE / 2.39x,
   fits all-cash comfortably, three diversified revenue streams, low customer concentration, and the
   CNC/plastics-cutting overlap with Hooman's existing domain makes it the **best sub-play** here.
2. **Minuteman Print & Promo, NY (#2)** — highest SDE ($310K) at a 1.93x multiple with seller financing;
   fits $500K cash via a seller note. Downside: commercial print is a declining substrate and it's
   franchised (royalty + transfer approval).
3. **Sign & Graphics, NM (#1)** — clean 2.75x, seller financing, 30-yr history, but franchised.

**Honest caveat:** every one of these is a full-time, hands-on, sales-and-production owner job in the US,
where Hooman faces an **E-2 visa requirement and NO SBA leverage** — so pricing built for a leveraged US
buyer means he must negotiate all-cash/seller-note and factor immigration. His AI/data edge is a
margin-improver, not a core fit. **Recommend treating this as a value/cash-flow backstop, and running a
parallel Canadian (BDC-financeable) sign/distribution search to sidestep the visa + no-SBA problems.**
Distribution-only and route businesses in this set are overpriced (4–5x) and should be skipped;
niche manufacturing + custom fabrication is the durable, improvable core of the category.
