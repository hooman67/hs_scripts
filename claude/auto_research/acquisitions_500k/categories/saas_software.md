## 1. Category economics (real, from the marketplaces browsed)

Sources browsed 2026-07-03: BizBuySell (software & app category, pages 1–5),
Empire Flippers marketplace, BizQuest software category, FE International.

**Typical multiples in the $200K–$600K software band:**
- BizBuySell market context (self-reported medians): median software asking price
  **~$1.0M**, valuation averages **~2.06x revenue / ~3.60x earnings (SDE/EBITDA)**.
  Source: bizbuysell.com/software-and-app-companies-for-sale.
- The **sub-$600K** band skews to two very different animals:
  1. **Real vertical B2B SaaS with cash flow** — trades ~**2.5x–3.5x SDE** (e.g. EH&S
     compliance SaaS $325K on $110K SDE = **~3.0x**; email-service-provider SaaS $345K on
     $115.7K SDE = **~3.0x**).
  2. **App/AI "starter" listings** — either giveaway multiples on distressed/declining
     revenue (Intermittent Fasting app $200K on $166K EBITDA = 1.2x, a red flag not a
     bargain) or inflated multiples on tiny MRR (AI Assistant app $575K at 5.5x).
- **Empire Flippers / Acquire micro-SaaS**: content/subscription assets commonly at
  **~30–40x monthly profit (≈2.5x–3.3x annual)**; the one in-band SaaS on EF (#94153) was
  32x monthly BUT profit −50% YoY.

**Cash yield a ~$500K all-cash buy realistically produces:**
- At a fair ~3.0x SDE multiple, **$500K buys ~$165K SDE ≈ $13–14K/month** pre-tax owner
  cash flow, ~33% cash-on-cash — IF the revenue is stable. That is a strong yield vs any
  passive alternative and the whole reason software beats most categories on this metric.
- Caveat: SaaS SDE is only "real" if churn is low and the product isn't AI-commoditizable.
  Many sub-$600K "SaaS" listings are services dressed as SaaS or have declining revenue,
  which drops the *durable* yield well below the headline.

**Owner-hours**: Highly variable. True product SaaS with a support/dev contractor can run
**~10–25 hr/wk** (part-time viable). "SEO services / agency-with-software" listings that
dominate this price band are effectively **full-time service businesses (30–50 hr/wk)** —
recurring revenue but owner-and-labor-dependent, not passive.

## 3. Financing reality for a CANADIAN citizen (no SBA)

**The core constraint holds.** SBA 7(a)/504 program rules (SBA SOP 50 10) require the
operating business to be at least 51% owned by US citizens or lawful permanent residents
(green-card holders). A **Canadian citizen with no US permanent residency generally cannot
use standard SBA leverage** to buy a US business. (SBA does allow certain "Legal Permanent
Resident" / conditional structures, but not a non-resident foreign national.) The classic
US "10% down, SBA covers the rest" playbook is **off the table** unless Hooman first
obtains LPR status. Verify his exact status before assuming any US-loan path.

**What this means per deal:**
- The listings above are almost all **US-based**, so US buyer-financing (SBA, most US bank
  acquisition lines) is likely unavailable to him.
- **All-cash within ~$500K is the reliable path.** Listings A ($325K), B ($345K), F
  ($377K) fit all-cash with buffer; D/E ($500K) fit all-cash with no buffer.
- **Seller note / vendor take-back** is the most realistic leverage: on software deals a
  10–30% seller note (paid from the business's own cash flow) is common and does NOT
  require citizenship. Prioritize sellers open to a note — it also aligns their incentives.
- **Canadian options (BDC, Canadian banks)** finance acquisitions but strongly prefer the
  *target* to be Canadian-domiciled with Canadian cash flow; they are a poor fit for buying
  a US software company. Realistically not a lever for these US listings.
- **Net**: treat every deal here as an **all-cash-or-seller-note** decision. Flagged per
  listing above (all in-band ones are FITS-ALL-CASH except C and H which are OVER-500K).

## 5. Category verdict

**This is a STRONG vein — arguably the strongest category for Hooman specifically** —
because it maximizes his two edges (technical diligence + value creation) and produces the
best cash yield per dollar (fair deals ~3x SDE → ~33% cash-on-cash all-cash). It fits the
all-cash-only Canadian financing reality well: real, durable, sub-$500K vertical B2B SaaS
exists at ~3x SDE and can be bought outright with cash to spare.

**Best sub-play: bootstrapped, boring, regulation-adjacent VERTICAL B2B SaaS at ~3x SDE**
— e.g. the EH&S/SDS compliance software ($325K / $110K SDE / ~3.0x) or the 17-year email
provider ($345K / $115.7K SDE / 88% margin). Sticky recurring revenue, mandate-driven
demand, diversified customers, priced fairly, all-cash-affordable with buffer, and directly
improvable by an AI/infra engineer.

**Avoid within the category**: consumer AI-wrapper apps (commoditization), "SaaS" listings
that are service/SEO agencies (owner-labor-dependent, no tech edge), declining-revenue
assets priced at a "discount" (#94153, −50% YoY), and ARR-priced growth stories at 15–20x
SDE (AI Fundraising SaaS) where you pay for a future that may not arrive.

**Diligence must-dos before any offer**: verify gated cash flow, pull cohort/churn data,
confirm no single-customer concentration, assess AI-commoditization risk of the core
feature, and confirm the deal works **all-cash or with a seller note** (no SBA).
