# Physical / Real-Asset Businesses (Semi-Absentee) — Acquisition Scan @ ~$500K

**Scope:** Laundromats / coin laundry, self-storage & RV/boat storage, car washes, vending/ATM
routes. **Buyer:** Hooman — Canadian citizen (NOT a US person), ~$500K own cash, willing to go
full-time. **Date:** 2026-07-03. **Sources:** BizBuySell, BizQuest, LoopNet (real current
listings). All numbers are as displayed on the marketplaces on the fetch date; where the
marketplace gated a field it is marked "gated / sign-in".

---

## 0. Financing reality for a Canadian buyer (READ FIRST — this reshapes the whole category)

- **SBA 7(a)/504 is OFF the table.** As of March 2025 (Exec. Order 14159) the SBA banned
  non-citizens/non-permanent-residents from SBA-guaranteed loans; previously foreign nationals
  could own ≤49% and still qualify — that flexibility is gone. A Canadian citizen with no green
  card **cannot** get the standard "10% down, SBA covers the rest" leverage that most US
  laundromat/car-wash/storage buyers rely on. (Source: DDG search synthesis of SBA SOP + EO 14159.)
- **Consequence:** the classic play — buy a $1.2M car wash or storage facility with $120K down +
  SBA — is unavailable. Hooman is effectively an **all-cash or seller-note buyer capped near
  ~$500K of enterprise value**, unless he uses:
  - **Seller financing / vendor take-back:** common in this category; sellers often carry 20–50%
    over 3–7 yrs. This is his single most important lever — it lets a ~$700–900K asset fit inside
    ~$400–500K cash down.
  - **Canadian financing (BDC / Canadian banks):** BDC lends to Canadian-domiciled businesses;
    financing a **US-located** hard asset from Canada is awkward and generally not what BDC does.
    A Canadian bank will not mortgage a US car wash for him. So Canadian leverage really only helps
    if he buys a **Canadian** laundromat/storage/car wash.
  - **Conventional US commercial bank / local credit union:** possible but they'll want a US entity,
    US credit history, larger down (25–40%), and often a US guarantor. Realistically a fallback.
- **Bottom line:** For US deals, model everything as **all-cash ≤$500K or seller-note-assisted up
  to ~$800K EV**. Do NOT assume SBA leverage. Flag any deal that "only works with SBA."

## 4. VENDING / ATM ROUTES

**Category economics (BizBuySell vending & ATM medians, live):** median asking **$145,000**,
median revenue **$154,000**, median owner earnings **$76,300** → ~1.9x SDE. **Cheapest, most
cash-fit category** — Hooman could buy multiple routes or one large route well within $500K.
Multiples are low (1.5–3x SDE) because routes are **low-moat**: locations churn, machines are
depreciating assets, and it's a driving/restocking labor business (semi-absentee only if you hire
drivers). Cash yield can look high (~40–50% at 2x) but is offset by location loss, theft/vandalism,
and product cost inflation. ATM routes are lighter-labor (cash loading + uptime) but face secular
**cash-usage decline** — a real durability question.

**Verdict:** **Cheapest and most cash-fit, but weakest moat and durability.** Great if the goal is

needs a seller note. ATM sub-segment is in secular decline (cash usage). Several listings show
"cash flow ≥ asking price," which is almost always a mis-scrape or aggressive owner add-backs —
treat with heavy skepticism. **Hooman's data/route-optimization edge is real here** (dynamic
restocking, location analytics) but the underlying asset is too churny to be a "best buy."

## 5. Head-to-head verdict for THIS category group

| Sub-category | Fits $500K all-cash? | Cash yield | Durability | Passivity | Hooman-fit | Overall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Laundromats** | Yes (several) | ~15–25% | High | High | High (utility-data diligence) | **Best fit** |
| Self-storage / RV-boat | Rarely (RE-priced) | ~6% unlevered | Highest | Highest | Med | Best asset, worst fit for a non-SBA cash buyer |
| Car washes (self-serve) | Yes | ~20–28% (volatile) | Med | Med | Med | Fair; maintenance/weather/competition risk |
| Vending/ATM routes | Yes (easily) | ~30–50% (churny) | Low (ATM declining) | Low unless staffed | Med-High (route optimization) | Cheapest, weakest moat |

**The strongest ~$500K vein in this category = LAUNDROMATS**, bought all-cash or with a seller
note, chosen on **verified water/utility data + a long secure lease + recent equipment.** It is the
only sub-category that is simultaneously cash-fit for a non-SBA Canadian, high-yield, semi-absentee,
AI-safe, and directly diligenceable with Hooman's data skills. Self-storage is the better *asset*
but the financing structure (no SBA, no US mortgage) makes it a poor fit at $500K — it would only
work as a small Canadian facility with BDC/bank debt. Car washes and vending are cash-fit but carry
either operational fragility (car wash) or moat/durability weakness (vending/ATM).

**Top single-deal candidates to diligence (all fit ~$500K):**
1. **Super-Sized Laundromat, Houston TX** — $650K ask / $230K SDE (~2.8x). Seller note to fit
   cash. Verify utility records; if real, best cash-on-cash in the whole scan.
2. **Laundromat, Fords NJ** — $285K / $95K SDE (~3.0x). Clean all-cash, high yield, dense market.
3. **2 Self-Service 5-Bay Car Washes, Saint Charles MO** — $450K / $166K SDE (~2.7x). All-cash;
   verify equipment age + meter data.
4. **The Realty TX Washateria (Highway 6), Houston** — $549K / $101K EBITDA (~5.5x). All-cash edge,
   professionally listed with EBITDA stated (easier diligence).

**Diligence alpha for Hooman (his edge):** in this category the #1 lever is verifying real cash
volume. For laundromats/car washes that means **regression of water/sewer/electric utility bills
against reported revenue** — a data task he can do better than 99% of buyers. For vending it means
**per-machine sell-through analytics.** This is exactly where his analytical ability converts into
a lower purchase price / avoided lemon.
