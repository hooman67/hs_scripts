## 1. The US Financing Reality — what each status unlocks and WHEN

The single most important fact about the US path: **his immigration status, not the deals, is the
binding constraint.** (Source: `WIDE_SEARCH_REPORT.md` §4; `PROJECT_STATE.md` 2026-07-04 entries.)

| Status | SBA loan? | Self-operate a US business? | What it unlocks | WHEN |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **TN visa (today)** | ❌ NO — SOP 50 10 (reinforced by the Mar-2025 EO 14159 change) requires the operating business to be ≥51% owned by US citizens / lawful permanent residents | ❌ NO — TN bars self-employment; hands-on US operating needs another visa | **All-cash US deals only** (~$500K ceiling), or **remote/semi-absentee** assets where he isn't the hands-on operator | Now |
| **E-2 treaty-investor visa (interim option)** | Still ❌ no SBA (not a citizen/LPR) | ✅ YES — lets him actively run the US business he invested in | A hands-on US operator role WITHOUT waiting for the GC. Canada IS an E-2 treaty country; **~$500K clears the "substantial investment" bar.** Cost: it's a real filing, tied to that one business, and adds friction | Interim (weeks–months to file; lawyer-required) |
| **Green card / LPR (SOON)** | ✅ YES — SBA 7(a)/504 reopens | ✅ YES — can operate US businesses freely | The **full US market at ~10% down**: franchises, self-storage, car-wash-with-RE, larger SDE. Same $500K down → ~$1–2M business | Expected soon (timing = Hooman's info) |
| **Buy under 2yo daughter (US citizen)** | Nominally yes (she's a citizen) | Messy | A theoretical way to satisfy the citizen-ownership test — but **legally messy, custodial/UTMA + control issues, LAWYER-ONLY**; do not pursue without counsel | Not recommended |

**His only leverage on TN = a SELLER NOTE / vendor take-back** (10–50% of price, paid from the
business's own cash flow). No citizenship test, aligns the seller's incentives, and lets a
~$650–700K asset fit inside ~$500K cash. **Strongly structure 30–50% as a seller note on any
decaying-risk asset** (FBA, content, car wash) to shift downside to the seller.
(SBA financing was explicitly REJECTED anyway — see §5, the personal-guarantee trap.)

**The timing logic (decisive):** *Canada works TODAY* (BDC ≈ SBA leverage + no visa issue owning/
operating a Canadian company remotely from the US on TN). *The US SBA path is locked until the GC
lands.* So even though the GC reopens the US, **waiting for it has an opportunity cost that buying
Canadian does not.** (Source: `CANADA_SEARCH_REPORT.md` §4.)

## 3. The Live-Listings Reality — what's actually on the shelf

The playbook (`buy_microsaas_PLAYBOOK.md`) plus two live scans (`buy_microsaas_LIVE_LISTINGS.md`,
`buy_microsaas_300K_UPDATE.md`) tested the SaaS end against **real current inventory** across
Acquire.com, Flippa, Empire Flippers, Microns, Latona's/MotionInvest.

### At the $150K ceiling — nothing clean
Of **~37 real listings captured (2026-07-01/02), ZERO were a clean, fairly-priced Buy-Box pass.**
33 died on the paper screen (consumer / AI-wrapper / SEO-content / single-platform add-on / over
budget / <18mo history). **Only 3 warranted a free-account seller call — the "dry-run" candidates —
and each had a live deal-killer:**

| # | Candidate | Price | Net/mo | Multiple | The specific kill-until-proven flaw |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | **IMS/ISO compliance SaaS** (Flippa #11797351) — best FIT (6yr compliance system-of-record) | $139K | ~€1,154 | ~9x profit (~2x overpriced) | Only **9 subscribers** → near-certain >20% concentration; undefined 11% churn |
| B | **Healthcare exercise-mgmt SaaS** (Microns) — best TECH/moat (real Java/AWS code, ~4yr) | $120K | ~$1,504 | 3.8x (only one inside fair 4–4.5x) | 35 subs; undefined "15%" churn; thin 45% margin; incumbents (Physitrack/Medbridge) |
| C | **Property-mgmt AI SaaS** (Flippa #12279687) — backup, best churn (2.9%), 5yr | $109K | ~CAD 1,616 | ~6.3x | Must confirm it's a workflow/records tool with a data moat, NOT a thin LLM wrapper; 16 subs |

**Delegation verdict (all three identical):** at ~$1.2–1.6K/mo net they sit **below the ~$4K/mo
threshold** where a VA+dev pencils → **Hooman IS the operator (8–12 hr/wk); economics after his time
≈ $0.** Part-time jobs with resale optionality, not passive income.

### At the $300K ceiling — crosses the delegation threshold
Raising the ceiling to $300K changed the math decisively: **$300K at a fair 4–4.5x buys ~$4.2–6.25K/mo
net, which clears the ~$4K/mo delegation threshold** (vs. ~$1.5–2.5K/mo at $150K, which did not). So
the old "Hooman is always the operator, ~$0 after his time" conclusion **no longer holds at the top of
the new band** — a ~$5.5K/mo asset can carry a $1.5–2.5K/mo VA+dev and still leave ~$3–4K/mo at
~8–12 hr/wk. **The two "good shape" listings deep-dived at $300K:**

- **Building-Ops Platform (Acquire) — PASS (OVER-CEILING).** The *ideal* shape: sticky vertical B2B
  CMMS/system-of-record, ~$438K ARR / ~$399K SDE (~$33K/mo net), 91% margin, AI-safe, delegable ~8x
  over threshold. **But implied ask ~$1.6–1.8M = 5–6x over even $300K.** Only reachable via **SBA
  7(a) (personal guarantee)** — which breaks the capped-downside thesis (and isn't available on TN
  anyway). "What good looks like"; still unbuyable. The buyable version is a 5–10x-smaller sibling
  that isn't listed today.
- **Amazon-Sourcing SaaS (EF #91852) — CONDITIONAL (FITS $300K).** Asking $193K; net $7,716/mo; 80
  subs; ~5.75yr. The **first asset that BOTH fits the budget AND is big enough to delegate**
  (~$5–6K/mo after VA+dev), AND the **only one where Hooman's data-engineering is genuine
  VALUE-CREATION** — the 40hr/wk load is scraper/data-pipeline maintenance (self-healing scrapers,
  LLM layout-change detection, match/QA automation = his wheelhouse). **BUT it breaks two Buy Box
  rules**: Amazon single-platform dependency (double: demand needs online-arbitrage to stay viable
  AND supply relies on scraping/SP-API/Keepa that can be blocked) + a **decaying-scraper moat**
  (effort/maintenance barrier, not proprietary data); the cheap ~2x-annual multiple on a 6yr SaaS is
  a **live decline flag.** Worth a Gate-2 call; **kill fast if the 24-mo Stripe trend is down or the
  margin is ad-fed.**

## 4. The SaaS-Exemplar Finding — the two favorites weren't real deals

Hooman's two favorite SaaS picks from the wide report — **(#2) EH&S/SDS compliance SaaS
~$325K/$110K SDE** and **(#3) email-service-provider SaaS ~$345K/$116K SDE** — were deep-dived to
check whether they were actual listings. (Source: `SAAS_DEEPDIVE_REPORT.md`.)

**Verdict: BOTH WERE CATEGORY EXEMPLARS, NOT LIVE DEALS.** The dollar figures were representative
archetypes ("what a ~3x deal in this category looks like"), not quotes from a real listing. A targeted
hunt across BizBuySell, Flippa, Acquire.com, Empire Flippers, Quiet Light, and FE International found
**zero confirmed, on-thesis, in-budget listings** in either segment on the public surface. Confidence:
MEDIUM (Acquire/EF/FE/QuietLight are login-gated + JS-rendered — a ~1-hr manual buyer-account pass is
still owed to fully rule the segments out).

- **EH&S / SDS = the more defensible buy, but trades OFF-MARKET.** Real **mandate-driven
  system-of-record moat** (OSHA HazCom / Tier II / GHS; the customer's chemical inventory + audit
  history IS their system of record) — durable + remote + mandate-driven, all three of Hooman's
  criteria. **But net-new growth is brutal** (VelocityEHS/MSDSonline dominates; it's a PE roll-up
  zone), so a small player is a **hold-and-milk asset**, and the good small players **get rolled up
  by incumbents before they ever list.** The only live hit was BizBuySell #2461775 ($880K rev / $413K
  EBITDA, vertical deliberately hidden, implied ~$1.5–2M+, over budget). **The path is buy-side
  outreach** (SafeTec, Sitehawk, KHA/365, MSDSDigital/SDS Manager, SafeSheet, MySDS Manager,
  Chemwatch) + a broker mandate + standing alerts — **not marketplace browsing.** Treat the
  $325K/$110K figure as an *aspiration to source toward*, not a deal on the shelf.
- **Email / ESP = DROP IT.** The **weakest segment in the entire search.** Commodity economics
  (Mailchimp/Klaviyo/Brevo free tiers to 500–2,500 contacts), race-to-the-bottom transactional
  pricing (Amazon SES $0.10/1,000), **fragile sending-IP reputation** (Google/Yahoo 2024 bulk-sender
  rules can impair the core asset overnight), and **AI trivializes cloning.** A 3x there is a decline
  discount, not a bargain. The only real listing found was a 12-yr email *client* — off-thesis, ~$5M+
  (15x over budget), 40% churn.

**Consequence for the pipeline:** the more *immediately actionable* US picks are the wide report's
**laundromat (#1) and pest-control (#3)** — which DO have live, verifiable listings today — rather
than either SaaS exemplar, whose acquisition path is a slower active-sourcing game.

## 5. Big Cross-Cutting Learnings

These recur across every track and are the highest-leverage takeaways. (Sources: `LEARNINGS.md`,
`buy_microsaas_PLAYBOOK.md`, `WIDE_SEARCH_REPORT.md`.)

1. **Moat is the KILL dimension.** Across 40+ cycles and the acquisition search, the thing that
   decides survival isn't market size, capital, or founder-fit — it's whether a defensible asset
   accrues to *the owner*. "Cheap + trivial to build/clone" is a *failure* signal, not a green light.
   In the acquisition world this shows up as: platform-dependent (Amazon/Shopify), commodity
   (email/ESP), and decaying-scraper moats all get demoted; **mandate-driven system-of-record**
   (EH&S/SDS, ISO/compliance) and **switching-cost + contract-book** (pest control) survive.

2. **BUY > BUILD for this founder.** His one real weakness is **distribution/sales to fragmented
   buyers** — and buying an existing business *inherits the customers*, sidestepping the 0→1
   distribution grind entirely. Every from-scratch build the project red-teamed lost 2–4 points to a
   false "no competition" premise; buying skips that. **His engineering edge is DILIGENCE ALPHA
   (spotting traps others can't), not value-creation alpha** — small B2B SaaS churns on
   relationships/support/domain trust, not code quality, so don't pay a premium expecting to
   re-rate via a rewrite. (The rare value-creation exception: data-pipeline-heavy assets like the
   Amazon-sourcing SaaS, where self-healing scrapers/LLM-QA are genuinely his wheelhouse.)

3. **TIME is the constraint, not capital or ideas.** ~10–20 hr/wk = ONE serious venture, and CutLog
   already spends it. The right filter for a SECOND stream: does it consume **capital + delegation**
   or his **scarce focus**? Prefer capital-consuming shapes. The **delegation-affordability rule**:
   delegation (VA+dev) only pencils **above ~$4K/mo net**; below that the owner IS the operator. The
   affordable small asset can't fund delegation; the delegatable one needs >$300K.

4. **Citizenship RESHAPES the whole problem.** The US path's entire handicap ("no SBA → 3–4x smaller
   SDE for the same cash") is a *US-asset* problem, not a *Canadian-buyer* problem. As a Canadian
   citizen buying on Canadian soil, **BDC (~10–25% down) substitutes cleanly for SBA** — so he is NOT
   under-levered in Canada. The decisive difference between US and Canada is therefore **TIMING +
   REMOTE-OPERABILITY, not leverage size**: Canada works today with no visa issue; the US SBA path is
   locked until the green card.

5. **AVOID SBA / personal-guarantee financing even when it becomes available.** SBA 7(a) acquisition
   loans require a personal guarantee (often a lien on the home) — that **destroys the capped,
   cash-only downside** that is the whole point of buy-and-hold. All-cash caps max loss at price minus
   resale; an SBA-leveraged deal can lose *more than the cash in* via the PG. (This is a preference
   independent of the eligibility block.)

6. **Structural traps to reject on sight** (they don't survive for this buyer): Amazon FBA
   single-brand, SEO/content & YouTube (AI-Overview decay), franchises + self-storage (priced for
   leverage he can't use on TN), commodity email/ESP.
