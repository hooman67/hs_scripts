## 4. Financing reality for a Canadian all-cash ~$500K buyer

- **SBA 7(a)/504 is OFF the table.** SBA SOP 50 10 (reinforced by the March 2025 EO 14159 change)
  requires the operating business to be ≥51% owned by US citizens or lawful permanent residents. A
  Canadian citizen with no US green card cannot use it. **VERIFY his exact residency first** — but
  base case = no SBA.
- **What that costs him:** the entire US small-biz market is priced for leveraged buyers putting
  ~10% down. With the SAME $500K cash, a US buyer reaches a **$1–2M** business (10% down + SBA 90%).
  Hooman reaches only a **~$500K all-cash** business — roughly **3–4x smaller SDE** for the same cash
  out of pocket. Franchises, storage, car-wash-with-RE, and higher-ticket HVAC/pest are the deals
  most distorted by this (their asking prices assume leverage he can't get).
- **His only real leverage = a SELLER NOTE / vendor take-back** (10–50%, paid from the business's
  own cash flow). No citizenship test, aligns seller incentives, and lets a ~$650–700K asset fit
  inside ~$500K cash. **Strongly advise structuring 30–50% as a seller note on any decaying-risk
  asset** (FBA, content, car wash) to shift downside to the seller.
- **Canadian financing (BDC / CSBFP / banks)** only really helps if he buys a **Canadian-domiciled**
  business — they will not conveniently mortgage a US hard asset. Worth a **parallel ON/BC search**
  (Sunbelt Canada, businessesforsale.com/canada) which would ALSO sidestep the E-2 visa issue.
- **Immigration flag:** operating a US business hands-on likely needs an **E-2 treaty-investor visa**
  (Canada IS an E-2 country; ~$500K clears the "substantial investment" bar). This is the #1
  diligence item for any US operator business, and a reason SaaS/remote/semi-absentee assets are
  cleaner than hands-on local service for him.

category and the deepest use of his two edges.

(delegable to techs). *Edge REAL:* route-density/dispatch optimization, churn/renewal prediction,

daily grind. *What goes wrong:* 40-yr owner-succession/transfer risk; applicator license (exam, not a
blocker); no cash buffer at $500K all-cash. The 1000-contract book is the durable moat.

## 6. Bottom line — does wider + bigger + full-time beat the part-time buy-SaaS plan?

**Partly yes, and it changes the recommendation.** Going full-time and up to $500K all-cash unlocks
**genuine $10–18K/mo owner cash flow** that the part-time ≤$300K plan could not reach — a real path to
strong income. But it does NOT crown a single new category: the honest read is a **barbell**.

- **If Hooman wants maximum use of his rare edges + best yield + remote/no-visa:** buy a **boring
  vertical B2B SaaS at ~3x** (EH&S/SDS or the email provider). This is the ONLY category where his
  AI/infra/code skills are true *value creation*, not a marginal ops tweak, and it delivers the best
  cash-on-cash (~33%). This is essentially the main-research conclusion, **validated even after
  widening the aperture** — SaaS earned it, wasn't defaulted to.
- **If he wants the most durable, lowest-hours, most diligence-verifiable asset for the cash:** buy a
  **verified laundromat** — the standout non-SaaS pick, uniquely fitting his no-SBA all-cash reality
  and his data-diligence edge, semi-absentee so the day job stays optional.
- **If he genuinely wants to go full-time operating for max absolute cash:** a **recurring-route pest/
  pool business** or the **Buncombe CNC fab** delivers $13–18K/mo, but his technical edge is only a
  margin lever there, and he takes on labor management + an E-2 visa.

**The traps to avoid** (all melting, platform-dependent, or priced for leverage he can't use): Amazon
FBA single-brand, SEO/YouTube content, franchises, and self-storage. **Recommended next step:** verify
residency/E-2 status, run a **parallel Canadian (ON/BC) search** to unlock BDC leverage + skip the
visa, and open diligence on the EH&S SaaS (his edge) and the Fords NJ laundromat (his cash-fit
durable asset) as the two lead candidates.
