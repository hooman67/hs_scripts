## 2. Vending route economics (the reality check)

Sourced numbers (VERIFIED via cited sources):

- **Gross margin / COGS:** product COGS runs **40–50% of revenue**; **net profit ≈ 25–30% of gross.** A "$1,200/mo machine" nets **~$330/mo (27.5%)**, *not* $800–1,000. (VendBuddy). The listing's per-machine net claim is **~2.5–3x the industry norm.**
- **Net per machine, industry range:** **$160–$825/month.** (VendBuddy). $800–1,000 sits at/above the very top of the observed range for *every* machine — implausible as a route average.
- **Valuation multiple:** vending routes sell for **0.5–1.1x SDE (0.7–1.7x EBITDA)**; median asking ~$30K on median cash flow ~$54K — i.e., buyers routinely pay **less than one year's earnings.** (Regalis Capital). Multiples are compressed because **"you're not buying equipment, you're buying location relationships"** that can walk.
- **Operating costs the SDE must survive:** location commission **10–15% of sales or $50–200/mo flat**; card processing **5–6%** of cashless; telemetry/software **$40–100/mo per machine**; repairs **$50–150/mo averaged**; insurance **$500–1,200/yr**. (VendBuddy).
- **Machine cost / capex:** used combo machines **$3,000–6,000 for 1–2 units**; HUMAN-type "healthy" machines were sold at **$12,000 + $1,200 placement.** (VendBuddy; UnhappyFranchisee). Machines depreciate and need bill/coin/card-reader service; **deferred capex is the classic hidden liability** in a route sale.
- **Why vending SDE is notoriously inflated (ASSUMED/industry-known):** heavy **cash handling** (unrecorded skim inflates apparent margin or hides shrink), **shrink/theft/spoilage** on perishable "healthy" SKUs, **owner labor booked at $0** (the route driver is often the owner — remove them and SDE drops by a real wage), and **no reserve for machine replacement.**

**Bottom line on economics:** even taking the gross at face value, a defensible SDE is **~$71–85K**, and a defensible *market* price at the asset-class multiple is **~$50–90K**, not $135K. The asking looks **~40–90% over the norm.**

## 3. The "healthier snacks" angle — differentiator or fad?

- **Not a durable moat.** Industry consensus: "healthy vending is real demand in offices/gyms/clinics — **but going 100% healthy is a trap**"; winners **mix** healthy + conventional. (VendBuddy). Healthy-only skews SKU cost up, shelf life down (**more spoilage/shrink**), and price points higher (slower turns).
- **The franchise history is a warning.** HUMAN Healthy Vending drew documented complaints that profit claims didn't match franchisee reality at **$12K+/machine.** (UnhappyFranchisee). "Healthy vending" as a *pitch* has a track record of over-promising. This listing leans on the same narrative ("consumers increasingly prefer healthier options").
- **No proprietary anything.** The "cloud monitoring / touchscreen" is commodity telemetry (Nayax/Cantaloupe/365 hardware). Nothing here is defensible IP or a founder-fit for an AI/ML engineer.

## 4. Bay Area specifics + competition

- **Location relationships are the whole asset, and the Bay is the worst market for them.** Premium locations (tech campuses, gyms, hospitals) are **locked up by Canteen/Compass, Aramark, and 365-powered micro-market operators**, and command the highest commissions. The industry is **consolidating** — 365 Retail Markets just closed an **$848M acquisition of Cantaloupe** (VendingMarketWatch), squeezing independents.
- **Cost structure is punishing:** CA min-wage + Bay Area labor means a hired route driver is **$22–30+/hr loaded** (ASSUMED, regional norm); gas/parking/tolls are high; a mid-market snack route's thin net doesn't absorb that well. The moment you pay someone to drive the route (which Hooman must — see §5/§6), **SDE compresses materially.**
- **Location churn risk:** month-to-month or handshake placements can be poached by a bigger operator offering a higher commission. Ask for **written, assignable location agreements** — their absence is a value-killer.

## 5. IMMIGRATION — the actual deal-killer (FLAG for an immigration lawyer)

This is consistent with the repo's existing `US_STATUS_AND_LEARNINGS.md` finding and it kills *this* deal as framed:

- **Hooman on TN cannot self-operate a US business.** TN bars self-employment / working for your own company; **USCIS reinforced this in a July 2025 policy-manual update** explicitly banning self-employment on TN. (rjimmigrationlaw.com; visaverge.com; ogletree.com). "Semi-passive route he oversees" is a gray zone — *overseeing/managing* is arguably active work, not passive investment. **Do not assume it's fine.**
- **Passive ownership/investment is the safer lane**, but "personally oversee the machines / drive to locations" is exactly the active operation TN prohibits. (bridgelegal.org; thelegalguide.org).
- **The wife's work authorization — FLAGGED, and it's likely a hard NO.** If she is a **TD dependent spouse, she CANNOT work in the US** — TD confers no employment authorization. (thelegalguide.org). So "his wife runs it" **does not solve the problem** unless she has independent status (green card / H-1B / L / her own TN / EAD). **Her status is UNKNOWN — this must be confirmed first.** If she can't legally restock or manage, the only compliant option is a **fully hired operator**, which (a) guts the already-thin SDE and (b) makes "personally oversee" impossible.
- **SBA financing on the listing is irrelevant:** SBA 7(a) needs ≥51% US-citizen/LPR ownership and a personal guarantee — **not available to Hooman on TN.** So the "financing available" hook doesn't apply to him; he'd be **all-cash**, paying a rich multiple for a low-multiple asset.

**Structuring question for the lawyer (don't self-answer):** Can Hooman hold this as a truly passive LLC investment with a *fully arms-length hired manager*, no active involvement by him, and a spouse who is not work-authorized — without violating TN? Likely requires either (a) waiting for the green card, or (b) a compliant W-2 operator + genuinely passive ownership. Either way it stops being "a business he/his wife personally oversees."

## 6. Is "semi-passive" real? (No.)

- The claim is "~1 hr/machine/week, 30 min onsite." For a route grossing $284K that's plausibly **8–20+ machines** → **8–20+ hrs/week of driving, restocking, cash pickup, bill-jam/reader service, and reorder/inventory** across the Bay Area, *plus* location relationship management and repairs. That is a **part-time-to-near-full-time route job, not passive income.**
- "Semi-passive" in vending means **someone drives the route** — the only question is whether it's the owner (SDE looks good, no wage booked) or a paid driver (SDE drops). **There is no version where nobody restocks.**
- For an Adobe AI/ML engineer with a demanding day job, this is a **time sink with commodity economics and no founder-market fit** — the opposite of what makes CutLog-type software attractive.

## 8. What to check on a site visit (if Hooman/wife go anyway)

**Financials (get before wasting a Saturday):**
- 2–3 years of **tax returns + bank/merchant-processor statements** (Nayax/Cantaloupe/365 dashboard export). Reconcile card revenue to claimed gross — **cash portion is where inflation hides.**
- **Exact machine count and per-machine monthly sales** from the telemetry dashboard (not a spreadsheet). Look for **1–2 machines carrying >40% of revenue** (concentration).
- Written, **assignable location agreements** with terms, commission %, and expiration. Handshake deals = the asset can walk.

**On-site, per location:**
- Machine **age, condition, card-reader model** (EMV/contactless or obsolete?), and whether telemetry actually works.
- **Foot traffic** at your visit time vs. claimed — count transactions/hour if possible.
- **Commission** the location actually charges; is the host happy or shopping for a new operator?
- **Product freshness / spoilage** on "healthy" SKUs; check for expired stock (shrink signal).
- Is there a **competing micro-market or another operator's machine** nearby?

**Operational reality:**
- Ride the **full route once** with the current owner: total drive time, restock time, cash pickup, jam/repair incidents. Time it. That's your real "hours."
- Ask **who currently drives the route** and what you'd pay to replace them (your true SDE hit).
- Machine **replacement schedule / deferred capex**: any coin-only or dead machines? Reader lease obligations?

**Before any of the above — the ONE gating call:** an **immigration attorney** on (a) whether Hooman can hold this passively on TN, and (b) his wife's exact status and work authorization. If both are "no," **stop here.**
