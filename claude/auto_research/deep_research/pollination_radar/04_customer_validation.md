# 04 - Customer Validation: Precision Pollination Monitoring Radar

## First 5 Customers to Approach

### 1. Mid-size Almond Grower (Tech-Forward)
- **Profile:** 400-800 acre almond operation in Kern or Fresno County, CA. Already uses precision ag (drone NDVI, soil moisture sensors, variable-rate irrigation). Owner-operator, age 35-55.
- **Why them:** Large enough to have budget pain from pollination ($80K-$200K/year spend), tech-savvy enough to trial novel hardware, small enough that decision-maker is accessible.
- **How to find:** California Almond Board member list, attendees at Almond Conference (Sacramento, Dec annually), UC Davis Extension precision ag workshops.

### 2. Large Almond Farming Corporation
- **Profile:** 2,000-10,000 acre operation (e.g., Wonderful Orchards, Blue Diamond member, Olam/Rivermaid). Has dedicated agronomist or "technology director" role.
- **Why them:** At scale, even 2-3% yield improvement from optimized pollination = $200K-$1M incremental revenue. They have R&D budgets and pilot programs.
- **How to find:** Almond Board of California corporate members, AgTech conference exhibitor halls, LinkedIn search "almond" + "precision agriculture."

### 3. Organic Berry Grower (Pacific Northwest)
- **Profile:** 100-500 acre organic blueberry/raspberry operation in Oregon or Washington. Organic certification makes pollination especially critical (no synthetic growth regulators to compensate for poor pollination).
- **Why them:** Organic premium ($3-5/lb vs. $1.50-2 conventional) means lost yield is extra costly. Organic growers tend to be sustainability-focused and interested in pollinator health data for marketing/certification.
- **How to find:** Oregon Blueberry Commission, Organic Growers Alliance of Oregon, farm stands at Portland farmers markets.

### 4. Pollination Broker / Bee Broker
- **Profile:** Companies like Scientific Ag or Olam Pollination that broker 10,000-50,000 hives across multiple grower clients. They coordinate between beekeepers and growers.
- **Why them:** Brokers have a business incentive to verify beekeeper service quality -- it protects THEIR reputation with growers. They could deploy radar units as a value-add service differentiator. Also, one broker sale = deployment across dozens of farms.
- **How to find:** American Beekeeping Federation conference, Project Apis m. (bee research nonprofit) donor list, Almond Board Pollination Task Force.

### 5. University Agricultural Extension / USDA ARS
- **Profile:** UC Davis Department of Entomology, USDA-ARS Bee Research Lab (Baton Rouge), Penn State Center for Pollinator Research.
- **Why them:** Researchers would use radar for studies even without commercial ROI. They have grant budgets ($50K-200K for novel monitoring equipment). A published paper using the radar system becomes the best possible marketing. They also have access to controlled apiaries for ground-truth validation.
- **How to find:** Google Scholar search for "pollinator monitoring technology" authors. USDA SBIR grant listings in pollinator health. Directly email lab PIs.

## Discovery Questions

### Problem Validation
1. "How do you currently verify that your rented hives are actively pollinating during bloom? Walk me through your process."
2. "In the last 3 years, have you ever suspected under-performance from a pollination service? What happened?"
3. "If you could see exactly which zones of your field had low bee activity during bloom, what would you do differently?"
4. "What does a 10% yield reduction cost you annually? How much of that do you attribute to pollination variability?"
5. "How many hives do you rent per acre? Who decides placement?"

### Solution Validation
6. "If a sensor could count pollinators in real-time across your field and show you a heat map, would that change your management decisions?"
7. "Would you use this data to negotiate with your beekeeper or pollination broker? How?"
8. "What price point would make this a no-brainer for your operation? At what price would you say 'interesting but not worth it'?"
9. "How many seasons would you need data before you trusted it enough to make hive placement decisions based on it?"
10. "If we could only give you one number per day during bloom -- average bee flights per acre -- would that alone be valuable?"

### Competitive/Alternative Validation
11. "Have you heard of BeeHero or AgriSound? Have you tried any technology-based pollination monitoring?"
12. "Do you currently do any manual bee counts? How much does that cost you in labor time?"
13. "Would you prefer to own monitoring hardware or pay for a service that deploys during bloom and retrieves afterward?"

### Logistics Validation
14. "Do you have cell service across your fields? What carrier works best in your area?"
15. "How many days of advance notice do you get before bloom starts?"
16. "Who in your operation would check the dashboard daily during bloom? You, an agronomist, a foreman?"

## Demand Signals (What Confirms Interest)

**Strong positive signals:**
- Grower names a specific dollar amount they lost due to suspected poor pollination
- Grower asks "when can I try it?" or "how soon is this available?"
- Grower offers to host a pilot unit on their property during next bloom
- Grower connects you to their beekeeper/broker saying "they need to see this too"
- Grower mentions they are already collecting data on pollination (manual counts, fruit-set surveys) -- shows existing behavior

**Moderate positive signals:**
- Grower says "that would be nice to have" but does not ask about price or timeline
- Grower can articulate the problem but is skeptical of technology solving it
- Broker says "our clients ask about this" but does not commit to piloting

**Negative signals (abort or pivot):**
- Grower says "I just trust my beekeeper" -- no felt pain
- Grower says "I already know my pollination is fine from fruit set" -- solved differently
- Multiple growers say "interesting" but zero offer to pilot -- politeness, not demand
- Price sensitivity at $1K-2K range -- market will not support radar hardware economics
- Beekeepers actively hostile to the concept (they do not want to be monitored) -- channel conflict

## Cold Outreach Plan

### Where Buyers Hang Out

**In-person events (highest conversion):**
1. **Almond Conference** -- Sacramento, CA (December annually). 3,000+ attendees. The single most important event for reaching almond growers.
2. **World Ag Expo** -- Tulare, CA (February). 100K+ attendees. Massive, but diluted across all of agriculture.
3. **UC Davis Almond & Walnut Research Conference** -- (May). Research-oriented growers.
4. **California Small Farms Conference** -- various locations. Smaller operations, more accessible.
5. **American Beekeeping Federation Conference** -- (January). Reaches brokers, beekeepers.
6. **Project Apis m. Annual Meeting** -- Pollination-focused, connects research and industry.

**Online communities:**
- AgTalk forum (agtalk.com) -- active grower discussions
- California Almond Board newsletters and webinars
- LinkedIn groups: "Precision Agriculture," "Almond Growers"
- Twitter/X: #almonds, #pollination, #AgTech
- Reddit: r/farming (limited but some specialty crop growers)

**Publications growers read:**
- Western Farm Press / Farm Progress
- Almond Board's "Almond Facts" magazine
- Growing Produce magazine
- Good Fruit Grower (PNW berries)

### Outreach Sequence

**Phase 1: Research/Network Building (Month 1-2)**
1. Join California Almond Board as an associate/technology member
2. Attend 1-2 UC Davis Extension field days as observer
3. Connect on LinkedIn with 50+ almond farm managers/agronomists
4. Subscribe to all relevant newsletters, join AgTalk

**Phase 2: Informational Interviews (Month 2-3)**
1. Email 20 growers/agronomists requesting 15-minute calls about "pollination management challenges" (no pitch)
2. Target: 5-8 conversations completed
3. Template: "Hi [Name], I'm exploring technology solutions for pollination management in tree nuts. I'm not selling anything -- I'd value 15 minutes to learn about your current approach and challenges. Would you be open to a brief call?"

**Phase 3: Demo/Pilot Pitch (Month 4-6, requires working prototype)**
1. Return to most interested informational interviewees
2. Offer: "Free pilot during your next bloom -- we deploy 2-3 sensor units on your property for the season. We need your time for 1 field walk + weekly 10-minute check-ins. You get the data at no cost."
3. Target: 2-3 pilot commitments for next bloom season

**Key constraint:** Almond bloom is February-March. If you miss that window, you wait 12 months. Berry bloom (April-June) is the backup. The entire customer validation timeline revolves around these narrow windows.
