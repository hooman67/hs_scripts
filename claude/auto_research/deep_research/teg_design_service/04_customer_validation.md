# 04 - Customer Validation: TEG Design-as-a-Service

## First 5 Customers to Approach

### 1. Cummins Inc. (Columbus, IN) - Diesel Engine Waste Heat Recovery
- **Why:** Cummins has published research on TEG-based exhaust heat recovery for heavy-duty trucks. They have active R&D programs but struggle with design iteration speed.
- **Target contact:** Director of Advanced Engineering, Electrified Power segment
- **How to find:** LinkedIn (search "thermoelectric" + "Cummins"); SAE conference proceedings authors
- **Budget authority:** $50K engineering services easily approved at director level
- **Approach:** Reference their published SAE papers on TEG exhaust recovery; offer to benchmark their current design vs. AI-optimized alternative

### 2. Holcim Group (Switzerland/US) - Cement Kiln Waste Heat
- **Why:** Cement manufacturing wastes enormous heat (kiln exhaust at 300-400C). Holcim has aggressive ESG targets and innovation budget.
- **Target contact:** VP of Innovation or Sustainability Engineering Manager
- **How to find:** LinkedIn; their published sustainability reports name innovation leaders
- **Budget authority:** $50K is trivial for a $27B revenue company's innovation budget
- **Approach:** Frame as ESG/carbon reduction story; estimate kWh recoverable from their typical kiln exhaust

### 3. Alphabet (Google) - Data Center Waste Heat Recovery
- **Why:** Google has published interest in waste heat recovery from data centers (low-grade heat, 40-60C). TEGs are one of few options at these low temperatures.
- **Target contact:** Data Center Sustainability team; search for "waste heat" researchers in Google DeepMind or X
- **How to find:** Published papers, Google sustainability blog posts
- **Budget authority:** Unlimited for promising sustainability tech
- **Approach:** Extremely long shot but high-value signal. Even a "no" with feedback is valuable.

### 4. II-VI / Coherent (Saxonburg, PA) - TEG Module Manufacturer
- **Why:** They manufacture TE modules and their customers constantly ask "what geometry/material should I use for my application?" They could white-label design services.
- **Target contact:** Applications Engineering Manager
- **How to find:** Company website contacts; TE conference exhibitor booths
- **Budget authority:** May not buy services but could refer customers or partner
- **Approach:** Position as complementary service that drives module sales for them; offer revenue share on referred projects

### 5. Small/Mid Industrial IoT Company (e.g., Perpetua Power, Everactive)
- **Why:** Companies building self-powered IoT sensors need optimized TEG designs for their specific heat sources. High volume of design needs, lower per-project value.
- **Target contact:** CTO or VP Engineering
- **How to find:** Crunchbase search for "thermoelectric" + "IoT"; LinkedIn
- **Budget authority:** $10-15K range (smaller budgets but faster decisions)
- **Approach:** Offer to optimize their existing TEG design for 20%+ power improvement

## Discovery Questions

### Understanding Current Pain
1. "How do you currently design/select TEG modules for your applications? Walk me through your last design project."
2. "How long did your last TEG design iteration take from specification to validated design?"
3. "What tools do you use? (COMSOL, ANSYS, in-house code, vendor datasheets?)"
4. "How much did your last TEG design project cost (internal labor + external services + tools)?"
5. "What's the biggest frustration in your TEG design process today?"

### Understanding Value
6. "If you could get an optimized TEG design in 5 days instead of 3 months, what would that be worth to your program?"
7. "How many TEG design projects do you run per year? How many are you NOT doing because of cost/time barriers?"
8. "What would a 20% improvement in TEG power output mean for your product economics?"

### Understanding Buying Process
9. "Who else would be involved in a decision to hire an external design service? (procurement, technical review, legal?)"
10. "What's your budget approval process for a $15-50K engineering service?"
11. "Have you ever hired external consultants for thermoelectric work? What was the experience?"

### Understanding Willingness to Pay
12. "If I could deliver an optimized TEG design package (simulation-validated, with CAD files) in 5 days for $15K, would that be interesting enough to try on your next project?"
13. "Would you prefer a fixed-fee project or hourly consulting?"
14. "Would a $3K feasibility study (go/no-go in 24 hours) be a useful first step?"

## Demand Signals (What Confirms Interest)

**Strong positive signals:**
- Customer agrees to a paid pilot project (even at discount)
- Customer shares specific thermal boundary conditions for a real project
- Customer asks about NDA/IP protection (means they have real proprietary applications)
- Customer introduces you to their procurement or engineering team
- Customer asks "can you start next month?"

**Moderate positive signals:**
- Customer takes a follow-up meeting after initial conversation
- Customer asks detailed technical questions about methodology
- Customer asks for references or case studies
- Customer shares their current design challenges in detail

**Weak/negative signals:**
- "Interesting, send me more info" (polite dismissal)
- "We handle this in-house" (no pain)
- "Our TEG projects are on hold" (no active need)
- "What's your publication record?" (credential gatekeeping - hard to overcome)
- Long silence after initial outreach (no interest)

**Kill signal (abandon this market):**
- 0 positive responses from 50+ qualified outreach attempts
- Multiple responses of "the market doesn't need this" from domain experts
- Discovery reveals that the real bottleneck is NOT design (it's manufacturing, or demand, or economics)

## Cold Outreach Plan

### Where Buyers Hang Out

1. **Conferences:**
   - International Conference on Thermoelectrics (ICT) - annual, 200-400 attendees
   - SAE World Congress (automotive thermal management track)
   - ACS/ASES energy conferences
   - PowerGen International (power generation)

2. **Online communities:**
   - LinkedIn groups: "Thermoelectric Energy" (2,400 members), "Waste Heat Recovery" (~1,000 members)
   - ResearchGate: search TEG-related publications, contact authors at industrial companies
   - ThermoelectricSociety.org member directory

3. **Publications:**
   - Journal of Electronic Materials (TE-focused articles often co-authored by industry engineers)
   - Energy Conversion and Management (applied waste heat papers)
   - SAE Technical Papers (automotive TEG applications)

4. **Industry associations:**
   - Thermoelectric Society (TES)
   - Alliance for Industrial Efficiency
   - Heat Is Power Association

### Outreach Sequence

**Week 1-2: LinkedIn warm-up (50 connections)**
- Connect with engineers who have "thermoelectric" or "waste heat" in profile/posts
- Engage with their content (comments, shares) before pitching
- Target: 50 connection requests, expect 20-25 accepts

**Week 3-4: Direct outreach (25 personalized messages)**
- Reference their specific publications or projects
- Template: "I noticed your work on [specific TEG project]. I'm building an AI-accelerated TEG design service that cuts design time from months to days. Would you be open to a 15-minute call to share your perspective on whether this addresses a real need?"
- Target: 25 messages, expect 3-5 responses, 2-3 calls

**Week 5-6: Content marketing (establish credibility)**
- Publish 2-3 LinkedIn articles: "How ML is Changing Thermoelectric Design" / "TEGNet: What Engineers Should Know"
- Share on relevant LinkedIn groups
- Target: Establish thought leadership, attract inbound inquiries

**Week 7-8: Conference networking**
- Attend one relevant conference (ICT or SAE thermal management session)
- Present a poster or give a talk if possible
- Collect 10-20 business cards from potential customers
- Follow up within 48 hours

**Ongoing: Referral network**
- Ask every conversation: "Who else in your field might benefit from this?"
- Build relationship with TE module manufacturers (II-VI, Marlow) as referral sources
- Offer 10% referral fee for introduced customers who sign
