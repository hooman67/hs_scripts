# Customer Validation Strategy: Compressed Air Leak Detection

## First 5 Customers to Approach

| # | Profile | How to Find | Why Them |
|---|---------|-------------|----------|
| 1 | **Small automotive parts manufacturer** (100-300 employees, 5+ compressors) | LinkedIn search: "Maintenance Manager" + "automotive" + your metro area | High compressed air usage (pneumatic tools, robots), cost-conscious, always running lean |
| 2 | **Plastics injection molder** (50-200 employees) | Local manufacturing directory (ThomasNet, MFG.com) | Injection molding uses massive amounts of compressed air; plants are notoriously leaky |
| 3 | **Food/beverage packaging plant** (mid-size) | Industry association lists (PMMI, FPA) | Compressed air for packaging lines; energy costs squeeze margins; ESG reporting creates motivation |
| 4 | **Metal fabrication shop** (50-150 employees) | Local fabricators via Google Maps + cold outreach | Laser cutters, plasma, CNC all use compressed air; shops are hands-on and will trial new tech fast |
| 5 | **Facilities maintenance contractor** (manages multiple plants) | LinkedIn: "Compressed air" in job title or description | If one contractor adopts, they deploy across ALL their client sites — force multiplier |

## Discovery Questions (Without Revealing Your Hand)

**Opening approach**: "I'm researching how manufacturing plants manage compressed air costs. Would you have 15 minutes to share how your plant handles this?"

### Questions to Ask:

1. **"How much do you spend on compressed air annually?"** (Validates market size per customer)
2. **"When was the last time you did a leak survey? Who did it?"** (Reveals current behavior)
3. **"After a survey, how quickly do new leaks appear?"** (Validates the "point-in-time snapshot" problem)
4. **"If you could see your leak status in real-time on a dashboard, what would that be worth to you?"** (Tests willingness to pay — without describing your product)
5. **"What would stop you from deploying 30 wireless sensors in your plant tomorrow?"** (Surfaces objections early: IT security, union rules, approval process)
6. **"Who would need to approve a $5,000 purchase for maintenance equipment?"** (Maps the buying process)

**DO NOT say**: "I'm building a product" or describe specific features. Frame as research only until you hear clear demand signals.

## Demand Signals (What Confirms Interest)

| Signal | Strength | What It Means |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| "Can I be your first customer?" | STRONGEST | Active demand — they want this NOW |
| "When will this be available?" | STRONG | They see the value and want to buy |
| "Send me a proposal / quote" | STRONG | Budget holder is engaged |
| "We'd try a pilot if it were free" | MODERATE | Interested but cautious — normal for new tech |
| "We spend $X on surveys and they don't last" | MODERATE | Pain confirmed, but hasn't asked for solution yet |
| "Let me connect you with [person]" | MODERATE | Willing to invest social capital — good sign |
| "Interesting, let me think about it" | WEAK | Polite but non-committal |
| "We already have [competitor] handling this" | NEGATIVE | Market already served for this customer |
| "We don't have budget for this" | NEGATIVE | Wrong customer profile |

**Minimum signal to proceed with build**: 2 out of 5 conversations produce STRONG signals, AND no one says "we already have this."

## Cold Outreach Plan

### Where These Buyers Hang Out

| Channel | Specifics | Approach |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| **LinkedIn** | Search "Maintenance Manager" OR "Plant Engineer" + manufacturing keywords | Direct message with research angle (not sales pitch) |
| **Local manufacturing associations** | AME (Association for Manufacturing Excellence), local NTMA chapters | Attend monthly meetings, mention your research |
| **Trade shows** | IMTS (Sep, Chicago), Fabtech (Oct), local compressed air shows | Walk the floor, talk to people at compressor vendor booths |
| **Compressed Air Challenge** (DOE program) | Training events bring together plant engineers specifically focused on air costs | Perfect audience — already aware of the problem |
| **Reddit / online forums** | r/maintenance, r/PLC, eng-tips.com compressed air forum | Lurk first, then ask questions about how people handle leaks |
| **Equipment distributors** | Companies that sell compressors (Atlas Copco, Kaeser, Sullair distributors) | They know which customers have leak problems — potential channel partner |

### Outreach Sequence

**Week 1**: Send 20 LinkedIn messages to maintenance managers within 50 miles
- Message template: "Hi [Name], I'm researching how manufacturing plants manage compressed air costs. I'd love to hear how [Company] handles leak detection — would you have 15 minutes for a call? No selling, just research."
- Expected response rate: 10-15% (2-3 calls)

**Week 2**: Follow up non-responders + post in online forums
- "What's your plant's approach to compressed air leak management? Do you do annual surveys or just fix things when they break?"

**Week 3**: Attend local manufacturing meetup or CAC event
- In-person conversations convert better than cold messages
- Ask for introductions to other maintenance managers

**Target**: 5 completed discovery calls within 3 weeks

## Letter of Intent / Pre-order Structure

If conversations reveal strong demand, move to concrete commitment:

**Pilot Agreement** (template):
- "We'll install 30 sensor nodes + 1 gateway in your plant for FREE for 90 days"
- "In exchange: you provide plant access, maintenance team feedback, and a testimonial"
- "At end of pilot, if savings exceed $X, you purchase the system at [price]"
- "You have first-mover pricing: 25% discount on hardware if you commit before [date]"

This is NOT a letter of intent for investment purposes — it's a customer validation commitment that proves demand while de-risking the purchase for the customer.
