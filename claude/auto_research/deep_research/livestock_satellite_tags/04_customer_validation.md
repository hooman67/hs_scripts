# 04 Customer Validation: Livestock GPS+Health Tags with Satellite Backhaul

## First 5 Customers to Approach

### Customer 1: Progressive Large-Scale Rancher
- **Profile:** 2,000-5,000 head cow-calf operation in Montana or Wyoming. Revenue $3-8M. Already uses satellite internet (Starlink) and digital record-keeping. Has experienced >$50K in losses from theft or missed calving in past year.
- **Where to find:** Montana Stockgrowers Association member list, speakers at Range Beef Cow Symposium, active on ranching Twitter/X accounts
- **Why first:** Large enough herd to justify early adoption cost, tech-forward enough to try unproven product, pain point is acute and quantifiable
- **Approach:** Warm introduction via Montana State University Extension or cattlemen's association contact

### Customer 2: University Research Ranch
- **Profile:** Land-grant university with working cattle herd used for research (Montana State, Texas A&M, University of Wyoming, Colorado State). 200-500 head research herd.
- **Where to find:** Animal Science department faculty directory, extension service contacts
- **Why second:** Lower sales barrier (research budget, not personal funds), provides scientific validation, generates published data, acts as reference customer
- **Approach:** Cold email to department head or precision livestock farming researcher with clear value proposition for research data collection

### Customer 3: High-Value Seedstock Producer
- **Profile:** Purebred cattle operation (Angus, Hereford) with animals worth $5,000-50,000 each. 300-1,000 head. Extremely high per-animal loss cost.
- **Where to find:** American Angus Association registered breeders list, National Western Stock Show exhibitors
- **Why third:** Per-animal value makes $50 tag + $3/month trivial. Theft of a single $20K bull justifies entire herd deployment. Already invests heavily in individual animal management.
- **Approach:** Exhibit at breed association annual meetings, targeted direct mail to top breeders in remote areas

### Customer 4: Corporate Ranch / Investment Ranch
- **Profile:** Institutional-owned ranch (e.g., pension fund, family office, corporate ag company). 5,000-20,000 head managed by hired manager. Examples: JA Ranch (TX), Waggoner Ranch (TX), Briscoe Ranch.
- **Where to find:** National Cattlemen's Beef Association corporate members, Western Livestock Journal features
- **Why fourth:** Professional management = data-driven decisions, corporate budget for technology, hired managers need remote visibility, accountability requirements from absentee owners
- **Approach:** Through ag-tech investment networks, ranch management consultants

### Customer 5: Government/Tribal Land Grazing Permittee
- **Profile:** Rancher with BLM or Forest Service grazing permits. Must track cattle location for compliance (prevent trespass onto restricted areas). 500-2,000 head on 50,000+ acres of public land.
- **Where to find:** BLM grazing permit databases (public record), Public Lands Council members
- **Why fifth:** Regulatory compliance creates non-optional demand. Geofencing directly addresses trespass liability ($1,000+ fines per incident). GPS data serves as compliance documentation.
- **Approach:** Through BLM range specialists who work directly with permittees

## Discovery Questions

### Pain & Current Solution
1. "How do you currently know where your cattle are on any given day? How often do you ride out to check?"
2. "In the last 12 months, how many head have you lost to theft, predators, or unknown causes? What was the dollar impact?"
3. "Have you ever missed a calving that resulted in calf loss? How often does that happen?"
4. "What's your biggest frustration with managing cattle on remote pasture?"
5. "Have you tried any GPS or tracking solution before? What happened?"

### Willingness to Pay
6. "If you could see every animal's location on your phone right now, what would that be worth to you per head per month?"
7. "You spend roughly [X hours/week] checking cattle. What would you do with that time if the tags handled monitoring?"
8. "At $50 per tag and $3/month per head, would you tag your entire herd or start with a subset? Which animals first?"
9. "What's your budget for technology/equipment per year? Where does monitoring fall in priority vs. fencing, water, genetics?"

### Product Requirements
10. "How often would you need a location update? Every hour? Every 4 hours? Once a day?"
11. "What alert would make you get in your truck immediately? (theft? calving? health? water?)"
12. "Do you prefer ear tags, collars, or boluses? Why?"
13. "Who else on your operation would need access to the data? Ranch hands? Vet? Accountant?"
14. "What existing software do you use for herd management? Would integration matter?"

### Deal Breakers
15. "What would prevent you from trying this? (cost, reliability, complexity, battery life?)"
16. "How long would the tag need to last before you'd consider it worth the investment?"
17. "If this worked perfectly for 6 months then the company went under, would you feel burned? What guarantee matters?"

## Demand Signals (Confirms Real Interest)

### Strong Positive Signals
- Customer asks "When can I get this?" or "How do I order?"
- Customer offers to pay for prototype units or pre-order
- Customer volunteers to host a field trial (offers cattle and access)
- Customer introduces you to other ranchers who "need this"
- Customer has already spent money on inferior solutions (cellular trackers that don't work on remote pasture)
- Customer can immediately quantify losses that exceed annual tag cost (e.g., "$80K in theft last year vs. $36K for tags")

### Moderate Positive Signals
- Customer asks detailed questions about battery life, durability, accuracy
- Customer asks about pricing tiers or volume discounts
- Customer says "I've been looking for something like this"
- Customer identifies specific use case (e.g., "My bulls keep breaking through to the neighbor's heifers")

### Negative Signals (Proceed with Caution)
- "That's interesting" without follow-up questions
- Price sensitivity at $3/month (suggests commodity mindset, race to bottom)
- "My grandson could probably build that" (dismissive)
- "I'd wait until [Allflex/Ceres Tag/etc.] does it" (recognizes incumbents will serve this need)
- Unwillingness to tag entire herd (suggests marginal value perception)

### Outreach Strategy

**Phase 1: Research validation (Weeks 1-4) — $0 cost**
1. Join 3-5 online ranching communities under real name
2. Ask open-ended questions about cattle monitoring pain points (DO NOT pitch product)
3. Identify 20 ranchers who describe problems your product solves
4. DM/email for 30-minute phone call: "I'm researching technology solutions for remote cattle monitoring. Would you spend 30 minutes telling me about your biggest challenges? No sales pitch — I'm learning."
5. Target: 10 calls completed, 5 with strong pain signals

**Phase 2: Concept validation (Weeks 5-8) — $2,000**
1. Create 1-page product concept sheet (render of tag, key specs, pricing)
2. Share with 10 most-engaged ranchers from Phase 1
3. Ask: "If this existed today at this price, would you buy it? For how many head?"
4. Attend one state cattlemen's association meeting (travel budget: $1,500)
5. Target: 3-5 letters of intent or verbal pre-commitments

**Phase 3: Field trial recruitment (Weeks 9-12) — $3,000**
1. Contact 3 university extension programs (Montana State, Texas A&M, Colorado State)
2. Offer free prototype deployment in exchange for research partnership
3. Identify 2 commercial ranchers willing to pilot 50-100 tags (subsidized at $25/tag, free service for 6 months)
4. Target: 1 university + 1 commercial ranch committed to pilot

**Phase 4: Pre-launch pipeline building (Months 4-6) — $5,000**
1. Exhibit at one regional cattle show (booth: $2,000-3,000 + travel)
2. Launch waiting list with $50 refundable deposit
3. Partner with 1 ranch supply retailer (e.g., local Tractor Supply or independent) for distribution validation
4. Target: 100 tags on waiting list with deposits ($5,000 in deposits = strong signal)
