**Created**: 2026-07-01. **Owner**: Hooman Shariati.
**This is a SECOND, distinct methodology.** The original `research-plan.md` (Track A) hunts
venture-scale *tech* startups (new technology, TAM>$1B, >15% CAGR, founder-fit). Track B is a
different search entirely, created after Hooman's 2026-07-01 pivot.

## The Track-B Mandate (what changed)

After 50 cycles of Track A proved the "obvious high-fit, high-growth software/AI" space is
picked clean (see `LEARNINGS.md` rounds 2–3), Hooman deliberately **relaxed almost every
Track-A constraint**:

| Dimension | Track A (venture tech) | **Track B (this plan)** |
|-----------|------------------------|--------------------------|
| Goal | Fundable venture | **Secondary income stream (lifestyle)** |
| Income target | $B outcome | **$5–30K/mo profit** |
| Market gates | TAM>$1B, SAM>$100M, >15% CAGR | **DROPPED. Low-growth & small are fine.** |
| Field | AI/software preferred | **AVOID crowded AI/software hype arena** |
| Founder fit | Pure software / AI he can build | **Any shape — he'll learn. Judged on specifics.** |
| Business shape | Build a product | **Build OR buy-and-operate OR service OR physical** |
| Time | N/A | **Part-time, ~10–20 hrs/week** |
| Capital | <$500K to raise | **$25–150K of his own** |
| Primary filter | Novel tech + moat | **CLEAR PATH TO SUCCESS (proven playbook)** |

**Governing principle:** Hooman is a dynamic generalist open to learning new things, as long as
there is a **clear, concrete path to success**. "Boring," low-growth, and un-sexy are now
POSITIVE signals — they mean less competition and less AI/VC/clone risk. This is the opposite
of Track A.

## Business Shapes On The Table (all in scope)

1. **Boring/micro software** — vertical or micro-SaaS in unglamorous, low-growth niches VCs
   ignore; directories; data products; internal-tool-as-a-product.
2. **Buy & operate — digital** — acquire a cash-flowing small SaaS, content site, newsletter,
   Shopify/FBA store, or app in the $25–150K range (Acquire.com, Flippa, Empire Flippers,
   MicroAcquire) and run/improve it.
3. **Buy & operate — physical/local** — small "boring" businesses (SBA-financeable, but keep
   Hooman's cash in at $25–150K down): laundromats, self-storage, vending, service routes,
   semi-absentee franchises. Manager-run so it fits a day job.
4. **Productized services** — done-for-you offerings in a specific niche (fixed scope, fixed
   price) that can be delegated/systematized over time.
5. **Physical products** — DTC / e-commerce / licensing in a defensible niche (NOT commodity
   Amazon churn). Hooman's EE background + mech-eng friends are a real asset here.
6. **Content / media / community / education** — niche newsletters, professional communities,
   courses, info products with durable audience moats.
7. **Marketplaces / directories** in boring verticals with real liquidity potential.
8. **"Picks & shovels" for boring industries** — tools, data, compliance, or software for
   un-sexy sectors (the durable version of Track A's best instinct, minus the growth gate).

## The Track-B Funnel

### Step 1 — Opportunity Discovery (per stream)
For an assigned business shape, find ~8–10 concrete opportunities or repeatable models that
FIT the mandate (lifestyle income, part-time, $25–150K, outside AI hype). For each: what it is,
who makes money at it today (real examples), and the rough model.

### Step 2 — Crowding & Durability Check
- Is this arena swarmed by VCs / clones / AI startups? (If yes → deprioritize — that's the
  thing we're fleeing.)
- Will it still exist and pay in 5–10 years? Is AI a threat or a tailwind (cheaper to operate)?
- Is there a structural reason competition stays low (boring, fragmented, licensing, local,
  relationship-driven, un-sexy)?

### Step 3 — Path-to-Success Check (THE core filter)
- Is there a **proven playbook** — do people demonstrably make $5–30K/mo doing this?
- Concrete first 90 days: how would Hooman start or acquire it?
- Realistic unit economics: revenue, margin, monthly profit, ramp time.
- What could go wrong, and how bad is the downside (can he lose the $25–150K)?

### Step 4 — Fit & Feasibility for Hooman
- Capital within $25–150K? Time within ~10–20 hrs/wk (or delegable)?
- Can a smart generalist learn/run it, or does it need a scarce license/specialist that makes
  it a non-starter? (Some licensing is fine if learnable; wet-labs/PhDs/FDA still out.)
- What's his unfair advantage if any (EE, mech-eng friends, AI skills, capital, analytical)?

### Step 5 — Scorecard (Track-B weights — DIFFERENT from Track A)

| Dimension | Weight | Notes |
|-----------|:---:|------|
| **Clear path to success (proven playbook)** | 20% | The #1 Track-B filter |
| Income potential ($5–30K/mo realistic) | 15% | |
| Durability / not-crowded / AI-safe | 15% | Boring = good |
| Capital fit ($25–150K) | 15% | Lower & safer = better |
| Time fit (part-time / delegable) | 15% | |
| Downside risk / capital safety | 10% | Can he lose his shirt? Lower risk = higher score |
| Execution feasibility (learnable by a smart generalist) | 10% | |
| **Weighted total** | | /10 |

Scale 1–10, higher = better on every dimension (for capital/downside, higher = safer/cheaper).

### Step 6 — Deep Research (on demand)
Same spirit as Track A Step 6 but re-pointed: for a chosen opportunity, produce a concrete
build/acquire plan — real listings or suppliers with prices, real unit economics, a 90-day
start plan, a downside/risk register, and a validation step before committing capital.

## Track-B Reject List (learned + inherited)
- Anything swarmed by VC/AI startups (the arena we're fleeing).
- Commodity Amazon-FBA churn / dropshipping races to the bottom (no moat, AI-and-clone bait).
- Requires a scarce specialist Hooman can't become (PhD, wet lab, FDA) — inherited from Track A.
- Needs full-time daily hands-on presence with no delegation path (breaks the part-time gate).
- Downside that risks more than the $25–150K capital (personal-guarantee blowups).
- Pure "get rich quick" / no proven playbook / relies on a trend that may evaporate.
