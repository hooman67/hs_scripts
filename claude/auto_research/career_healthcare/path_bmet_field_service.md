## 1. What the role actually is (day-to-day) and the career ladder

### 1a. What BMETs / FSEs actually do
BMETs are **electro-mechanical technicians who assemble, configure, install, calibrate, maintain,
inspect, and repair medical equipment** — ventilators, defibrillators, patient monitors, infusion
pumps, imaging systems, lab analyzers, life-support systems. [VERIFIED — BLS OOH; Wikipedia BMET]

Core daily duties (BLS): install equipment; test and calibrate parts; repair/replace parts;
perform scheduled preventive maintenance (PMs); keep maintenance/repair records (compliance
documentation for Joint Commission / regulatory audits is a big part of the job). [VERIFIED — BLS OOH]

The work is **hands-on, diagnostic, and physical** (rolling carts, crawling behind imaging
gantries, lifting, on-site at hospitals). It is *troubleshooting hardware + electronics + some
software/networking*, which maps almost perfectly onto an EE skill set. [ASSUMED from role description]

### 1b. The ladder (lowest → highest pay & responsibility)

| Level | What they do | Employer | Pay tier |
|-------|--------------|----------|----------|
| **BMET I (entry)** | PMs, basic repairs on general biomed (pumps, monitors, beds) under supervision | Hospital biomed dept, or ISO | Lowest |
| **BMET II / III (senior)** | Independent repair across broad equipment, mentors juniors, handles more complex modalities | Hospital, ISO | Mid |
| **Imaging / MRI FSE (OEM)** | Installs, calibrates, and repairs high-value modalities (MRI, CT, PET, cath labs, ultrasound). Specialist. Region-based, travels to customer sites. | **OEM: GE HealthCare, Siemens Healthineers, Philips, Canon Medical** — or large ISO | **Highest (tech track)** |
| **Clinical Engineer / HTM Manager** | Capital planning, budgets, vendor management, department leadership, systems integration | Hospital, large ISO | Highest overall (management track) |

Key distinction for Hooman: **the "field service engineer" title he'd target is the imaging FSE at
an OEM.** It pays the most on the technical track, is the most specialized, and is the closest thing
to "engineer" in prestige — but it carries **heavy regional travel and on-call** (see §5).
[VERIFIED — role structure per Wikipedia BMET + industry sources; pay ordering VERIFIED in §3]

**Not to be confused with:** the *MRI/radiologic technologist* who scans patients. That is a
different license (ARRT/state), a different (patient-facing, clinical) job, and NOT the target here.
The FSE fixes the machine; the technologist operates it. [VERIFIED — distinct occupations]

## 2. Entry path & time — can an EE get in within ≤1 year?

**Short answer: YES, entry within a year is realistic — arguably faster than a year to a first job,
because the EE/biomed degree substitutes for the usual 2-year associate BMET program.**

### 2a. The normal entry path (for someone without an engineering degree)
- Typical entry credential: a **2-year associate degree in Biomedical Equipment Technology** (or a
  1-year military BMET program at METC, Fort Sam Houston). [VERIFIED — BLS OOH; Wikipedia BMET]
- BLS lists typical entry as **Associate's degree**, "moderate-term on-the-job training," and
  **no prior work experience required**. Certification is **optional** ("can demonstrate
  competency"), not mandatory to be hired. [VERIFIED — BLS OOH]

### 2b. What changes for Hooman (EE + biomed undergrad)
- **He does NOT need the 2-year associate degree.** The associate BMET program exists precisely to
  teach electronics, circuits, and biomedical instrumentation fundamentals — content an EE degree
  already covers and exceeds. Employers routinely hire EE/electronics-engineering-tech grads
  directly into BMET/FSE roles; the biomed option makes his profile stronger than the median new
  hire. [ASSUMED — strong inference; EE ≥ associate-BMET curriculum. Not a formal BLS-stated
  substitution, so tagged ASSUMED.]
- **OEM FSE training is employer-provided.** GE HealthCare, Siemens, Philips, and Canon run their
  own **training academies** — new FSEs are hired for aptitude (EE/electronics background is a
  primary qualifier) and then put through **weeks-to-months of paid, modality-specific factory
  training** (MRI, CT, etc.). You are trained *on the job, on payroll*. [ASSUMED — well-documented
  OEM practice; not fetched from an official OEM page this session, so ASSUMED.]
- **Realistic timeline:** With an EE/biomed degree, Hooman could plausibly be **hired directly**
  (0 months of self-funded schooling) into a BMET or associate-FSE role, then trained by the
  employer. If he wants a credential in hand first, the entry-level cert path is a few months of
  self-study (below). **Well within the ≤1-year budget.** [ASSUMED]

### 2c. Certifications (AAMI) — optional but useful
The credential body is **AAMI** (Association for the Advancement of Medical Instrumentation),
exams administered via the certification commission. Relevant credentials: [VERIFIED — Wikipedia BMET]
- **CBET** — Certified Biomedical Equipment Technician (the core general credential)
- **CRES** — Certified Radiology Equipment Specialist (imaging-focused; relevant to the FSE target)
- **CLES** — Certified Laboratory Equipment Specialist
- **CHTM** — Certified Healthcare Technology Manager (management track)
- **CABT** — Certified Associate in Biomedical Technology (entry-level associate credential)

**CBET experience requirements by education pathway** (this is the key data): [VERIFIED — 24x7 Magazine,
"Taking the CBET Exam? Consult This Beforehand"]

| Pathway | Full-time BMET work experience required |
|---------|------------------------------------------|
| Associate degree in **biomedical equipment technology** | **2 years** |
| Associate degree in **electronics** | **3 years** |
| Military biomedical technology program | **2 years** |
| **No formal degree** | **4 years** |

**Important nuance:** CBET requires *work experience regardless of degree* — a fresh EE grad
cannot sit CBET on day one; he'd need ~2–3 years on the job first (an EE degree most closely maps to
the "associate in electronics → 3 years" or better). **So CBET is a mid-career credential, not an
entry ticket.** [VERIFIED for the experience table; the exact treatment of a *bachelor's* in
engineering (whether it lands at the 2-yr or 3-yr line) was NOT confirmed this session — UNVERIFIED.]
- **CABT** is the **entry-level credential** intended for newcomers/students with little or no
  experience — the appropriate one for Hooman to pursue *before* he has BMET tenure. Its exact
  eligibility rules were not confirmable from an accessible source this session. [UNVERIFIED — could
  not fetch AAMI's page (403); confirm directly at aami.org before relying on it.]

**Bottom line on entry:** Certification is **not required to get hired** (BLS: optional). The EE
degree is the real key that unlocks direct entry and lets him skip the associate program. He can be
working inside a year; CBET/CRES come **later**, after 2–3 years on the job, and boost pay/mobility.

## 4. Demand, durability & who hires

### 4a. Growth (strong)
- BLS projects **13% growth 2024→2034** for medical equipment repairers — **"much faster than
  average"** (vs 3% all-occupations). **+8,800 jobs**, **~7,300 openings/yr** on average (mostly
  replacement + growth). Base of **68,000 jobs (2024) → 76,800 (2034)**. [VERIFIED — BLS OOH]
- Drivers: aging population, ever-more medical devices per facility, and equipment complexity.
  [ASSUMED — standard BLS rationale]

### 4b. AI-resistance / automation-resistance (a genuine strength)
- The core work is **physical, on-site hardware diagnosis and repair** in a regulated clinical
  environment. You cannot remotely swap an MRI gradient coil, re-shim a magnet, or replace a CT
  tube from a laptop. Hands + presence + regulatory sign-off are irreducible. [ASSUMED — strong
  structural argument; hardware field service is among the least automatable technical work.]
- AI will *augment* this job (predictive maintenance, remote diagnostics, guided repair) but the
  augmentation *increases* the value of the human who can physically execute and certify the fix.
  This is the opposite of software roles Hooman is trying to hedge against. [ASSUMED]
- **This is the strongest single argument for the path** given Hooman's "AI-resistant demand" thesis.

### 4c. Geographic flexibility (high)
- Every hospital, imaging center, and lab in every city needs this work → jobs exist **everywhere**,
  not just tech hubs. Highly portable across the US and internationally. [ASSUMED — universal demand]
- FSE roles are **region-based** (you own a territory), which is a plus for staying put *and* a
  minus (travel within the region — see §5).

### 4d. Who hires
1. **Hospitals & health systems** — in-house biomed/clinical-engineering departments (highest BMET
   pay per BLS at $74,560). [VERIFIED — BLS]
2. **OEMs** — **GE HealthCare, Siemens Healthineers, Philips, Canon Medical** — hire imaging FSEs,
   provide factory training academies, company car, best specialist pay. [ASSUMED — well-known
   industry structure]
3. **Independent Service Organizations (ISOs) / HTM outsourcers** — **TRIMEDX, Agiliti, Sodexo HTS,
   Crothall Healthcare** — manage biomed for hospitals under contract; large employers of BMETs and
   FSEs. [ASSUMED — named ISOs are the industry leaders; not re-fetched this session]

## 5. Honest downsides

1. **Pay ceiling & the cut from Adobe (dominant issue).** Best-case imaging-FSE ~$120–145K;
   California senior BMET ~$80–95K. Versus a Bay Area EM package (~$350K+), this is a **60–75%
   income drop.** No realistic path in this field returns to EM comp. [VERIFIED comp; UNVERIFIED EM comp]
2. **Travel — especially FSE.** Imaging FSE = **heavy regional travel**; you cover a territory,
   drive/fly to hospital sites, sometimes multi-state. In-house hospital BMET travels far less
   (one or a few facilities). If Hooman wants to stay local, **hospital BMET > OEM FSE** on
   lifestyle, but FSE > BMET on pay. Trade-off is direct. [ASSUMED — standard FSE role structure]
3. **On-call & unsocial hours.** Medical equipment is 24/7; imaging downtime costs hospitals money,
   so FSEs and senior BMETs carry **on-call rotations, nights/weekends, emergency call-outs.**
   Overtime pay (~$9K/yr on the Indeed CA FSE figure) partly compensates. [VERIFIED overtime figure;
   on-call structure ASSUMED]
4. **Physical demands.** Lifting, crawling behind gantries, confined spaces, standing, cart-pushing.
   Fine now; a consideration over a multi-decade horizon. [ASSUMED]
5. **Entry pay is low.** Entry BMET/FSE ~$44–55K. He starts near the bottom of a new ladder and
   climbs on tenure + certs (CBET at ~2–3 yrs, CRES for imaging). The prestige/authority reset from
   "manager of engineers" to "junior tech" is real and worth naming. [VERIFIED entry pay]
6. **MRI-specific hazard.** Working around superconducting magnets (ferromagnetic-object risk,
   cryogens/quench) requires strict safety discipline. Manageable, but real. [ASSUMED]
