## Why FSE wins on "most assured + keep Adobe" (your top two)

1. **No unpaid gap, no waitlist, no forced quit.** This is the decisive one. MRI's clinical phase is
   an unavoidable ~6–12 month period where you're doing full-time-ish daytime clinicals for free and
   likely can't hold your Adobe role — the whole path hinges on an uncertain Adobe LOA conversation.
   FSE is a clean job-switch: interview while employed, accept a written offer, give notice, start.
   You control the timing and never lose income. **This maps perfectly to "quit only when forced."**
2. **Direct entry is verified, not hoped-for.** GE HealthCare's FSE-1 postings (Sacramento, SF) and
   United Imaging's Customer Service Engineer (Bay Area) explicitly accept an EE/biomed degree with
   **no prior medical-imaging experience** and train you. That was the single biggest unknown and it
   came back positive with cited postings.
3. **The degree does more work here.** Your EE degree substitutes for the 2-yr associate BMET degree
   AND is exactly what the FSE-1 reqs ask for — you're a *target* candidate, not a career-changer
   sneaking in.

**The one honest FSE risk:** OEMs weight *recent hands-on electromechanical troubleshooting*, and your
last decade is software management. Direct entry is plausible, not automatic. **Mitigations:** target
the explicit "will train / no-exp" FSE-1 reqs (GE, United Imaging); grab the **CABT** cert (true
no-experience entry credential); show any hands-on/hardware hobby work; and keep **"start as a general
hospital BMET, specialize into imaging"** as a fallback route if direct imaging-FSE offers don't land.

## When MRI tech is the better pick instead
- If you **strongly prefer patient-facing** work over solo hardware repair.
- If Adobe **will grant a reduced-schedule/LOA** for the ~11-month clinical (turns MRI's biggest
  weakness into a non-issue) — worth a quiet HR conversation.
- If you value the **higher, more predictable long-run floor** (Kaiser NorCal $162–172K on a union
  grid) and will ride out the 1–2 year new-grad valley — ideally in the **Central Valley**
  (Fresno/Bakersfield: explicit "will train," sign-on bonuses, ~60–70% better COL-adjusted income).

## The Canada question (does the answer change?)
**No for FSE, partially-yes for MRI — but only if you're optimizing lifestyle over money.**
- **FSE:** US is clearly better — Canada hires imaging FSEs but the market is thinner and pays ~half
  (USD). Don't move for FSE.
- **MRI:** Canada is *more assured* for MRI (cleaner CAMRT credentialing, cheap domestic tuition
  ~$26K CAD, unionized public-hospital jobs in every town incl. the small cities you'd consider,
  citizenship removes all friction) — but pays **~half in USD** (~$69K vs ~$135K), though small-city
  cost-of-living (home ~4× income vs Bay Area ~10×) offsets much of that. Same clinical-placement
  constraint as the US (no part-time cold-start route). **Canada is a lifestyle/security play, not a
  financial one**, and it only makes sense paired with MRI + a deliberate move.

## Bottom line & next steps

**Lead with FSE.** It best satisfies your two hardest criteria (most-assured + keep-Adobe-until-forced),
your degree makes you a target hire, the pay ceiling is high and immediate with no unpaid valley, and
your travel objection is solvable by targeting dense-metro OEM territories or a zero-travel in-house
hospital role. Pursue MRI tech in parallel only if you lean patient-facing or Adobe grants an LOA.

**Cheap, high-signal next steps (do these before committing a dollar):**
1. **Apply/express interest in 2–3 GE HealthCare FSE-1 + United Imaging CSE openings now** — you can
   interview while at Adobe at zero cost/risk, and a real offer is the ultimate assurance test.
2. **Two 20-min informational interviews:** a working OEM imaging FSE (confirm real travel/on-call +
   Bay Area comp + the rusty-hands concern) and a Bay Area MRI tech / Gurnick program director.
3. **The one MRI-deciding question:** ask Adobe HR (quietly) whether a reduced-schedule/LOA is
   possible — that single fact determines whether MRI is painful or easy.
4. **If leaning MRI:** confirm Gurnick's ARRT(MR) eligibility + a Bay Area clinical placement, and
   price the Central Valley variant.

**Verify-before-acting flags** (from the deep-dives): GE/United live-posting requirements (portals
were JS-blocked; quotes via Indeed/LinkedIn); the "rusty hands" bar at real FSE interviews;
Gurnick ARRT eligibility + gadolinium/venipuncture rule for non-CRT MRI techs in CA.
