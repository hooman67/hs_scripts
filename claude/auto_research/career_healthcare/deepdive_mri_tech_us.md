## 2. THE KEEP-ADOBE QUESTION (the critical one)

**Can the DIDACTIC (classroom) portion be done evenings/weekends/online while working full time? — YES.**
- **VERIFIED:** Gurnick, Pulse, Valley View, Smith Chason all deliver didactic **online.**
- **VERIFIED:** Smith Chason offers a fully **evening (5:30–10:30 PM)** or **weekend** didactic cohort — the
  single most working-professional-friendly classroom schedule found.
- **VERIFIED:** Valley View (Stockton) runs its **online didactic in the evening.**
- **Caveat (VERIFIED):** Gurnick's didactic, while online, uses **required live daytime lectures and no night
  classes**, with up to ~24 hr/week in early quarters — hard to combine with a 9–5. So *for the didactic
  phase, Smith Chason's evening/weekend cohort or Valley View's evening cohort are the keep-Adobe-friendly
  choices; Gurnick's is not.*

**How much FULL-TIME (business-hours, unpaid) CLINICAL ROTATION is required? — this is the binding constraint.**
- **VERIFIED (ARMRIT handbook):** the primary-school route requires **≥1,000 documented MRI clinical hours.**
- **VERIFIED (ARRT):** ARRT does not set an hour count but requires a fixed **competency list** (7 patient-care
  + 8 MRI-safety + 17 mandatory + 12/32 elective imaging + 7 QC procedures), **each observed in person by the
  program director/designee — remote/virtual scanning is NOT accepted.** In practice ARRT-track programs also
  run ~1,000+ clinical hours.
- **1,000 hours ÷ 40 hr/wk ≈ 25 weeks ≈ ~6 months of full-time clinical** (VERIFIED arithmetic).

**Can clinicals be part-time / evening / weekend? — PARTIALLY, and this is the good news.**
- **Gurnick (VERIFIED):** clinical is **2–4 days/week, ~21 hr/week over 48 weeks** — i.e., **NOT 40 hr/week.**
  This is roughly a **half-time** daytime commitment stretched over ~11 months. That opens a real possibility:
  if Adobe permits a **reduced schedule (~3 days/week) or a leave of absence** for ~11 months, he might not have
  to fully quit.
- **Smith Chason (VERIFIED):** externship times "vary by site: **daytime hours, evenings, or weekends as
  approved**" — so an evening/weekend clinical placement is *possible* but **site-dependent and not guaranteed.**
- **Hard limit (VERIFIED):** clinical competencies are **supervisor-gated** — they can only be logged when a
  qualified MRI technologist/preceptor is on shift to observe. Evening/weekend clinicals therefore exist only
  where an outpatient center staffs a preceptor on those shifts. **He cannot self-schedule around Adobe freely.**

## 4. CALIFORNIA LICENSURE — nailed down (this was a prior blocker)

**VERIFIED — California does NOT license or certify MRI technologists.** MRI is non-ionizing (magnetic fields
+ radio waves), so it falls **outside CDPH Radiologic Health Branch (RHB) jurisdiction** (RHB regulates X-ray
machines and radioactive materials only). MRI appears nowhere in RHB's certification categories.
- CDPH RHB scope confirmed via the page (fetched through the `r.jina.ai` reader proxy that bypassed the prior
  SSL/403 block): https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CEH/DRSEM/Pages/RHB.aspx
- California is **not** among the handful of states that license MRI (ARMRIT lists only WA, NM, NH, ND).
  https://www.armrit.org/

**So, to legally work as an MRI tech in CA, he needs ONLY:**
1. **National certification — ARRT (MR) or ARMRIT**, and
2. **BLS/CPR** (employer/clinical requirement, not a state statute).
No CA state exam, no CA application, no CA fee, no renewal with the state.

**Contrast (why MRI is easier than X-ray/CT):** To operate X-ray/CT in CA you must hold a **CRT (Certified
Radiologic Technologist)** from CDPH-RHB — a real state license with its own exam, application, fees, and
add-on **fluoroscopy** and **mammography** permits. **MRI skips this entire apparatus.** (VERIFIED that RHB
administers CRT/fluoro/mammo; exam/fee specifics ASSUMED.)

**The one caveat — contrast injection / venipuncture (UNVERIFIED, flag):** Starting an IV and injecting
**gadolinium** is a separate regulated act from operating the scanner. In CA this is commonly done by an RN, or
by a tech with a **venipuncture certificate**. The exact rule for a **non-CRT MRI tech** is genuinely ambiguous
and site/policy-dependent — the specific CDPH venipuncture page 404'd on every attempt. **This does not change
the headline (no MRI operating license), but confirm directly with CDPH-RHB or a CA imaging employer whether an
MRI tech needs a separate venipuncture cert to push gad.** Many programs include a venipuncture module.

**ARRT vs ARMRIT for CA (VERIFIED):** Irrelevant to *state law* (CA licenses neither). For *employability*,
**ARRT(MR) is more universally accepted**; ARMRIT is accepted by some CA systems (**Sutter Health Oakland
posting explicitly lists "ARMRIT … OR ARRT (MRI)"** as acceptable — VERIFIED) plus LA County DHS, and is
recognized by ACR/CMS/Joint Commission for facility accreditation. **For "most assured," get ARRT(MR).**
