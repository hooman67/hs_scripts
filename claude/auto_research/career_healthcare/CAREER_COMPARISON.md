## The recommendation — the "imaging pair"

The top two are complementary, not competing, and both were near-invisible until we filtered on
*your* specific background. Both land ~$135–142K in the Bay Area, both are EE/physics-heavy, both
are ≤1-yr entry, and they sit on opposite sides of the same machine:

- **#1 MRI Technologist — operate the scanner.** The single best **degree-leverage** fit of the
  entire set: MRI is applied electromagnetics + RF + gradient physics, which is literally your EE
  undergrad. ~$135K Bay Area, no CA state license (non-ionizing radiation), ~12–18-mo primary
  program (ARMRIT). AI-resistant: AI is coming for the radiologist who *reads* the image, not the
  tech who *positions the patient and runs the machine*. **Downsides:** patient-facing/physical,
  shift/weekend work, and the binding real-world constraint is unpaid full-time clinical-rotation
  hours (a degree can't waive them) + selective-admission waitlists that can stretch "12 months"
  into 2–3 calendar years.

- **#2 Imaging Field Service Engineer — repair the scanner.** The path your degree targets *most*
  directly: OEMs (GE HealthCare, Siemens, Philips, Canon) hire EEs and train on payroll, so entry
  is genuinely ≤1 year with no separate program. Highest ceiling (~$142K) and **most AI-resistant**
  (physical on-site regulated hardware repair). **Downside:** heavy regional travel (the in-house
  hospital BMET variant trades ~$50K of ceiling for a local, no-travel, predictable-hours life).

**Both are a 60–75% cut from Adobe EM comp** — that's the honest, unavoidable headline. Within the
career set you asked me to rank, these two maximize pay AND degree-leverage simultaneously, which is
why they top the list.
