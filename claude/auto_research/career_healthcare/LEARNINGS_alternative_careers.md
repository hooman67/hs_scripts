## 3. Durable learnings by theme

### Degree-leverage & the ≤1-year filter reshaped the whole field
- **The two hard constraints (leverage degree + ~1 yr) are extremely discriminating** — they
  eliminated most of healthcare imaging (the technologist family is mostly 2-yr associate programs)
  and all of the electrician trade. What survived clustered tightly around **imaging** (operate or
  repair the scanner) because that's where an EE degree is genuinely on-target.
- **MRI physics = applied electromagnetics/RF/gradients = his EE undergrad.** Of every healthcare
  role, MRI tech and imaging FSE leverage his specific degree the most. This is why they rose to the
  top when nothing else did — the filter *found* them, they weren't obvious going in.
- **A prior bachelor's satisfies credential-level degree requirements but NOT program length.**
  VERIFIED: ARRT accepts "an associate or higher degree in any subject" — so his bachelor's clears
  the degree gate for MRI cert. But it does NOT shorten the modality-specific program (still 18–21mo)
  and cannot waive the unpaid full-time clinical hours. **"Degree gets you in the door, not through
  the program faster"** is the recurring pattern.

### The "keep-Adobe" axis is the sharpest differentiator between the two winners
- **FSE is a JOB, not a course** → he can interview while employed, accept a written offer, and
  switch paycheck-to-paycheck. **No unpaid gap, no waitlist, no forced leave.** This is the single
  biggest reason FSE wins his top-two criteria.
- **MRI's clinical phase forces the Adobe question.** Didactic (~first 6 months) can be done online
  evening/weekend (Smith Chason evening/weekend cohort; Valley View evening) while working full-time.
  But ~1,000 hours of **in-person, supervisor-observed clinical** (can't be remote) forces a
  reduced-schedule/LOA or quit around **month 6** for 6–12 months. **No part-time cold-start clinical
  route exists in the US OR Canada** — the online/part-time MRI programs all require an existing
  imaging credential first.
- **The one fact that decides MRI's difficulty: will Adobe grant a reduced-schedule/LOA?** Gurnick's
  clinical is only ~21 hr/wk over 48 weeks, so with Adobe flexibility it could be a step-back rather
  than a clean quit. Worth a quiet HR conversation before choosing MRI.

### "Most-assured" hinges on different risks for each path
- **FSE's risk is fixable at the interview: "rusty hands."** Direct EE-to-FSE entry is VERIFIED real
  (GE HealthCare FSE-1 in Sacramento/SF + United Imaging CSE in the Bay Area explicitly accept an
  EE/biomed degree with NO prior medical-imaging experience and train you). His degree even
  substitutes for the 2-yr associate BMET degree. The gap is that OEMs weight *recent hands-on
  electromechanical troubleshooting* and his last decade is software management. **Mitigations:**
  target explicit "will-train/no-exp" FSE-1 reqs (GE, United Imaging), grab the CABT entry cert,
  show hands-on work; keep "start as a general hospital BMET → specialize into imaging" as a fallback.
- **MRI's risk is a structural market reality: the new-grad hiring wall.** Training is assured
  (local Gurnick program exists in San Jose; CA does NOT license MRI techs — non-ionizing, so only
  national cert ARRT-MR/ARMRIT + CPR needed; private schools have ~no waitlist). BUT nearly every
  Bay Area posting demands experience ("Tech II, 1+ yr MRI"). New grads start at outpatient centers
  ($38–67/hr) for **1–2 years** before reaching top pay (Kaiser NorCal $162–172K/yr).

### Certifications & licensing facts worth keeping
- **CA does NOT license/certify MRI technologists** (VERIFIED — non-ionizing, outside CDPH-RHB).
  Only national cert + BLS/CPR needed. Contrast: X-ray/CT require a CDPH CRT license (state exam/fees).
- **ARRT(MR) is the most-assured credential** (universal); ARMRIT is cheaper/more flexible and
  accepted by some CA employers (Sutter confirmed) but slightly narrower.
- **CBET is a mid-career cert (needs on-job years first), NOT an entry ticket.** CABT is the true
  no-experience entry credential. A bachelor's does NOT waive CBET's experience requirement but lets
  him sit CBET Candidate status. **No cert is required to be HIRED as an FSE** (voluntary).
- **Gadolinium/venipuncture** for a non-CRT MRI tech in CA is an ambiguous separate regulated act
  (UNVERIFIED) — confirm with CDPH-RHB / an employer.

### Pay reality (the honest headline across everything)
- **Every path is a ~60–75% cut from Adobe EM comp (~$350K+).** Unavoidable. Within the set:
  MRI tech ~$120–172K experienced (Bay Area, once past the new-grad valley); FSE ~$78K entry →
  $105–198K senior + OT + company car; in-house imaging engineer (SCVMC) $143–226K.
- **Travel objection to FSE is SOLVABLE** (correction to the first-pass assumption): OEM FSE is
  home-based, assigned-metro, company car, home most nights; dense-metro territory is small; and
  zero-travel in-house hospital imaging-engineer roles exist (SCVMC, El Camino, Stanford, UCSF).

### Geography learnings
- **Central Valley (Fresno/Bakersfield) is the smart MRI variant** — explicit "will train" postings +
  sign-on bonuses (softens the new-grad wall) AND ~60–70% better COL-adjusted income than SF
  (Fresno home = 3.5× income vs SF's 11.6×).
- **Canada is a lifestyle/security decision, not a financial one.** Both careers pay ~half in USD
  (MRI ~$69K, FSE ~$44–80K). His citizenship makes it frictionless and MRI credentialing is cleaner
  (CAMRT single national exam; cheap domestic tuition ~$26K CAD; unionized public-hospital jobs in
  every town). But same clinical-placement constraint, and the pay gap only closes via small-city
  cost-of-living. **Only makes sense paired with MRI + a deliberate move.** Don't move for FSE
  (US market is thicker and pays double).

## 4. Meta-learnings about the research itself
- **Filtering on the person's *specific* background surfaced non-obvious winners.** MRI tech and
  imaging FSE were near-invisible until we filtered on "EE-biomed degree + ≤1yr + healthcare" — a
  reminder that generic "best healthcare careers" lists would have missed the two best-fit roles.
- **First-pass assumptions needed correction by deep research** (same pattern as the whole project):
  the "~12-month MRI program" was really 18–21mo; "FSE = heavy travel" was really "solvable in a
  dense metro"; "does a local program even exist" resolved to YES (Gurnick). Cheap-to-check facts
  moved the recommendation materially — never stop at the first-pass scan.
- **The decisive question is often an internal/relational one, not a research one:** here it's
  "will Adobe grant a reduced-schedule/LOA," which no amount of web research answers — it's a
  conversation Hooman must have.
