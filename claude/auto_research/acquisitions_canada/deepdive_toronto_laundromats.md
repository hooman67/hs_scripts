## 2. Laundromat acquisition economics (the rules of this game)

**Valuation = a multiple of SDE (Seller's Discretionary Earnings), and the lease sets the multiple.**

- **SDE multiples: 3–5x** for owner-operator single stores; **2.5–5.0x** on 2026 real-sale data;
  ~2x–4x SDE / 4–7x EBITDA on larger comparable M&A. Median laundromat sale price **$250K**
  across 855 transactions. *(VERIFIED — ctacquisitions.com, valzura.com, 1800bizbroker.com.)*
- **A laundromat's value is set by three mechanical realities: machine age, lease length, and
  utility bills.** *(VERIFIED — valzura.com.)* Factors like lease-vs-own, equipment age, card-vs-coin,
  and store density move the final price **by 25%+.** *(VERIFIED — ctacquisitions.com.)*
- **Utilities run 20–25% of gross revenue** for commercial laundry — the single largest variable
  cost and the thing that quietly destroys SDE when gas/water inflate. *(VERIFIED — industry sources.)*

### Why the LEASE is the #1 value driver — and why 2.5 yrs is near-fatal for Scrubby's

A laundromat is a bolted-down box of plumbing, gas lines, and 72 machines. **The business has
almost no value the day the lease ends** — you cannot move a laundromat without re-plumbing and
re-venting a new shell (six-figure buildout) and you lose the customer base. So the lease term is
not a detail; **it is the asset's remaining useful life.** Consequences of Scrubby's ~2.5-yr tail:

1. **You are buying ~2.5 years of cash flow, then a renegotiation you don't control.** At renewal the
   landlord holds all leverage (your machines are captive) and can raise rent sharply or refuse. A
   laundromat priced at 3–5x SDE *implicitly assumes 10–15+ years of runway*; paying a going-concern
   multiple for 2.5 years of certainty is structurally overpaying. **ASSUMED**: fair value of a
   2.5-yr-lease store is closer to **1–2x SDE** (a cash-flow annuity), not 3–5x.
2. **Financing collapses.** Lenders (CSBFP-backed banks, BDC) want the **loan amortization ≤ remaining
   lease term.** A 2.5-yr lease can't support a 7–10 yr equipment loan → **effectively all-cash only.**
   *(ASSUMED from standard lender practice; VERIFY with the bank.)*
3. **Resale is impaired** — your future buyer inherits an even shorter tail, so you may be forced to
   renew on bad terms *before* you can exit.

**Mitigation, and the ONLY thing that rescues Scrubby's:** make the purchase **conditional on the
landlord granting a fresh long lease (e.g. 5+5) BEFORE closing.** If the landlord won't, walk. Do not
buy 2.5 years at a going-concern price. **This single term matters more than the $345K price.**

6210 Finch's **5+5+5 (up to 15 yrs)** is precisely the opposite — a long, financeable, resaleable
runway. That lease is a large part of why it can ask $725K.

## 3. The utility-vs-revenue verification technique (Hooman's edge — where his EE/data skill is real)

Net income is never published in this category ("financials on NDA"), and coin revenue is trivially
inflatable by a seller. **Water/gas consumption is not inflatable** — it is metered by a utility and
physically proportional to machine turns. This is the single highest-leverage diligence move.

**The method** *(VERIFIED — laundromatresource.com, flowaudit.net, trycents.com, unclefed.com/IRS
laundromat audit technique guide):*

1. Pull **24–36 months of water bills** (and gas + hydro) directly from the utility, not the seller.
2. Convert billed water volume → **number of wash cycles**: a commercial front-load washer uses
   ~**20–30 gallons per turn** (varies by capacity). Total gallons ÷ gallons-per-turn ≈ total washes.
   **ASSUMED** per-cycle figure — calibrate to the exact Electrolux/other models on site.
3. Multiply washes × **average vend price** (e.g. $3.50–$6.00/wash in Toronto) → an **independent
   revenue estimate.** Dryers add ~25–40% on top of wash revenue.
4. **Compare to the seller's claimed revenue.** If claimed revenue >> water-implied revenue, the
   seller is inflating (or running unmetered side income). If it matches, the top line is real.
5. Cross-check the **reverse ratio**: if utilities are only ~20–25% of *real* revenue, then
   utility_dollars ÷ 0.22 ≈ revenue. A store claiming $338K/yr on utility bills implying $180K is a
   red flag. *(VERIFIED ratio.)*

**Caveat** *(VERIFIED)*: water/gas **not consumed by machines** (bathroom, mop sink, HVAC) must be
excluded; the water-bill method is **one tool, not the whole audit** — pair it with processor reports
and tax returns. This is exactly Hooman's wheelhouse: a simple regression of monthly utility volume
against monthly processor revenue will expose any fabricated months.

## 4. Unattended / card-app model — pros & cons (the reason this is even on the list)

**Pros**
- **Near-zero labor** → 5–15 owner-hrs/wk; the only physical-asset shape a US-resident can run
  semi-remotely. Machines report online (cycle counts, faults, revenue) → **real-time processor data
  = built-in fraud detection** for Hooman's post-purchase monitoring.
- **Card/app kills coin-skim risk** and gives clean, auditable revenue (great for *your* future resale).
- **Dynamic pricing / remote price changes / marketing** — modest but real edge for a data/cloud founder.

**Cons**
- **Still needs a trusted local pair of hands**: (a) a **cleaner** several times/week (unattended ≠
  self-cleaning; dirty stores die), and (b) an **on-call appliance tech** for breakdowns (a dead bank
  of machines on a Saturday is lost revenue you can't fix from the US). **This is where the wife or a
  local operator is load-bearing** — budget ~$1.5–3K CAD/mo for cleaning + a tech-on-retainer.
- **App/processor fee drag** (typically ~5–10% of card revenue) vs. "free" coins.
- **Card conversion capex** if not already done (Scrubby's is coin; 6210 Finch already pay-by-phone).
- **Unattended stores attract more wear/vandalism** without a human presence — camera + remote-lock
  systems are table stakes.

**Read**: 6210 Finch is *already* the target model (unattended, 2017 gear, pay-by-phone). Scrubby's is
an **attended, coin, older-model** store that you'd have to *convert* — added capex on a 2.5-yr lease
makes the conversion economically irrational.

## 5. Equipment age / replacement capex

- Commercial washers/dryers last **~7–10 years**; **plan replacement at the 8-yr mark.**
  *(VERIFIED — industry sources.)*
- Replacement cost **~$3,000–$6,000 per machine** (high-capacity extractors $15K+), plus
  **$1,000–$5,000 install** each, and **$500–$1,500/yr maintenance** per unit. *(VERIFIED.)*
- **6210 Finch**: 2017 Electrolux = **~9 years old at purchase → at/near end of life.** A 51-machine
  re-equip at ~$4,500 + install could be **$230K–$450K CAD** looming within a few years. This must be
  priced into the $725K — you may be buying a machine fleet with limited remaining life at a
  going-concern multiple. **VERIFY each machine's install date and condition.** **ASSUMED** capex range.
- **Scrubby's**: age undisclosed; 72 machines. If old, replacement liability is even larger — and
  irrational to fund against a 2.5-yr lease.

## 6. Toronto coin-laundry competition

Dense, mature market — both sites sit in **high-apartment, renter-heavy catchments** (Flemingdon Park;
Mount Olive/Rexdale), which is the *right* demographic (renters without in-suite laundry = captive
demand, recession-resistant). *(ASSUMED from neighborhood profile.)* But density also means **a new
competitor or a building adding in-suite laundry can dent revenue**, and there is **no moat** beyond
location + lease. Verify how many laundromats sit within ~1.5 km and whether nearby buildings are
adding in-unit machines. **UNVERIFIED — do a map + permit scan.**

## 7. Financing — mostly cash (this is the hard truth)

- **CSBFP** (Canada Small Business Financing Program) finances **equipment, leasehold improvements,
  real property, intellectual property, and working capital** — up to ~$500K for equipment/leaseholds.
  It does **NOT finance goodwill.** *(VERIFIED — ised-isde.canada.ca lists "working capital,
  intellectual property, renovations, equipment"; goodwill absent. Consistent with CANADA_SEARCH_REPORT §4.)*
- **BDC** does business-transfer financing but "likes hard assets/real estate" and is **cool on pure
  leasehold goodwill.** *(ASSUMED from BDC practice + prior report.)*
- **Implication**: in a laundromat *leasehold* deal, a large share of the price **IS goodwill**
  (the price above depreciated equipment value). Neither CSBFP nor BDC funds that. So:
  - **6210 Finch $725K**: perhaps the *equipment* portion (~$150–250K depreciated) is CSBFP-eligible;
    the **goodwill balance (~$450K+) is cash.** Realistically a **~$500K+ cash** deal → **over budget.**
  - **Scrubby's $345K**: 2.5-yr lease **fails the "amortization ≤ lease term" test** → **all-cash.**
- **Net**: treat both as **substantially cash purchases.** This erases the "BDC leverage" advantage
  that made the Canadian SaaS path attractive — a laundromat does **not** exploit his citizenship-
  financing edge.

## 9. Per-listing verdict

### 6210 Finch — **CONDITIONAL PURSUE, but over budget; make it prove itself.**
- **Best profile in the category**: unattended, pay-by-phone, long 5+5+5 lease, right demographic —
  genuinely semi-remote-operable, the one thing that matters for a US-resident buyer.
- **But**: (a) **$725K is over his ~$500K cash budget** and mostly-cash (goodwill unfinanceable);
  (b) **2017 equipment is ~9 yrs old → a $230–450K re-equip cliff** you're paying a premium to inherit;
  (c) net income is **undisclosed** — never pay a going-concern price on "very high net income."
- **Action**: submit an **offer conditional on (i) 60 days to verify processor + utility + tax records,
  (ii) a machine-by-machine age/condition audit, and (iii) re-price down for equipment life.** If the
  water-bill regression confirms strong real revenue AND you can get the price toward ~$550–600K
  reflecting equipment age, it's the **only laundromat worth a serious swing.** Otherwise pass.

### Scrubby's — **PASS unless the landlord grants a fresh long lease pre-close.**
- **Fits all-cash ($345K)** and has a **VERIFIED $338K coin top line** — attractive on the surface.
- **Fatal flaw**: **~2.5-yr lease** with no disclosed renewal. You'd pay a 3–5x going-concern multiple
  for what is really a ~2.5-yr cash-flow annuity, it's **not financeable**, it's **attended + coin +
  older gear** (wrong model for a remote owner, plus conversion capex), and **resale is impaired.**
- **Action**: **only** proceed if you can get the purchase **conditional on a new 5+5 lease signed
  before closing** at a known rent. If the landlord signs, re-run the economics as a long-lease store
  (then it's a real candidate at $345K). **If not, walk — the price is irrelevant.**

**Overall**: 6210 Finch is the better business but over budget and equipment-aged; Scrubby's is
affordable but structurally broken by its lease. **At most one swing — and only after the checklist
below clears.** Consistent with the parent report: this category ranks **below Canadian SaaS** for
this buyer (mostly-cash, semi-remote-at-best, no financing edge), but the unattended-laundromat shape
is the one physical asset where his diligence-alpha and remote-ops interest are real.

## 10. "What to verify" checklist (processor reports + utility records + lease)

**Before any offer goes hard:**

- [ ] **Payment-processor / card-app reports, 24–36 mo, exported by YOU from the processor account**
      (not a seller PDF). Reconcile monthly card + coin totals. For 6210 Finch this is the primary
      revenue proof; for coin-only Scrubby's, demand coin-collection logs + bank deposit records.
- [ ] **Utility records pulled directly from the utility** (Toronto Water, Enbridge gas, Toronto Hydro),
      24–36 mo. Run the **water-bill → wash-count → revenue** reconstruction and a monthly
      **utility-volume vs. revenue regression** to catch fabricated months.
- [ ] **Utilities as % of revenue** — confirm it lands in the 20–25% band; a suspiciously *low* ratio
      implies inflated revenue, a *high* ratio implies thin real SDE / aging inefficient machines.
- [ ] **LEASE — read the actual document**: exact months remaining, renewal options & rent-escalation
      formula, assignment/transfer clause, demolition/relocation clause, who pays TMI, any
      personal-guarantee requirement. **For Scrubby's: get a written new-lease commitment BEFORE close.**
- [ ] **Tax returns / T2 + HST filings, 3 yrs** — tie top line to CRA-filed numbers, not a spreadsheet.
- [ ] **Machine-by-machine age & condition audit** with a commercial-laundry tech; build the
      replacement-capex schedule (esp. 6210 Finch's 2017 fleet).
- [ ] **Local operator plan** — confirm the cleaner + on-call tech (wife or hired) and cost it into SDE.
- [ ] **Competition scan** — laundromats within ~1.5 km + any nearby buildings adding in-suite laundry.
- [ ] **Financing pre-check** — confirm with a CSBFP lender/BDC exactly what (equipment only) is
      financeable and that lease term supports any amortization; assume mostly cash.
- [ ] **s.167 GST/HST election** on the asset sale (accountant) to avoid tax on the transfer.
