## CATEGORY ECONOMICS (Canadian B2B distribution / niche mfg / light industrial)

- **Typical SDE multiples (Canada, sub-$1M SDE):**
  - Owner-operator trades/service (GC, HVAC, electromechanical): **2.0–3.0× SDE** — cheap because
    cash flow ≈ the owner's own labour and doesn't transfer cleanly.
  - Niche/precision manufacturing & machine shops: **2.5–3.5× SDE** typical; the Precision-Machined
    listing at 4.8× is above-market.
  - Specialty distribution/wholesale: **2.5–4× SDE** but heavy on inventory/working capital, so
    effective cash needed is higher than the multiple implies.
  - Recurring-revenue B2B services (service contracts, maintenance): **3–4×+**, deservedly.
- **Owner-hours:** Trades/GC/HVAC are typically **full-time owner-operator, 45–60 hrs/wk**, on-site.
  Distribution can be **semi-absentee** with a warehouse/ops manager. Management-led constructors
  (like the $6.2M BC listing) exist but sit well above this budget.
- **Cash yield:** At the low multiples common here (2–3× SDE), unlevered cash-on-cash yield to an
  all-cash buyer is roughly **30–50%** on paper — but that "yield" is largely a salary for a job you
  must show up to, which is the core caveat for a remote/absentee buyer.
- **Durability / AI-safe:** Physical fabrication, motor repair, HVAC service, GC — all AI-safe demand
  and not disruption-prone; that's the upside of this category. The downside is none of it is
  software-scalable, and all of it is skilled-labour-gated.

**Verdict on category economics:** Real, durable cash flow at honest (often cheap) multiples —
this is where Canadian SDE actually shows up. But the cash flow is overwhelmingly *owner-labour*,
skilled-trade-licensed, and physically on-site — the exact opposite of Hooman's remote/US constraint
and software-scalable edge.

## FINANCING NOTES (BDC / CSBFP / bank acquisition loans — Canadian citizen)

- **BDC business-acquisition financing** (verified, bdc.ca): BDC explicitly finances business
  purchases including **goodwill, IP, and client lists**, refinances vendor notes, and offers
  cash-flow-matched repayment. Requirement: Canadian-based, revenue-generating, good credit. BDC
  does **not** publish a fixed down-payment %; in practice for these sizes expect **~20–35% equity/
  down**, and BDC frequently **co-lends alongside a chartered bank** rather than funding 100%.
- **CSBFP (Canada Small Business Financing Program):** government-backed, but note it lends against
  **equipment, real property, and leasehold improvements — NOT goodwill or share purchases.** Cap is
  **$1.15M total** (of which up to $500K for equipment/leaseholds, higher tranche for real property).
  For a share deal or a goodwill-heavy service business, CSBFP alone won't fund it — you pair it with
  a BDC/bank term loan for the goodwill portion.
- **Seller financing (vendor take-back):** common and present in this batch — the **BC GC (#44625001),
  Electromechanical (#54714002)** both advertise seller financing; a VTB of 10–30% is normal and de-
  risks the deal for both sides and for BDC.
- **Practical structure for a ~$500K deal:** ~$100–175K Hooman cash down + BDC/bank term loan (5–10 yr)
  + 10–25% seller VTB. That comfortably reaches the ~$700K–$1M price band, so the HVAC ($1.1M) and a
  negotiated Precision-Machined ($880K) become financeable even though they exceed $500K all-cash.
- **Canadian specifics:** HST registration required (~13% ON / 5% GST + PST in BC); trades need
  provincial licensing (ON: TSSA G2/313A for gas/HVAC, ESA for electrical; BC: TQ/Red Seal, WorkSafeBC,
  bonding for GC); asset vs share purchase changes tax (CCA/goodwill amortization vs LCGE for seller);
  a non-resident-managed CCPC is possible but a Canadian-resident director/manager is often needed —
  reinforcing the remote-operability issue below.
