## Why SaaS is the natural best-fit category for this founder

- **Remote-operable by default.** Nearly every software business here can be run from the US on
  TN status; no on-the-ground Canadian presence required (the biggest practical win vs. every
  other category in this search).
- **Founder-market fit is maximal.** AI/ML + data engineering + cloud/infra + strong diligence.
  He can personally do the value-creation (migrate off legacy stack, add AI features, cut infra
  cost, automate ops) that a non-technical acquirer can't.
- **The catch:** Canadian SaaS priced under ~$500K CAD is overwhelmingly either (a) tiny/pre-
  revenue "AI app" flips launched in 2026, or (b) genuinely good businesses whose asking prices
  sit at $700K–$1.2M+ — i.e., just above the all-cash ceiling but reachable **with financing**.
  Pure all-cash sub-$500K CAD SaaS with durable recurring revenue is thin. The real opportunity
  is the ~$700K–$1M tier bought with ~$400–500K down + BDC/seller paper.

## Category economics (Canadian SaaS, sub-$1M tier)

- **Typical multiples:** Micro/small Canadian B2B SaaS trades **~3–5x SDE** (or ~2–4x ARR for
  cleaner recurring-revenue businesses; the media/AI listings push to 4x+ revenue on story). The
  BizQuest staffing SaaS (~4x SDE, ~2.2x revenue) is a fair mid-point comp. "AI app" flips launched
  in 2026 are priced on narrative (∞x) — ignore.
- **Cash yield:** A well-bought 4x-SDE SaaS = ~25% pre-tax owner earnings yield on purchase price.
  On a $1.35M deal with ~$338K CAD SDE, that services a BDC/seller note comfortably.
- **Owner-hours:** Genuinely low for a mature, contractor-supported SaaS (the BizQuest one runs on
  5 contractors, home-based). Owner time concentrates in **sales/rainmaking** — the transferable
  risk to diligence. Founder's engineering edge lets him cut infra/dev cost and add AI features
  without hiring, protecting margin.
- **Reality of the ≤$500K CAD all-cash tier:** dominated by tiny/pre-revenue apps (grocery,
  restaurant, pet). Durable recurring-revenue SaaS at ≤$500K all-cash is rare; the good ones sit
  at $700K–$1.35M and require financing.

## Financing (Canadian, SaaS-specific)  — Hooman IS eligible (Canadian citizen)

- **CSBFP (Canada Small Business Financing Program):** As of the **June 2022 expansion**, the
  program raised the max to **$1.15M CAD total per borrower**, of which up to **$500K CAD** can be
  for equipment, leasehold improvements, **intangible assets, and working capital** (the 2022
  change explicitly added intangibles + working capital — previously it was equipment/property
  only). This matters because **SaaS acquisitions are almost all goodwill/intangibles**, which the
  old CSBFP would NOT fund. Now a chunk can be. Lender fronts the loan; government guarantees ~85%.
  Confirm current terms with the lender — the ISED overview page confirms coverage now includes
  "working capital costs, intellectual property, renovations, equipment and more."
  Source: https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-small-business-financing-program/en
- **BDC acquisition financing:** BDC lends against business acquisitions including goodwill-heavy
  deals, typically expecting the buyer to inject **~10–25% equity (down payment)** and often
  pairing a term loan with a seller note; BDC is more comfortable than a chartered bank with
  intangible/cash-flow-based (vs. asset-backed) SaaS deals. Terms are cash-flow-underwritten, so a
  business with clean, provable SDE and low churn (like the BizQuest staffing SaaS) is exactly
  what they'll finance. (BDC "buy a business" hub was 404 at fetch time — confirm live figures
  with a BDC account manager; the 10–25% equity + seller-note structure is the standard norm.)
- **Practical structure for the top pick (~$1.35M CAD):** ~$400–500K CAD Hooman equity (his cash)
  + BDC/bank term loan + a seller note (seller financing is offered). Keeps it inside his cash
  down-payment ability while acquiring a >$500K asset — the whole point of the Canadian route.
- **Canadian specifics to remember:** HST/GST on any service-revenue portion; software SaaS is
  generally taxable supply — register for GST/HST. No provincial trade-licensing for pure software
  (unlike trades), EXCEPT regulated verticals: the **lending** listing (money-services/consumer-
  lending licensing) and any **payroll/HR** SaaS touching payroll remittances (compliance surface,
  not a license to sell). CAD vs USD pricing matters — the top BizQuest deal is quoted in **USD**.

## Remote operability (running from the US on TN)

**Best category in the whole search for remote operation.** Every listing above is software and
can be operated from the US; several are explicitly "home-based / relocatable / remote." No
Canadian physical presence needed to run them. Caveats:
- The business must be **Canadian-domiciled** (corp registered in ON/BC) to unlock BDC/CSBFP —
  keep the entity Canadian even while Hooman operates remotely.
- Ones with **employees** (cybersecurity SaaS: 6 staff) are less "solo-remote" than contractor-run
  ones (staffing SaaS: 5 contractors) — payroll/management overhead.
- TN status doesn't restrict owning/managing a *Canadian* company remotely; the constraint is on
  operating a *US* business. This category sidesteps it cleanly.

## Category verdict

**Strong BUY category — the best-fit and most remote-operable of the whole Canadian search — but
the true sub-$500K-all-cash shelf is thin.** The genuinely durable, cash-flowing Canadian SaaS
(the BizQuest 16-yr BC staffing platform: <2% churn, 55% margin, ~4x SDE, ~$338K CAD SDE) sits at
~$1.35M CAD and is a **finance-with-BDC/seller-note** deal, not all-cash. The all-cash ≤$500K tier
is dominated by 2026 "AI app" flips with no moat (skip). **Recommended play:** target one proven,
low-churn vertical B2B SaaS in the $700K–$1.35M range, put ~$400–500K CAD down, and lever the rest
with BDC + seller paper — using Canadian citizenship for the financing he can't get in the US.
Lead candidate: **#1 BC Staffing-Mgmt SaaS**; watch **#2 Quebec SaaS** (if it lands near $500K)
and **#4 Ontario cybersecurity SaaS** (if priced modestly) as closer-to-all-cash alternatives.
