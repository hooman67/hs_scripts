## Method & source reality

Real listings were pulled by browsing live marketplaces (2026-07-04):
- **BizBuySell (Canada filter)** — usable via `www.bizbuysell.com/canada-businesses-for-sale/N/`
  pagination. This was the productive source. Deal-detail pages are public and gave real numbers.
- **businessesforsale.com/canada** — 403 blocked to automated fetch (Cloudflare); could not scrape.
- **Flippa / Empire Flippers / Acquire.com** — listing bodies are JS-rendered / gated; country=Canada
  metadata not exposed to fetch. Empire Flippers requires an account to see seller location. These
  platforms DO carry Canadian FBA/e-com brands but locations are hidden pre-account; flagged "gated".
- **Sunbelt Canada / DealStream / Kijiji / VRBB** — 403 / broken / no results to automated fetch.

Net: BizBuySell Canada surfaced ~8 genuine e-commerce/DTC listings across 10 result pages. Pure
DTC brands are a *thin* slice of the Canadian SMB market — most "Canada for sale" inventory is
brick-and-mortar retail, food service, and trades. That scarcity is itself a finding.

All figures below are as-listed by the seller/broker; SDE and revenue are **seller-reported,
unaudited** — treat as ASSUMED until QoE diligence. CAD unless noted USD.

## Category economics (Canadian DTC/e-com reality)

- **Typical SDE multiples:** small Canadian DTC brands under ~$500K trade **2.5x–4x SDE**; higher
  quality (strong repeat %, trademark, clean books, >30% margin) reaches **4x–4.5x** (e.g. the
  Vancouver beauty brand at ~4.2x). This is *lower* than clean US SaaS but in line with US small
  e-com. Inventory is frequently bundled into "asking price," inflating the headline multiple — always
  separate **enterprise value from working capital**.
- **Cash yield:** at 3–4x SDE, unlevered pre-tax yield ≈ **25–40% of purchase price**, but real owner
  take-home is lower once you pay a fulfillment operator you're replacing and normalize add-backs.
- **Margins:** consumables/personal-care (deodorant, beauty) run **25–35% SDE margin**; inventory-heavy
  categories (general e-com, furniture, apparel) run **thin 4–10%** and are working-capital traps.
- **Owner hours:** 20–40 hrs/wk typical; the binding constraint is almost always **physical fulfillment
  / manufacturing**, not the storefront. The best remote-fit deals are ones where a co-packer + 3PL
  already exist or can be contracted.
- **Moat quality ranking observed:** subscription/Subscribe-&-Save recurring > trademarked own-formula
  consumable > multi-channel branded goods > single-marketplace reseller > dropship (avoid).

## Financing notes (Canadian leverage)

- **CSBFP** (Canada Small Business Financing Program): purpose-built for **equipment, leasehold
  improvements, and real property** — **NOT for goodwill, inventory, or share purchases.** Most small
  DTC acquisitions are asset/goodwill + inventory deals, so **CSBFP is largely unusable** for the
  brand-value portion. It can finance a warehouse buildout or equipment if the deal includes any.
- **BDC:** does small-business acquisition/transition financing and is the more relevant lender here.
  Expect **20–35% buyer equity down**, personal guarantee, and a strong appetite for tangible-asset or
  cash-flow-backed deals. Pure-goodwill DTC brands (little equipment, seasonal inventory) are harder to
  lever — BDC/banks discount goodwill heavily. Realistically, small DTC brands under $500K are often
  done **mostly cash + seller note.**
- **Seller notes** are common and available on several listings above (deodorant, smart-home, staffing
  SaaS) — typically **10–25% of price**, 2–4 yr, and the single best leverage source for these deals.
- **Non-resident lending friction:** Hooman is a **Canadian citizen** (good — no immigration bar to own/
  operate), but living/banking in the US may complicate BDC underwriting (Canadian address, credit file,
  guarantee). Expect to need a Canadian personal banking history or to relocate for the cleanest terms.
- **HST/GST:** register for GST/HST; asset sales can use the **s.167 election** to avoid GST on the
  transfer of a business as a going concern — build into the purchase agreement.

## Remote-operability from the US (the real constraint)

The honest verdict: **most Canadian DTC brands are NOT truly remote** because the moat (own-brand
consumable) is tied to physical manufacture/fulfillment in Canada. Ranking within this set:
- **REMOTE:** Smart-Home E-com+SaaS (app + cloud subscriptions; e-com paused) — the only genuinely
  laptop-runnable one, and coincidentally the best founder-fit.
- **SEMI-ABSENTEE:** Deodorant, Beauty, Furniture, HelloDough — sellable/marketable remotely, but each
  needs a Canadian-based maker/3PL for production & shipping. Runnable from the US *if* you install/
  keep an operator or co-packer; not fully hands-off.
- **ON-SITE-REQUIRED:** Calgary general e-com, apparel, building-supply — warehouse + staff + inventory
  demand ground presence in Canada.

## Category verdict

**Cautiously positive but not the top category for this founder — MODERATE.** Canadian DTC brands under
$500K exist and are cheap (2.5–4x SDE) with seller financing available, and a couple are genuinely good
businesses (the **Ontario natural-deodorant brand at $349K/$100K SDE, 13-yr, 1,000+ Subscribe & Save

**semi-absentee at best from the US**, not remote; (2) the category **barely uses Hooman's AI/data edge**;
(3) **CSBFP won't finance goodwill** and BDC discounts it, so leverage is limited — these are mostly
cash+seller-note deals; (4) inventory-bundled pricing and gated financials require heavy diligence.
Best single pick if pursuing this lane: **the Ontario deodorant brand** (durable niche, real recurring
revenue, retirement seller, seller financing, all-cash-affordable), with a **contract co-packer + 3PL**
installed so it can be run semi-absentee while Hooman is US-based. The Smart-Home SaaS+e-com is the only
truly-remote/founder-fit option but is a turnaround, not a clean cash-flow buy.
