## 2. Industry economics — the recurring-revenue problem (the crux)

**The single most important structural fact about this business:** it appears to be a
**project/installation shop, not a monitoring/RMR company.** In the electronic-security industry,
enterprise value is overwhelmingly driven by **Recurring Monthly Revenue (RMR)** — monitoring,
managed-access, cloud-video, and service contracts. Industry M&A convention values RMR at
**~28–50x monthly RMR** (i.e., 2.5–4x *annual* recurring), while one-time install/project revenue
is valued at a low SDE/EBITDA multiple because it must be re-won every year. (ASSUMED — standard
industry knowledge; primary RMR-multiple sources were 403/404/CAPTCHA-blocked during research and
could not be freshly cited. Treat the exact multiple as directional, not verified.)

**Implication:** $400K of *project* revenue with no disclosed monitoring book means:
- Revenue is **lumpy and re-won each year** — the $150K "pipeline" is the visible half of a
  business that starts near-zero every January. There is no contracted annuity underpinning the
  $130K SDE.
- The valuation is a **pure SDE multiple on project income**, which is the *lowest-quality* form of
  security-industry earnings. A buyer is paying ~3x for a job, not for an annuity.
- **Upside thesis (real but unproven):** convert install customers into **service/monitoring/managed
  contracts** to build an RMR base — this is the standard value-creation lever and where a
  software-minded owner could add durable value. UNVERIFIED that the customer base is convertible.

**Market backdrop:** commercial security / systems-integration (CCTV, access control, building
automation) is a **large, growing, and highly fragmented** market — thousands of small local
integrators, a long tail below national players (Convergint, Securitas Technology, Johnson
Controls/Tyco, ADT Commercial). Growth drivers: cloud/AI video analytics, access-control upgrades,
IP migration. Fragmentation = both opportunity (roll-up/consolidation) and threat (low barriers,
price competition, commoditizing hardware). (ASSUMED — directional; market-report sources
(Grand View, Mordor, MarketsandMarkets) were 403/404/mis-served during research.)

## 3. Licensing — does the OWNER need a licence? (mostly VERIFIED — the good news)

Two separate BC regimes apply. **Neither requires Hooman personally to be a tradesperson**, but both
require the *company* to hold licences that depend on qualified *people*:

### (a) Security Services Act (electronic security work) — VERIFIED framework
- BC regulates security work under the **Security Services Act (SBC 2007, c.30)**, administered by
  the **Security Programs Division** (Ministry of Public Safety). *(VERIFIED — gov.bc.ca security
  services pages.)*
- **Two licence types:** a **Security Business Licence** (to operate a business selling/installing
  security alarms) and **Security Worker Licences** (for the individual "alarm installer" workers).
  *(VERIFIED — gov.bc.ca.)*
- The **business** holds the Security Business Licence; the Act requires disclosure of "the
  individuals who control or are able to control the business entity," and the business must
  **ensure adequate supervision** of installers (including "alarm installer under supervision"
  workers). *(VERIFIED — gov.bc.ca / Security Licensing Policy.)*
- **Owner does NOT need to be a licensed installer.** A director/owner can hold a Security Business
  Licence (subject to a **criminal-record check** on controlling individuals — ASSUMED standard) and
  employ/subcontract licensed workers. The Technical Lead / subs carry the worker licences.
  *(ASSUMED — consistent with the framework; confirm with Security Programs Division 1-855-587-0185.)*

### (b) Technical Safety BC — electrical contractor licence + FSR — VERIFIED framework
- BC electrical work is governed by the **Electrical Safety Regulation (BC Reg 100/2004)** under
  **Technical Safety BC**. A business doing regulated electrical work needs an **electrical
  contractor licence** with a designated **Field Safety Representative (FSR)** who declares the work
  complies. *(VERIFIED — bclaws.ca / Technical Safety BC.)*
- **The FSR can be an EMPLOYEE, not the owner.** The regulation states an FSR "can make these
  declarations on behalf of a contractor" and "at least one person who holds appropriate FSR
  certification is required at each job site" — i.e., the owner need not hold it personally; the
  Technical Lead (or a hired FSR) satisfies the requirement. *(VERIFIED — Electrical Safety
  Regulation text.)*
- **Caveat (UNVERIFIED):** the regulation provides **no blanket exemption** for low-voltage/comms
  wiring under 30V; whether this integrator's specific work triggers a Class-B/restricted FSR
  requirement depends on the exact scope. Some structured-cabling/comms work is exempt; alarm/access
  wiring often is not. **Must confirm which FSR class the business holds and WHO holds it.**

### The verdict on licensing → this is the KEY-MAN RISK, not an owner-barrier
Hooman does **not** need to be a tradesperson or FSR — that's the good news, and it's why this is
the *only* trades deal in the whole search that a non-tradesperson can own. **But** the licences
that make the company legal to operate ride on the **Technical Lead** (likely FSR + design lead) and
on **licensed subcontractors**. If the Technical Lead leaves post-close and no FSR is retained, the
company **cannot legally pull permits or sign off electrical work** until it recruits a replacement
FSR — a scarce, poachable credential. **This is a genuine deal-affecting key-man dependency**, not a
paper formality. VERIFIED as a structural risk; the *severity* is ASSUMED pending the FSR audit.

## 6. Deal-killers & red flags

1. **Absentee-across-a-border on a 2-employee field business** — the #1 killer *for this buyer now*.
   No GM layer; sub-heavy; local sales/service. This is not remotely operable from the US.
2. **Licensed key-man (FSR / Technical Lead).** If he leaves and no FSR is retained/recruited, the
   company can't legally sign off electrical work. Verify who the FSR is, the class, and lock a
   retention agreement + non-compete as a closing condition.
3. **No recurring revenue.** Project-only earnings = lowest-quality; ~3x is full for a job you re-win
   annually. The $150K "pipeline" is not contracted annuity.
4. **Unaudited, single-point SDE** on a lumpy business — normalize over 3 years of tax returns.
5. **"AI proposal automation" is marketing, not IP** — pay $0 goodwill for it; it's a commodity SaaS.
6. **Inventory ($80K) sits on top of price** and may be stale/obsolete tech stock.
7. **Founder's stated exit ("AI ventures") = the software brain leaves with him.**
