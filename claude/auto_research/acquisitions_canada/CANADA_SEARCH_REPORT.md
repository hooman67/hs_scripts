## 4. THE KEY COMPARISON — Canada vs US for THIS buyer

### Financing mechanics side by side

| | **Canada (available NOW)** | **US (only after green card)** |
|---|---|---|
| Program | BDC + CSBFP + chartered-bank acquisition lending | SBA 7(a)/504 |
| Eligibility now | ✅ Canadian citizen — fully eligible | ❌ TN holder barred (SOP 50 10 needs ≥51% US citizen/LPR owner) |
| Typical buyer equity | **~10–25% down** (BDC), goodwill financed on cash-flow underwriting | **~10% down**, SBA guarantees ~90% |
| Goodwill / intangibles | BDC finances goodwill/IP/client lists; CSBFP now covers IP + working capital up to $500K (2022 expansion) but **NOT share-purchase goodwill** | SBA readily finances goodwill |
| What $500K reaches | **~$1.3–2M** business (25% down) via BDC + seller note | **~$1–2M** business (10% down) via SBA |
| Immigration | ✅ No visa issue — can own AND operate a Canadian company remotely from US on TN | ❌ Operating a US business hands-on on TN is barred (needs E-2 or green card) |

**Key insight**: For the same $500K cash, BDC (~25% down) reaches a ~$1.3–2M business — **roughly the
same size the US SBA path reaches** (~10% down → $1–2M). The old story that "no-SBA cripples the
Canadian buyer" is a US-*asset* problem, not a Canadian-*buyer* problem. As a Canadian citizen he
is NOT under-levered on Canadian soil — BDC substitutes cleanly for SBA. The wider US report's
"3–4x smaller SDE for the same cash" penalty **does not apply** when he buys a Canadian company.

**The decisive difference is TIMING + REMOTE-OPERABILITY, not leverage size:**

- **Canada works TODAY.** He can own and operate a Canadian company remotely from the US on TN with
  no immigration wrinkle. The US SBA path is **locked until the green card lands** — and even then a
  hands-on US operating business needs him physically in the US (fine post-GC) but a Canadian remote
  SaaS needs him nowhere.
- **He lives in the US now**, so remote-operability is the gating filter. Canadian SaaS (fully remote)
  clears it; Canadian trades/physical (on-site) do not — they are relocation-contingent.
- **Once the green card lands**, the US market re-opens with SBA leverage AND he could operate US
  physical businesses. But that's a *future* option; it does not beat a durable remote asset he can
  buy and compound starting now.

**Which unlocks a bigger/better business for his $500K?** Roughly **equal leverage** (BDC ≈ SBA in
down-payment terms), so "bigger" is a wash. **"Better" goes to Canada** on two axes that matter for
his current life: (1) it is available immediately with no visa risk, and (2) the best Canadian
deal (remote SaaS) fits his skills and his US-residency far better than the best US deal (a NJ
laundromat / on-site SaaS he can't legally operate hands-on until the GC lands).

## 6. Bottom line — is the best Canadian deal better than the best US deal?

**Yes — the best Canadian deal is better for Hooman right now, and the imminent green card doesn't
change that.**

The US report's #1 was a laundromat and #2 a vertical B2B SaaS, both bought all-cash because SBA is
off the table on TN. The Canadian search removes BOTH of the US path's structural handicaps: **BDC
gives him ~SBA-equivalent leverage (≈25% down) TODAY**, and **there is no visa problem** owning/
operating a Canadian company remotely. That combination lets him reach a **~$1.3–2M durable, fully-
remote, high-founder-fit SaaS** — the **BC Staffing-Management SaaS** — instead of a ~$500K all-cash
US asset he either can't legally operate hands-on (US SaaS/service) or can only run semi-absentee
(NJ laundromat).

The green card, when it lands, re-opens SBA and US operating businesses — but that is a *future*
option that, at best, matches the leverage he *already* has in Canada. It does not produce a better
asset than a remote Canadian SaaS he can start compounding now. Waiting for it has an opportunity
cost; buying Canadian does not.

**Clear recommendation:**
1. **Primary**: pursue the **BC Staffing-Management Vertical SaaS** — structure ~$400–500K CAD down
   + BDC/bank term loan + seller note, keep the entity BC-incorporated, run it remotely from the US.
   Confirm live BDC equity % and terms with a BDC account manager (10–25% + seller-note is the norm;
   the "buy a business" page 404'd at research time). Diligence the sales owner-dependence hardest.
2. **All-cash alternative** (if he wants no debt / a cleaner ticket): the **Richmond Low-Voltage
   Integrator** ($389K, EE/AI fit, seller VTB) or the **Ontario Cybersecurity SaaS** if repriced.
3. **Cash-flowing brand fallback**: the **Central-ON deodorant DTC** with a co-packer + 3PL installed.
4. **Do NOT** prioritize Canadian trades/physical/manufacturing while he's US-resident — they're
   cheaper and BDC-leverageable but on-site and license-gated; revisit only if/when he relocates.

**Sourcing caveat**: Flippa / Acquire.com / DealStream / businessesforsale.com detail pages were
403/JS-blocked during research; a manual, account-based browser pass is needed to fully sweep the
true ≤$500K all-cash micro-SaaS band, where more Canadian-domiciled inventory likely sits.
