## Prompt-ready guidance

The following is suitable as a **printer-specific addition**, alongside your existing product-preservation, human-presence and brand-blue rules:

```text
PRINTER PLACEMENT, SUPPORT AND FUNCTIONAL ORIENTATION

Treat the printer as a working appliance integrated into the requested room,
not as a decorative object or a laptop workstation.

Preserve the source printer
Keep the printer's source position, size, orientation, visible faces, proportions,
finish, controls, trays and existing open/closed parts unchanged. Build the
furniture and environment around that fixed view. Do not rotate the printer
toward the camera or reveal a different face to make the scene easier.

Identify its functional front
Use the control panel, paper drawers and output opening to identify the operating
side when visible. Do not confuse a broad side panel with the front. If the front
is not visible, preserve the source view rather than inventing its appearance.

The operating side should face a plausible accessible part of the room or working
surface, not directly into a wall, monitor back, cabinet side or another obstacle.
A printer can face across the frame; it does not have to face the camera.

Choose a believable support
Use furniture appropriate to the requested setting: a work desk, shared office
table, sturdy console, sideboard, cabinet top or suitable printer stand. The
support must be wide and deep enough for the printer's actual base footprint,
with believable furniture thickness and structural support.

Keep the chassis and its supporting feet securely on one level surface. Preserve
a convincing contact relationship and natural contact shadow beneath the base.
Do not let the body float, sink into the furniture, straddle separate surfaces
or balance over an edge. Existing extended trays may project beyond the body;
do not mistake them for unsupported chassis.

Align the furniture to the fixed printer
For a conventional rectangular support, make its width and depth directions
coherent with the printer's lower chassis edges and the camera perspective.
Use the rigid base, not an angled control panel, rounded lid or projecting tray,
to judge orientation.

The printer and support should read as normally aligned furniture and equipment,
not as independently rotated objects. In perspective, corresponding edges
recede coherently; they need not be horizontal or literally parallel on screen.
Do not twist the tabletop, introduce an unexplained diagonal or change the
printer to resolve a mismatch.

Allow practical access
Leave usable space in the direction of paper output and access to controls and
paper drawers. Keep existing rear feeders and raised components clear of walls,
shelves and other objects. Leave room above a scanner lid where it would open.
Do not invent exact clearances or additional mechanisms.

A printer may sit toward the back or side of a desk, or on a separate cabinet.
It need not be centered on a table, directly in front of a chair, or flush with
the nearest edge. Avoid both precarious edge placement and an unnecessarily
deep tabletop that makes the printer difficult to reach.

Separate printer access from computer seating
A nearby working chair normally faces its keyboard and monitor. Do not turn the
chair toward the printer unless the requested activity is directly operating it.
A shared or cabinet-mounted printer can be approached standing, without a
dedicated chair. Preserve a clear approach to its operating side.

Make the surroundings task-related
Use a few objects appropriate to the setting, beside rather than on the printer:
paper, documents, photo prints, stationery or books. A home activity area may have
drawing materials and storage; an office may have a neighboring laptop/monitor
workspace, task lamp and document storage.

Keep these objects outside paper paths and away from controls. Do not cover the
printer with decorative props or fill every surrounding surface. Do not add a
computer, chair, person or cable merely to imply that the printer works.
Existing human-presence instructions remain authoritative.

Special cases
Industrial printing equipment uses its own machine bed and structural support;
do not place it on household furniture. A cropped reference with an unseen base
does not establish the type or dimensions of its support.

Final visual check
The printer is unchanged; its base is securely supported; furniture perspective
fits the fixed chassis; its operating side has a plausible access route; paper
paths and opening parts are unobstructed; nearby objects belong to the activity
without competing with or blocking the printer.
```
