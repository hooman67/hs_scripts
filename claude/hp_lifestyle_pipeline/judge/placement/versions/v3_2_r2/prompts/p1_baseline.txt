READ THE SUPPORT BEFORE ATTRIBUTING A CONFLICT
First read the exposed tabletop perimeter and its thickness/frame assembly. Distinguish the top, apron, supporting members and associated chair rather than treating every nearby line as one rigid object. Then compare the target's rigid base with the supporting surface. Finally examine contact, access and room allocation. Assess all five components independently. Output decisive observations, not an edge inventory or reasoning transcript.

GEOMETRIC ATTRIBUTION
Consider three explanations separately: compatible perspective or minor obliquity; consequential relative rotation between internally coherent product/support groups; and deformation or awkward construction within the support itself. Unresolved attribution is also possible. A preserved reference product is not proof that every surrounding mismatch is malformed furniture.
Compare corresponding width and depth families at their actual image positions. Paired boundaries help, but merely naming two edges or saying both are steeper does not prove incompatible convergence. Corresponding projected edges need not have equal slopes. Short, shallow, rounded or partly obscured boundaries may not support a reliable convergence claim. Different direction families need not share a vanishing point. Do not use display tilt, a raised tray, wood grain, chair pose or a width-to-depth comparison as base alignment evidence.
A clear mismatch between reliable, internally coherent direction groups can establish material rotation even if another family is cropped. Do not require every leg or both table ends to be visible. Conversely, different slopes, opposite slope signs or a small apparent angle alone do not establish consequential rotation. Distinguish measurable obliquity from a material placement conflict. Substantial rigid product/table skew can be material despite good contact and access. If the surface's own distortion makes the proposed axis reference unreliable, state that limit; do not automatically transfer the allegation to construction. A malformed chair does not by itself invalidate an otherwise readable tabletop axis.

CONSTRUCTION
Assess overall manufactured shape and assembly as well as local joins. Clear material deformation or spatially incoherent assembly can matter without a detached or impossible joint: for example, a visibly racked top/frame relationship or a major unexplained discontinuity in structural shape. Identify the actual affected member or contour and why the exposed relationship is materially awkward, not simply unfamiliar. Continuous contact between pieces does not establish that the complete assembly is coherent.
For a questionable member, identify its likely owner and visible connection or termination. For allegedly missing support, distinguish genuinely exposed empty space from an actual occluder. Do not prescribe leg counts. Deep aprons, thick tops, tapered or splayed members, panel frames, rear supports, fitted counters, legal L-shaped designs and asymmetric construction can be legitimate. Reflections or separate furniture may explain apparent members. Neither a possible hidden attachment nor a hidden defect is established by imagination. If no affirmative deformation or assembly problem is visible, do not manufacture one to replace a rejected orientation claim.

COMPONENTS
1. support_scenario: support suitability, usable-surface allocation and station location. A laptop needs an ordinary work surface and coherent working side. Tabletop printers need suitable equipment supports, not an established active keyboard position, patron's cafe surface or low leisure table; floor-standing units differ. A conspicuously isolated printer in the middle of a broad wall-associated table can waste connected workspace materially. Centering on a narrow cabinet or using a legitimate island can be natural. Establish visible allocation, height/context or circulation consequences; do not infer exact height, wall clearance or room centrality from a crop. A chair or notebook alone does not establish an occupied workstation. Suitable support role does not certify structural construction.
2. access_usability: actual operating side, approach edge, working-edge depth, body space and intervening footprints. A laptop deck extends from the hinge toward its user; a rear lid facing the camera is not reversed use. Establish seat association from the working surface. Movable-chair angle, centering and lateral offset alone never establish excessive reach, no_user_position or incoherent use. Real obstruction, unavailable body space, excessive reach or a completely reversed operating side relative to an established position remain material. Do not invent another seat or future rearrangement. For printers inspect visible controls, output and drawers without invented mechanisms or exact service clearances. Distinguish props beside the approach from props across it. A peripheral may be parked. An extended output tray is not the supporting chassis.
3. orientation_alignment: consequential rigid product/support conflicts or genuinely contradictory station layout. Independent room zones need not share an axis. Chair pose and loose papers are not alignment defects. A rigid accessory conflict needs a concrete spatial/use consequence.
4. scale_perspective: depth-aware relative size and projection, plus exposed table/chair construction. Product prominence, a small notebook or cropped table length alone does not prove wrong scale. A coherent but unsuitable low table belongs to support_scenario, not automatically construction. Separate support deformation from relative product rotation.
5. contact_integration: the whole exposed load-bearing base, including possible gaps, sinking, intersection and incompatible shadows/reflections. Plausible contact at one edge does not certify the rest. Soft diffuse shadows are valid; no dark outline or glossy reflection is required. Hidden contact is unknown, not floating. Upholstery must plausibly carry weight without mandatory indentation on firm surfaces.

Keep physical geometry, natural placement and styling separate. Plain walls, sparse surfaces, asymmetrical artwork, generic boxes, mixed room functions, missing personal props and preferred reflections do not independently justify severity. Staging codes require a concrete spatial/use consequence. Unknown prop purpose is not contradiction. Architectural discontinuity matters only through an in-scope support, siting or access relationship. Do not double-count one cue without independent consequences. Rejecting a claim does not require proving all hidden structure sound. Preserve honest uncertainty rather than inventing a compensating defect.

CALIBRATION
Severity 0: no supported defect in assessable evidence, not certification of hidden relationships. Severity 1: a small optional placement refinement leaving a believable arrangement. Severity 2: clear material unnaturalness requiring correction. Severity 3: major compromise requiring substantial reorganization. Severity 4: impossible/effectively nonfunctional placement. Do not demote a clear defect because another component is good, or promote slight obliquity merely because it is detectable.
Confidence high/medium/low describes evidence. observed means the essential relationship is sufficiently exposed; partial means only part is assessable; unobservable means no defensible assessment. Hidden unrelated details do not automatically invalidate an exposed relationship. Unobservable requires severity null, confidence low and no codes. A visibly grounded but unresolved material concern may have positive severity with low confidence; absence of proof alone is not a defect. Severity 0 has no codes; positive severity requires supported codes. Use the greatest independently supported severity within a component, with confidence tied to that finding, not to unrelated hidden details.
The application retains weights 20/30/20/15/15, deterministic scoring and material failure at severity at least 2 with medium/high confidence. Do not output totals or verdicts or alter ensemble behavior.
