SUPPORT CONSTRUCTION
Read the tabletop perimeter, thickness and frame as related but distinct parts; inspect the associated chair separately. Assess overall structural shape and assembly, not only whether local pieces touch. Clear material deformation or spatially incoherent construction can matter without an impossible joint: a visibly racked top/frame relationship or a major unexplained discontinuity in structural shape can be sufficient. Name the actual affected member or contour and why the exposed relationship exceeds ordinary design variation. A thick apron or unfamiliar silhouette alone is not enough.
For suspicious members, identify likely ownership and visible connections or terminations. For allegedly missing support, distinguish genuinely exposed empty space from actual occlusion. Do not count legs or assume a freestanding table template. Deep aprons, thick slabs, tapered/splayed members, panels, rear supports, fitted counters, legal L-shaped designs and asymmetric frames can be coherent. Separate furniture and reflections can explain apparent members. Do not invent hidden fixings to dismiss an exposed contradiction or invent hidden defects to reject an unfamiliar design. A possible concealed attachment is not a certified one. Continuous contours and a few plausible joins do not certify the complete assembly. Conversely, concealed joins alone are not a construction concern.

RIGID ALIGNMENT
Compare competing rigid groups: the target base/body, supporting surface perimeter, and the support's own thickness/frame. Distinguish compatible perspective or minor obliquity, consequential relative rotation between coherent groups, and distortion within the support. Attribution may remain unresolved. A preserved reference product does not automatically make the support malformed.
Compare corresponding width/depth families using paired boundaries and their convergence at their actual image positions. Naming two edges does not prove a conflict merely because both are steeper. Short, shallow, rounded or obscured edges may not establish convergence reliably. Equal image slopes are not required; opposite slope signs alone are not decisive. Different direction families need not share a vanishing point. Do not use display tilt, a raised tray, wood grain, chair pose or width-to-depth comparisons. Curved supports need not supply rectangular axes.
One reliable exposed family can establish substantial rotation even if another is cropped; complete furniture visibility is unnecessary. Clear substantial product/table skew is material despite good contact or access. Small apparent obliquity is not automatically material. If the surface has an exposed distortion that prevents a defensible comparison, state the limit rather than manufacturing product yaw. Do not transfer a weak orientation claim into construction without independent construction evidence. A malformed chair does not automatically invalidate readable tabletop axes.

RELATIVE SCALE/PERSPECTIVE
Compare product, furniture and familiar objects with depth accounted for. Product prominence, cropped table length, a small notebook or reference-render size alone does not establish wrong scale. Separate projective inconsistency from relative rotation and malformed assembly. Do not duplicate one cue across criteria without an independent consequence.

PRODUCT CONTACT
Inspect the whole exposed load-bearing base for gaps, sinking/intersection and incompatible shadows/reflections. Contact at one edge does not certify the rest. Soft diffuse shadows are valid; no dark outline or glossy reflection is required. Hidden contact is unknown, not floating. A printer's output tray is not its supporting chassis. Upholstery should plausibly carry weight without mandatory indentation on firm surfaces.

EVIDENCE AND CALIBRATION
A rejected claim need not be replaced by another defect or by proof that all hidden structure is sound. Evidence states what is visible; counter_evidence states an actual compatible cue or plausible alternative supported by the view; limit states what cannot be resolved. Do not populate these fields with invented facts.
Severity 0 means no supported defect in assessable evidence; 1 a small optional refinement; 2 a clear material defect requiring correction; 3 a major compromise; 4 impossible/effectively nonfunctional geometry. Confidence high/medium/low describes evidence. observed means the essential relationship is sufficiently exposed; partial means some is assessable; unobservable requires severity null, confidence low and no codes. For observed/partial use integer severity 0-4. A concrete but unresolved material concern may have positive severity with low confidence; missing evidence alone is not a defect. Severity 0 requires empty codes; positive severity requires supported codes. Do not output scores or a scene verdict.
