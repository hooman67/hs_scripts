READ USE AND ALLOCATION FIRST
Identify the operating side and usable approach. Ask whether nearby objects are between that approach and the operating area, beside it, or unresolved. Then examine what the supporting furniture contributes to the room: its visible floor/wall relation, height, connected usable space and neighboring activity. After these observations, independently test rigid geometry and contact. Do not let a convincing activity story substitute for those tests. Report concise observations, not an exhaustive inventory or reasoning transcript.

A rear-view laptop may correctly face a chair behind it: locate the keyboard/palm-rest deck extending from the hinge toward the user. Determine seat association from the surface and working side; visitor chairs and distant stools are not automatically operator seats. Movable-chair rotation, lateral offset and imperfect centering alone are harmless. They do not prove reach, no user position, wrong-facing use or an incoherent activity. Preserve material findings for independently visible excessive depth, intervening objects, unavailable body space or a completely reversed operating side relative to an established user position. Do not invent an alternative chair or future rearrangement. Props beside the deck differ from props occupying the approach. An unfamiliar box need not obstruct or lack a plausible role; a mouse may be parked rather than actively used.

Printers need suitable equipment supports and practical standing approaches. An occupied personal keyboard position, patron's café surface or low leisure table is unsuitable for a tabletop printer. A floor-standing unit is different. Inspect visible controls, output and drawers without inventing mechanisms or exact service clearances. A tray can project normally while the chassis remains supported. A chair only matters when its footprint actually blocks the approach. On a broad wall-associated station, a conspicuously isolated middle placement wasting connected workspace can be materially unnatural despite reachable controls. A small dedicated cabinet can center its printer. A freestanding station can be legitimate; establish visible displacement, allocation or circulation consequences before calling it stranded. No literal wall contact or camera-facing front is required. Use exposed floor relationships and multiple depth-aware cues for support height, not one ratio or a cropped view.

GEOMETRY IS A SEPARATE TEST
Compare corresponding rigid-base and support width/depth families where exposed. Use paired boundaries and convergence at their image positions, not equal slopes, display tilt, an output tray, chair pose or width-to-depth comparison. Distinct 3D families can have different vanishing points. Curved supports need not supply rectangular axes. Distinguish compatible perspective, consequential rigid product/support rotation and support distortion. A decisive exposed family can establish conflict despite another hidden family; weak single-edge evidence cannot. If the support itself prevents a defensible axis comparison, identify that limit rather than asserting product rotation. Material laptop/table and printer/table rotation is a defect without requiring blocked use.

Inspect tabletop outline/thickness/joins and associated chair/stool construction separately. For a suspicious member or apparently absent support, identify its likely owner, exposed connection/termination or genuinely visible empty support region, and any actual occluder. Do not count legs or require hidden ones. A rear support, independent divider foot, reflection, legal L-shaped design or panel frame may explain unusual appearance. Hidden joins are unknown, not disconnected and not certified. Material malformed construction requires an exposed contradiction, not unfamiliar design. Compare relative size with depth; a large-in-frame product or long cropped table is not sufficient scale evidence. Inspect the whole visible base for weight-bearing contact, gaps, sinking/intersection and incompatible shadows/reflections. Soft shadows are valid; upholstery should plausibly support weight without a mandatory indentation rule.
