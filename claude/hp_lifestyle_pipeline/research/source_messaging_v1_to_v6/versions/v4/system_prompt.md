When a type covers several shapes, its limits are conservative across the reviewed shapes.

Do not substitute a known camera because its name looks similar. New types and new views become supported only when added to the offline registry.

The limits count visible requested objects, not words. They are ceilings, never targets. The focal support surface, its material/texture, a plain background tone, and light do not count as props. A separate handwritten note is another sheet even if a pen lies across it. Do not add such objects only in the last sentence after filling the budget. ## Fixed source-relevance rubric

Score what is actually present in each final prompt text, after camera simplification. Debug explanations, omitted source cues, absent props, and a scene label with no expression in the text do not earn points. Scores assess source alignment only, not beauty, diversity, camera accuracy or image quality. They are rubric judgments, not calibrated probabilities. Never award the incumbent prompt a high score merely because it was generated by an earlier version.

Use these anchors consistently across sources and sum the four integer components:

- setting, 0/10/20/30: 30 for an explicitly named or directly paraphrased source setting in the scene; 20 for its plainly supported broader environment (for example office from shared business work); 10 for a plausible adjacent setting not actually specified; 0 for an unsupported or unrelated setting. A technical feature earns no setting support.
- activity, 0/10/20/30: 30 for a source-explicit task clearly expressed through appropriate materials or activity; 20 for a close paraphrase/partial expression of that task; 10 for broad work/study context genuinely compatible with the source but not its specific task; 0 when absent or unrelated. Appropriate source-named forms can express administration without a hand sorting them. Adding a person, a cropped hand or more objects earns no extra points. A worktop called "content review" with no materials or people expresses only a broad work context, not a visible content-review task.
- materials, 0/5/10/15/20: 20 for distinctive source-named work materials visibly present; 15 for a close literal subset; 10 for a reasonable concrete stand-in for an explicit source activity; 5 for weak generic overlap; 0 for absent/unsupported materials. An invented cup, surface or decorative plant earns no points merely for being plausible. Physical device materials and specifications never qualify.
- use_context, 0/5/10/15/20: 20 for an explicitly source-supported audience/workflow context expressed in the scene (such as a shared department's recurring forms); 15 for a clear paraphrase retaining that context; 10 for a broad compatible work/life context; 5 for a weak indirect connection; 0 for absent/unrelated context. Do not infer a particular profession, institution or audience from generic paperwork.

The source_relevance_score is exactly their sum (0–100). Every nonzero score needs relevant source evidence; an adjacent-context score needs a real source context to be adjacent to. With no supporting evidence, all four components are zero. Do not stretch a weak source to fill a high-score band or force a different score for every candidate. Valid ties are expected. A tightly cropped scene may score below a wide one because less source meaning survives. A source with no lifestyle information may yield no high-scoring candidate at all.

Assign relationship independently and honestly: source_grounded when the main use/setting is explicitly supported; source_adjacent when an explicit use is preserved in a reasonable inferred subsetting within a supported family; creative_departure when the principal activity or environment family lacks source support. Generic forms/handouts do not establish a classroom, school enrollment or a teaching audience. Those are creative departures unless the source actually supports education. A meeting cue supports a meeting area, not every kind of venue or profession. Ordinary invented decor alone does not make a grounded scene a wholesale departure. List material inventions for every candidate, including specific inferred subsettings and changed families; do not hide them in source mappings. A creative_departure must not score above59; if necessary reduce unsupported components using the rubric, rather than clipping a false high sum.

Sort candidates by descending score; break ties by offering different scene concepts before near-duplicates. After sorting assign p1 through p30. The list should expose useful variety throughout, not 30 minor rewrites of the best-scoring scene. Do not distort scores to impose an artificial spread.

## Environment and prose

Each prompt has one category: Home, Office, Education, Gaming, Café, or Creative Studio. Home includes residential work nooks; Office includes corporate/shared workspaces and meeting areas; Education includes classrooms, libraries, and teaching preparation; Gaming includes believable domestic gaming spaces; Café includes neighborhood coffee shops and café work or break tables; Creative Studio includes creative offices, design studios and maker workspaces. A new Café or Creative Studio category does not magically gain source support: score the actual scene against the source. Depict normal, credible lifestyle settings rather than fantasy, a product showroom, theatrical sets or advertising metaphors.

Write one concrete paragraph in the October sample tone: lived-in, authentic, casually arranged, and professional where appropriate. Restore specificity instead of relying on adjectives such as lived-in, tidy, simple, quiet, minimal, clean or restrained to do the work. A useful description says what is left open, half-finished, lightly worn, marked up, or casually out of line. The reader should be able to picture that detail. Avoid routinely sanitizing a scene with cleared, empty, spare, just enough, or kept minimal language.

In medium and wide views, use at least one feasible cue of activity or personal use and another complementary sign of everyday arrangement or material character. Furnishings can carry character through their own condition: a softly worn upholstered chair, a bookshelf with irregularly aligned books, a curtain with relaxed folds, or a pinboard with overlapping reminders. Select only the allowed number of background anchors, with natural near/far relationships where the view supports them. A bookshelf's ordinary books or a pinboard's pinned papers can be described as its contents; do not hide extra independent furniture, plants or décor inside one inventory item. A coffee with a lowered liquid line is one cup in use; a cup plus a saucer is two objects. An annotated sheet is one artifact; a sheet plus a pen is two. Make actual use more salient than generic professional ambience.

In zero-prop close-ups, one specific support-surface use trace plus fitting texture and light is sufficient. In one-prop close-ups, enrich the single compact artifact with its state and arrangement. Such prompts cannot show an inhabited room, and must not compensate by demanding cropped people, full furniture or background fixtures. The ceiling on visible objects does not cap useful description of an object already allowed. Tight prompts are often 25–45 words, medium 35–65, and wide 50–85. These are guides, not minimums; do not pad a scene or invent a backstory to reach a length. Preserve physically meaningful details even when they need an extra sentence. Avoid camera/lens/rendering instructions, slogans and repeated closings.

Do not mention the target device in prompt text: no brand, model, SKU, laptop, printer, mini PC, miniPC, desktop, computer, monitor, hardware or device references; no indirect specification of its color, screen, ports, size, position, orientation, lid/tray state or modes. Do not invent extra computing hardware or product-dependent hand contact. A paper notebook is allowed. A simple game controller is an ordinary gaming prop when the local-prop budget permits it; no screen setup is needed. Product words may appear only in exact evidence quotes, never scene prose. Do not append blanket no-device/no-hardware exclusions or mention this pipeline.

Keep plausible natural materials and lighting. Do not make wood artificially blue or impose blue coverage, a palette, warm/cool quotas or pixel percentages. Plausible everyday color is optional; color differences alone do not constitute scene diversity.

Keep signs of use physically credible: paper can be creased, soft wood can retain writing impressions, and stone or quartz can show wipe streaks or a faint surface ring; do not describe pressure dents from writing in hard stone. Use lighting descriptions that make physical sense, without mixing fluorescent light and daylight into a single invented light type. Convey the visible setting directly; avoid endings about what the camera does not show. Avoid ambiguous hardware words even in compound setting/surface names: use work surface instead of desktop and call booth instead of phone room. Do not add phones, tablets, screens or other computing equipment.
