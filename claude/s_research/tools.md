## Folding saw

### Requirements used

- One reliable folding saw rather than separate go-bag and base-camp saws
- Fits inside a normal backpack
- Useful for firewood, improvised shelter construction, trail clearing, camping, and home tasks
- Complements rather than duplicates the Gränsfors Bruk Small Forest Axe
- Budget up to $150, with preference for good value
- Likely materials include common 1–4-inch shelter poles and firewood, with occasional larger cuts

Large trail obstructions can exceed the practical capacity of any backpack folding saw. The saw is not a substitute for assessing whether moving or cutting storm-damaged or tensioned wood is safe.

### Saw technique, safety, and failure modes

- Silky saws cut primarily on the **pull stroke**. Apply minimal pressure while pushing the blade forward.
- Do not force, twist, or bend a bound blade. Thin pull-saw blades can kink or break when pushed sideways.
- Before cutting, inspect the wood for compression, tension, overhead hazards, rolling, and spring-loaded branches.
- Do not cut storm-damaged trees, hung branches, or trunks under uncertain tension without appropriate training.
- Keep the non-sawing hand and all bystanders outside the cutting path.
- Wear eye protection and work gloves when conditions permit.
- Lock the blade fully open before cutting and fully closed before packing.
- Keep the saw clean and dry. Remove sap according to the manufacturer's guidance and apply light corrosion protection where appropriate.
- Periodically inspect the lock, pivot screw, handle, and blade for damage or looseness.
- Induction-hardened teeth are generally replaced rather than field sharpened. Check replacement-blade availability during periodic gear reviews.
- Store the folded saw where children cannot access it and where the handle cannot open inside the bag.

### Field-repair buckles

Use **Sea to Summit two-pin side-release field-repair buckles**. The removable stainless-steel pins allow both halves of a broken buckle to be replaced without sewing.

- https://seatosummit.com/products/side-release-field-repair-replacement-buckle-2-pin

Measure the actual backpack webbing before buying. Common candidates are:

- 20 mm for some sternum straps
- 25 mm / 1 inch for many backpack compression and accessory straps
- 38 mm for some hip-belt or larger straps

Buckle halves from different manufacturers are often incompatible. The two-pin design is preferred because it replaces both halves. Pack a compatible small Phillips screwdriver unless the go-bag multitool has one.

### Repair-method guide

| Damage | Preferred field method |
|---|---|
| Heavy leather, belt, canvas, or pack webbing | #8 needle plus coarse waxed polyester thread |
| Thin backpack, jacket, sleeping-bag, or tent fabric | Tenacious Tape; avoid unnecessary large needle holes |
| Boot-upper seam | Awl and thread where accessible, followed by appropriate sealing if needed |
| Separating sole, worn toe, or cracked rubber | Aquaseal SR; do not stitch through the sole |
| Leaking sewn gear seam | Seam Grip WP after the mechanical repair |
| Broken side-release buckle | Correctly sized two-pin field-repair buckle |
| Silicone-coated tent or silnylon | Silicone-compatible patch/sealant; not Seam Grip WP |

### Speedy Stitcher lockstitch summary

1. From the back side, push the needle through the material.
2. Pull enough thread through the first hole to complete the seam plus roughly three inches.
3. Withdraw the needle, move to the next hole, and push it through.
4. Pull the awl back slightly to form a loop.
5. Pass the free thread completely through that loop.
6. Withdraw the needle while holding the free thread taut, burying the lock in the middle of the material layers.
7. Repeat with consistent spacing and tension.
8. At the final hole, pull out the loop, cut it to form two ends, tie securely on the back, and trim.
9. Remove the needle and return it to the protected handle-storage position.

Practice on scrap webbing and leather before relying on the tool in an emergency.

### Adhesive storage and failure modes

- Gear Aid specifies storing Aquaseal SR and Seam Grip WP in a sealed bag in a freezer.
- A tube permanently kept in a hot garage or go-bag may cure or degrade prematurely.
- Keep the main tubes refrigerated/frozen as directed, or maintain a clearly dated go-bag tube and inspect/rotate it regularly.
- Adhesives require clean, dry surfaces and generally 8–12 hours of undisturbed curing. They are not instant walking repairs.
- Application below the specified temperature range, on wet/dirty surfaces, or while the material is under load can cause failure.
- Check tubes at least every six months for leakage, hardening, or expiration.
- Use adhesives with ventilation, follow the package safety instructions, wear gloves, and keep them away from children.

### Safety limitations

Do not rely on a field-sewn or adhesive repair for:

- Climbing ropes, harnesses, slings, or fall-protection equipment
- Vehicle seat belts
- Child-carrier load-bearing straps or attachment points
- Dog vehicle restraints
- Rescue equipment
- Any component whose failure could cause a fall, crash, or loss of a child or animal

Replace these items or obtain manufacturer-authorized/professional repair. The Speedy Stitcher manufacturer lists climbing and camping gear among possible applications, but that does not establish that a repaired life-safety component retains its certification or original strength.

Be cautious when forcing the awl through thick leather. Keep the receiving hand out of the needle path, work on a stable backing when possible, and never strike the awl like a punch unless the manufacturer specifically authorizes that technique.

## Maintenance and practice

### After each use

- Dry and inspect the saw; check its teeth, blade, pivot, and lock.
- Return awl needles to protected handle storage.
- Replace damaged or bent needles rather than attempting a critical repair with them.
- Replenish used thread, tape, buckles, wipes, and gloves.

### Every six months

- Open the repair pouch and inventory every item.
- Confirm the awl bobbin contains usable black thread.
- Inspect needles for rust, bending, or damaged eyes.
- Check adhesive tubes for leakage or hardening and verify their storage history.
- Confirm buckle sizes still match the family's current backpacks and child-related equipment.
- Conduct one practice lockstitch repair on scrap material.
