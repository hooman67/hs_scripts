# Preparedness Research Plan

## Decision supported

What system should Hooman implement to provide his household with safe and usable water for 90 days across the target emergencies, at an acceptable cost, footprint, and maintenance burden?

## Research questions

1. What potable and total-water quantities are defensible at survival-minimum, moderate-conservation, and higher-comfort levels?
2. Which portions must be potable, and which can be stored or sourced as non-potable sanitation water?
3. Which storage architecture best balances safety, shelf life, access, seismic resilience, heat exposure, footprint, and cost?
4. What treatment, filling, inspection, rotation, dispensing, and emergency-operation procedures are required?
5. Which assumptions vary by hazard, season, breastfeeding status, or loss of utilities?

## Scope and exclusions

Included: four humans, one dog, 90-day shelter-in-place water, garage/backyard placement, three service levels, target hazards, budget alternatives, and operational redundancy.

Excluded from the first batch: final food/medical/tool plans, evacuation water beyond interface requirements, construction engineering, purchases, and personalized medical advice.

## Competing hypotheses

- H1: A mostly modular, commercially packaged potable supply minimizes maintenance and single-point failures.
- H2: One or more large purpose-built containers provide better cost and space efficiency with acceptable maintenance.
- H3: A hybrid system—protected potable reserve plus separate sanitation capacity and treatment capability—best meets the three-tier requirement.
- H4: Storing the full comfort-tier volume is inferior to combining a survival reserve with redundant replenishment/treatment, because footprint, heat, seismic mass, and access dominate.

All hypotheses receive the same evidence standard.

## Source hierarchy

1. CDC, EPA, FEMA/Ready.gov, USDA/FDA, California and local public-health/emergency/water authorities, and applicable official standards.
2. Peer-reviewed research and recognized standards organizations.
3. Manufacturer documents for exact product specifications and maintenance requirements.
4. Retail listings for current price and availability only.
5. Forums, social media, and preparedness blogs for discovery only.

## Evidence schema

For each decision-driving claim: confidence classification, exact claim, source/publisher, full URL or absolute path, publication date, retrieval date, geography/population, units, limitations, and implications.

For each product/configuration: food-contact claim and evidence, nominal/usable capacity, container count, filled weight, footprint, height, opening/dispensing method, stacking/racking rules, light/temperature guidance, treatment/rotation instructions, seismic restraint, redundancy, current unit and delivered price, and source date.

## Normalization and calculations

- Planning horizon: 90 days unless a sensitivity calculation states otherwise.
- Human and animal needs are modeled separately before aggregation.
- Drinking/cooking, basic hygiene, bathing, dishes, laundry, and toilet use remain separate line items.
- Show gallons/day, liters/day, 90-day total, contingency margin, container count, footprint, and filled weight.
- Use 1 US gallon = 3.785411784 liters and water mass of approximately 8.34 pounds per US gallon for planning; verify important structural calculations before recommendations.
- Independently recompute a sample of totals and reconcile component sums.

## Quality gates

- Open every decision-driving source; snippets and copied citations do not count.
- Cross-check consequential minimum quantities and treatment rules.
- Preserve qualifications for infants, breastfeeding, illness, heat, smoke/fire, and contamination type.
- Separate authority minimums from modeled comfort use.
- Identify contamination modes that boiling, disinfection, or consumer filters cannot make safe.
- Seek contrary evidence for long storage life and outdoor/garage suitability.
- Do not recommend a system whose filled mass, anchoring, dispensing, or access plan is unresolved.

## Staged batches

1. B1 — official quantity, safety, storage, treatment, heat, and local hazard guidance.
2. B2 — transparent three-tier demand model and sensitivity analysis.
3. B3 — architecture comparison and failure-mode/red-team analysis.
4. B4 — representative product specifications, availability, footprint, and price.
5. B5 — synthesis, proposed implementation, maintenance schedule, and cheap validation step.

## Stopping rules

Stop desk research when quantity/treatment claims are supported at appropriate confidence, candidate architectures are normalized and red-teamed, product data are current enough for a decision, and remaining uncertainties require measuring the garage/backyard or performing a small physical trial.

## Required outputs

- Canonical evidence-backed water summary.
- Three-level quantity table and calculations.
- Architecture and budget comparison.
- Placement, anchoring, dispensing, treatment, rotation, inspection, and emergency-use procedures.
- Explicit unresolved gaps and user decisions.
