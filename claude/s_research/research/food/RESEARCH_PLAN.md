# Food Preparedness Research Plan

As of: 2026-10-28

## Decision supported

What staged, economical, nutritionally credible food reserve should Hooman implement for two adults, a breastfeeding mother, a 2-year-old child, and a breastfed infant during outages lasting from three days to 90 days?

## Confirmed scope

- Foods normally eaten include rice, beans, meat, and dairy.
- Hooman prefers red meat; his wife does not eat red meat but eats chicken and fish.
- The household prefers fresh food in ordinary life and generally avoids canned food.
- Available cooking systems are a utility gas stove, electric Ninja multicooker, outdoor charcoal Weber grill, and purchased Coleman Triton propane stove with usable propane. The Triton is the primary emergency cooker; rocket and backpacking stoves are deferred.
- Storage is available in the pantry and garage.
- Infant formula and a separate dog-food plan are excluded at Hooman's direction.
- There are no reported food allergies, intolerances, medical diets, or toddler food restrictions.
- Brown rice is preferred over white rice.
- The first implementation target is 14 days; there is no hard budget or storage-space limit for this layer.
- The intended warehouse is Costco San Jose #148, 2201 Senter Road.

## Competing architectures

1. Rotating deep pantry based on normal grocery foods.
2. Bulk dry staples plus separately stored proteins, fats, fruits, vegetables, and dairy.
3. Commercial freeze-dried emergency meals.
4. Hybrid system: rotating pantry for the first month plus protected bulk staples for later months.

## Evidence and decision gates

- Use current federal nutrition, breastfeeding, food-safety, and emergency guidance first.
- Separate calorie adequacy from nutrition variety and food preferences.
- Calculate package-label calories independently before claiming duration.
- Model refrigeration loss and loss of utility gas.
- Reserve a no-cook layer; never make survival calories depend entirely on fuel.
- Treat charcoal grills, camp stoves, and rocket stoves as outdoor-only combustion appliances.
- Prefer food the family already eats and can rotate; compare freeze-dried foods only after normal-food options are normalized for calories, protein, water, fuel, price, and shelf life.
- Use printed manufacturer dates and storage instructions; do not infer indefinite life from dry appearance.
- Keep infant formula and dog-food calories outside the current totals, and state that feeding the dog from household stores shortens the modeled duration.

## Stages

1. B1: 14-day calorie model, food-safety rules, and starter shopping list.
2. B2: test menus, cooking-water and fuel requirements, package-label calorie audit, and family acceptability.
3. B3: 30-day rotating pantry with current local prices and storage containers.
4. B4: 90-day hybrid design, cost/space/weight comparison, and failure-mode review.
5. B5: implementation, inventory, rotation schedule, and outage meal cards.

## Stopping rule

The first phase is ready when the household owns at least 84,000 audited shelf-stable calories, can prepare three days without cooking and the remaining eleven days without utility gas, and has stored the food safely with an inventory and rotation date.
