# Phase 1 Foundation: Income and Enjoyment Property

**Status:** screening design; no investment recommendation  
**As of:** 2026-09-10  
**Scope:** United States and Canada; approximately USD 300,000; cash or large down payment; long-term rental (LTR), lawful remotely manageable short-term rental (STR), mixed personal/rental use, or land-first development.

This document defines how candidates will be compared. It does **not** claim that any market or archetype is financially viable. No market should be ranked until the two pilots below have been completed, audited, and used to revise this framework.

### Five decision-critical unknowns

These do not block methodology design, but they block a final ranking or purchase recommendation:

1. Current U.S. immigration status, U.S./Canadian tax residency, filing status, and intended ownership (individual, joint, trust, corporation, or LLC).
2. Minimum acceptable unlevered yield/cash-on-cash return, maximum annual negative cash flow, and whether personal-use value may justify a lower financial return.
3. Expected holding period, desired personal-use nights and seasons, and acceptable travel time from California.
4. Effort tolerance: fully passive professional management versus willingness to supervise managers, repairs, permitting, or construction.
5. Risk limits for climate/insurance, HOA or special-assessment exposure, currency movement, development overruns, and illiquidity.

Until answered, scenarios will show outcomes across explicit assumptions rather than silently choosing values.

## 2. Investment archetypes

Archetypes are evaluated in separate cohorts before any cross-archetype comparison. A stabilized rental and undeveloped land do not have comparable timing, execution risk, or utility.

| ID | Archetype | Income engine | Enjoyment engine | Central underwriting risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Nature-accessible LTR | Twelve-month residential lease | Owner visits nearby, not occupancy of the rented unit | Tenant law, local rent level, management and limited owner use |
| A2 | Lawful destination STR | Nightly/weekly stays | Reserved personal-use windows near beach, lake, mountain, forest, or attraction | Parcel-level legality, seasonality, management, insurance and owner-use displacement |
| A3 | Resort/amenity condo or cabin | STR or seasonal rental under onsite/professional management | Amenities, lock-and-leave use | HOA/condo rental rules, fees, reserves, special assessments and manager concentration |
| A4 | Duplex/ADU or two-unit hybrid | One unit rented; one unit reserved or used intermittently | Dedicated owner space and project flexibility | Legal unit status, local rental rules and duplicated maintenance |
| A5 | Rural home/homestead with rentable component | LTR, guest unit, farm lease, or lawful STR | Land, gardening and projects | Utilities, access, septic/well, fire risk, maintenance and remote operation |
| A6 | Land-first phased development | No operating income until a legal rentable unit is complete | Maximum design/project flexibility | Entitlement, access, utilities, carrying period, construction cost and execution risk |

### Cross-archetype rule

First report each archetype's financial return, effort, risk, liquidity, and personal utility separately. If a combined decision score is later used, personal enjoyment must be a separately disclosed user score—not a monetary return hidden inside NOI.

## 3. Disqualifiers and holds

### Hard disqualifiers

A property is removed if any of the following is established:

- The intended ownership structure or intended use is prohibited.
- STR use is prohibited for the exact parcel/unit, or the required permit cannot legally transfer or be obtained.
- Recorded covenants, condo declaration, HOA rules, leasehold terms, or lender terms prohibit the intended rental use.
- Total cash required at closing plus immediate work, furnishing, taxes/fees, and minimum operating reserve exceeds the capital ceiling.
- A required local responsible party or manager cannot be contracted on acceptable terms.
- The property cannot obtain the insurance coverage defined in the frozen criteria at an acceptable quoted premium.
- Title, legal access, water, wastewater, utilities, occupancy approval, or buildability fails for the intended plan.
- A qualified immigration attorney concludes the required owner activity is inconsistent with Hooman's then-current work status.

### Mandatory holds, not silent zeroes

An unresolved material field produces `HOLD / UNKNOWN`, not a low score. Examples: no written insurance indication, unverified HOA rules, seller revenue without source records, unverifiable permit transfer, missing title/access documents, or no property-specific development opinion.

Financial thresholds that Hooman has not yet set—minimum return, maximum negative cash flow, and acceptable reserve—cannot be used as disqualifiers.

### Completeness gate before scoring

At market-screen level, every candidate needs comparable purchase/sold data, legally available income model, demand/rent evidence, tax/fee schedule, management assumption, hazard screen, insurance availability signal, exit-liquidity measure, and travel/enjoyment characterization.

At property level, replace market proxies with address-specific title, zoning/use, HOA/condo, tax, insurance, inspection, utilities, management quote, and documented trailing revenue or defensible rent comps. A market-level score cannot become an offer recommendation.

## 5. Comparable financial model

All calculations retain native currency and also translate to USD using a dated Bank of Canada exchange rate. FX translation is not a hedge. Never mix asking prices with sold prices, city data with ZIP/postal-code data, or one property type with another without an explicit mismatch flag.

### 5.1 Capital stack

```text
total_project_cost = purchase_price
                   + buyer_closing_costs
                   + immediate_repairs_or_development
                   + furnishing_and_setup
                   + financing_costs
                   + FX_and_transfer_costs
                   + initial_operating_and_capex_reserve

cash_invested = total_project_cost - loan_proceeds
```

`cash_invested` must not exceed the USD capital ceiling. The model will show an all-cash case and, when financing is available, a large-down-payment case. It will not assume financing availability for a cross-border borrower.

### 5.2 Revenue

```text
LTR gross_scheduled_rent = monthly_contract_or_market_rent * 12
LTR effective_revenue = gross_scheduled_rent - vacancy_and_credit_loss + other_income

STR available_nights = calendar_nights - owner_blocked_nights - maintenance_blocks
STR room_revenue = sum(seasonal_available_nights * seasonal_occupancy * seasonal_ADR)
STR effective_revenue = room_revenue + other_guest_revenue - cancellations_and_refunds
```

ADR and occupancy must come from the same geography, property class, and period. Seller/PMS statements, booking-platform calendars, and vendor estimates are different evidence classes and must not be blended silently.

### 5.3 Operating performance

```text
NOI = effective_revenue
    - property_tax
    - insurance
    - HOA_or_condo_fees
    - utilities_and_connectivity
    - management
    - platform_and_payment_fees
    - owner_paid_cleaning_and_supplies
    - repairs_and_routine_maintenance
    - grounds_snow_pest_pool
    - licences_and_compliance
    - accounting_and_local_administration
    - replacement_reserve

unlevered_yield_on_cost = NOI / total_project_cost
debt_service_coverage_ratio = NOI / annual_debt_service
pre_tax_cash_flow = NOI - annual_debt_service
cash_on_cash_return = pre_tax_cash_flow / cash_invested
```

Taxes collected from guests and remitted are tracked as pass-through liabilities, not revenue. Income tax, depreciation/CCA and tax credits are modeled in a separate tax layer because they depend on residency, ownership, treaty and personal circumstances.

### 5.4 Personal use

```text
owner_use_opportunity_cost = sum(owner_blocked_nights_by_season * expected_net_contribution_per_night)
personal_trip_cash_cost = travel + owner-paid turnover + incremental utilities/supplies
```

Report both:

1. financial return after owner-use opportunity cost; and
2. non-financial enjoyment, scored directly by Hooman and his wife.

An optional “avoided lodging value” may be shown separately, capped at what the family would realistically have paid for a comparable trip. It is never rental revenue or NOI.

U.S. mixed-use tax treatment can change when personal use exceeds the greater of 14 days or 10% of days rented at fair rent. **`PRIMARY`, `HIGH`** (IRS Publication 527 for 2025 returns; C007). Exact treatment must be reviewed by a tax professional.

### 5.5 Land-first model

Land uses a phased discounted-cash-flow schedule, not a stabilized-rental cap rate:

```text
phase_0 = land + closing + survey + title + feasibility
phase_1 = design + entitlement + impact/development charges + utility/septic/well studies
phase_2 = sitework + construction + furnishing + commissioning
annual_carry = tax + insurance + security/maintenance + debt + professional fees
revenue = 0 until legal occupancy and all rental permissions are effective
```

Bear/base/bull cases vary approval duration, construction cost, opening date, stabilized occupancy/rent, insurance, and exit liquidity. Contingencies must be explicit estimates, not universal percentages presented as fact.

### 5.6 Stress tests required for all operating properties

- Occupancy/rent and ADR decline; seasonality concentration.
- One major repair plus ordinary replacement reserve.
- Property-management fee increase or manager failure.
- Insurance premium increase, deductible loss, and non-renewal.
- New HOA/condo special assessment or rental restriction.
- STR suspension, permit loss, or conversion to LTR.
- Canadian-dollar movement against USD for Canadian assets.
- Exit costs and slower sale; zero appreciation is the base comparison anchor.
- Personal use during peak-revenue dates.
- Financing reset/refinance risk if debt is used.

## 7. Cross-border findings that shape the screen

- U.S. tax residency cannot be inferred from Canadian citizenship or TN status. The IRS substantial-presence test uses at least 31 days in the current year and 183 weighted days across the current and preceding two years, subject to exclusions and exceptions. **`PRIMARY`, `HIGH`** (IRS; C005).
- If Hooman is a U.S. resident alien for tax purposes, the IRS states that worldwide income, including foreign rental income, is generally subject to U.S. tax and reported on a U.S. return. **`PRIMARY`, `HIGH`** as a general rule; individual application remains professional-verification work (IRS; C006).
- For a non-resident of Canada, a payer or Canadian agent generally withholds 25% of gross Canadian-source rent. A section 216 election can tax net rental income instead, and an approved NR6 permits withholding on estimated net income with a filing obligation. **`PRIMARY`, `HIGH`** as a general Canadian rule; treaty/entity application requires a CPA (CRA T4144; C009–C010).
- Canada denies deductions, including CCA, for the calculated non-compliant portion of a short-term rental. A rental is non-compliant when the province/municipality prohibits it at that location or required registration/licensing/permits are not satisfied. **`PRIMARY`, `HIGH`** (CRA T4036; C011–C012). Therefore, local legality is both an operating and tax gate.
- The current federal Canadian purchase-ban statute defines a “non-Canadian” individual as someone who is neither a Canadian citizen, registered Indian nor permanent resident. Given the stated Canadian citizenship, an individual purchase appears outside that definition. **`INFERRED`, `MEDIUM`** because title/entity/co-owner facts and legal application remain unverified (Department of Justice statute + user input; C013).
- The current Underused Housing Tax Act says no tax is payable for 2025 and subsequent calendar years. **`PRIMARY`, `HIGH`** (Department of Justice statute; C014). Filing/history/entity consequences should still be confirmed by a Canadian tax professional rather than assumed.
- If Hooman remains in TN status, the boundary between passive ownership and prohibited/authorized work involved in self-management is **`UNKNOWN`, `UNKNOWN`**. Do not design a U.S. operating model around Hooman personally performing hosting, maintenance, or management until immigration counsel confirms what is permitted.

## 8. Professional-verification checklist

### Cross-border CPA or tax lawyer

- Determine U.S. and Canadian tax residency and treaty position for the purchase year and holding period.
- Compare individual/joint/entity ownership; confirm federal, state/provincial, local and cross-border reporting.
- Model U.S. worldwide reporting, foreign tax credits, passive-activity rules, depreciation, Canadian CCA, section 216/NR6, GST/HST, lodging/sales taxes, and personal-use allocation.
- Model sale: basis, depreciation/CCA recapture, FIRPTA if applicable, Canadian non-resident disposition procedures, foreign tax credits, and currency gains/losses.
- Confirm whether any historic or ongoing UHT filing exposure exists under the contemplated title structure despite the current no-tax rule for 2025 onward.

### Immigration lawyer

- Under the then-current TN/LPR status, distinguish passive investment from prohibited or authorized self-employment.
- Define activities Hooman may perform and what must be delegated to a third-party manager.

### Local real-estate/land-use lawyer and municipality

- Confirm title/entity eligibility, intended use, permit availability/transfer, occupancy limits, local-contact rules, taxes and enforcement history for the exact parcel.
- Review HOA/condo declaration, rules, amendments, minutes, reserves, litigation, insurance and special assessments.
- For land: confirm legal access, allowed density/use, development agreement, setbacks, environmental/hazard overlays, water, wastewater/septic, power and rental eligibility after completion.

### Insurance broker, inspector and contractor

- Produce written address- and use-specific coverage, exclusions, deductibles, replacement-cost assumptions and business-income limits.
- Inspect structure/systems and, for condos, building-envelope and association risks.
- For development, provide staged budgets, schedules, contingencies, utility/sitework assumptions and local comparable projects.

### Property manager

- Confirm coverage, response time, owner-use process, fee schedule, markups, housekeeping/maintenance, permit/tax responsibilities, revenue reporting, owner portal, termination rights and references.

## 9. Pilot markets for the next sequential phase

### U.S. pilot: Panama City Beach, Florida — managed beach-condo STR

**Selection rationale:** This is a high-information pilot for the beach/amenity and remote-management objective. It forces the workflow to resolve city boundaries, state and city licensing, county tourist-tax registration, fire inspection, condo rules/reserves, coastal hazard and insurance, seasonality, management, and peak-date personal use. Price fit and returns are **`UNKNOWN`**; selection is not an endorsement.

Verified regulatory starting point:

- Within Panama City Beach voting wards, the city says vacation rentals must have a valid Vacation Rental Certificate; registration requires evidence of a Florida DBPR license, Bay County TDC/TDT registration and a local business-tax receipt, with inspection/re-registration requirements. **`PRIMARY`, `HIGH`** for the city jurisdiction only (City of Panama City Beach; C015–C016).
- Florida DBPR defines and licenses vacation-rental condos and dwellings and says a whole unit rented more than three times per year for periods under 30 days/one calendar month, or held out as regularly rented, requires licensing under its guide. **`PRIMARY`, `MEDIUM`** because the guide itself warns it may not contain every requirement (Florida DBPR; C017).

**Pilot pass condition:** one address-level condo case with verified city/county jurisdiction, zoning/use, DBPR and city path, condo documents, official tax/assessment record, 12-month sold comps, auditable revenue evidence, manager quote, written insurance indication, FEMA/local hazard screen, reserve study/special-assessment review, and complete all-cash plus financed stress models.

### Canadian pilot: Canmore, Alberta — existing Tourist Home resort unit

**Selection rationale:** This is a high-information mountain/amenity pilot for a professionally managed Canadian recreational property. It exercises cross-border tax/FX, a legally distinct STR property class, constrained legal inventory, local licensing, elevated property tax, provincial tourism levy, hazard/zoning, condo governance and personal-use tradeoffs. Price fit and returns are **`UNKNOWN`**; selection is not an endorsement.

Verified regulatory starting point:

- Canmore defines a Tourist Home as a property with an official development permit that can be used for short- or long-term rental or personal residence. Effective March 11, 2025, the land-use bylaw eliminated Tourist Homes as a permitted use for new conversion/development, while existing Tourist Homes remain. **`PRIMARY`, `HIGH`** (Town of Canmore; C018–C019).
- A Tourist Home used as an STR requires one business licence per individually titled property and its licence number in online advertising. Canmore states Tourist Homes are taxed at about three times the residential rate. **`PRIMARY`, `HIGH`** (Town of Canmore; C020–C021).
- Alberta states that its tourism levy increased from 4% to 6% effective April 1, 2026, with operator/host/broker responsibility depending on who collects payment. **`PRIMARY`, `HIGH`** as the provincial rule; transaction-specific responsibility still needs confirmation (Government of Alberta; C022).

**Pilot pass condition:** one existing Tourist Home address with development-permit and title/use verification, licence path, condo documents, assessment/tax record, 12-month sold comps, auditable rental history, manager quote, written insurance indication, municipal hazard map review, cross-border tax model, native-CAD/USD stress model, and explicit owner-use restrictions/cost.

## 10. Pilot execution and stop/go rule

Run the pilots sequentially enough to preserve learning:

1. Build the Panama City Beach property packet first and audit every field.
2. Revise the schema for jurisdiction boundaries, condo evidence, owner-use and management.
3. Build the Canmore packet and add cross-border, FX, Canadian tax, property-class and non-compliant-STR fields.
4. Audit both pilots and freeze schema version 1.
5. Only then parallelize comparable candidate-market collection into separate outputs; synthesis and scoring remain centralized.

Proceed to broader screening only if both pilots yield reproducible sources and the missing user thresholds are resolved. If either market is structurally disqualified, keep the lessons and select a replacement pilot from the same archetype rather than treating the failed market as evidence against the entire archetype.
