# Income and Enjoyment Property

## Decision

Determine whether Hooman should continue renting his California principal residence and deploy approximately USD 300,000 into a separate property in the United States or Canada that can generate income while also providing meaningful personal enjoyment.

## Starting parameters

- Available capital: approximately USD 300,000.
- Financing preference: cash purchase or a large down payment because current borrowing costs are unattractive.
- Geography: United States and Canada.
- Income models to consider: long-term rental, lawful short-term rental, seasonal rental, mixed personal/rental use, and land-first development where feasible.
- Enjoyment models:
  - nature, including beach, lake, mountain, or forest;
  - room for projects, gardening, or eventual homestead use;
  - a beach-oriented destination Hooman's wife would enjoy;
  - alternatively, a property with substantial on-site or nearby amenities.
- Operational constraint: any short-term rental must be legal and realistically manageable remotely.
- Long-term objective: build a monitoring process capable of identifying unusually attractive deals rather than making a one-time selection.

## Required research stages

1. Define comparable investment archetypes and disqualifiers.
2. Establish cross-border ownership, financing, taxation, insurance, licensing, and management questions that require primary sources or professional advice.
3. Pilot one US and one Canadian market before broad parallel screening.
4. Compare returns after all costs and personal-use opportunity costs.
5. Stress-test regulation, vacancy, seasonality, climate hazards, insurance, management failure, construction overruns, currency exposure, and exit liquidity.
6. Select markets for ongoing monitoring only after evidence audit and sensitivity analysis.

## Candidate archetypes to test

- Cash-flowing long-term rental in a nature-accessible secondary market.
- Licensed vacation rental near a beach, lake, mountain, or major attraction.
- Duplex or accessory-unit property combining personal use and rental income.
- Cabin, cottage, or resort-adjacent unit with professional management.
- Buildable land with a phased cabin, homestead, or short-term-rental plan.
- Canadian recreational property with seasonal rental potential and manageable cross-border obligations.

These are research buckets, not recommendations.

## Phase-one result

The screening and underwriting foundation is complete. It deliberately makes no investment recommendation and does not invent returns.

Two sequential learning pilots were selected:

- Panama City Beach, Florida: a professionally managed beach-condo short-term rental archetype.
- Canmore, Alberta: an existing, properly designated Tourist Home archetype.

These are method-validation pilots, not preferred markets. Their purpose is to expose US and Canadian data, regulation, tax, management, insurance, seasonality, and property-level diligence requirements before broader market research is parallelized.
