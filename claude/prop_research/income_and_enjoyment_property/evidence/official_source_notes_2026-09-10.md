## S01 — IRS: taxation of U.S. residents

- Publisher: Internal Revenue Service
- Title: “Taxation of U.S. residents”
- URL: https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/taxation-of-us-residents
- Page last reviewed/updated: 2026-06-25
- Retrieved: 2026-09-10
- Location: opening summary
- Exact passage: “If you are a U.S. resident, you must report all interest, dividends, wages, or other compensation for services, income from rental property or royalties, and other types of income on your U.S. tax return. You must report these amounts from sources within and outside the United States.” The page also states: “Resident aliens are generally taxed in the same way as U.S. citizens. ## S04 — IRS: U.S. real property owned by a nonresident alien

- Publisher: Internal Revenue Service
- Title: “Nonresident aliens – Real property located in the U.S.”

- Page last reviewed/updated: 2026-07-23
- Retrieved: 2026-09-10
- Location: rental-income discussion
- Exact passage: “Income from real property located in the United States (U.S.) that is owned by a nonresident alien (NRA) is taxed at a 30% (or lower treaty) rate if it is not effectively connected with a U.S. trade or business.” The page further says an NRA can elect under IRC 871(d) to treat income from U.S. real property held for production of income as effectively connected income, so it is taxed on a net basis, and must file Form 1040-NR.

## S05 — CRA T4144: nonresident Canadian rental withholding

- Publisher: Canada Revenue Agency
- Title: “T4144: Income Tax Guide for Electing under Section 216”
- URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4144/income-tax-guide-electing-under-section-216.html
- Source version: T4144(E) Rev. 25
- Retrieved: 2026-09-10
- Location: “Non-resident tax” and “Electing under section 216”
- Exact passage: “When you receive rental income from real or immovable property in Canada, the payer or agent (such as the property manager) must withhold non-resident tax of 25% on the gross rental income paid or credited to you. The payer or agent must send the tax to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) on or before the 15th day of the month following the month the rental income is paid or credited to you.” The guide says the withholding is generally the final Canadian tax obligation unless the owner elects under section 216, which may reduce tax and generate a refund.
- Limitation: General nonresident rule; status, treaty, co-ownership and entity treatment need professional application.

## S06 — CRA T4144: NR6 and section 216

- Publisher: Canada Revenue Agency
- Title: “T4144: Income Tax Guide for Electing under Section 216”

- Source version: T4144(E) Rev. 25
- Retrieved: 2026-09-10
- Location: “Withholding on net rental income (Form NR6)”
- Exact passage: “If you elect under section 216, you can elect to have tax withheld on your net rental income instead of the gross amount.” The guide says the owner and agent complete Form NR6, the agent continues gross withholding until written CRA approval, and after approval the agent can withhold 25% of net rental income. - Limitation: Filing deadlines and mechanics vary with facts and year; verify with a cross-border CPA.

- Exact passage: “Non-compliant short-term rental – refers to a short-term rental located in a province or municipality that meets one of the following conditions: the province or municipality does not permit operating the short-term rental at that location; [or] the province or municipality requires registration, a licence, or a permit to operate the short-term rental, and the short-term rental does not comply with all the proper registration, licensing, and permit requirements.”
