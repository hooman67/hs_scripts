## Methodology: Simpson Diversity Index (SDI)

**Formula**: SDI = 1 - Σ(p_i²) where p_i is the proportion of each racial/ethnic group

**Interpretation**:
- **0.0–0.2**: Very homogeneous (dominated by one group)
- **0.2–0.4**: Low diversity
- **0.4–0.6**: Moderate diversity
- **0.6–0.8**: High diversity
- **0.8–1.0**: Very high diversity

## Data Quality & Confidence Assessment

### HIGH Confidence Data
- ✓ 2020 Decennial Census figures (official, collected by Census Bureau)
- ✓ Census tract-level demographic breakdowns
- ✓ Historical population counts

### MEDIUM Confidence Data
- ◐ 2023 American Community Survey estimates (rolling estimates; final data release pending 2025)
- ◐ Projected diversity indices (extrapolated from partial ACS data)

### LOW Confidence Data
- ✗ Simpson Diversity Index for small geographic areas (not routinely published by Census Bureau; requires manual calculation)
- ✗ Community presence assessments (Persian/Brazilian populations; based on secondary sources and business directories)
- ✗ Hate incident history (requires separate research; not included in comprehensive source)

### Data Limitations

1. **Geographic mismatch**: County-level data lumps together highly diverse urban cores with homogeneous rural areas. For neighborhood-level decisions, Census tract data needed (not included here).

2. **2023 ACS incomplete**: Full demographic breakdowns for 2023 not yet published; estimates based on partial releases and 2021-2023 rolling averages.

3. **Racial categorization**: Census categories may not capture Persian/Iranian ethnicity accurately (often coded as "White" or "Asian"; cultural identity often missing).

4. **Community presence**: "Persian" and "Brazilian" community assessments are qualitative; no official Census category tracks these groups.

5. **Diversity ≠ Inclusion**: High SDI doesn't guarantee immigrant-friendly policies or lack of discrimination. Recommend supplemental research on hate crime history, sanctuary status, and local politics.

## Recommended Next Steps for Property Research

### For Step 2 (Neighborhood Scoring)

1. **Download Census tract-level data** from data.census.gov for specific neighborhoods within each jurisdiction
2. **Calculate tract-level SDI** to identify pockets of higher diversity within Folsom/Roseville/Placer
3. **Research specific neighborhoods**:
   - Folsom: older areas near downtown may have higher diversity
   - Roseville: south/southwest areas may have larger Latino and immigrant populations
   - Placer County: southern areas (Rocklin, Lincoln) may have higher diversity than northern rural areas

4. **Verify Persian/Brazilian community presence** in target neighborhoods:
   - Search for Iranian/Persian restaurants, markets, mosques within 10 miles
   - Search for Brazilian social groups, Portuguese-speaking meetups
   - Check Nextdoor/Facebook groups for immigrant family presence

5. **Research hate crime history** for each jurisdiction:
   - FBI Hate Crime Statistics (https://www.fbi.gov/investigate/civil-rights/hate-crimes)
   - SPLC (Southern Poverty Law Center) hate group map
   - Local news archives for racial incidents (last 5 years)

6. **Assess sanctuary status**:
   - Sacramento County: Declared sanctuary jurisdiction (2017) — supports immigrant families
   - Folsom: Part of Sacramento County; check city-level policies
   - Roseville: Check city council records and police policies
   - Placer County: Not known as sanctuary jurisdiction; verify local immigration enforcement
