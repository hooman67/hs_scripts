## Four Research Components - Summary

### Component 1: Violent Crime Rates (per 1,000 residents, 2025)
**Status:** COMPLETE | **Confidence:** HIGH | **Data Year:** 2025
- **Source:** FBI Uniform Crime Reporting (UCR) via City-Data.com
- **Metric:** Violent crimes per 1,000 residents (murder, rape, robbery, aggravated assault)
- **Coverage:** All 10 cities, 2023-2025 trend data
- **Key Finding:** All cities trending downward; Los Gatos, San Mateo, Gilroy show largest improvements
- **Quick Ranking:** Safest=Saratoga (39.5), Highest=San Jose (283.4)
- **Data Gap:** San Jose neighborhood-level crime not available (requires premium source or SJPD request)

### Component 2: Racial/Ethnic Diversity Indices (Simpson Index, 2024)
**Status:** COMPLETE | **Confidence:** HIGH | **Data Year:** 2024
- **Source:** US Census Bureau ACS (2019-2024 cycles) via City-Data.com
- **Metric:** Simpson Diversity Index (D = 1 - Σ(p²), range 0-1, higher=more diverse)
- **Coverage:** All 10 cities, all major racial/ethnic categories
- **Key Finding:** San Mateo most diverse (0.7274, tri-ethnic balance); Cupertino least (0.4182, Asian-dominant)
- **Methodology:** Calculated from published Census percentages using standard formula
- **Data Gap:** None significant

### Component 3: Percent Non-White Population (2024)
**Status:** COMPLETE | **Confidence:** HIGH | **Data Year:** 2024
- **Source:** US Census Bureau ACS 5-year estimates via City-Data.com
- **Metric:** 100% - % White (non-Hispanic alone)
- **Coverage:** All 10 cities, detailed racial/ethnic breakdown
- **Key Finding:** Milpitas most non-white (87.3%); Los Gatos most White-majority (58.8% White)
- **Data Gap:** None

### Component 4: Water Status & Constraints (Current)
**Status:** COMPLETE | **Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH | **Data Year:** 2024-2026
- **Source:** California DWR, USBR, SCVWD, CA State Water Board, 2024-2026 news
- **Metric:** Drought status, SWP allocation %, Colorado River crisis, infrastructure timeline
- **Coverage:** Regional (all 10 cities in SCVWD service area)
- **Key Finding:** Medium-high water stress through 2026; Sites Reservoir relief begins 2034
- **Data Gaps:** 
  - City-specific water rate increases (not quantified in accessible sources)

  - Post-2026 Colorado River rules (uncertain; final decision due December 2025)
