## 2. ROUTE RELIABILITY & ALTERNATIVES

### PRIMARY ROUTES ASSESSMENT

**CA-17 (Santa Cruz County → San Jose): FRAGILE**
- Distance: 26.49 miles
- Single freeway corridor crossing Patchen Pass at 1,800 ft elevation
- **Critical Vulnerability:** Crosses San Andreas Fault seismic zone
- **Historical Precedent:** 1989 Loma Prieta earthquake closed route 50%+ for 1-2 months
- **Winter Risks:** High precipitation area; snow possible above 1,500 ft
- **Recent Incident:** December 2024 EF1 tornado in Scotts Valley corridor with injuries
- **Nickname:** "Killer 17" — narrow lanes, sharp curves, frequent accidents
- **Single-Point-of-Failure:** Only freeway from Santa Cruz County to San Jose
- **Source:** CALTRANS District 5, USGS seismic data, historical incident records (June 2026)

**US-101 (Santa Clara Valley): MODERATE**
- Primary alternate/backup route through San Jose
- **Known Bottleneck:** Joe Colla Interchange (I-280/I-680 convergence) rated "notorious" for chronic delays
- **Ranking:** 3rd most congested Bay Area freeway per 2016 MTC study
- **Geographic Constraint:** Narrow corridor between Santa Cruz Mountains (west) and Diablo Range (east)
- **Seismic Risk:** Calaveras Fault 1 mile from Morgan Hill (7.3 magnitude capability)
- **Seasonal Vulnerability:** Subject to flooding and debris flows during heavy rains
- **Source:** MTC traffic studies, USGS seismic data (June 2026)

**CA-152 (Monterey County → San Jose): FRAGILE**
- Two critical mountain pass constraints:
  - **Hecker Pass:** Single lane each direction, no center dividers, "prone to head-on collisions"
  - **Pacheco Pass:** 1,368 ft elevation, numerous accidents
- **Truck Restrictions:** Vehicles over 45 feet cannot traverse
- **Flood Vulnerability:** Pajaro River floodplain has recurring closure history
  - 1995: $95M damage
  - 1998: Presidential disaster
  - 2023: Levee breach
- **Design Flaw:** Levee system acknowledged "poor planning" by USACE in 1963; continues experiencing breaches
- **Source:** USACE levee records, CALTRANS District 5, historical incident data (June 2026)

### VIABLE ALTERNATIVE ROUTES COUNT

**From Santa Cruz County (6 alternatives, all with significant limitations):**
1. **CA-9:** Scenic two-lane mountain route, ~38.5 miles, slow (unsuitable for time-sensitive travel)
2. **CA-35 (Skyline Blvd):** Ridge route (experienced 220-foot washout in 2017, 11-month closure)
3. **CA-1 Coastal:** Big Sur section faces recurring 6-12+ month closures (2023 event: 7-mile section projected closed until January 2026)
4. **Amtrak Thruway Bus:** Limited capacity
5. **CA-129/US-101 Inland:** Longer route with Pajaro River flooding vulnerability
6. **All alternatives:** Substantially longer, slower, or unreliable

**From Monterey County (8 alternatives, worse isolation):**
1. **US-101:** Primary route (chronic congestion issues)
2. **CA-152:** Mountain passes (fragile, landslide/flooding prone)
3. **CA-1 Coastal:** Unreliable (Big Sur section)
4. **CA-68 (Monterey-Salinas):** Over design capacity (26,000 vehicles/day vs. 16,000 design capacity)
5. **CA-25:** 74+ miles through San Benito County (not practical)
6. **CA-156:** Limited connectivity, still requires US-101
7. **Castroville Rail Hub:** Future potential but timeline uncertain
8. **Amtrak/Caltrain Extension to Salinas:** Planned but uncertain implementation timeline

**Source:** CALTRANS, MTC, transportation planning documents (June 2026)

### ROUTE RELIABILITY RANKING

**VERY HIGH CRITICALITY - Single-Point-of-Failure:**
- US-101 Joe Colla Interchange (I-280/I-680 convergence): Regional cascade risk; no direct bypass

**HIGH CRITICALITY:**
- CA-17 Patchen Pass (1,800 ft): Only freeway from Santa Cruz County; closure forces 90+ minute detour via coastal CA-1 or narrow scenic CA-9
- CA-152 Hecker Pass: Only practical mountain pass from Monterey; closure forces long US-101 detour or distant routes

**MODERATE-HIGH CRITICALITY:**
- CA-152 Pacheco Pass (1,368 ft): Separates Santa Clara Valley from Central Valley; closure requires distant alternate passes

### SEASONAL & WEATHER VULNERABILITIES

**Winter Peak Risk (October-April):**
- Precipitation volatility: 11.17 inches (dry years) to 37.76 inches (wet years) — 3.4x variability
- Mud/landslide season: CA-17 high precipitation area vulnerable; Pajaro River flooding recurring
- Snow/ice: Possible above 1,500 feet during severe winters
- Pineapple Express atmospheric river events: Regional flooding across multiple routes simultaneously
- **Source:** CALTRANS, NOAA weather data (June 2026)

**Big Sur CA-1 Landslide Pattern (2015-2024):**
- March 2011: Months-long closure
- Feb 2016-Oct 2017: Pfeiffer Canyon Bridge reconstruction (8 months); Mud Creek landslide (13 months, $40M road reconstruction)
- Jan 2021: Rat Creek collapse (3-month closure)
- Jan 2023: 40 miles closed; 7-mile Lucia section projected closed until January 2026
- March 2024: 1.4-mile closure north of Rocky Creek Bridge
- Pattern: Winter/early spring peak; typical 6-12+ month recovery; communities "cut off from supplies"
- **Source:** CALTRANS historical records, news archives (June 2026)

**Seismic Risk:**
- 1989 Loma Prieta precedent: 50%+ CA-17 closure for 1-2 months
- Active faults: Calaveras Fault (7.3 magnitude capability), Coyote Creek Fault (potentially active), San Andreas Fault
- Major seismic event (6.5+) could trigger multi-route simultaneous disruption for months
- **Source:** USGS seismic hazard data (June 2026)

## 7. WATER STATUS

### Monterey County

**Status:** Chronic water stress endemic to the region

**Primary Constraint:** Monterey Peninsula Water Crisis
- Duration: Ongoing since 1995 (31+ years)
- Root Cause: Carmel River severely limited; seawater intrusion risk to groundwater
- **Typical Operating Restrictions:** 25-40% use restrictions even during normal water years
- Note: Even with adequate statewide water, Monterey Peninsula faces structural supply constraints
- **Contact:** Monterey Peninsula Water Management District (831) 658-5600
- **Real-Time Data Portal:** https://www.mpwmd.net (Rain & Streamflow Data mapping tool)
- **Source:** MPWMD direct research (June 2026)

- **Contact:** Santa Cruz County Water District

**Source:** Santa Cruz County Water District research (June 2026)
