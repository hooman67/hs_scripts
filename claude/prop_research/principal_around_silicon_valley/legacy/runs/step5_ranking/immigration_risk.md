## Scenario 2: Should Wife's Name Be on Title?

### STRONGLY RECOMMENDED: YES

**Reasons to put both names on title:**

1. **California is a community property state** — property acquired during marriage is presumed community property regardless of whose name is on the deed. Having both names makes this explicit and avoids probate complications.

2. **Continuity protection** — if Hooman's visa is interrupted (TN denial, employer change gap, etc.), wife's name on title ensures uninterrupted ownership and residency. A sole-ownership scenario could complicate things if the sole owner loses legal presence.

3. **Mortgage qualification** — most lenders will include both names anyway on a joint application. Having wife on deed matches the mortgage obligation.

4. **Survivorship** — joint tenancy with right of survivorship means property automatically passes to surviving spouse without probate.

5. **No downside** — there is no immigration disadvantage to having both spouses on title.

### Title Vesting Options

| Vesting Type | Recommendation | Notes |
|-------------|---------------|-------|
| Joint Tenancy | RECOMMENDED | Equal ownership, right of survivorship, avoids probate |
| Community Property with Right of Survivorship | ALSO GOOD | Same benefits plus stepped-up cost basis at death (tax advantage) |
| Tenants in Common | NOT RECOMMENDED | No survivorship; requires probate |
| Sole ownership (Hooman only) | NOT RECOMMENDED | Creates complications if visa issues arise |

**RECOMMENDED VESTING: Community Property with Right of Survivorship** — combines the survivorship benefit with the California community property tax advantages (double step-up in basis).
