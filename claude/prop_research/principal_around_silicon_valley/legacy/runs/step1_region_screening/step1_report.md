## Detailed Results by Region

### Region 1: Sacramento & Central Valley — ✅ PASS

**Commute**: 60–90 min (off-peak–rush)  
**Route**: I-80 → 101/880-S (moderate reliability; Donner Summit elevation 7,227 ft gets winter closures Nov–May, but US-50 and US-395 alternatives available)  
**Median 3BR Price**: $950K (Folsom, Roseville, El Dorado Hills cluster)  
**AI/ML Jobs**: ~28 within 60 min (Intel Folsom, state capital tech, limited but present)  
**Crime**: 4.2/1K (below threshold)  
**Diversity**: 0.45 / 47% non-white (acceptable)  
**Water**: Stressed (CA DWR system under drought pressure; Folsom Lake operational but competing demands)  

**Verdict**: PASS all deal-breakers. Lower job density and longer commute vs. Peninsula/East Bay, but significantly cheaper and still suburban comfortable. Advances to Step 2 for detailed neighborhood analysis (Folsom, Roseville, El Dorado Hills).

### Region 3: East Bay — ✅ PASS

**Commute**: 45–50 min (off-peak–rush)  
**Route**: I-680-S → I-880-S → 101-S (reliable; multiple alternatives via I-580, US-205, local streets)  
**Median 3BR Price**: $1.25M (Pleasanton, Livermore, Walnut Creek corridor)  
**AI/ML Jobs**: 150+ within 60 min (good density; accessible to Silicon Valley + Oakland/Berkeley tech)  
**Crime**: 3.5/1K (well below threshold)  
**Diversity**: 0.56 / 48% non-white (highly diverse; one of CA's most multicultural regions)  
**Water**: Adequate (East Bay watersheds stable; not under acute stress)  

**Verdict**: PASS all deal-breakers. Excellent commute reliability with multiple route options. More affordable than Peninsula. High diversity makes this an ideal region for a brown Middle Eastern family. Strong candidate. Advances to Step 2 for detailed neighborhood analysis.

### Region 4: Peninsula & South Bay — ✅ PASS (Strongest candidate)

**Commute**: 15–18 min (off-peak–rush to DTSJ)  
**Route**: US-101 / SR-85 / I-880 (highly reliable; 5+ alternative routes; no single point of failure)  
**Median 3BR Price**: $1.55M (spans San Jose, Campbell, Los Gatos, Saratoga, Cupertino, Mountain View, Milpitas, Redwood City)  
**AI/ML Jobs**: 4,000+ within 60 min (highest density in region; multiple employer clusters)  
**Crime**: 2.6/1K (lowest among passing regions)  
**Diversity**: 0.58 / 58% non-white (highly diverse; San Jose itself 50%+ non-white)  
**Water**: Stressed (SCVWD under drought; some neighborhoods under restrictions)  

**Verdict**: PASS all deal-breakers. **Strongest candidate** — shortest commute, highest job security, most diverse, safest. Price premium justified. Water stress is manageable (not critical). Must advance to Step 2 as priority region for detailed neighborhood analysis.

---

### Region 5: Santa Cruz & Monterey — ⚠️ CONDITIONAL PASS

**Commute**: 45–70 min (off-peak–rush to DTSJ)  
**Route**: CA-17 → US-101 (**FRAGILE** — single practical route; crosses San Andreas Fault; precedent of 50+ day closures from Loma Prieta earthquake 1989)  
**Median 3BR Price**: $1.1M (excellent value)  
**AI/ML Jobs**: ~25 within 60 min (very low; mostly Santa Cruz locals, many commute OUT to SJ)  
**Crime**: 3.5/1K (acceptable)  
**Diversity**: 0.70 / 59% non-white (highest diversity in list; strong Latin American community)  
**Water**: Stressed (Monterey chronically stressed; 30-year structural deficit with 25–40% year-round rationing as permanent condition)  

**Verdict**: CONDITIONAL PASS. Technically passes all filters (commute acceptable off-peak; prices well under budget; high diversity). However, **critical vulnerability**:
- **CA-17 single-point-of-failure**: This is the ONLY practical north-south route for Santa Cruz. Multi-day closures from mudslides/landslides/rockfall are not hypothetical — they happened during Loma Prieta and regularly during wet seasons. Risk for stranded commute to DTSJ.
- **Monterey chronic water stress**: Not a formal "Critical" status but represents permanent 25–40% rationing as baseline. Long-term property value and lifestyle concerns.

Beach access and land availability are excellent. Recommend advancing to Step 2 with flag: coastal neighborhoods (Aptos, Capitola, Watsonville) may mitigate CA-17 risk by offering closer-to-DTSJ alternative routes or lower earthquake exposure. Revisit if remote work becomes permanent.
