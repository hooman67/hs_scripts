## 4a: Purchase Scenarios

### Assumptions
- **Mortgage rate:** 6.54% (30-year fixed, June 2026)
- **Down payment:** 20% for all scenarios (no PMI)
- **Property tax:** Neighborhood-specific effective rates (including assessments)
- **Insurance:** Neighborhood-specific (standard ~$2,000-$3,500/yr; wildfire zones significantly higher)
- **HOA:** $0 assumed (targeting SFH without HOA)
- **DTI thresholds:** 28% front-end (housing only), 36% total (housing + other debt)

### Monthly Payment Formula
P&I = Loan Amount x [r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n - 1]
Where r = monthly rate (6.54%/12 = 0.545%), n = 360 months

### Detailed Risk Narratives

#### FREMONT - Risk Score: 5.5/10 (Moderate)

**What could cause values to DROP:**
- **Tech layoff wave:** Fremont's buyer pool is heavily tech (Tesla, Meta, Apple, startups). A 2024-style tech downturn specifically hitting hardware/AI could reduce demand. However, Fremont's diverse employment base (manufacturing, biotech, healthcare at Washington Hospital) provides some cushion.
- **Interest rate spike:** If rates hit 8%+, the $1.4M-$1.7M price point becomes unaffordable for many first-time buyers. However, Fremont's strong rental market provides a floor.
- **BART/transit changes:** Currently a transit advantage; if service deteriorates, commute calculus shifts.
- **School redistricting:** Mission San Jose premium is school-driven; any rezoning could shift values between micro-neighborhoods.

**Downside scenario:** 10-15% correction in a severe tech recession + rate spike. Recovery within 3-5 years given fundamentals.

**Upside catalysts:** AI industry boom (proximity to Meta AI, Tesla AI), continued Asian immigration demand, BART extension improvements, housing shortage persistence.

---

#### SAN MATEO - Risk Score: 4.5/10 (Low-Moderate)

**What could cause values to DROP:**
- **Remote work permanence:** Peninsula premium is partly commute-driven. If remote work eliminates the need to be near SF/Peninsula offices, the premium erodes.
- **Interest rate sensitivity:** At $1.8M-$2M, buyer pool is thin. Rate increases disproportionately reduce qualified buyers at this price point.
- **Caltrain construction disruption:** Extended electrification work could temporarily reduce transit appeal.
- **SFO noise/development:** Certain San Mateo neighborhoods experience aircraft noise; regulatory changes could impact livability perceptions.

**Downside scenario:** 8-12% correction. Peninsula scarcity and SFPUC water infrastructure provide strong floor. Land-constrained geography limits new supply.

**Upside catalysts:** Biotech corridor expansion (South SF spillover), Caltrain electrification completion (faster DTSJ commute), continued SF exodus to Peninsula suburbs, limited housing supply.

---

#### OAKLAND HILLS - Risk Score: 8/10 (High)

**What could cause values to DROP:**
- **WILDFIRE (Primary Risk):** The 1991 Oakland Hills fire destroyed 3,354 homes. Climate change is increasing fire frequency/severity. A major fire event would cause catastrophic value destruction (50%+ in affected areas, 15-25% in nearby unburned areas due to fear premium).
- **Insurance market collapse:** California's insurance crisis is accelerating. If major carriers fully exit the hills, buyers face FAIR Plan only (expensive, limited coverage). Uninsurability = uninvestability for leveraged buyers.
- **Oakland fiscal crisis:** City budget deficits, police staffing issues, and political instability create headwinds for city services and perception.
- **Crime perception spillover:** Hills are safe, but "Oakland" brand carries flatlands crime associations that can deter buyers and impact resale.

**Downside scenario:** 20-30% correction possible if insurance becomes unavailable or a fire event occurs within proximity. Recovery could take 5-10 years.

**Upside catalysts:** Oakland revitalization, remote work demand for space/nature, insurance market reform (AB 2167 regulatory intervention), relative affordability attracting buyers priced out of Peninsula.

**CRITICAL RISK:** The insurance situation alone makes Oakland Hills a speculative bet. Annual insurance costs could rise from $5,500 to $12,000-$15,000, effectively adding $500-$800/mo to housing costs and reducing the pool of buyers at resale.

---

#### PLEASANTON - Risk Score: 4/10 (Low)

**What could cause values to DROP:**
- **Tech layoff sensitivity:** Pleasanton buyers are heavily dual-income tech (Dublin/Pleasanton corridor, BART commuters to SF/South Bay). A sustained tech downturn would reduce demand.
- **Commute fatigue:** If Adobe mandates 5-day office return AND traffic worsens, the 45-min commute becomes a quality-of-life issue that could push buyers toward closer-to-SJ options.
- **Tri-Valley oversupply:** Dublin and San Ramon are adding significant new housing inventory. While Pleasanton itself has limited land, regional supply additions could moderate appreciation.
- **School demographic shifts:** Pleasanton's premium is school-driven. Demographic changes or state-level education policy shifts could narrow the quality gap.

**Downside scenario:** 8-10% correction in severe downturn. Strong school demand provides firm floor; Pleasanton has historically been among the most resilient Bay Area markets in downturns.

**Upside catalysts:** Continued Tri-Valley development (commercial/retail amenities improving), BART connectivity, housing shortage deepening, school reputation compounding.
