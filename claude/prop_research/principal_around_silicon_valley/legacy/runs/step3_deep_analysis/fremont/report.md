### Vaccination Policy & Exemption Options (CRITICAL)

**California Law (SB 277, effective 2016):**
- Eliminated personal belief exemptions for public and private schools
- Only medical exemptions remain for school attendance
- Applies to ALL public schools, private schools, and childcare facilities
- SB 276 (2019) further tightened medical exemption process: requires state review of doctors who write many exemptions

**Current Medical Exemption Landscape:**
- Alameda County historically has lower medical exemption rates than statewide average (~1-2% of kindergarteners)
- Individual school rates vary; private schools and smaller schools tend to have slightly higher rates
- Medical exemptions require a physician to document a qualifying medical condition

### Schools/Options for Unvaccinated Children

**Option 1: Homeschooling (NO vaccination required)**
- California homeschoolers are NOT subject to vaccination requirements
- Three legal options: (a) file private school affidavit as home-based school, (b) use an ISP (Independent Study Program), (c) enroll in public school independent study
- HSLDA confirms: "Immunization requirements: No" for California homeschoolers

**Option 2: Independent Study Charter Schools**
- California Virtual Academies (CAVA) -- K-12 online public charter
- Connections Academy California
- Epic Charter Schools
- These are public schools but operate via independent study; vaccination requirements technically apply but enforcement varies significantly for fully remote students
- NOTE: As of 2024-2025, CA enforcement for independent study students who never physically attend campus is inconsistent

**Option 3: Private Schools with Higher Exemption Rates**

- **Peace Terrace Academy** (Islamic, PK-8, 264 students)

- Smaller schools are more likely to have families using medical exemptions

**Option 4: Montessori / Waldorf Programs (Preschool -- no vax requirement under age)**
- **Montessori West** (4 Fremont campuses) -- infant through elementary (grades 1-6)
  - Cameron Hills, Ardenwood, Mission Valley (Kimber Park), Weibel/Warm Springs locations
  - Programs: infant (3mo+), toddler (18mo-3yr), primary (3-6yr), elementary (1-6)
  - Operating since 1974; Google rating 4.4/5
  - Tuition: ~$15,000-$20,000/year (estimated based on Bay Area Montessori averages)

**Option 5: Homeschool Co-ops in the Area**

## Section 3d: Survivability & Environment

### Water Supply

**Provider:** Alameda County Water District (ACWD)
- **Service area:** Fremont, Newark, Union City (Tri-City area)
- **Headquarters:** 43885 S Grimmer Blvd, Fremont, CA 94538
- **Primary sources:**
  - State Water Project (via South Bay Aqueduct from Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta)
  - San Francisco Public Utilities Commission (Hetch Hetchy system -- backup/supplemental)
  - Local groundwater (Niles Cone Aquifer -- managed and recharged)
  - Alameda Creek watershed (local surface water)
- **Supply outlook: ADEQUATE** -- ACWD has diversified sources including the massive Niles Cone aquifer which they actively manage and recharge. The district has invested heavily in groundwater banking and desalination readiness.
- **Drought resilience:** Moderate-to-good. Multiple supply sources provide redundancy. The Niles Cone aquifer serves as a drought buffer. However, State Water Project allocations can be reduced in severe drought years.
- **Conservation:** ACWD promotes water efficiency with rebates and recognition programs.

### Well Drilling Feasibility

- **Niles Cone Aquifer** underlies most of Fremont
- Domestic wells are possible but require permits from ACWD
- Water table depth: typically 15-50 feet in most residential areas
- **Regulations:** Requires well permit, compliance with ACWD groundwater management plan
- Generally feasible but municipal water is reliable enough that wells are uncommon in residential areas

### Wildfire Risk

- **CAL FIRE FHSZ designation:** Most of Fremont (flatlands) is in a **Non-VHFHSZ** zone (no significant wildfire risk)
- **Exception:** The eastern hills near Mission Peak and Sunol ridge are designated **Moderate to High** fire hazard severity zones
- **Neighborhoods affected by elevated risk:** Upper Mission San Jose (homes backing into hills), areas near Mission Peak trailhead
- **Flatland neighborhoods (Centerville, Irvington, Warm Springs, Niles):** LOW to NO wildfire risk
- **Overall assessment: LOW** for the target price range neighborhoods

### Flight Path Proximity

- **Nearest commercial airport:** San Jose International (SJC) -- approximately 15 miles south
- **Oakland International (OAK):** approximately 25 miles north
- **Hayward Executive Airport:** small airport ~8 miles north, minimal commercial traffic
- **Flight path impact:** Fremont is generally NOT under major flight paths. Some south Fremont areas may occasionally hear SJC departures but it is not a significant concern.

### Air Quality

- **Current AQI (June 2026):** 31 (Good) -- PM2.5 at 5.5 ug/m3
- **Annual average:** Generally "Good" by EPA standards
- **Concerns:** Alameda County received "F" grade for PM2.5 and ozone (2016-2018 period), averaging 8.8 unhealthy ozone days and 11.2 unhealthy PM2.5 days per year
- **Wildfire smoke:** During September-November fire season, AQI can spike to 150-300+ (similar to all Bay Area)
- **Tesla factory impact:** Some community concern about industrial emissions, though factory is electric vehicle manufacturing (lower emissions than traditional auto)

## Section 3e: Immigration & Legal

### Sanctuary Status

**California is a SANCTUARY STATE** under SB 54 (California Values Act, 2017):

Key provisions protecting immigrants:
- Local law enforcement PROHIBITED from using resources to "investigate, interrogate, detain, detect, or arrest persons for immigration enforcement purposes"
- Cooperation with ICE limited to cases involving serious/violent felonies
- Public schools, courthouses, and health facilities must adopt policies limiting immigration enforcement
- Agencies cannot restrict programs or change classifications based solely on immigration status

**Alameda County:** Additional local sanctuary policies reinforce state protections. Alameda County has been one of the strongest sanctuary jurisdictions in California since before SB 54.

**City of Fremont:** Operates under both state and county sanctuary protections. Fremont's highly diverse population (83.4% non-white, 51% foreign-born) means the political will to protect immigrants is extremely strong.

### Political Climate
