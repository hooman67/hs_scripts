### Vaccination Policy Analysis (CRITICAL)

**California Law (SB 277, effective 2016):**
- Eliminated personal belief exemptions for public and private school enrollment
- Only medical exemptions remain for brick-and-mortar schools
- SB 276 (2019) added physician oversight for medical exemptions

**Options for Unvaccinated Children in California:**

1. **Homeschooling (Private School Affidavit - PSA):** NO vaccination requirement. Parents file a private school affidavit with the state. Per HSLDA: "Immunization requirements: No" for homeschoolers.

2. **Independent Study Programs (ISPs):** Many charter-affiliated ISPs operate as "non-classroom-based" instruction and do NOT require vaccinations. Key options near Folsom:
   - **California Virtual Academies (CAVA)** - K-12 online public charter, no in-person attendance required
   - **Inspire Charter Schools** - Flexible hybrid model, parent-directed with state funding
   - **Compass Charter Schools** - Virtual and homeschool enrichment model
   - **Clarksville Charter School** (El Dorado Hills, near Folsom) - Hybrid/independent study model

3. **Homeschool Co-ops:** Sacramento area has a robust homeschool community. Groups include:
   - Sacramento Area Homeschool Co-ops (multiple active groups)
   - Folsom/El Dorado Hills homeschool networks (Facebook groups with 500+ members)
   - Classical Conversations communities in the Folsom area
   - Wild + Free Sacramento (nature-based homeschool community)

4. **Forest Schools / Outdoor Education:** Growing movement in Sacramento foothills area. Not classified as traditional schools, so vaccination requirements vary.

5. **Fusion Academy Folsom** (420 Palladio Parkway) - Private, WASC-accredited, 1:1 instruction for grades 4-12. As a private school it requires vaccination records per SB 277, but with only medical exemption option.

### Best Schools for Unvaccinated Children

1. **Homeschool with PSA filing** - Zero vaccination requirement, fully legal
2. **Clarksville Charter School** (nearby El Dorado Hills) - Independent study/hybrid model
3. **California Virtual Academy (CAVA)** - Online public charter, no campus attendance
4. **Inspire Charter Schools** - Funded homeschool enrichment, parent-directed
5. **Compass Charter Schools** - Virtual option with enrichment activities

## Section 3d: Survivability & Environment

### Water Supply

**Provider:** City of Folsom Water Division
**Source:** Sacramento River and Folsom Lake (American River watershed)
**Federal Contract:** Received permanent Central Valley Project (CVP) water contract in February 2020

**Folsom Lake Stats:**
- Capacity: 976,000 acre-feet
- Surface area: 11,450 acres
- 75 miles of shoreline
- Multi-purpose: drinking water, hydroelectric power, flood control, fishery management

**Water Outlook: STRESSED during drought, but ADEQUATE long-term**
- Folsom Lake levels fluctuate significantly (440 ft summer to 405 ft winter)
- During 2021-2022 drought, lake dropped to historic lows (~17% capacity)
- Permanent CVP contract provides legal water security
- Sacramento River system is one of California's most reliable water sources
- City has mandatory conservation measures during drought declarations
- Multiple backup sources through regional Sacramento area water agreements

### Well Drilling

- Groundwater depth: 30-80 feet in Folsom area (varies by location)
- Sacramento County allows domestic wells with permits
- Feasible in older neighborhoods with larger lots
- Newer developments typically have CC&Rs that restrict well drilling
- Not necessary given reliable municipal supply

### Wildfire Risk

**CAL FIRE FHSZ Designation:** Most of Folsom is NOT in a Fire Hazard Severity Zone (FHSZ)

- The majority of Folsom (flat suburban areas) has **NONE to LOW** wildfire risk
- Eastern edges near Folsom Lake and oak woodland areas may be designated MODERATE
- The city is NOT in the Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI) for most residential areas
- No recent significant wildfires have affected Folsom proper
- **However:** Smoke from Sierra Nevada wildfires regularly affects air quality in summer/fall

### Air Quality

- **Current AQI (June 2026):** 25 (Good)
- **Primary Pollutant:** Ozone (O3) - typical of Sacramento Valley
- **Summer average AQI:** 50-80 (Moderate, ozone-driven)
- **Wildfire smoke events** (August-October): AQI can spike to 150-300 (Unhealthy) for 1-3 weeks
- **Winter/Spring:** Generally Good (AQI 20-40)
- PM2.5 currently meets WHO annual guidelines

### Flight Path

- Folsom is NOT under any major airport flight path
- Mather Airport (decommissioned military, now general aviation) is 10 miles southwest - minimal impact
- Sacramento International Airport is 35 miles northwest - no flight path impact
- Very quiet skies relative to Bay Area locations

### Growing Zone & Food Production

- **USDA Hardiness Zone:** 9b (minimum temps 25-30F)
- **Growing Season:** ~300 days (one of the longest in California)
- **Climate:** Mediterranean - hot dry summers (100F+ common), mild wet winters
- **Excellent for:** Tomatoes, peppers, citrus, figs, stone fruits, grapes, olives, year-round gardening
- **Solar Potential:** Outstanding - 269 sunny days/year, south-facing roofs receive peak solar radiation
- **Lot sizes** of 0.15-0.23 acres provide significant backyard gardening/food production potential

## Section 3e: Immigration & Legal

### Sanctuary Status

**California is a sanctuary STATE** under the California Values Act (SB 54, 2017):
- Restricts all California law enforcement from cooperating with federal immigration authorities
- Local/state agencies cannot transfer individuals or share info with ICE without judicial warrant
- Prohibits use of local resources for immigration enforcement
- Applies to K-12 schools, community colleges, and CSU systems
- Schools cannot inquire about legal status or release student info to immigration authorities

**Sacramento County** aligns with state policy. While Folsom itself has not adopted separate sanctuary city legislation, it operates under the statewide protections of SB 54.

### Political Climate Toward Immigrants

- Sacramento County leans strongly Democratic (+18.8% Democratic advantage)
- 44.6% registered Democrats vs. 25.8% Republicans
- County voted 61% for Democratic presidential candidate in 2020
- **Folsom specifically** is more politically mixed/moderate-conservative compared to Sacramento proper
- However, the California Values Act protections apply regardless of local politics
- 20.2% of Folsom residents are foreign-born (significant immigrant population)
- 19.9% Asian population suggests strong acceptance of immigrant communities

### Immigrant Support Organizations

- **Sacramento Immigration Coalition** - regional advocacy and legal services
- **Opening Doors** (Sacramento) - refugee and immigrant services
- **Sacramento Area Council of Governments (SACOG)** - inclusive planning
- **UC Davis Immigration Law Clinic** (45 min away)
- **Iranian-American community organizations** in Sacramento metro area provide cultural support
- Multiple mosques and Islamic community centers in the Folsom/Sacramento area

### Diversity of Local Government/Police Force
