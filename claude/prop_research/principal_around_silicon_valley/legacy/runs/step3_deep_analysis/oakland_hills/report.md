### Vaccination Policy Deep Dive (CRITICAL)

**California Law (SB 277, effective 2016):**
- Eliminated personal belief exemptions for vaccination
- Only MEDICAL exemptions remain for school enrollment
- Applies to ALL public AND private schools, daycares, and in-person programs

**California SB 276 (2019):**
- Added state oversight of medical exemptions
- Physicians issuing more than 5 exemptions/year are reviewed
- California Department of Public Health can revoke questionable exemptions

**Options for Unvaccinated Children:**

1. **Home-Based Private School (Homeschool)**
   - California allows parents to file as a private school (PSA affidavit)
   - NO vaccination requirement for home-based private schools
   - File annual Private School Affidavit with CA Department of Education
   - Full parental control over curriculum

2. **Independent Study Programs (ISPs)**
   - Public charter ISPs may NOT require vaccination (varies by program)
   - Some independent study charters operate as "non-classroom-based"
   - Examples: Connecting Waters Charter (East Bay), Epic Charter, California Virtual Academy
   - IMPORTANT: Check individual program policies; some still require vaccination records

3. **Homeschool Co-ops (Not Schools)**
   - Co-ops that are not classified as "schools" do not fall under SB 277
   - East Bay has active homeschool community
   - Groups meet in parks, community centers, private homes
   - No vaccination requirement for informal co-ops

4. **Medical Exemption Route**
   - Requires legitimate medical contraindication
   - Must be from licensed physician (MD or DO)
   - Subject to CDPH review under SB 276
   - Difficult to obtain without genuine medical reason

### Best Schools for Unvaccinated Children

1. **Home-Based Private School (Homeschool) with local co-op participation** - Most flexible, no vaccination requirement
2. **Connecting Waters Charter School** (Independent Study) - East Bay charter offering non-classroom-based education

## Section 3d: Survivability & Environment

### Water District & Supply

**District:** East Bay Municipal Utility District (EBMUD)
- Serves: ~1.4 million people across 331 square miles
- Primary source: Mokelumne River watershed (~90% of supply)
- Storage: Camanche and Pardee reservoirs + 4 terminal reservoirs in East Bay hills
- Capacity: Water rights for up to 325 million gallons daily
- Infrastructure: 91 miles of transmission aqueducts, 4,100 miles of water mains, 6 treatment plants
- Backup: Sacramento River facility for multi-dry years
- Drought history: Declarations in 2008, 2014, 2022 (managed with rationing)

**Outlook: ADEQUATE**
EBMUD is one of the most reliable water utilities in California. Multiple source redundancy (Mokelumne + Sacramento River backup), substantial reservoir capacity, and proactive drought planning. While drought declarations have occurred, the district has never faced a critical supply failure.

### Well Drilling Feasibility

- **Regulations:** Oakland Hills is served by EBMUD; private wells are uncommon and require permits
- **Depth to water table:** Varies significantly in hills terrain (100-300+ feet)
- **Feasibility:** LOW - Rocky hillside geology, steep terrain, and adequate municipal supply make wells impractical and unnecessary
- **Recommendation:** Rely on EBMUD; water storage tanks are more practical for emergency preparedness

### Wildfire Risk

**CAL FIRE FHSZ Designation: VERY HIGH FIRE HAZARD SEVERITY ZONE (VHFHSZ)**

The Oakland Hills are designated as a Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone by CAL FIRE. This is the HIGHEST possible designation.

**Historical Context - 1991 Oakland Hills Firestorm:**
- Burned: 1,520 acres
- Destroyed: 2,843 single-family homes + 437 apartments/condos (~3,280 structures)
- Deaths: 25
- Injuries: 150
- Damage: $1.5 billion (1991 dollars; ~$3.07 billion today)
- Rate: One house destroyed every 11 seconds at peak
- Cause: Diablo winds (65-70 mph) + dry conditions

**Post-1991 Improvements:**
- Standard fire hydrants throughout Oakland Hills
- Enhanced wildland firefighting equipment
- New water cisterns and dedicated hills fire station
- Improved radio communications
- Vegetation management programs
- 2008 test: Similar brush fire at nearly identical location contained to 2 acres within 90 minutes

**Current Mitigation:**
- Oakland Wildfire Prevention Assessment District (annual property assessment)
- Mandatory vegetation management/defensible space requirements
- Friends of Joaquin Miller Park volunteers work with CAL FIRE on fuel reduction
- Stricter building codes for new construction in fire zones
- Goat grazing programs for hillside fuel reduction

**Insurance Implications:**
- Homeowner's insurance increasingly difficult/expensive in VHFHSZ
- Some carriers have stopped writing new policies in Oakland Hills
- California FAIR Plan (insurer of last resort) available but expensive
- Annual premiums may be $5,000-$15,000+ for hills homes
- This is a SIGNIFICANT ongoing cost consideration

## Section 3e: Immigration & Legal

### Sanctuary City/County Status

**Oakland: DECLARED SANCTUARY CITY**

Oakland has long-standing sanctuary city policies. Key points:
- Oakland passed sanctuary resolutions prohibiting city employees from assisting federal immigration enforcement
- Policy prohibits Oakland police from inquiring about immigration status
- City refuses ICE detainer requests
- City Administrator has publicly reaffirmed sanctuary policies (2025)
- Alameda County also does not comply with ICE detainer requests

**California State Protection:**
- California Values Act (SB 54, 2017) - state-level sanctuary law
- Limits state/local law enforcement cooperation with federal immigration authorities
- Applies statewide regardless of local policy

## Executive Summary

Oakland Hills (Montclair/Rockridge/Joaquin Miller/Redwood Heights) offers a compelling combination of natural beauty, strong community character, and relative affordability within the Bay Area. The neighborhoods provide excellent parks and outdoor recreation, a charming village center, good schools by OUSD standards, and strong sanctuary protections. The 40-minute commute to Downtown San Jose is manageable but not ideal. The CRITICAL concern is wildfire risk -- the entire area sits in a Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone with catastrophic historical precedent (1991 fire). Insurance costs and availability are a growing problem. The Brazilian community is virtually nonexistent locally, which is a significant lifestyle gap. Schools work well for the family if homeschooling is chosen (co-ops, ISPs), but in-person options require vaccination under California law.
