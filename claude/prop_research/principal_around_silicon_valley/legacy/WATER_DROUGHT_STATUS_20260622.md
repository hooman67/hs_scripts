# Water and Drought Status: Marin, Sonoma, and Napa Counties
## California Property Research — June 22, 2026

**Research Date:** June 22, 2026  
**Data Collection Method:** Multi-source web research (DWR, USGS, CNRFC, county water agencies)  
**Confidence Level:** High (primary sources); Medium (county-specific data — limited recent updates available)

---

## Executive Summary

**Current Status:** Northern Bay Area counties (Marin, Sonoma, Napa) are in **STABLE to STRONG water position** as of mid-June 2026, following recovery from earlier dry period concerns. No active state or county emergency proclamations related to drought or water shortage identified for June 2026.

**Key Finding:** Marin Water's seven reservoirs at **93.12% capacity** (74,094 acre-feet) as of June 15, 2026—approximately 11% above historical average for this date. This is the most recent confirmed data point available.

**Statewide Context:** California drought remains "a recurring feature of climate" (DWR), but current conditions do not meet emergency thresholds. State Water Project allocation at 45% of requested supplies (May 2026), up from 30% earlier in the year. Lake Oroville recreational facilities reopening as water levels permit.

## STATEWIDE CONTEXT (California DWR)

### Current Conditions & Recent Updates (June 2026)

**State Water Project Allocation:** 45% (as of May 2026) — up from 30% earlier in year

**Snowpack Status:**
- Record-high March temperatures eliminated California's snowpack months ahead of schedule
- April 2026 surveys: No measurable snow remaining at Phillips Station (traditional monitoring location)
- Snowpack normally accounts for one-third of state's annual water supply

**Water Planning Initiative:**
- California Water Plan Advisory Committee inaugural meeting: May 2026
- Target: Statewide water supply goal of 9 million acre-feet by 2040 (Senate Bill 72)

**Lake Oroville:**
- Multiple status updates published June 2026
- Trailheads reopening as water levels permit (June 19, 2026 announcement)
- Water levels sufficiently high to support recreational facility operations

**Groundwater Management:**
- Final Groundwater Bulletin 118 Update for 2025 released
- Local agencies progressing toward sustainable groundwater management compliance
- North San Joaquin Water Conservation District Lakso Recharge Basin demonstrates year-round groundwater recharge capability

**Drought Response Status:**
- No statewide emergency proclamations identified for June 2026
- Previous emergency measures (groundwater recharge CEQA suspensions, temporary water rights permitting) concluded due to "improved conditions"
- Drought characterized as "recurring feature of climate" rather than current emergency

**Sources:** DWR News Releases and Blog (water.ca.gov), DWR Current Conditions portal

**Statewide Context:**

- DWR implementing CASGEM Program (California Statewide Groundwater Elevation Monitoring)

## KEY TAKEAWAYS FOR PROPERTY RESEARCH

### Implications for Marin, Sonoma, Napa Home Purchase Evaluation

1. **Water Security:** All three counties currently stable; no emergency restrictions. Marin in strongest position (93% reservoir capacity).

2. **Well/Groundwater Access:**
   - Marin: Not heavily dependent on groundwater; surface water system reliable
   - Sonoma: Russian River system provides primary supply; groundwater sustainability being managed under SGMA
   - Napa: Groundwater important component; Valley Subbasin undergoing sustainability planning; check specific property's source

3. **Future Outlook:**
   - Snowpack reduction (now eliminated by March) increases reliance on winter precipitation and storage
   - Statewide planning for 9 million acre-feet supply by 2040 (may affect allocation to regions)
   - Climate variability intensifying (per DWR); long-term resilience planning ongoing

4. **Property-Specific Recommendations:**
   - For Marin properties: Excellent water security; confirm local water district service
   - For Sonoma properties: Verify connection to Russian River system or local groundwater sustainability plan
   - For Napa properties: Confirm water source (well vs. district); review groundwater sustainability fee implications; check Valley Subbasin plan status

5. **Red Flags (Currently Not Present):**
   - No emergency water restrictions
   - No active drought proclamations
   - No acute groundwater depletion warnings for study region
   - Flood risk low (all forecast points below critical stages)

6. **Yellow Flags (Monitor):**
   - Early snowpack elimination could impact late-summer/fall water availability in drought years
   - Sonoma/Napa groundwater sustainability planning in progress — may affect future costs/access
   - Statewide allocation at 45% (not maximum) — potential for tightening if dry years continue
