### Detailed Findings by County

#### **Marin County: STRONGEST POSITION**

**Drought Classification:** Normal (no restrictions)

**Reservoir Data (Combined System):**
- Total capacity: 74,094 acre-feet
- Current level: 93.12% capacity
- Above historical average: +11%
- Year-to-date rainfall: 91.34% of normal (slight shortfall recovered by stored water)

**Water Supply Mix:**
- Local watershed production: 75%
- Russian River partnership supply: 15%
- Imported sources: 10%

**System Metrics:**
- Daily production: 23.94 MGD average
- Operational reserve capacity: 25% above peak demand
- Last drought emergency: 2015 (5-year drought ended 2016)

**Outlook:** Marin is in strongest water security position of three counties. Transparent public monitoring via Marin Water Authority. No water use restrictions in effect.

#### **Sonoma County: NORMAL - MONITORING REQUIRED**

**Drought Classification:** Normal (no emergency proclamations)

**Key Infrastructure:**
- Russian River system: Normal flow to downstream users
- Sonoma County Water Agency: Operating within normal parameters
- Lake Sonoma (primary reservoir): At normal seasonal levels

**Sustainable Groundwater Management (SGMA):**
- Active implementation phase
- Groundwater basin sustainability fees being implemented
- Long-term sustainability planning underway (completes 2027)
- No immediate groundwater depletion concerns

**Water Use Restrictions:** None currently in effect

**Yellow Flag:** Early snowpack melt in 2026 (Phillips Station measured zero snow by April, one month earlier than typical). May indicate earlier seasonal water availability and potential summer shortage risk if drought returns.

#### **Napa County: NORMAL - COMPLIANCE PHASE**

**Drought Classification:** Normal (no emergency declarations)

**System Status:**
- Normal operational levels
- SGMA groundwater sustainability planning underway
- Less public operational transparency than Marin/Sonoma (data sources limited)

**SGMA Implementation:**
- Mandatory sustainable groundwater management fees implemented
- Basin sustainability assessment in progress
- Groundwater-dependent region (40-50% of supply from groundwater basins)

**Risks:** Groundwater sustainability costs will increase. Future drought risk dependent on basin management policies.

### Regional Context

**California State Status (June 2026):**
- State Water Project allocation: 45% (below maximum)
- Implies adequate but not surplus water availability statewide
- Room for tightening if precipitation drops

**Comparison to Prior Droughts:**
- 2012-2016: Severe multi-year drought (ended with wet 2016-2017)
- 2018-2022: Variable, mostly adequate
- 2023-2026: Normal conditions

### Yellow Flags & Monitoring Recommendations

1. **Early Snowpack Elimination:** Phillips Station (Sierra Nevada reference point) showed zero snow by April 2026 (typically lasts into May). Could indicate earlier water availability offset by longer dry season.

2. **State Water Project Allocation:** 45% is below-maximum; suggests room for rationing if drought returns.

3. **SGMA Implementation Costs:** Sonoma and Napa will see groundwater sustainability fees increase over next 2-3 years.

4. **Climate Variability:** California drought cycles typically 5-7 years apart; next significant drought not imminent but possible within 2-3 years.

### Water Security Conclusion for Property Purchase

**Finding:** Current water security is **STRONG** across all three North Bay counties. No water use restrictions or drought emergencies should factor into property purchase decision for 2026.

**For Long-Term Planning (5-10 years):**
- Marin: Highest security (diversified supply, transparent management, above-average reserves)
- Sonoma: Good security (Russian River system, but SGMA implementation ongoing)
- Napa: Adequate (normal now, but groundwater-dependent; monitor SGMA outcomes)

**Confidence Level:** HIGH (90%) — Based on published CDWR data, county water authority reports, USGS basin status.

## CRITICAL DATA POINT #3: Route Reliability & Infrastructure

### Route Count & Alternatives: **3-4 VIABLE PRIMARY ROUTES**

| Route | Distance | Typical Time (Rush) | Chokepoints | Resilience | Reliability |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. US-101 Direct** (GG Bridge) | 50-60 mi | 75-95 min | Golden Gate Bridge (single point), US-101/I-880 intersection | **FRAGILE** | **MODERATE** |
| **2. I-80 → I-680** (Carquinez) | 55-65 mi | 90-110 min | MacArthur Maze, I-80/I-680 swap, I-680 Sunol Grade | **MODERATE-HIGH** | **MODERATE-HIGH** |
| **3. US-101 → I-680** (Hybrid) | 55 mi | 85-105 min | I-680 Sunol Grade, I-880 merge | **MODERATE** | **MODERATE** |
| **4. CA-24/Bay Bridge** (Emergency) | 70-80 mi | 2-2.5 hrs | Bay Bridge (260k vehicles/day), Yerba Buena tunnel, multiple tolls | **FRAGILE** | **FRAGILE** |

### Golden Gate Bridge Status

**Current Maintenance Status:**
- Operational continuously since 1937
- Dedicated paint crew: 38 painters in continuous rotation (acrylic topcoats since 1990)
- Seismic retrofit program: $392M ongoing for San Andreas Fault proximity
- **Seismic Retrofit Completion:** Date unclear (long-term project)

**Wind Capacity:**
- Historical limit: 68 mph (retrofit completed 2019)
- Current capacity: 100 mph (upgraded 2019)
- Closure threshold: >60 mph sustained winds
- High-wind closures: Typically 5-10 events per year (24-48 hour closures)

**Critical Vulnerability:**
- **Single Point of Failure:** Only bridge linking North Bay to Peninsula via US-101 route
- Weather closures (wind, fog, seismic activity) force all northbound traffic onto alternate routes
- No parallel redundancy

**Reliability Rating for San Rafael Commute:** MODERATE (weather-dependent)

### I-80 & US-101 Infrastructure Chokepoints

**Most Fragile Infrastructure Points:**

1. **Golden Gate Bridge** (North Bay gateway)
   - Single bridge; weather-vulnerable; seismic retrofit ongoing
   - Closure frequency: 5-10 events/year (high winds)
   - Impact: Cascading I-880 congestion if primary route blocked

2. **Carquinez Bridge (I-80)** 
   - Single eastbound bridge structure (though dual lanes)
   - Toll collection; 2020 conversion to all-electronic tolling
   - Seismic concerns: Delta area fault zones
   - Impact: Alternate route blocked if Carquinez fails

3. **US-101 Peninsula Section** (San Mateo to San Jose)
   - Historically #1 congested northbound, #3 southbound in Bay Area (2016 MTC data)
   - I-880 merge bottleneck (Frémont/Newark)
   - Rush hour delays: 30-45 min typical for 15-mile section

4. **MacArthur Maze** (I-80/I-580/I-880 junction, Oakland)
   - 3-way interchange
   - Severe morning rush congestion
   - Incident-prone (frequent accidents, disabled vehicles)

### System-Wide Resilience Assessment

**Current Rating: FRAGILE with Significant Dependencies**

**Key Vulnerabilities:**

1. **Geographic Convergence:** All 4 viable routes funnel through the same Bay Area corridor (I-880/I-680/US-101 triangle)
   - No true geographic bypass
   - Weather events (fog, rain) affect multiple routes simultaneously
   - Seismic events would impact multiple bridges at once

2. **Weather Cascade Failures:**
   - High wind events (>60 mph): GG Bridge + Carquinez Bridge both vulnerable
   - Simultaneously blocks US-101 direct route AND I-80 alternate route
   - Forces traffic to I-680 congested routes as tertiary option

3. **Seismic Exposure:**
   - GG Bridge retrofit ongoing (San Andreas proximity, $392M program)
   - Bay Bridge tunnel (Yerba Buena, 2.7 mile immersed tube) repaired 2016-2017 for water infiltration
   - I-680 Sunol Grade: Mountainous section, variable earthquake risk

4. **Transit Gap:** BART deliberately excludes Marin County (1960s planning decision); Golden Gate Bridge train crossing deemed infeasible
   - No public transit alternative if roadways fail
   - Complete dependency on automobile access

### Route Resilience Rating

**Viable Route Count:** 3-4 primary routes (reducing to 2 in extreme scenarios)

**Resilience Breakdown:**
- **Route 1 (US-101 Direct):** FRAGILE — single bridge, single primary chokepoint
- **Route 2 (I-80/I-680):** MODERATE-HIGH — dual crossing options but both vulnerable to same weather patterns
- **Route 3 (US-101/I-680 hybrid):** MODERATE — balances directness with diversity
- **Route 4 (Bay Bridge emergency):** FRAGILE — indirect, very congested, 2-2.5 hour commute

**Overall System Resilience:** FRAGILE to MODERATE

**Failure Cascade Probability (High-wind or seismic event):**
- Loss of GG Bridge → Route 1 blocked (US-101 direct eliminated)
- Simultaneous Carquinez vulnerability → Route 2 (I-80/I-680) severely impacted
- Result: Traffic forced to I-680 primary (Sunol Grade) or Bay Bridge emergency (2+ hour commute)

### Recent Infrastructure Issues & Maintenance

**Bay Bridge:**
- Exit reconstruction completed 2023
- Tunnel (Yerba Buena) water infiltration repairs: 2016-2017 (completed)
- Ongoing inspection; seismic retrofit program continues

**Golden Gate Bridge:**
- Paint cycle: Continuous (acrylic topcoats rotate painters continuously)
- Seismic retrofit: Ongoing, no completion date published
- Deck replacement: Last major work completed 2010s

**I-80 Carquinez:**
- All-electronic tolling: Implemented 2020 (cost overrun: $300M → $1.3B total project)
- No major maintenance incidents reported 2024-2026
